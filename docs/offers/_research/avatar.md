## The one truth that orders everything

The buyer's deepest fear is not price and not features. It is **personal accountability with a name on it.** The product's own offer doc says it cleanest: _"Either way, you are the one who answers. You answer when the auditor calls. You answer when the board asks where a dollar went. You answer when a deadline slips. That is the real fear. Not the price."_ (`docs/offers/founder-setup-offer.md`).

External research confirms the mechanism. The ED is the person who _"signs the management representation letter during audits"_ and is often _"carrying too much of the financial decision-making alone"_ ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/assurance/audit-readiness-for-nonprofits-best-practices-for-controllers-and-cfos)). The dollar amount is small relative to the exposure: one missed report is _"the single most common audit finding"_ ([Grassi](https://www.grassiadvisors.com/blog/grant-compliance-is-a-growing-risk-area-5-priorities-to-protect-nonprofit-funding/)).

**Hormozi read:** the Dream Outcome here is not "more grants" — it is the _removal of personal, public exposure._ Status protection and likelihood-of-success dominate the value equation; time and effort reduction are the proof that the protection is real. Lead with pull (fundraising/cash/board), differentiate with compliance (the moat) — matching the internal sequencing thesis.

## AVATAR 1 (PRIMARY) — The Executive / Development Director

### (a) Identity & context

- **Title:** Executive Director or Development Director at a $500K–$10M nonprofit. Often the same person wears both hats in a 5–25-person org.
- **Demographic/psychographic:** Mission-driven, non-technical-but-sophisticated, risk-averse, time-poor. Frequently arrived after a _failed_ Salesforce or Blackbaud rollout, or is drowning in spreadsheets. Works at a desk during business hours; checks the app between meetings on a phone (`.impeccable.md`).
- **Emotional baseline:** wants **confidence and control, not delight** (`.impeccable.md`). The interface is meant to be _"a source of calm in a chaotic nonprofit environment."_
- **The committee behind them:** development leadership + finance/compliance + the ED/COO who owns total cost of ownership (`docs/grantpipe offering.md`). The ED is the one who feels all three.

### (b) Dream outcome — in their words

> _"I walk into the board meeting and I already know the answer to 'where did the money go,' because it's one screen, not a weekend of merging spreadsheets. I never get the call I can't answer. When the auditor emails, I send a link, not a panic."_

This maps directly to the product's promise of _"a board-ready snapshot tying donors → grants → restrictions → reporting dates"_ (`docs/grantpipe offering.md`) and the offer's close: _"Stop dreading the setup… Leave with your grants live."_

### (c) Top 5 pains / fears / frustrations (verbatim-style)

1. **Board-meeting scramble.** Real-world: EDs _"find themselves scrambling to put together board reports minutes before meetings, or spending hours loading reports… board members aren't even reading"_ ([Gail Perry Group / search synthesis](https://gailperrygroup.com/fund-development-committee-go-wrong/)). Generating board-ready reports in QuickBooks _"often involves exporting data to Excel, manually formatting columns, creating pivot tables, merging multiple spreadsheets… a process that consumes hours before every board meeting"_ ([Mahoney CPA](https://mahoneycpa.com/best-practices-for-tracking-restricted-funds-in-quickbooks-online-for-nonprofit-organizations/)).
2. **The deadline that slips.** _"Even one missed deadline or lost funder contact can derail months of work… everything across scattered spreadsheets and sticky notes"_ ([Grant Frog](https://grantfrog.com/5-best-grants-management-software/)). Late/inaccurate reporting is _"the single most common audit finding"_ ([Grassi](https://www.grassiadvisors.com/blog/grant-compliance-is-a-growing-risk-area-5-priorities-to-protect-nonprofit-funding/)).
3. **Two sources of truth that never agree.** _"Many growing nonprofits track donor restrictions in spreadsheets disconnected from their accounting systems, creating a disconnect between development tracking and proper accounting"_ ([search synthesis / NetSuite](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/crm/nonprofit-donor-management.shtml)). This is the #1 ranked pain in the internal roadmap (P1, "two sources of truth").
4. **The implementation that ate a year.** From a Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud reviewer, _Director of Operations_: **"It took us a year to get it really set up and functioning the way we'd envisioned."** And an _Executive Director_: **"To really setup well an outside consultant is needed which is very expensive."** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/265078/Salesforceorg-Nonprofit-Cloud/reviews/)).
5. **Knowledge that walks out the door.** The offer doc names it: _"this work lives in one person's spreadsheet… in one person's inbox. When that person leaves, the knowledge leaves too."_ Staff turnover is one of the three structural churn/retention drivers (`docs/grantpipe offering.md`).

### (d) Dream-outcome dimensions (Hormozi)

| Dimension                 | What this avatar actually wants                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Status**                | Look competent and _in control_ to the board and the auditor; never be the person who "lost track of a dollar." This is the dominant lever.                                 |
| **Time-to-result**        | Board snapshot in minutes, not a lost weekend; setup in one session, not a year. The product leans here: _"complete a migration sprint and produce one compliance report."_ |
| **Likelihood of success** | "Audit-ready by default," human-confirmed AI ("you check each one"), evidence packages already assembled — they need to _believe_ it will hold up.                          |
| **Effort & sacrifice**    | "No consultants required"; "no spreadsheet cleanup"; unlimited users so they don't ration access.                                                                           |

### (e) What they tried and why it failed

- **Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud:** powerful but _"not intuitive AT ALL… requires an incredible amount of time"_ and _"can do anything…if you can afford to pay someone a lot of money to set it up"_ (_Grants Associate_, [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/265078/Salesforceorg-Nonprofit-Cloud/reviews/)). Verdict: _"Overall, I would choose another program for nonprofits."_ **Failure mode: consultant dependency + time sink.**
- **Spreadsheets + a donor CRM + email reminders** (the literal system GrantPipe replaces, per `.impeccable.md`): fragile, fragmented, no proactive deadline trigger. **Failure mode: nothing watches the deadline for you.**
- **GrantHub** users are a live displacement cohort — Foundant sunset GrantHub/GrantHub Pro **Jan 31, 2026**, forcing a workflow replacement exactly when ROI-in-30-days matters most ([Nerdisa](https://nerdisa.com/granthub/); internal `docs/feature-opportunities-2026-06.md`). **Failure mode: the tool itself disappeared.**

### (f) Cost of inaction

- **Financial:** hours-per-board-cycle of staff labor; consultant retainers (Salesforce/Blackbaud); audit adjustments and a slower, more expensive audit when _"weak controls make audits slower, more expensive, and more stressful"_ ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/assurance/audit-readiness-for-nonprofits-best-practices-for-controllers-and-cfos)).
- **Emotional:** chronic dread before every board meeting and audit; _"unnecessary stress and chaos… scrambling… burnout"_ ([Grantsights](https://grantsights.com/blog/grant-audit-preparation-guide)).
- **Status:** the public answerability moment — the auditor call, the board's "where did the dollar go," the slipped deadline. This is the loss GrantPipe is actually insuring against.

## AVATAR 2 (SECONDARY) — The Finance / Operations Director

### (a) Identity & context

- **Title:** Finance Director, Controller, or Director of Operations & Finance. Often the only finance person, or finance + a part-time bookkeeper, supported by an annual outside CPA.
- **Profile:** More technical than the ED on accounting (knows ASC 958, fund accounting, functional expense), less technical on software. Lives in QuickBooks Online + Excel. Owns restricted-fund tracking, month-end close, and audit prep. GrantPipe explicitly does **not** replace QuickBooks — it _reads_ from QBO read-only (`docs/offers/founder-setup-offer.md`), which is exactly what makes it safe to adopt for this avatar.

### (b) Dream outcome — in their words

> _"Every restricted dollar reconciles to the donor's intent without me rebuilding it by hand at year-end. When the auditor asks for the release schedule, it's already there. Month-end stops being a manual archaeology dig through 2,000 transactions."_

Matches the product's restricted-fund accounting + restriction lifecycle (terms/additions/releases) + rollforward and _"see where every restricted dollar went, anytime"_ (`docs/offers/founder-setup-offer.md`).

### (c) Top 5 pains / fears / frustrations (verbatim-style)

1. **The month-8 horror story.** _"A finance director discovered in month 8 that none of their grant-funded expenses were properly classified, facing manual review of 2,400 transactions to reconstruct grant expenditure reports for five different funders"_ ([Mahoney CPA](https://mahoneycpa.com/best-practices-for-tracking-restricted-funds-in-quickbooks-online-for-nonprofit-organizations/)). This is the single most visceral finance quote in the research.
2. **Restricted funds are the hardest job they do.** _"Managing restricted funds is one of the most challenging accounting tasks nonprofit organizations face, and a donor restriction overlooked in a spreadsheet can trigger a last-minute scramble at year-end, resulting in audit adjustments when balances don't reconcile with donor records"_ ([Mahoney CPA](https://mahoneycpa.com/best-practices-for-tracking-restricted-funds-in-quickbooks-online-for-nonprofit-organizations/)).
3. **QuickBooks wasn't built for this.** _"Without automated fund balance enforcement, nonprofits risk accidentally spending restricted funds on unintended purposes"_ — the QBO community itself debates _"CLASS or LOCATION for tracking Restricted Funds"_ with no clean answer ([QuickBooks Community](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/reports-and-accounting/class-or-location-for-tracking-restricted-funds/00/1433234)).
4. **Misclassification = audit finding.** _"Failing to properly track and honor those restrictions is one of the most common and most serious audit findings"_; common mistakes are _"misclassifying restricted and unrestricted funds, incorrectly allocating functional expenses"_ ([KLR](https://kahnlitwin.com/blogs/mission-matters-blog/5-costly-nonprofit-audit-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them)). This is why the internal roadmap puts the Restriction Auto-Classifier as the _"data-quality floor everything downstream sits on."_
5. **Enterprise accounting tools are out of reach.** Sage Intacct / MIP / Financial Edge do this — but at a price and implementation this org _"can't afford… and is too complex for Aplos, [so] this work is done in spreadsheets and outsourced to a CPA"_ (`docs/feature-opportunities-2026-06.md`). Financial Edge itself draws: _"the platform seems dated and somewhat clunky"_ and _"expensive with high costs for extras"_ ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/27468/Financial-Edge-NXT/reviews/)).

### (d) Dream-outcome dimensions (Hormozi)

| Dimension                 | What this avatar wants                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Status**                | Be the finance person whose books _survive the audit clean_ — no adjustments, no findings. Professional credibility with the CPA.                                                                                           |
| **Time-to-result**        | Kill the year-end reclassification dig; month-end close that doesn't require reconstructing intent from memory.                                                                                                             |
| **Likelihood of success** | Automated fund-balance enforcement and correct net-asset classification _at gift entry_, so errors never accumulate. Get the CPA-credibility traps exactly right (expended-vs-awarded, elective de minimis, $50K MTDC cap). |
| **Effort & sacrifice**    | Reconcile _to donor intent automatically_ instead of manually; read from QBO so they don't rip out the GL they trust.                                                                                                       |

### (e) What they tried and why it failed

- **QuickBooks Online classes/locations + spreadsheets:** no fund-balance enforcement, restriction releases done by hand, board reports require Excel pivot gymnastics. **Failure: QBO doesn't model donor intent or restriction lifecycle.**
- **Sage Intacct / MIP / Blackbaud Financial Edge:** capable but priced and implemented for orgs 5–10× larger; _"quote-only, consultant-heavy"_ (`docs/feature-opportunities-2026-06.md`). **Failure: out of budget and over-engineered for a $500K–$10M org.**
- **Aplos / entry-level fund accounting:** _"too complex for Aplos"_ in the other direction once real restriction lifecycle + allocation enter. **Failure: too shallow for multi-funder complexity.**

### (f) Cost of inaction

- **Financial:** audit adjustments, a longer (billable) audit, and the labor of reconstructing classification — the 2,400-transaction dig is real lost weeks ([Mahoney CPA](https://mahoneycpa.com/best-practices-for-tracking-restricted-funds-in-quickbooks-online-for-nonprofit-organizations/)).
- **Emotional:** the dread of discovering in month 8 that the whole year is mis-coded; year-end scramble anxiety.
- **Status:** an audit finding is a permanent mark on the finance function's competence — and it lands on the ED's representation letter too.

## AVATAR 3 (TERTIARY) — The Grant Consultant / Small Agency (multi-client)

### (a) Identity & context

- **Title:** Independent grant consultant or owner of a 1–10-client grant agency. Bills clients for deliverable grant work; their tooling is directly tied to billable leverage.
- **Profile:** Higher willingness-to-pay than a single nonprofit because the tool multiplies their capacity across clients. Both Instrumentl and Grantable explicitly build consultant/agency SKUs (Grantable's Agency Hub add-on at $300/mo), confirming the segment pays (`docs/grantpipe offering.md`). They need per-client isolation and client-facing reporting.

### (b) Dream outcome — in their words

> _"I run every client's deadlines, funds, and reports from one place, each client sees only their own data, and I look like a firm three times my size. I stop losing a deadline in a sea of tabs and I hand the client a portal instead of a PDF."_

### (c) Top 5 pains / fears / frustrations (verbatim-style)

1. **Deadlines lost across clients.** _"Even one missed deadline or lost funder contact can derail months of work, especially when you're tracking everything across scattered spreadsheets and sticky notes"_ ([Grant Frog](https://grantfrog.com/5-best-grants-management-software/)). For a consultant, a missed client deadline is a lost _client._
2. **No clean multi-client workspace.** The need is explicit: _"manage multiple clients' portfolios in one account while giving each client access only to their specific data"_ ([Grant Frog](https://grantfrog.com/5-best-grants-management-software/)). Most stitch this together in spreadsheets per client.
3. **The GrantHub rug-pull.** Consultants who standardized client delivery on GrantHub now must re-platform — _"GrantHub Pro was sunset… January 31, 2026… It is no longer available as a product"_ ([search synthesis](https://nerdisa.com/granthub/)). A live, time-boxed displacement moment.
4. **"Searching in the dark" before centralization.** A real Instrumentl reviewer: **"Before, I was searching in the dark. Now, I've never missed a single deadline."** ([Capterra synthesis](https://www.capterra.com/p/233384/Instrumentl/reviews/)). That's the relief state the consultant is buying.
5. **Looking small / unprofessional to clients.** _(composite, from agency-tooling review themes)_ — without client portals and consolidated reporting, the consultant emails PDFs and looks like a solo operator instead of a firm; the agency tooling they want exists precisely to _"standardize delivery: templates, client portals, consolidated reporting"_ (`docs/grantpipe offering.md`, Proposal C).

### (d) Dream-outcome dimensions (Hormozi)

| Dimension                 | What this avatar wants                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Status**                | Look like a bigger, more systematized firm; deliver a client-facing portal, not a PDF. Win and retain clients on professionalism. |
| **Time-to-result**        | Onboard a new client fast; one dashboard across the whole book of business.                                                       |
| **Likelihood of success** | Never miss a client deadline — _"never missed a single deadline."_ Their reputation _is_ the product.                             |
| **Effort & sacrifice**    | Stop maintaining N spreadsheets for N clients; metered by _client workspace_, not per-seat.                                       |

### (e) What they tried and why it failed

- **Per-client spreadsheets + a shared calendar:** doesn't scale past a handful of clients; no isolation; deadlines slip in tab-overload. **Failure: no multi-client model.**
- **GrantHub / GrantHub Pro:** worked, then was sunset Jan 2026. **Failure: discontinued.**
- **Instrumentl:** strong on discovery + deadlines and _has_ a consultant plan, but it's discovery-first (funder search/990 insights), not a unified donor→grant→fund→GL ledger. **Failure for the compliance-delivery consultant: no restricted-fund / finance depth to deliver with.**

### (f) Cost of inaction

- **Financial:** a missed client deadline can mean a lost retainer or a non-renewed contract — direct revenue.
- **Emotional:** the constant low-grade fear of a deadline buried in another client's tab.
- **Status:** reputation is the entire asset; one public miss with a client damages the referral engine the practice runs on.

## Cross-avatar synthesis — how to wield this

**Shared dream outcome (the through-line):** _"I am never the person who can't answer the question."_ For the ED it's the board/auditor; for the Finance Director it's the clean audit; for the consultant it's the client deadline. All three are buying **answerability under pressure** — confidence and control, not delight (`.impeccable.md`).

**Where the wallet is already open vs. the moat (do not confuse them):**

- **Pull (open wallet, daily urgency):** fundraising, cash visibility, board reporting. _Most $500K–$10M orgs are NOT subject to a single audit_ (triggers at $1M federal awards **expended**, 2 CFR 200.501) — so compliance alone won't open the wallet (`docs/feature-opportunities-2026-06.md`).
- **Differentiation (the moat):** the unified donor → grant → fund → GL → report ledger in one schema, audit-ready by default, no consultants. _"Lead with pull; differentiate with compliance."_

**Hormozi Value-Equation positioning (per avatar emphasis):**

- ED → maximize **Perceived Likelihood** (audit-ready by default, human-confirmed AI) and **Status** (never the one who can't answer).
- Finance → maximize **Likelihood** (auto-classification, fund-balance enforcement, CPA-credible numbers) and minimize **Effort** (no year-end reclassification dig).
- Consultant → maximize **Status** (look like a bigger firm) and minimize **Time/Effort** (one workspace, client portals).

**Proof and honesty constraints (binding):** founder Angel Campa is a solo builder with **no nonprofit-sector experience** — write from the builder perspective; **never fabricate testimonials, user counts, or social proof** (`.impeccable.md`). The strongest honest emotional asset already written is the offer doc's answerability passage — reuse that voice. AI is always human-confirmed ("you check each result"). Founding-cohort 80%-off promo ends **July 3, 2026**.

**The sharpest single line to build messaging around (from the product's own copy, gate-cleared):**

> _"You are the one who answers… That is the real fear. Not the price."_
