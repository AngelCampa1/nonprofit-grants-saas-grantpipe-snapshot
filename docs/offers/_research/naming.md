## Part 3 — Recommended Primary Name + Headline

### Recommended name: **The Founding Nonprofit Setup**

Why this one:

- **It is honest.** There is a real founding cohort and a real deadline (80% off first year, ends July 3, 2026). The name leans on scarcity and urgency that actually exist. No manufactured countdown. That matters for a risk-averse buyer who smells fake.
- **It carries all five MAGIC slots without strain.** Magnetic reason-why = "founding" (join now, before it closes). Avatar = "nonprofit." Goal = "setup," meaning you leave set up and running. Interval = the founding window. Container = "Setup," which also signals low effort and a fast start.
- **It answers the biggest objection in the offer: belief.** The research names perceived likelihood as the binding constraint. This buyer was burned by a year-long Salesforce or Blackbaud setup. A name built on the word "Setup" promises the opposite: you get set up, fast, with a founder on the call. The word does quiet objection-handling.
- **It fits the brand.** Rigorous, humane, earned. No superlatives. No hype. It sounds like a real thing a real founder offers, because it is.

One honest caution: "Setup" undersells the depth of the product on its own. Carry the depth in the headline and subhead, not the name.

### Recommended primary headline: **"Always know where every dollar went."**

Why this one leads:

- **It speaks straight to the deepest fear** across all three avatars: the question you have to answer. The board, the auditor, the client. This is the emotional core the research keeps returning to.
- **It leads with pull, not the moat.** It is a fundraising and cash-confidence promise first. Compliance is the proof underneath, not the lead. That matches the buyer truth: most of these orgs are not facing a single audit, so the wallet opens for confidence and control, not compliance.
- **It passes both gates cleanly.** Six words. One idea. Plain words a child knows. Active voice. No fake absolute (the product really is built on a double-entry ledger, so "always know" is a capability claim about the system, not a fabricated guarantee about outcomes).
- **It pairs with the name.** "The Founding Nonprofit Setup" tells you what you join. "Always know where every dollar went" tells you what you get. Together they cover the offer and the dream in two short lines.

CTA note: "Start free for one month" leads with a verb, names the action, and keeps the real offer term. It clears the generic-CTA gate.
