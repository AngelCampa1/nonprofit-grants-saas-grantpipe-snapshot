## 2. Warm Outreach — The Founder-Setup Call as the Warm Close

### The Realistic Honest Picture

A solo founder cannot run an outbound SDR motion, a LinkedIn DM campaign, and a product development cycle simultaneously. Do not try. The warm outreach role of a solo founder is not volume — it is **the highest-leverage touch at the moment the buyer is already warm**.

The warm close mechanism already exists in the product: the founder-setup call (✅, Audit-Ready / Enterprise). The acquisition problem is getting the buyer to that call without wasting her time or yours.

**The sequence that works:**

1. Buyer downloads a MOFU or BOFU lead magnet (e.g., `nonprofit-audit-readiness-assessment`, `single-audit-prep-timeline`, `grant-software-roi-calculator`, `salesforce-npsp-migration-map`).
2. Nurture sequence runs (family: audit-readiness, crm-evaluation). Day 1 delivers the asset. Day 3–5: a single follow-up email with a teaching angle matched to the asset family (already defined in `SEQUENCE_BY_FAMILY`).
3. **Day 7 or Day 10: a plain-text email from Angel personally.** Not a marketing automation template. A short email (4–6 sentences) written in first person, referencing what the subscriber downloaded, asking one specific question about their situation. Example: "You downloaded the single audit prep timeline last week. Are you in the window where you're getting ready for this, or is the audit already scheduled? Either way I'm curious what's making the prep harder than it should be." No CTA. Just a question.
4. Replies go into a 1:1 conversation. The conversation ends with an offer to do the founder-setup call — only if the fit is obvious (they have multiple grants, they mention an audit, they are clearly in the target budget range).

This is the entire warm outreach motion. It is scalable to the volume a 117-asset lead magnet engine produces without burning the founder's time on non-buyers. The rule: **only offer the setup call to people who responded to the plain-text email**. Cold-list setup-call offers waste both parties' time and lower perceived scarcity of the call.

**The asset families where the plain-text email ROI is highest** (in order): `audit-readiness`, `federal-grant-ops`, `crm-evaluation` (especially `salesforce-npsp-migration-map` and `granthub-migration-checklist`), `grant-compliance`. These are the MOFU/BOFU families where the buyer already has pain identified. Do not run the plain-text follow-up on TOFU families (`board-fundraising-toolkit`, `new-development-director-90-day-checklist`) — the buyer is not close enough yet.

**One timing note on warm outreach**: the GrantHub sunset (Jan 31, 2026) is already past. Former GrantHub users are now operating without a dedicated tool. This is the single warmest displacement cohort available. The `granthub-migration-checklist` lead magnet is already built. The LinkedIn cadence should have at least one weekly post referencing this specifically: "GrantHub went away five months ago. If you're still figuring out the replacement, here's what GrantPipe covers and where the gaps are." No overselling. The trust is in the honesty.

## 3. Cold Outreach — The Honest Case Against It

Cold outreach for this product at this stage is the wrong move. Here is the reasoning:

**The buyer is risk-averse and distrusts being sold to.** A cold LinkedIn message or email from an unknown person asking about grant management software reads as spam, regardless of targeting quality. The avatar has almost certainly been approached by Salesforce implementation partners and nonprofit technology consultants. A cold outreach from a solo founder lands in the same bucket and reduces rather than increases the trust that the entire acquisition engine is trying to build.

**The alternatives already exist.** The 117 lead magnets, the YouTube channel, and the LinkedIn cadence are inbound engines that attract the buyer at the moment of her own intent. They are already running. Adding cold outreach would dilute the founder's time away from these compounding assets without a comparable return.

**If cold outreach were to be done**, the only format worth trying is not email blast or LinkedIn cold DM — it is **a targeted offer to a closed community** the buyer already trusts. The specific communities: NTEN (Nonprofit Technology Enterprise Network), AFP (Association of Fundraising Professionals) chapter listservs, and local nonprofit resource center email lists. The offer is never "try GrantPipe" — it is "I built a free [specific checklist] for this community. Here's the link." That is not cold outreach. That is free value offered to a specific audience, which converts to lead captures, which feeds the warm nurture engine. Budget: zero. Time cost: 1 hour per community per quarter.

**The argument against cold outreach stands: do not run it as a systematic channel.** The one exception is community-specific free-asset drops, which are warm-adjacent, not cold.

Retargeting with the specific exit lead magnet already configured on each landing page (`award-setup-worksheet`, etc.) is a continuation of a conversation that already started. CAC here is low because the traffic source cost is paid once (organic or direct).

The right paid channel for this buyer is **Google Search, not Meta**. The avatar is not scrolling Facebook or Instagram looking for grant management software. She is actively searching. The high-commercial-intent keywords are "grant management software" (1,600/mo), "grant tracking software", "restricted fund accounting software", and "single audit software" — all of which have paid search landing pages already built. When the budget exists, start with exact-match on those terms, send to the existing LP pages, and use the exit lead magnets as the lead capture fallback for non-converting visitors.

**One paid experiment worth running now at near-zero cost**: YouTube video cards.
