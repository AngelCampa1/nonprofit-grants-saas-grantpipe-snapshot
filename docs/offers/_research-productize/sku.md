# GrantPipe — Bonuses & Deliverables as Named SKUs

_Internal strategy. All buyer-facing lines below are marked "draft copy" and must clear humanizer → third-grade-copy → zero-lies → fits-the-whole-place before shipping._

---

## Framework: Three buckets, one rule

**Bucket A — Top-of-funnel lead magnets (stay free, stay ungated).** These generate list growth. They do not belong in a paid stack — putting them there dilutes the perceived exclusivity of what a paying customer gets.

**Bucket B — Included bonuses (bundled with paid plans, named as distinct deliverables).** These exist today, require minimal or no build, and each one should feel like something a consultant would charge for. They are named so a buyer can rehearse their internal conversation: "I'm also getting X, Y, and Z."

**Bucket C — New productized playbooks (build effort S/M).** PDF-format deliverables, entirely backed by ✅ capability, producible by one person in a focused session. Named to match the buyer's real problem statement.

---

## Bucket A — Stay Top-of-Funnel (do NOT include in paid stack)

These are the mass-volume list builders. Including them in a paid package would be a mistake: the buyer already has access to them for free, so naming them as "bonuses" signals you're padding the stack.

- All 30 state charitable-registration compliance checklists
- All 17 city funder maps and deadline calendars
- All grant template packs (grant-proposal-budget-template, grant-narrative-template-pack, grant-proposal-sample-pack)
- All compliance timeline templates (single-audit-prep-timeline, audit-prep-week-by-week-checklist)
- Operational templates (grant-kickoff-meeting-template, funder-report-template, grant-closeout-checklist)
- Migration checklists (granthub-migration-checklist, salesforce-npsp-migration-map, crm-migration-data-map-template) — keep top-of-funnel because they pull migration-intent searchers into the list

**Why these stay free:** They are problem-aware content. Once someone is paying, they have already solved the problem these resources address. Putting them inside the paid tier is like giving a hotel guest a coupon for the hotel. Keep them as traffic and trust, not as stack filler.

## Bucket B — Named Included Bonuses (bundled with paid plans)

Each one is a discrete artifact. Named so a buyer can picture what they are getting. Ship-status ✅ on every item — these are either already delivered by the product or are one-person production tasks against existing tools.

---

### B1 — "The Audit Evidence File"

**What it is:** A named, organized evidence package — the set of export artifacts a buyer can hand an auditor or funder today. Includes: restriction classification log (CSV), fund balance rollforward (PDF/export), journal entry drill-down for any grant (PDF), funder-monitoring-evidence-template (existing lead magnet), and read-only auditor portal access link. Not a new build — it is an explained, named assembly of what the product already produces. The deliverable doc is a one-page "what to hand the auditor" guide (PDF, one-person S effort to write).

**Ship-status:** ✅
**Build effort:** S (document what already exists; produce the one-page guide)
**Which avatar:** Finance/Operations Director first; Executive Director second
**Where it appears:** Audit-Ready and Enterprise plans, as a named inclusion
**Framing (draft copy):** "When the auditor calls, you send a link and a file — not an inbox search."

---

### B2 — "The Funder Portal Pass"

**What it is:** A named bonus that surfaces the auditor/funder read-only portal as a concrete, packaged deliverable — not just a product feature. Artifact: a one-page "how to invite your funder" guide (PDF) plus a sample external-reviewer access policy (external-reviewer-access-policy-template, existing lead magnet repackaged as an included doc, not a tofu giveaway). The guide walks the buyer through exactly what a funder sees when they click the link.

**Ship-status:** ✅
**Build effort:** S (write the how-to guide; repurpose existing template)
**Which avatar:** Executive/Development Director (funder relationships) and Finance Director (audit requests)
**Where it appears:** Growth, Audit-Ready, Enterprise
**Note:** Moves the portal from a buried feature to a named thing the buyer can point to in their head: "I have a portal I can share."

### B3 — "The Compliance Watch" (rebranded from product feature to bonus artifact)

Delivered as a one-page "what your system is watching" brief (PDF): lists every active deadline, spend-down alert, reporting window, and state registration date the system is tracking for the buyer's org. One-person production: the product generates the data; the brief is a template (S build).

**Ship-status:** ✅
**Build effort:** S (template PDF; product generates the content per-org)
**Which avatar:** Executive/Development Director ("I stop tracking dates manually")
**Where it appears:** Starter and above, named in plan inclusions
**Framing (draft copy):** "Your calendar, watched. Emailed before anything slips."

### B4 — "Day-One Data Session" (the founder setup, named as a deliverable)

**What it is:** A productized, named 90-minute work session with the founder. Has a defined agenda (see Playbook C3 below), a defined output list (at least one live grant, its deadlines on the calendar, its fund linked, its spend budget entered), and a defined follow-up artifact (the "What We Set Up" summary doc emailed after). This is not a vague "onboarding call" — it is a named deliverable with a defined output the buyer can rehearse before signing.

**Ship-status:** ✅ (guided setup via founder — the only keepable version today)
**Build effort:** S (write the agenda template and the follow-up doc template; the session itself is the product)
**Which avatar:** Executive Director, Finance Director (both attend together in many cases)
**Where it appears:** Audit-Ready and Enterprise annual plans only
**Scarcity note:** Cap is real (~4 sessions/month, one founder). Show the open-slot count only when ≤ 3 and verified. When full, offer the next month's slot — never block the buyer from the plan itself.
**Framing (draft copy):** "Bring one award letter. Leave with your first grant live."

**Ship-status:** ✅ (assessment exists; formatted report is a one-person M build — HTML-to-PDF template, per-org generation)
**Build effort:** M (wire assessment results to a PDF report template; auto-populate from account data)
**Which avatar:** Executive Director (sees a score → board report); Finance Director (sees the gap list → action items)
**Where it appears:** All paid plans; free version stays tofu for list growth
**Framing (draft copy):** "Your score. Your three gaps. Your next steps — from your own data."

## How to Present Bonuses Without Fake Dollar Totals

The red-team position from the grand-slam doc is correct and binding: **do not sum the stack to a "$45,000 value" headline.** A burned buyer — the exact avatar GrantPipe is selling to — has been through a Salesforce pitch with inflated "value" numbers. Fake math is the #1 trust-destroyer for this buyer.

**What works instead, with specific execution guidance:**

### 1. Name each bonus as a job-to-be-done, not a price

Framing (draft copy): "When the auditor calls, you have The Audit Evidence File ready. When a funder asks for access, you send The Funder Portal Pass link. When a donor goes quiet, The Donor Lapse Watch Brief already flagged them." This gives each bonus a concrete moment in the buyer's life — no dollar tag needed.

### 2. Use the honest comparison anchor once, specifically

The single dollar figure that earns trust is the sourced $30,000–$80,000/yr assembled-stack-plus-consultant estimate [planning estimate — verify the range before publishing]. Use it once, clearly labeled as an estimate of what the buyer is currently spending across their assembled stack. Then say: "GrantPipe is one system that replaces most of that stack and includes the setup call that replaces the consultant." Do not tag individual bonuses with invented line-item prices.

### 3. Lead with the guarantee, not the stack

The guarantee suite (Free Look, First Month No Risk, Your Data Always, Day-One Setup, Confirmed-Number Promise, Anti-guarantee) is the differentiated trust move — zero competitors publish any of these. Put the guarantees before the bonus list, not after. The bonuses support the guarantees; the guarantees do the trust work.

### 4. Present bonuses as a "what's included" table, not a "value" list

A table with two columns — **What you get** and **Why it matters** — signals a product that earned its inclusions, not a stack padded with noise. The founder-setup offer doc already uses this format effectively. Apply the same format to the full plan inclusions page. Each row names one artifact and one outcome it prevents or enables.

### 5. The one honest "this is what it replaces" move

Below the inclusion table, one sentence (draft copy): "The assembled stack most orgs use — a donor CRM, a grant tracker, QuickBooks, spreadsheets, and a fractional controller or consultant — costs $30,000 to $80,000 a year, with no single place to answer the board's question [planning estimate]. GrantPipe is that single place." This is the value anchor. It is not a fake sum; it is a real description of a real alternative. Every bonus in the table is quietly pointing at part of that $30K–$80K number that now disappears.

## Which existing lead magnets become "included bonuses" vs. stay top-of-funnel

**Move into paid "included" status (repackage, not duplicate):**

- `nonprofit-audit-readiness-assessment` — the free version stays tofu; the per-account formatted report (B5) is the paid version
- `auditor-evidence-checklist` + `funder-monitoring-evidence-template` + `external-reviewer-access-policy-template` — these four audit-readiness slugs are the raw material for B1 (Audit Evidence File) and B2 (Funder Portal Pass); repurpose them as the "included document pack" inside those bonuses rather than listing them as separate unlockable PDFs
- `grant-compliance-cost-audit` — BOFU/MOFU; once someone is a paid user, offer it as part of the onboarding flow (helps them quantify what they were spending before GrantPipe) rather than as a lead magnet

**Stay top-of-funnel, do not move into paid stack:**

- All 30 state compliance checklists
- All 17 city funder maps
- All grant template packs and operational checklists
- All migration checklists (granthub, salesforce-npsp, crm-migration-data-map) — they pull the right migration-intent searchers; keep free
- Interactive assessments (free score stays public; only the formatted per-account report version moves to paid)

**The one BOFU asset worth flagging:** `grant-software-roi-calculator` and `nonprofit-crm-cost-calculator` are already tagged bofu in the sequence metadata. These are conversion assets, not list-building assets. They belong in the sales flow (pricing page, comparison pages) as inline calculators — not as emailed PDFs. If there is not yet an inline version of the ROI calculator on the pricing page, that is a higher-leverage move than any new playbook build.
