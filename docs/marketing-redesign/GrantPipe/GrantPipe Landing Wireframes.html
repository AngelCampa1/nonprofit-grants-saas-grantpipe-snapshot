GrantPipe — Landing Page Wireframes

Five rough takes on the marketing homepage. Each explores a different way to introduce the product to grant managers at nonprofits. Goal: a strong first impression — clear value, real screenshot energy, calm tone. Click tabs to compare; use the Tweaks panel to flip tone, density, or persona.

5 directions b&w lo-fi 2026-05-02 audience: grant manager · nonprofit
Direction 01

Pipeline

Lead with a live-feeling kanban of grants moving through stages. Visual proof of the product's core mental model, right in the hero.

[mini kanban thumb]
  • Pro: instantly explains what the product is
  • Watch: can feel busy if not curated
Direction 02

Editorial

Big confident headline, calm whitespace, a story told in three chapters. Treats the homepage like an op-ed, not a feature dump.

[centered hero thumb]
  • Pro: warm, human, memorable
  • Watch: less product proof above the fold
Direction 03

Dashboard

Data-dense. The hero IS the product — KPIs, a grants table, a forecast chart. For visitors who want proof fast.

[dashboard thumb]
  • Pro: credibility with finance & ops folks
  • Watch: can intimidate first-time browsers
Direction 04

Before / After

Two columns: "spreadsheets & shared drives" vs. "GrantPipe." Names the pain explicitly, then resolves it. Conversion-focused.

[two-column compare]
  • Pro: very clear "why switch"
  • Watch: negative framing — keep it kind
Direction 05

Playful Pipe

An actual illustrated pipe with grants flowing through it. Friendlier, more brandable. Loosens the "serious software" tone a bit.

[illustrated pipe thumb]
  • Pro: distinct, memorable, ownable
  • Watch: some nonprofits skew conservative
Recommendation

Likely winner: 01 + 04

Pipeline hero (01) gives the strongest product-truth above the fold. The Before/After block from (04) makes a great mid-page section. Editorial (02) fits if brand voice leans warmer.

Tweak panel → try Serious tone for nonprofit board demos, Playful for ICP video.
