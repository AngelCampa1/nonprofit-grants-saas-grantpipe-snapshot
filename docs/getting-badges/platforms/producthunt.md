Sources: [How to post a product](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product), [Product Hunt featuring guidelines](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/9883485-product-hunt-featuring-guidelines), [access to post](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/481909-how-can-i-get-access-to-post), [categories](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/8104478-how-to-add-a-category-to-a-product), and [scheduling](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/2724119-how-to-schedule-a-post). Verified on 2026-05-15.

## Platform Requirements

- Use the logged-in personal Product Hunt account. Company accounts cannot post, vote, or comment.
- Product Hunt recommends posting your own product rather than relying on a hunter.
- Submit the direct product URL, not a blog post or press page.
- Thumbnail should be square. Product Hunt recommends 240x240.
- Gallery images should be 1270x760.
- Gallery needs at least 2 images before it is viewable.
- Product videos must be YouTube links.
- Description is limited to 260 characters.
- Add up to 3 categories/topics.
- You can create a draft or schedule within 30 days.
- Scheduling is available within 30 days. Confirm exact go-live timing in the Product Hunt UI before launch.
- Product Hunt looks for useful, live, practical, well-crafted digital products.

## First Comment

```text
I built GrantPipe because the grant-funded nonprofit workflow is usually split across tools that were never designed to agree with each other.

The donor record is in one system. The active grant tracker is in a spreadsheet. Restricted funds are handled in accounting files. Reporting deadlines live in a calendar. Evidence sits in folders, emails, and staff memory. That setup works until reporting week or compliance follow-up forces the team to rebuild the story from scratch.

GrantPipe brings donor CRM, grants, restricted funds, compliance deadlines, evidence, reporting, and fund accounting into one workspace. It includes Grants.gov federal opportunity search, plus manual and imported tracking for non-federal opportunities.

The product is built for mid-sized nonprofits with roughly $500K-$10M annual budgets, especially teams managing multiple active grants without wanting a custom Salesforce build or consultant-heavy implementation.

I would value direct feedback on the positioning: does "grant compliance software for nonprofits" make the product clear enough, or does "nonprofit CRM for grant-funded teams" land better?
```
