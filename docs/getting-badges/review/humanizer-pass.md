# Humanizer Pass

Reviewed on 2026-05-15.

## Rules Applied

- Use direct, plain language.
- Keep the founder-builder voice.
- Avoid generic SaaS filler.
- Avoid hype.
- Avoid unsupported proof.
- Avoid em dashes.
- Use concrete workflow language: donor records, grants, restricted funds, deadlines, evidence, reporting, fund accounting.
- Vary copy by platform so profiles do not read like duplicate submissions.

## Words Avoided

- Revolutionary
- Industry-leading
- Seamless
- Leverage
- Optimize
- Transformative
- Game-changing
- Best-in-class
- Trusted by
- Loved by

## Why The Copy Should Land

The copy leads with the operational problem buyers recognize: donor work, grant compliance, restricted funds, evidence, and reporting are connected in real life but often split across separate tools.

It avoids pretending GrantPipe has third-party validation before those profiles and reviews exist.

It positions the product against the tools buyers actually compare: donor-only CRMs, grant management tools, spreadsheets, and consultant-heavy Salesforce builds.

BetaList copy was reviewed separately because that audience is broader than nonprofit software buyers. The final version still explains the early-stage startup angle, but it does not drift into generic founder-launch language.

## Final Voice Check

Pass.

The copy sounds like a builder explaining a specific nonprofit operations product, not a generic SaaS template.
