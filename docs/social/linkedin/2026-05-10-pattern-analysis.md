# LinkedIn Pattern Analysis

_Captured 2026-05-10. Source: linkedin.com/company/113210122/admin/page-posts/published_

## Headline finding

**Sample size is too small to do real pattern analysis.** GrantPipe's company page has a small follower base and a handful of published posts, with minimal engagement across all of them. There is no signal to extract about hook style, time-of-day, or topic resonance from this dataset.

We will not invent patterns from this small a sample. We build the strategy from first principles plus LinkedIn B2B SaaS benchmarks, then re-run this analysis after 4 weeks of new posting (target date: 2026-06-08) when sample size makes the question answerable.

## What the data does tell us

1. **Format monoculture.** Every post published so far is long-form (1,100 to 1,400 characters). No short hooks, no carousels, no polls, no images, no questions, no founder POV. The variance the algorithm rewards is missing.
2. **Topic concentration.** Every post is about compliance or donor-CRM gap. That is a strong wedge but a narrow surface. The algorithm needs reasons to show the page to people outside the immediate ICP. ICP-adjacent posts and build-in-public posts are absent.
3. **Em dashes everywhere.** Prior copy uses em dashes routinely (for example, between two clauses inside a sentence such as "are not standard features. They require custom configuration."). New copy must remove them per brand voice direction.
4. **Bursty cadence.** A cluster of posts in a short window suggests a recent push, not a sustained presence. The algorithm rewards consistency over bursts; predictable slots will perform better.
5. **One mild outlier.** The oldest post at capture carried a little engagement while the newer ones carried none. The lift is too small to attribute to anything but follower exposure window.

## Working hypotheses to validate over the next 4 weeks

These are not findings. They are bets we will test.

- **Hooks under 180 characters before the truncation point will out-engage long openers.** LinkedIn truncates around line 3 on desktop and line 2 on mobile. The first 180 characters are the entire ad.
- **Question hooks and number-led hooks will out-perform definition or "myth" hooks** for a young page that needs reach, not retention.
- **Posts with an image (a screenshot of a real GrantPipe view, a fund ledger row, a compliance calendar, a glossary card) will out-engage text-only posts** by a meaningful margin. Image posts should be ~30% of the bank.
- **Tuesday and Thursday between 09:30 and 12:30 ET will be the best slots** for B2B nonprofit-finance audiences. Mondays before noon and Fridays after 14:00 will under-perform.
- **ICP-adjacent posts (the work the audience does, not GrantPipe directly) will out-pace direct product posts in raw reach** and serve as discovery surfaces. They earn the right to drop in the wedge later.
- **Build-in-public posts from a named founder voice will out-engage company-voice product posts on a young company page.** We hold most of the bank in company voice but reserve a small share for "from the founder" posts.

## Cadence baseline (no historical signal yet)

Because there is no historical signal, the slot grid in `2026-05-10-strategy.md` is built from LinkedIn B2B SaaS benchmarks:

- Highest engagement windows (US Eastern): Tuesday and Thursday 09:00 to 12:00, Wednesday 11:00 to 14:00.
- Secondary windows: weekday 16:30 to 18:00 (post-work commute scroll), Sunday 18:00 to 21:00 (week-prep scroll).
- Lowest engagement: Friday after 15:00, Saturday before 10:00, Monday before 08:00.

We will rotate posts across these bands deliberately so the post-mortem in 4 weeks can compare like for like.
