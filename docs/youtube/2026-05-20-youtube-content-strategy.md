# GrantPipe YouTube Content Strategy

_Created 2026-05-20. Search volumes are US, from Google Ads data (DataForSEO) — a proxy for YouTube demand since these queries also surface video results and reflect how buyers describe their problems._

## Goal

Build awareness among **grant managers** and **executive/finance leaders at grant-funded nonprofits**. Win **search** first (YouTube is the #2 search engine), not virality. Buyers are time-poor and risk-averse — they search for how-to and compliance answers, not entertainment. Rank for those, then convert with a soft "GrantPipe does this automatically" outro.

## Content Pillars

1. **Grant compliance & federal rules** — highest-trust, lowest competition, the core differentiator
2. **Fund & nonprofit accounting fundamentals** — biggest volume, top-of-funnel awareness
3. **Grant operations / how-to** — practical workflows where spreadsheets break (the wedge)
4. **Tools, templates & careers** — high commercial intent + recurring career-search terms

## Sequencing & Conversion

- **Start with #16, #17, #5, #7, #1.** Template videos (16, 17) attract the spreadsheet-bound user who churns to GrantPipe; the compliance/fund-accounting explainers (5, 7, 1) have volume _and_ establish authority. This covers commercial-intent + awareness from day one.
- **#18 is the money video** but the most competitive — film it once 6–8 supporting videos exist so the channel has authority signals.
- **Per video:** keyword in title + first line of description, chaptered timestamps (YouTube ranks these), and a pinned comment + end screen linking to the matching free template/lead magnet on grantpipe.com — not a hard "buy" pitch. Compliance buyers are risk-averse; teach, don't sell.
- **Repurpose:** each video maps to a blog post (lead magnets like the grant compliance checklist already exist) — embed the video on the post for dual SEO.

## Conversion Conventions (apply to every video)

These are the standard moves for turning a how-to viewer into a GrantPipe lead. Follow them on every video unless there's a specific reason not to.

### Soft GrantPipe plug

Every video earns its plug by teaching first. The plug lives in the outro, after the viewer already got the full value, and follows this shape:

1. **Name the wall.** Describe the concrete point where the manual/spreadsheet approach stops working (no audit trail, split-fund allocation, sheer volume). Be honest that the tool you just taught is the right tool _today_.
2. **Connect to the product by capability, not features.** "Software that keeps the audit trail for you / keeps restricted funds separate by the system / tracks deadlines without anyone babysitting a tab." Tie each capability back to a pain the video already established.
3. **State the builder relationship plainly.** "That's exactly why I built GrantPipe." First person, no hype. The founder is a builder, not a former grants officer — never imply sector experience.
4. **Defuse the sell.** Explicitly say you're not pitching hard ("I'm not going to pitch it here"). Risk-averse compliance buyers trust restraint. Let them leave knowing where to look later.

Reinforce in the description and pinned comment with one calm line and the grantpipe.com link, plus an end-screen card in the last ~8 seconds. Never a hard "buy now."

### Lead magnets are email-gated

Every free template/checklist is delivered **by email**, not as a raw public link. Email capture is the entire point of the lead magnet — it's how a YouTube viewer becomes a nurtured lead.

- On-screen, in narration, in the description, and in the pinned comment: say the template is free and that we'll **send it to their inbox** when they enter their email. Never claim "no email," "no email wall," or "no signup" — those contradict the funnel and have been explicitly removed.
- Mechanism: a clean vanity URL (`grantpipe.com/{slug}`) redirects to the email-gated `/free/{slug}/` page. After capture, the asset is delivered from R2 via a signed, time-limited download link (see CLAUDE.md → lead-magnet delivery). Both the Google Sheets and Excel versions go in the same email.

### Branding

Use the real GrantPipe logo mark (`production/assets/grantpipe-mark.svg`), never a CSS-drawn placeholder, in the wordmark, thumbnail, and outro.

## Caveats

- **No Google Search Console MCP connected**, so existing grantpipe.com query data wasn't mined. If GSC is connected later, prioritize videos around terms the site already gets impressions for.
- Founder context: don't fabricate sector experience. Frame these as **"builder explains the system,"** not "former grants officer." The compliance topics (5–10) are where a rigorous, precise voice builds more trust than a generic nonprofit influencer.
