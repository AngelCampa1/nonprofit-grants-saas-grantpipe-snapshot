Build a grant tracking spreadsheet that holds up at audit time, and grab the free template (Google Sheets + Excel) below.

Most grant trackers answer the wrong question. They tell you the money arrived: applied, approved, received, done. But your funder and your auditor are asking something else. Are you spending restricted dollars on what they were promised for, against the right budget line, before the report is due? A status column can't answer that, and that gap is what gets nonprofits in trouble.

So in this video I build the whole thing from a blank sheet, the way it has to be built if you ever expect to sit across from an auditor. No add-ons, no macros. Just plain Google Sheets or Excel. I build grant-compliance software for a living, so I'm showing you the structure that survives an audit, not the one that just looks tidy on a Monday morning.

WHAT YOU'LL BUILD
- A Grant Register: one row per grant, with a Grant ID system, funder type, and the period of performance most trackers leave off.
- The restricted-funds column almost everyone forgets, plus a plain-language Restriction Purpose field next to it.
- A Budget vs. Actual tab that tracks spend by the categories the funder approved, not one lump sum.
- An Expense Log that feeds your Spent totals automatically with a single SUMIFS formula, so logging a receipt updates the budget on its own.
- A Reporting tab that color-codes deadlines amber before they're due and red once they're late.
- A four-number dashboard your executive director or board can read in one glance.

FOUR THINGS THAT TRIP PEOPLE UP (all covered in the video)
- Period of performance: spending the week before a grant officially starts can get disallowed, even when the expense was perfectly reasonable.
- Restricted vs. unrestricted: one yes/no column decides whether you can defend how the money was spent.
- The category-shift rule: on federal awards, moving more than roughly 10% of the total between budget categories usually needs the funder's sign-off first.
- The single audit: spend a million dollars or more in federal money in one year and it stops being optional. Full video on that one coming soon.

WHERE A SPREADSHEET STOPS WORKING
I'm honest about this in the video, because at some point it will. The cracks show up when more than one person edits the file and nobody can say who changed the award amount, when a single expense has to split across two grants with no clean way to allocate it, and when the reporting tab quietly becomes someone's full-time job. That's not you doing it wrong. It's a spreadsheet doing exactly what a spreadsheet does, right up until your compliance load outgrows it.
