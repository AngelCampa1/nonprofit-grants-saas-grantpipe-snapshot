## Saturated vs. missing

- **Over-covered:** (1) CPA-exam lectures (Farhat, Darius Clark, Rutgers) — built for accounting students, not nonprofit operators; (2) hour-long CPA-firm "updates & common findings" webinars (Moss Adams, Eide Bailly, Citrin Cooperman, Cherry Bekaert, Sikich, GRF, Wegner) that rank but have miserable per-view retention; (3) sub-2-minute faceless shorts that define the term and stop; (4) state-auditor uploads aimed at local governments, not 501(c)(3)s.
- **The gap to own — a tight, plain-English "who needs one and why" explainer built for nonprofit finance leaders, with the CORRECT 2024 threshold.** No top result is all of: (1) under ~12 minutes, (2) clearly answers _who triggers a single audit and why it exists_ for a nonprofit ED/finance director audience (not CPA-exam candidates), AND (3) states the current **$1,000,000** threshold front and center. The two short nonprofit-facing videos that do cite $1M (James Moore 2:15, Oregon CNP 8:41) are thin or program-niche; the most intent-matched explainer (ND State Auditor, "do you need one?") is 37 minutes and government-flavored. The whole "explained" lane is owned by a 5-year-old auditor video (CPA Hall Talk) that predates the threshold change.

## Length & title patterns

- **Sweet spot: 8–12 min.** The strongest evergreen non-webinar performers cluster short (Farhat 17–19 min CPA lectures aside, the intent-matched operator content is 2–10 min, and the hour-long webinars underperform per-view). Our planned ~10–12 min tight explainer fits cleanly: long enough to credibly cover who/why/threshold/process, short enough to beat the webinars on watch-through. Avoid the 30–60 min webinar trap entirely.
- Titles: exact phrases "Single Audit," "What Is a Single Audit," "Single Audit Requirements," and "Single Audit Explained / Overview" dominate. "Who needs one," "Do you need one," and "When do you need a single audit" recur as the real searcher question. Lead with the exact keyword + the operator question, e.g. "The Single Audit Explained — Who Needs One & Why (2024 Rules)." Putting the year/"2024 rules" in the title is a differentiator since the field is full of pre-2024 content.

## How we win

1. **Open on the threshold change as the hook.** The 2024 Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200) raised the single-audit threshold from **$750,000 to $1,000,000** in federal funds expended. Most ranking videos — including the dominant "explained" results (CPA Hall Talk 5:08, ND State Auditor) and the CPA-exam lectures — predate this and either say $750k or don't address it. First 15 seconds: "If you heard the number was $750,000, that changed — here's the rule that applies now." Instant relevance + freshness signal.
2. **Answer the real question: who needs one and why.** Searchers are nonprofit operators asking "does this apply to me," not CPA-exam students. Frame around expenditure (not award) of $1M+ in a fiscal year, the single-vs-program-specific distinction, and why the Single Audit Act exists. This is the intent the 37-min ND video matches but no tight video owns.
3. **Plain-English over jargon, but precise.** The lane is split between dense CPA-exam GAGAS lectures and one-minute shorts that say nothing actionable. A rigorous-but-accessible 10-minute walkthrough beats both — this is exactly the GrantPipe "rigorous, humane" voice.
4. **Cover what fragments across the webinars in one place:** the $1M expenditure trigger, what the auditor tests (the SEFA, major program determination, compliance requirements), common findings, and how to prepare. Consolidate the hour-long firm webinars' substance into one tight segment.
5. **Builder POV, not auditor POV.** Speak as the team that builds grant-compliance software and has to model these rules precisely — never imply former-auditor or nonprofit-sector experience. Edge: the existing field is 90% CPA firms pitching audit services; an independent, vendor-neutral explainer that doesn't end in "hire our firm" is differentiated.
6. **Close on tracking, not a hard pitch.** Show where the single audit gets painful manually — reconstructing the SEFA, tracking federal expenditures across funds to know if you've crossed $1M, keeping the audit trail. Position GrantPipe as the system that makes that continuous instead of a year-end scramble.
