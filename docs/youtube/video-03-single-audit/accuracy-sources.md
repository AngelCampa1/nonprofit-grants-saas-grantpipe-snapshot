# Accuracy Fact Sheet — "The Single Audit Explained" (Video #5)

_Verified 2026-05-21 against primary sources (eCFR 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart F, Single Audit Act, OMB 2024 revision, GSA Federal Audit Clearinghouse) and corroborating CPA-firm explainers. Every numeric/legal claim in `script-final.md` must trace to a line here. No claim ships without a source._

## Core verified facts

1. **What it is.** The Single Audit is an organization-wide audit required by the **Single Audit Act of 1984, amended 1996 (31 U.S.C. 7501–7507)**, implemented through **2 CFR Part 200, Subpart F** (the Uniform Guidance). Subpart F replaced the old **OMB Circular A-133** (consolidated into 2 CFR 200 in the 2013/2014 rulemaking). Source: eCFR 2 CFR 200 Subpart F; §200.500.

2. **The threshold ($1,000,000).** A non-Federal entity that **expends $1,000,000 or more in Federal awards during its fiscal year** must have a single audit (or program-specific audit). Source: 2 CFR §200.501(a).

3. **Threshold was raised.** The 2024 OMB revision of the Uniform Guidance raised the threshold from **$750,000 to $1,000,000**. Effective for **fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2024** — i.e., fiscal years **ending on or after September 30, 2025**. (Many older videos still say $750k — this is the correction hook.) Source: OMB final rule April 2024; CBIZ/BDO/Plante Moran explainers; §200.501.

4. **Basis = EXPENDED, not received/awarded.** The trigger is dollars *spent* in the fiscal year, not the size of the award or cash received. "The determination of when a Federal award is expended is based on when the activity related to the Federal award occurs." Source: 2 CFR §200.502.

5. **Federal awards include pass-through money.** Funds expended as a **recipient (direct) OR a subrecipient (pass-through/subaward)** both count toward the threshold. Money received as a **contractor** for goods/services does **not** count. Source: 2 CFR §200.501(f), §200.502.

6. **Who it applies to.** Non-Federal entities: **states, local governments, Indian tribes, institutions of higher education, and nonprofit organizations.** It does **not** apply to for-profit organizations (the pass-through entity sets requirements for for-profit subrecipients). Source: 2 CFR §200.501, §200.501(h).

7. **Below the threshold = exempt (that year).** An entity that expends **less than $1,000,000** is exempt from federal audit requirements for that year, but its records must remain available for review/audit by the federal agency, pass-through entity, and GAO. Source: 2 CFR §200.501(d).

8. **Program-specific audit option.** Instead of a full single audit, an entity may elect a program-specific audit if it **expends federal awards under only one federal program (excluding R&D)** AND no statute/regulation/award terms require a financial-statement audit. Source: 2 CFR §200.501(c), §200.507.

9. **A single audit has two parts.** It covers BOTH (a) an audit of the entity's **financial statements** and (b) a **compliance audit of federal awards.** It is NOT just a financial-statement audit. Source: 2 CFR §200.514.

10. **Not every dollar is tested — risk-based "major programs."** The auditor uses a **risk-based approach** (2 CFR §200.518) to select certain federal programs as "major programs" for detailed compliance testing, rather than testing all programs. (Keep high-level in script; do not over-specify Type A/B mechanics.) Source: 2 CFR §200.518.

11. **SEFA.** The auditee prepares a **Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA)** listing all federal money expended by program. It drives the audit. Source: 2 CFR §200.510(b).

12. **Independent auditor + GAGAS.** The audit is performed by an **independent auditor** (a CPA firm the auditee hires), following **Generally Accepted Government Auditing Standards (GAGAS), the "Yellow Book."** The government does not perform it. Source: 2 CFR §200.509, §200.514(a).

13. **Submission: Federal Audit Clearinghouse (FAC).** The **reporting package** and **data collection form (SF-SAC)** are submitted electronically to the **FAC**. The FAC moved from the **U.S. Census Bureau to the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) on October 1, 2023**; submissions now go through **fac.gov** (GSA). Source: 2 CFR §200.512(d); GSA FAC.

14. **Deadline.** Due the **earlier of 30 calendar days after the auditee receives the auditor's report, OR 9 months after the end of the audit period.** Source: 2 CFR §200.512(a).

15. **Reporting package contents.** Financial statements + SEFA; summary schedule of prior audit findings; auditor's reports; a **corrective action plan**; and the **schedule of findings and questioned costs.** Source: 2 CFR §200.512(c), §200.511.

16. **Findings & corrective action.** Noncompliance, control weaknesses, or **questioned costs** become **audit findings**; the auditee must prepare a **corrective action plan** for each. Source: 2 CFR §200.511, §200.516.

17. **Low-risk auditee (high-level only).** An entity meeting §200.520 conditions over the prior two years can qualify as a **low-risk auditee**, which reduces the percentage-of-coverage the auditor must test (20% vs. 40% of total federal awards expended). Source: 2 CFR §200.518(e)–(f), §200.520.

18. **Audit cost is allowable.** A reasonably proportionate share of the cost of a required single audit is an **allowable cost** that can be charged to federal awards. Source: 2 CFR §200.425. (Use cautiously; phrase as "generally allowable.")
