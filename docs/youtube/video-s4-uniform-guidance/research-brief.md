## The one idea

If your nonprofit takes federal money, one rulebook governs all of it: **2 CFR Part 200, the Uniform Guidance.** It answers three practical questions: how do you run a federal grant, what can you charge to it, and who checks your work. That's the whole spine. Everything else hangs off "one rulebook, three jobs."

## Angle / why this video is different from the slop

Most "Uniform Guidance" content is a lawyer reading regulation numbers, or a 90-minute webinar. Two things make ours honest and useful:

1. **It uses the post-2024-revision numbers.** OMB rewrote Part 200 in 2024 (89 FR 30046, generally effective for awards on/after Oct 1, 2024) to use plainer language and raise several thresholds. A huge amount of older content still quotes the old numbers. Getting the four changed numbers right is the credibility signal and the most search-relevant content:
   - De minimis indirect cost rate: 10% → **up to 15%** of MTDC (2 CFR 200.414(f))
   - Equipment capitalization: $5,000 → **$10,000** per unit (2 CFR 200.1)
   - MTDC subaward inclusion cap: first $25,000 → **first $50,000** of each subaward (2 CFR 200.1)
   - Single audit threshold: $750,000 → **$1,000,000** expended in a fiscal year (2 CFR 200.501)
2. **Builder honesty.** No "I spent 20 years as a federal grants officer." Framing: "I build software that has to enforce these rules, so I had to learn exactly what they require." True, and the right authority for a builder.

## Structure (chapters)

0. **Cold open / hook** — Take federal money and one rulebook covers all of it: the Uniform Guidance, 2 CFR 200. Plain English, ~7 minutes, no lawyer-speak. Builder note.
1. **What it is** — OMB's single rulebook for federal grants. In 2013 it replaced eight separate circulars (nicknamed the "Super Circular"). It binds the agency, any pass-through (like a state that re-grants the money), and you the recipient — and anyone you subaward to.
2. **One rulebook, three jobs** — six subparts on paper, but you live in three: the admin rules (how you run the award), the cost principles (what you can charge), and the audit rules (who checks you). Map it plainly.
3. **The cost test** — every dollar you charge has to pass three words: reasonable, allocable, allowable. Plain examples. Quick direct-vs-indirect: indirect (overhead) costs keep the lights on across everything; you recover them with an indirect rate.
4. **What changed in 2024** — the core beat. The four numbers that moved up, why each one helps a smaller nonprofit (more overhead recovery via the higher de minimis; fewer things treated as "equipment"; a bigger slice of each subaward counts toward your indirect base; and fewer small orgs forced into a single audit). State the effective date plainly: awards issued on or after October 1, 2024 (single audit keys to fiscal years that begin on or after that date).
5. **The single audit** — what it is: an organization-wide audit, not one per grant; triggered when you spend $1,000,000 or more in federal money in your fiscal year; done by an independent CPA under "Yellow Book" standards and filed with the Federal Audit Clearinghouse. The threshold jump means some orgs that used to need one now don't.
6. **Outro + soft plug** — recap: one rulebook, three jobs, numbers moved up in 2024. You don't need a consultant to start; you need to know which rules apply and a system that tracks the proof. Soft GrantPipe mention; email-gated **Grant Compliance Checklist** lead magnet ("we'll send it to your inbox").

## Out of scope / do-not-claim (the no-lie guardrails)

- **Use ONLY the four verified changed numbers and the agreed single-audit basics.** Each MUST match CLAUDE.md "Verified Facts": single audit **$1,000,000**; de minimis **up to 15% MTDC** (a ceiling, not a flat rate); MTDC subaward cap **first $50,000**; equipment **$10,000** (or the org's own lower limit — say "up to $10,000").
- **Do NOT put the FFATA subaward reporting threshold on screen.** Repo CLAUDE.md says it "remains $25,000"; outside research says grant subawards moved to $30,000 (2 CFR 170, eff. Oct 1 2020) with $25,000 only for FAR contract subcontracts. This is contested and off-spine — exclude it entirely rather than assert a number. Flagged separately for Angel.
- **Do NOT put a debarment/suspension CFR section number or its threshold on screen.** (CLAUDE.md cites 200.213 at $25k; 2024 renumbered the UG pointer to 200.214, threshold lives in 2 CFR 180.220.) Off-spine — omit.
- **Effective-date phrasing:** "awards issued on or after October 1, 2024"; single audit "fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2024." (That is mathematically the same as "fiscal years ending on or after Sept 30, 2025," since fiscal years are 12 months — either is correct; use the "begins" phrasing because it's clearer spoken.)
- De minimis 15% and equipment $10k are **ceilings** — phrase as "up to."
- No "the only three words in the reg" claim — "reasonable, allocable, allowable" is the accurate popular shorthand for the cost test; the full allowability factors are longer (200.403).
- Do not claim GrantPipe user counts, testimonials, or that Angel was a federal grants/audit professional.
