## The one idea

Regular (for-profit) accounting answers one question: **did we make a profit?** Fund accounting answers a different one: **did we use each pot of money the way we promised?** Nonprofits get money with strings attached, so they track it in separate "funds," each with its own rules, instead of pooling everything into a single bottom line. Fund accounting is accountability accounting, not profit accounting.

That single reframing is the hook and the spine of the whole video. Everything else (restricted vs unrestricted, the statements, the worked example) hangs off it.

## Angle / why this video is different from the slop

Most "what is fund accounting" content on YouTube is either a CPA droning through GAAP, or a software ad. Two things make ours honest and useful:

1. **It uses the current FASB model.** A lot of older explainers still say nonprofits have *three* net asset classes (unrestricted, temporarily restricted, permanently restricted). FASB collapsed that to **two** classes in ASU 2016-14 (effective for fiscal years beginning after Dec 15, 2017). Getting this right is the credibility signal. We lead the "building blocks" chapter with the modern two-bucket model and only mention the old three buckets as history.
2. **Builder honesty.** No "I spent 20 years as a nonprofit controller." The framing is: "I build software that has to model these rules exactly, so I had to learn precisely how the money is supposed to move." That's true and it's the right authority for a builder.

## Structure (chapters)

0. **Cold open / hook** — the two questions. Profit vs "did we keep our promises." Set up that nonprofits answer the second one.
1. **What fund accounting is** — money lives in separate funds, each with a purpose and rules; you track accountability per fund, not one merged bottom line. Analogy: labeled envelopes, not one checking account.
2. **Why nonprofits need it** — restricted money. A funder gives you $X "only for the after-school program." A normal profit-and-loss statement can't show whether you honored that. Fund accounting can. Tie to the real consequence: funders and auditors ask "show me you used it as restricted."
3. **The building blocks** — funds + the modern two net-asset classes: *without donor restrictions* and *with donor restrictions*. Explain restricted vs unrestricted plainly. Note the old three-class model is retired (ASU 2016-14) so viewers aren't confused by older articles.
4. **A worked example** — one small org, two funds (a restricted grant for one program + general operating). Show how the same $ lands in different funds and why you can't borrow from one to cover the other without it being visible.
5. **How it differs from regular accounting + the statements** — Statement of Financial Position (not "balance sheet"), Statement of Activities (not "income statement"); "net assets," not "owner's equity"/"profit." Quick contrast table.
6. **Outro + soft plug** — recap the one idea; fund accounting is doable without a CPA army if your system tracks funds for you. Soft GrantPipe mention; email-gated **Restricted Fund Tracking Spreadsheet** lead magnet ("we'll send it to your inbox").
