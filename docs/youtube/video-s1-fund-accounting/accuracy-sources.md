## Errors to AVOID (common in online fund-accounting explainers)
1. **Do not say there are still three net asset classes.** Two is current (since FY beginning after Dec 15, 2017). Three is history only.
2. **Do not conflate GASB and FASB buckets.** GASB governmental fund balance has five categories (nonspendable/restricted/committed/assigned/unassigned, GASB 54); that is NOT the FASB "with/without donor restrictions" model. This video is FASB nonprofit only.
3. **Restricted ≠ board-designated.** Donor restrictions are external and only the donor can lift them; board designations are internal and reversible, and live in "without donor restrictions." Never call board reserves "restricted."
4. **Released-from-restriction amounts are a reclassification, not new revenue.** Don't imply the org "earns" money again when a restriction is met.
5. **Don't overstate the simplification.** "Temporarily/permanently restricted" are gone as face-of-statement classes but the purpose/time-vs-perpetual distinction survives in required disclosures.
6. **Don't claim every nonprofit MUST run separate legal funds/ledgers or special software.** Fund accounting is a net-asset-classification + reporting discipline; many small nonprofits track restrictions via classes/tags. Legally separate funds are a governmental (GASB) thing. (This is also why the soft GrantPipe plug must stay soft — a tool *helps* track funds, it is not legally mandatory.)
