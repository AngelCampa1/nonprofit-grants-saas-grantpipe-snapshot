WHY ALLOCATION MATTERS
A funder rarely hands you money for one thing. A single award might cover part of a program, part of staff time, and part of overhead, and each slice answers to a different rule. If you track the award as one lump sum, you can't prove you stayed inside any of those slices. Splitting it across funds up front means that when the report comes due, the breakdown is already there. You're not reverse-engineering it from a bank statement at 11pm.

WHY THE FOUR NUMBERS BEAT A SINGLE BALANCE
A spreadsheet usually shows one number: how much money is in the account. That hides the two questions a funder actually asks. Allocated tells you how much of the award you've committed to specific funds. Unallocated is what's still loose and waiting for a home. Remaining to Spend is what's actually left once real spending is counted. Keeping these four apart means you can answer "is this grant fully assigned?" and "how much can we still spend?" without doing math in your head. When you change an allocation, all four update together, so they can never quietly fall out of sync the way two spreadsheet cells do.
