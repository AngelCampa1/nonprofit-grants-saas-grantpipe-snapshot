WHAT YOU'LL NEED BEFORE YOU START
- A few minutes and your nonprofit's basics: legal name, the month your fiscal year starts, and the timezone your team works in.
- An export of your current donor list as a CSV. Most tools and spreadsheets can save one. If you're on another CRM, look for an export to CSV option.
- That's it. You don't need to clean every cell first, because the preview step lets you check the file before anything is written.

WHAT'S IN THE VIDEO
- The three setup fields that actually matter: your organization name, your fiscal year start, and your timezone. Each one quietly changes what you see later, so getting them right on day one saves you a cleanup down the road.
- Why the fiscal year start matters more than it looks: it decides how your reports group a year, so a nonprofit running July to June sees the right periods instead of a default calendar year.
- Importing a CSV of contacts, donations, grants, or grant opportunities from whatever you use now, a spreadsheet or an old system you're leaving behind.

- Reading the result after you commit: what got added, what was skipped as a duplicate, and what needs a fix. No mystery, no guessing.

AN HONEST NOTE ON IMPORTS
Importing is where most setups go wrong, so the preview step is the part to slow down on. Check the count of new records against the rows in your file. If the numbers don't match, something got skipped as a duplicate, and that's usually a good thing, but it's worth a look. You can always re-run an import after you fix the source file. Nothing here is one-way, and nothing commits until you say so.

If your spreadsheet is messy, and most are, the fix is almost always in the source file, not the tool. The usual culprits are one column doing two jobs, a full name where you need a first and last, or amounts saved as text instead of numbers. Sort those out in your spreadsheet, export a fresh CSV, and the import goes clean the first time. Spending five minutes on the file beats an hour untangling records later.
