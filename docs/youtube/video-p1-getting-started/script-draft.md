### Cold open — the part everyone dreads

[VISUAL: GrantPipe wordmark on warm paper, then hard-cut to the real signed-in app landing on the onboarding Welcome screen — screen 1. Cursor already on screen.]

Setting up new software is where good intentions go to die. You sign up, you get a blank screen, and somewhere there's a spreadsheet you're scared to touch.

So let's just do it. In the next few minutes you'll set up your organization and get your donors into GrantPipe. Three fields, one file. And before anything gets saved, you'll see exactly what GrantPipe is about to do.

I built this. Let me walk you through it.

### Set up your org — it's three fields, not a quiz

[VISUAL: Welcome step — highlight the three value bullets, then click "Get started" to advance to Org setup (screen 2). Zoom to the form.]

When you first sign in, GrantPipe asks you three things. Not a questionnaire. Three.

[VISUAL: Highlight the Organization name field; type "Heartland Senior Services".]

Your organization's name. This is what prints on your reports and exports, so spell it the way you want a funder to see it.

[VISUAL: Open the Fiscal year start month dropdown; select the org's start month.]

The month your fiscal year starts. Most nonprofits aren't on a January calendar, and this is why your year-end reports never seem to line up. Set it once here and your dashboards and financial reports follow it.

[VISUAL: Highlight Timezone field, default America/New_York.]

And your timezone. This one's quiet but it matters — it's how GrantPipe knows when a deadline is actually due, so a report isn't marked late because a server somewhere is on a different clock.

That's the whole setup. Save it, and you've got a workspace.

### Bring your data in — pick what you're importing

[VISUAL: Onboarding Import step (screen 3) — "Do you have a spreadsheet of donors or contacts?" Click "Import a CSV". Cut to the Import screen, Choose source step (screen 4) with the 4-step bar visible.]

Next: your data. If you've got a spreadsheet of donors, you're not typing them in one by one.

GrantPipe imports four kinds of records — contacts, donations, grants, and grant opportunities. Start with contacts, since that's usually the donor list everyone already has.

[VISUAL: Open the preset dropdown — show Generic CSV, Bloomerang, DonorPerfect, Salesforce NPSP.]

If your data came out of Bloomerang, DonorPerfect, or Salesforce's nonprofit pack, pick that preset and the columns line up for you. Anything else — Excel, an old Blackbaud export, whatever you've got — choose Generic CSV and map the columns yourself. It's not hard, and there's a template you can download to match.

### Upload and preview — the part where nothing can go wrong

[VISUAL: Choose source — show the 10MB note + ".csv" requirement. Advance to Upload step (screen 5), select a real CSV file. Advance to Preview (screen 6).]

Upload your file — any CSV up to ten megabytes. Then GrantPipe shows you a preview.

[VISUAL: Zoom to the preview table: total row count + first five rows mapped. Hold on the "Nothing is saved during preview" line.]

Here's the part I want you to actually relax about. This is a preview. Nothing is saved. GrantPipe reads your file, tells you how many rows it found, and shows you the first five so you can check the columns landed where you expected. If something looks off, you back out and fix the spreadsheet. Nothing has touched your account yet.

This is the whole point. You get to look before you leap.

### Commit and read the result — honestly

[VISUAL: Click "Commit import". Cut to the result summary (screen 7): inserted / duplicate / failed counts.]

When the preview looks right, you commit. Now GrantPipe writes the records — and it tells you the truth about what happened.

[VISUAL: Highlight each number as it's named — inserted, then duplicates, then failed.]

Three numbers. How many it added. How many were duplicates it skipped, because it recognized a contact you already had instead of making a second copy. And how many failed — with the exact line and field, so you know what to fix.

[VISUAL: Show a row error in the "Line N, field: message" format, then the Import history list (screen 8) with the completed import.]

If a few rows fail, that's normal — a missing email, a date in a weird format. Fix those lines, re-import just them. And every import you ever run is logged here, with its counts, so you've always got a record of what came in and when.

[VISUAL: Navigate to the Donors list (screen 9), now populated with the imported contacts.]

And there they are. Your donors, in GrantPipe.
