# GrantPipe SEO expansion research

## Executive summary

GrantPipe already has the bones of a strong SEO program: a productized homepage, transparent pricing plus an AI-readable pricing file, capability pages for grant management/compliance/reporting/tracking, topic hubs, guides, best-of pages, compare/alternative pages, state pages, role pages, workflow pages, and free-resource infrastructure. The opportunity is not to invent a completely new SEO motion. It is to fix trust and consistency gaps, then scale the parts of the site that are already closest to revenue: competitor alternatives, compliance workflows, migration content, and template-driven lead magnets. citeturn13search2turn6view7turn7view7turn13search3

The most important finding is that GrantPipe's current content is strategically strong but operationally uneven. Several hubs in navigation still say "More content coming soon,” including the best-lists hub, by-role hub, workflows hub, free-resources hub, and integrations hub. The compare hub also shows an inconsistency: Google's indexed snippet says the compare section covers 43 articles across three categories, while the rendered page currently shows "0 articles across 3 categories.” That kind of mismatch weakens trust, crawl clarity, and conversion paths. citeturn7view0turn7view3turn7view4turn7view5turn7view6turn13search3turn11view0

The second major finding is a compliance accuracy problem. The current authoritative threshold in 2 CFR 200.501 is **$1,000,000** in federal expenditures during a fiscal year, not $1,000,000; the revised Uniform Guidance took effect on October 1, 2024, and guidance from secondary interpreters notes that the audit-threshold change applies for audits for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later. Multiple GrantPipe pages still state the old threshold or mix the old and new thresholds on the same page. Those pages should be corrected before GrantPipe scales more compliance content, because trust is the entire sale in this category. citeturn14search0turn15search12turn15search8turn16search0turn16search1turn16search2turn16search4turn16search5

The market angle is clear. Most well-known nonprofit CRMs still center donors, fundraising, or grant discovery. Fewer vendors combine donor CRM, active-grant workflow, restricted-fund tracking, compliance reporting, and accounting/fund-accounting in one product. That gives GrantPipe a strong position for "grant-funded nonprofit” queries, especially where the buyer's pain shows up after awards are active: grant closeout, spend-down tracking, restricted-fund reconciliation, SF-425 / SEFA readiness, board reporting, and moving off spreadsheets. citeturn13search2turn22search0turn23search0turn24search0turn25search4turn26search4

The content plan below prioritizes **100 new SEO pages** across BOFU, MOFU, TOFU, and lead magnets, but the first publish wave should be narrower: pages that directly convert migration pain and compliance urgency into demos, trials, and downloads. In practice, the fastest path to qualified pipeline is: fix accuracy issues, standardize pricing references, strengthen internal links, and publish the first 20 pages centered on alternatives, migration, compliance checklists, and high-intent workflow pages. citeturn13search2turn13search3turn10search7turn28search9

## Current GrantPipe content map

### Current site architecture

GrantPipe's current information architecture, based on live navigation, indexed pages, and search snippets, includes product/capability pages, a pricing page plus `pricing.txt`, topic hubs, guides, best-list pages, comparison infrastructure, role/state/workflow directories, free-resource infrastructure, books/accounting messaging, and integrations. The homepage positions the product around "fundraising, compliance, and nonprofit accounting on one record,” while the pricing file explicitly names the target audience as executive and development leaders at mid-sized nonprofits with roughly $500K-$10M budgets. The topic hub says there are 53 articles across four categories, and search results show an active comparison program. citeturn13search2turn6view7turn7view7turn13search3

Several of these sections appear intentionally programmatic, which is fine for SEO, but many still read like scaffolding rather than finished hubs. That is especially visible where the page copy says "More content coming soon,” where there are very few links surfaced on the rendered page, or where the page appears to exist mostly as a sign-up wall rather than a content destination. citeturn7view0turn7view3turn7view4turn7view5turn7view6

### Content gaps and trust risks

The biggest trust gaps are factual drift and "in-progress” presentation. Pages and snippets discovered in April 2026 used outdated `$159-$799/mo self-serve` pricing and "pre-launch” language even though the homepage and pricing file now show current self-serve tiers at $329 / $539 / $1,079, with Enterprise handled as a custom path below pricing. That inconsistency appears in legacy best-list and comparison pages and will confuse both buyers and AI retrieval systems. citeturn17search7turn17search8turn17search11turn19search8turn13search2turn6view7

The compliance risk is more serious. GrantPipe has multiple pages that still say the single-audit threshold is $1,000,000, even though the current regulation says $1,000,000 and supporting implementation notes clarify the effective-date nuance. There is also at least one page that contains both the old and new thresholds, which is more damaging than simply being stale because it makes the content internally contradictory. citeturn14search0turn15search12turn15search8turn16search0turn16search1turn16search2

### What this means for BOFU content

GrantPipe has the best chance to win when it frames the buying question around **what happens after the award** and when it narrows the audience to **grant-funded nonprofits with donor relationships and active compliance work**, rather than trying to beat fundraising-first CRMs at pure fundraising or beat grantmaker systems at applicant portals. The strongest BOFU claims are therefore about workflow fit, bounded implementation, migration away from spreadsheets or expiring tools, and fewer handoffs between development and finance. citeturn13search2turn22search0turn24search0turn25search4turn28search9

- Avoid repetitive exact-match overuse from one page
- Put one CTA link near the first answer block and one near the final summary / FAQ section
