Massachusetts foundation philanthropy is concentrated in Greater Boston but reaches across the Commonwealth through a mix of community, family, corporate, and operating foundations. The nine funders profiled below represent the largest sustained sources of grant capital for Massachusetts nonprofits across direct service, capacity-building, and institutional support.

This list is not exhaustive - hundreds of smaller family and corporate foundations operate in the state - but it covers the funders that show up most consistently in mid-market Massachusetts nonprofit grant pipelines.

---

## 1. The Boston Foundation - Greater Boston community foundation

**Annual grants:** $200M+ across Greater Boston and beyond

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Selectively, through competitive grant programs

The Boston Foundation is the community foundation for Greater Boston. It distributes more than $200 million annually through a mix of competitive grant programs and donor-advised fund (DAF) grants. The foundation's research function - the Indicators Project - tracks regional outcomes and informs grantmaking strategy. For Massachusetts nonprofits, the Boston Foundation is both a direct funder and a fiscal home for hundreds of donor-advised funds whose holders may recommend grants.

**Best fit:** Established Greater Boston nonprofits with audited financials, strong governance, and outcomes data that maps to the foundation's strategic priorities.

## 2. Cummings Foundation - Three-year grants in eastern Massachusetts

**Grant range:** Approximately $100,000-$500,000 over three years

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Yes, through the $30 Million Grant Program

Cummings Foundation operates one of the largest open competitive grant programs in Massachusetts. The annual cycle awards three-year grants to nonprofits serving Middlesex, Essex, or Suffolk counties. The application portal opens for a defined window each spring. Selection is competitive - typically thousands of letters of inquiry - but the process is transparent and the multi-year structure reduces funder-churn cost for grantees.

**Best fit:** Direct service nonprofits in the three eligible counties with measurable outcomes and the capacity to plan three years ahead.

## 3. Klarman Family Foundation - Boston-based private foundation

**Grant range:** $50,000-$2,000,000+

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Rarely

Klarman Family Foundation is a Boston-based private foundation funding Jewish life, biomedical research, education, and arts and culture. Its grants tend to be larger and longer-term than typical Massachusetts family foundations. Most grants flow from invitation following established relationships. For organizations whose mission aligns with one of the foundation's published focus areas, persistent program officer engagement is the path forward.

**Best fit:** Established Boston-area nonprofits in Jewish life, biomedical research, education, or arts and culture with documented institutional capacity.

## 4. Barr Foundation - New England with a Massachusetts emphasis

**Grant range:** $50,000-$1,500,000+

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** No

Barr Foundation is a Boston-based private foundation focused on arts and creativity, climate, and education. The foundation publishes detailed strategy briefs for each focus area, which makes self-screening easier than at many large funders. Grants are typically by invitation following multi-year relationship development with program officers. Multi-year grants are common.

**Best fit:** New England nonprofits whose strategy aligns tightly with one of Barr's published focus areas and who can sustain a long program officer relationship.

## 5. MassMutual Foundation - Corporate foundation with statewide reach

**Grant range:** $25,000-$250,000

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Yes

MassMutual Foundation is the corporate foundation of MassMutual Financial Group. Headquartered in Springfield, the foundation has a stronger Western Massachusetts presence than most major Massachusetts funders. Focus areas include economic mobility, financial wellness, and education. Open application cycles run on predictable timing.

**Best fit:** Massachusetts nonprofits with measurable outcomes in financial wellness, economic mobility, or workforce development - particularly in Springfield and Western Massachusetts.

## 6. Liberty Mutual Foundation - Boston-area corporate philanthropy

**Grant range:** $25,000-$500,000

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** For some priority areas

Liberty Mutual Foundation is the corporate foundation of Liberty Mutual Insurance. Its sustained focus on homelessness, housing stability, and youth development has made it one of the most consistent corporate funders for Boston-area human services. Some priority areas accept open applications; others operate by invitation.

**Best fit:** Boston-area nonprofits addressing homelessness, housing, or youth development with measurable outcomes and a clear theory of change.

## 7. Eos Foundation - Hunger and women's economic security

**Grant range:** $25,000-$250,000

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Selectively

Eos Foundation is a Massachusetts private foundation with a distinctive focus on hunger, women's economic security, and gender equity. The foundation supports both direct service and policy and advocacy work, which is rarer among major Massachusetts funders. Grants are typically by invitation following outreach to program staff.

**Best fit:** Massachusetts nonprofits in hunger, women's economic security, or gender equity with measurable outcomes and a clear policy or systems angle.

## 8. Yawkey Foundation - Statewide Massachusetts focus

**Grant range:** $25,000-$500,000

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Yes, through annual cycle

Yawkey Foundation honors the legacy of Tom and Jean Yawkey with statewide grantmaking in Massachusetts. Focus areas span youth, education, health, human services, and conservation. The annual application cycle is predictable, and the foundation funds capital, program, and capacity grants. Statewide reach makes Yawkey one of the more accessible large funders for organizations outside Greater Boston.

**Best fit:** Massachusetts nonprofits with statewide or regional impact across youth, education, health, human services, or conservation.

## 9. Highland Street Foundation - Family foundation with broad Massachusetts reach

**Grant range:** $25,000-$500,000

**Open to unsolicited proposals:** Through ongoing program staff relationships

Highland Street Foundation is a Massachusetts family foundation supporting youth, education, health, and the arts. The foundation is widely known for the Free Fun Friday summer program but funds a broad portfolio of community grants throughout the year. Grants typically flow through ongoing program staff relationships rather than open application cycles.

**Best fit:** Massachusetts nonprofits in youth, education, health, or the arts with measurable outcomes and capacity for a multi-year funder relationship.

## How to Build a Massachusetts Foundation Pipeline

Three filters help structure a Massachusetts foundation pipeline:

1. **Geographic eligibility.** Cummings (three counties), Boston Foundation (Greater Boston), MassMutual (statewide with Springfield emphasis), Yawkey (statewide). Match each prospect against your service area.
2. **Application posture.** Open competitive (Cummings, Yawkey, MassMutual) versus invitation-only (Barr, Klarman). Open cycles fit a structured pipeline; invitation cycles require relationship development.
3. **Mission fit.** Most large Massachusetts funders publish focus areas. Cold proposals into off-strategy areas waste cycles for everyone.

Once a pipeline is built, restricted fund tracking matters. Massachusetts foundations expect grant-specific reporting, expenditure documentation, and clean audit trails.
