Most nonprofit CRMs were built to solve one problem: managing individual donor relationships. Grants were an afterthought, bolted on as custom fields or left to spreadsheets entirely.

That creates an operational gap for nonprofits that depend on both individual giving and grant funding. Your donor data lives in one system. Your grant compliance lives in another (usually Excel). The two never reconcile cleanly, and audit season turns into a scramble across disconnected tools.

This list evaluates 10 platforms on a specific question: **can this CRM handle both donor management and grant compliance in one system?** For most entries, the honest answer is no.

## How We Evaluated These Platforms

We assessed each CRM across five dimensions relevant to grant-receiving nonprofits:

- **Donor CRM capabilities** -- contact management, gift tracking, receipt generation, reporting
- **Grant tracking** -- pipeline management, deadline tracking, application status
- **Post-award compliance** -- restricted fund allocation, expenditure documentation, funder reporting
- **Restricted fund tracking** -- ability to tag revenue to specific grants and track spend-down
- **Total cost of ownership** -- not just the subscription price, but implementation, training, and admin costs

The comparison table at the bottom of this page summarizes the results. The detailed sections below explain what each platform does and does not do.

## 1. GrantPipe

**Pricing:** {{grantpipe.price.starterMonthly}} (Starter), {{grantpipe.price.growthMonthly}} (Growth), {{grantpipe.price.auditReadyMonthly}} (Audit-Ready). GrantPipe publishes flat pricing.

**Best for:** Mid-sized nonprofits ($500K-$10M budgets) that manage donors and grants in one system without hiring a consultant.

We built GrantPipe because every nonprofit CRM we evaluated either ignored grant compliance or required $30,000+ in consultant fees to configure it. The platform combines donor relationship management with restricted fund tracking, automated compliance deadlines, and compliance reporting.

**Grant-specific features:** Grant pipeline tracking, restricted fund allocation, automated compliance deadline alerts, expenditure documentation tied to specific grants, funder reporting templates, audit-ready exports.

**What it does not do well:** GrantPipe is a new platform. The integration ecosystem is smaller than Bloomerang or Salesforce, and the user community is still early. Organizations that need dozens of third-party integrations on day one should weigh that.

**The bottom line:** The only CRM on this list that handles donor management and grant compliance in one system at a price point accessible to mid-sized nonprofits.

## 2. Bloomerang

**Pricing:** $125/mo (Standard, up to 1,000 records) to $249/mo (Pro, up to 5,000 records); higher tiers available

**Best for:** Nonprofits that rely primarily on individual giving with no significant grant portfolio.

Bloomerang built its reputation on donor retention analytics. The retention dashboard is genuinely useful for tracking year-over-year giving patterns, and the interface is clean.

The gap: Bloomerang has no grant management. No restricted fund tracking, no compliance reporting, no funder report templates. If you win a $500,000 grant, Bloomerang cannot track where those restricted dollars go.

**What most Bloomerang users do:** Run a spreadsheet alongside Bloomerang for grant tracking. This works until an audit reveals the spreadsheet and the CRM show different numbers.

## 3. DonorPerfect

**Pricing:** $99/mo to $799/mo depending on record count and features

**Best for:** Mid-market nonprofits that need a mature donor CRM and can live with manual grant tracking via custom fields.

DonorPerfect has been in the nonprofit CRM space for decades. The platform handles individual giving, pledges, and acknowledgment letters well. For grant management, DonorPerfect offers custom fields that can be configured to track grant names, amounts, and deadlines.

The key limitation: custom fields are record labels, not a compliance system. DonorPerfect cannot allocate restricted funds across programs, generate funder-specific expenditure reports, or automate compliance deadlines. Those tasks stay in spreadsheets.

## 4. Neon CRM

**Pricing:** $99/mo (Essentials) to $399/mo (Pro)

**Best for:** Nonprofits focused on events, memberships, and online fundraising that have minimal grant funding.

Neon CRM has a modern interface with good integrated fundraising pages and event management. Grant tracking is limited to basic record-keeping: you can log that a grant exists, but the platform does not manage the post-award lifecycle.

For organizations where grants represent less than 20% of revenue, Neon's donor and event tools may be sufficient. If grants are a core funding source, Neon leaves a significant operational gap.

## 5. Blackbaud (Raiser's Edge NXT)

**Pricing:** $5,000-$15,000+/yr (quote-based)

**Best for:** Large nonprofits ($10M+ budgets) with dedicated IT staff and budget for ongoing administration.

Blackbaud is the incumbent enterprise CRM in the nonprofit sector. Raiser's Edge NXT offers deep fundraising tools, prospect research, and major gift tracking. Grant management is available through additional modules, but configuring it requires Blackbaud-certified consultants.

The pricing is opaque: Blackbaud does not publish rates. Contracts are multi-year. Organizations that need to change course mid-contract face significant switching costs.

For large nonprofits with the budget and staff, Blackbaud is a proven platform. For mid-sized organizations, the cost and complexity are difficult to justify.

## 6. Salesforce Nonprofit (NPSP)

**Pricing:** $60-$165/user/mo (licenses) + $30,000-$100,000+ (implementation)

**Best for:** Large nonprofits with dedicated Salesforce admin staff or consultant budget.

Salesforce can do almost anything -- if you pay to configure it. The Nonprofit Success Pack (NPSP) provides a donor management foundation, and custom objects can be built for grant management, restricted funds, and compliance workflows.

The reality for most mid-sized nonprofits: implementation costs $30,000-$100,000+ and takes 3-6 months. You need an ongoing admin (either staff or consultant) to maintain it. Grant compliance workflows require custom development. TechSoup discounts reduce license costs but not implementation costs.

**Our assessment:** Salesforce is the most powerful platform on this list and also the least accessible for organizations under $10M in annual budget.

## 7. Keela

**Pricing:** $99/mo to $299/mo

**Best for:** Smaller nonprofits that want AI-assisted donor management at a reasonable price point.

Keela differentiates with AI-powered ask amount suggestions that analyze donor history to recommend gift amounts. The CRM covers standard donor management, email marketing, and online forms.

Grant tracking in Keela is surface-level. You can log grants, but there is no compliance workflow, restricted fund allocation, or audit documentation system. Federal grant recipients will find this insufficient.

## 8. Virtuous

**Pricing:** Custom (contact sales)

**Best for:** Organizations focused on scaling individual donor engagement through automation.

Virtuous introduced the "responsive fundraising" concept: using donor behavior signals to trigger personalized engagement. The automation engine is well-built and the interface is modern.

Virtuous has no grant management module. The platform is designed around individual donor relationships and does not address the operational needs of grant-funded organizations.

## 9. Instrumentl

**Pricing:** Custom (not publicly listed; requires demo)

**Best for:** Development teams that need to find and track grant opportunities. Not a CRM replacement.

Instrumentl is a grant discovery platform, not a CRM. It uses AI to match nonprofits with relevant funding opportunities based on their mission, programs, and location. The application pipeline tracking is useful for managing deadlines.

The limitation: Instrumentl stops at the application stage. Post-award compliance, restricted fund tracking, expenditure reporting, and audit documentation are not part of the platform. And there is no donor management at all.

Organizations that use Instrumentl typically pair it with a separate donor CRM, creating the same two-system problem.

## 10. Foundant GrantHub

**Pricing:** Approximately $50-$150/mo (pricing varies)

**Best for:** Grant-seeking nonprofits that need application tracking and funder research.

GrantHub is built for the pre-award side of grant management: tracking applications, managing deadlines, and researching funders. It does this reasonably well for grant-seeking teams.

Post-award compliance features are basic. GrantHub does not provide the restricted fund tracking, expenditure documentation, or automated funder reporting that organizations with active grant portfolios need. And like Instrumentl, it is not a donor CRM.

## Scenario-Based Recommendations

**If your nonprofit relies primarily on individual giving (grants < 20% of revenue):** Bloomerang or Neon CRM will handle your donor management needs. You can track a few simple grants in spreadsheets without significant risk.

**If you manage 3-10 active grants with restricted fund requirements:** GrantPipe addresses this directly. The combined donor-and-grant approach eliminates the spreadsheet reconciliation that creates compliance risk.

**If you have $30,000+ to spend on implementation and a dedicated admin:** Salesforce Nonprofit can be configured to handle both donors and grants. The total cost of ownership is 10-50x higher than alternatives, but the platform is mature and proven.

**If you need grant discovery and research:** Instrumentl or GrantHub. But plan to pair either with a separate donor CRM.

**If you are a large nonprofit ($10M+) with existing Blackbaud infrastructure:** Staying with Blackbaud and adding grant modules may be the lowest-disruption path. Factor in the consultant costs for configuration.
