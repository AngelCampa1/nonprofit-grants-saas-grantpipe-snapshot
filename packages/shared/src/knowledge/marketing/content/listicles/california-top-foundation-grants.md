California's foundation landscape is the most concentrated in the country. A small number of large private foundations and community foundations distribute the majority of foundation dollars in the state. The Urban Institute counts roughly 192,000 active California nonprofits, and Candid's foundation stats put California foundation grantmaking around $14.5 billion in 2022.

This guide covers ten foundations that fund California nonprofits in volume, with eligibility, application process, and typical grant ranges. It is not a comprehensive list - California has thousands of active grantmakers - but starting with these ten gives most organizations a working map of the state's largest grant capital.

For broader context on California nonprofit operations, see the [California state page](/nonprofit-software/california). For formation specifics, see the [California nonprofit startup guide](/resources/guides/california-nonprofit-startup-guide).

## How California Foundation Funding Works

Three structural points worth understanding before you apply anywhere:

1. **Cold applications rarely advance.** Most California foundations rely on staff identification, peer referrals, and existing grantee networks. The exceptions - Weingart, California Wellness, James Irvine - publish open application processes, but even these reward organizations that have built relationships before applying.
2. **Letters of inquiry are the standard entry point.** A 2-3 page LOI describing your organization, the proposed work, and the funding request comes first. Full proposals come only by invitation.
3. **Community foundations distribute through donor-advised funds.** California Community Foundation and Silicon Valley Community Foundation each manage thousands of donor-advised funds. Most grants come from individual donors recommending nonprofits to foundation staff, not from competitive grant programs.

## 1. William and Flora Hewlett Foundation

Headquartered in Menlo Park. The Hewlett Foundation funds education, environment, performing arts, U.S. democracy, global development, and effective philanthropy. 2023 grantmaking totaled approximately $580 million, per the foundation's annual report.

**Application:** Most grants are by program-officer initiative. The foundation accepts very limited unsolicited proposals. The path in is alignment with one of the program strategies, which the foundation publishes in detail at hewlett.org.

**Typical grant size:** $50,000 to $5 million, multi-year general operating support common.

**Best fit:** Established California organizations whose work directly aligns with a Hewlett program strategy.

## 2. David and Lucile Packard Foundation

Los Altos-based, with three program areas: conservation and science, children/families/communities, and population and reproductive health.

**Application:** LOIs accepted in select programs; many grants by invitation. The Children, Families, and Communities program has the most California-focused grantmaking, with priorities in early learning, children's health, and community engagement, particularly in the San Joaquin Valley and Bay Area.

**Typical grant size:** $50,000 to $2 million.

## 3. James Irvine Foundation

San Francisco-based and California-focused. Every grant the Irvine Foundation makes funds work in California. The current strategy centers on the Better Careers (workforce development), Priority Communities (place-based), Fair Government, and Arts portfolios.

**Application:** LOIs accepted for Better Careers and Priority Communities portfolios. The foundation publishes deadlines and review cycles transparently.

**Typical grant size:** $100,000 to $1 million.

## 4. California Wellness Foundation

Statewide health funder. Calwellness publishes a quarterly LOI cycle and is one of the most procedurally accessible large California foundations.

**Application:** Online LOI form, reviewed quarterly. Decisions typically within 4 months of LOI submission.

**Typical grant size:** $50,000 to $400,000, with strong emphasis on general operating support and BIPOC-led organizations.

## 5. The California Endowment

LA-based private health foundation with about $4 billion in assets. Major commitment to 14 Building Healthy Communities sites across California - long-term, place-based investments in specific cities and neighborhoods.

**Application:** Place-based work routes through community partner organizations in each BHC site. Statewide policy and advocacy grants are by invitation.

**Typical grant size:** $100,000 to $5 million.

## 6. California Community Foundation

LA County's community foundation, with about $2.5 billion in assets. Distributes grants through both competitive programs and donor-advised funds. The CCF discretionary grants for LA County nonprofits are open and competitive.

**Application:** Online application for competitive cycles; donor-advised fund grants flow through individual donor recommendations.

**Typical grant size:** $10,000 to $250,000.

## 7. Silicon Valley Community Foundation

Mountain View-based community foundation, the largest in the U.S. by assets. Serves San Mateo and Santa Clara counties.

**Application:** Initiative-based grants on housing, economic security, and immigrant integration; donor-advised fund grants flow through individual donor recommendations.

**Typical grant size:** $25,000 to $500,000.

## 8. Weingart Foundation

LA-based private foundation focused on equity in Southern California (LA, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, Ventura counties). Default grant type is unrestricted general operating support, with explicit racial equity lens.

**Application:** Open application cycles with published deadlines.

**Typical grant size:** $50,000 to $300,000.

## 9. Heising-Simons Foundation

Los Altos-based, with programs in science, climate, education (especially early childhood), and human rights. Significant California early childhood and pre-K policy grantmaking.

**Application:** Most grants by invitation. Some programs accept LOIs.

**Typical grant size:** $100,000 to $1.5 million.

## 10. Conrad N. Hilton Foundation

Agoura Hills-based, with major commitments to chronic homelessness and transition-aged foster youth in LA County, hospitality workforce development, Catholic sisters, and global initiatives.

**Application:** Most California funding flows to named anchor partners in homelessness and foster care work.

**Typical grant size:** $250,000 to $5 million.

## How to Build a California Grant Pipeline

A working California grant strategy combines:

- **3-5 anchor relationships** with foundations whose strategy directly aligns with your work
- **Open application cycles** at Weingart, California Wellness, Irvine, and your local community foundation
- **DAF cultivation** through community foundation program officers who advise individual donors
- **State agency grants** for organizations whose work fits state priorities (separate from foundation funding)

Tracking applications, LOIs, deadlines, and reporting requirements across this pipeline is where most organizations break down. Spreadsheets work for the first 3-5 grants and start to crack at 10.
