Development directors manage an unusual range of responsibilities: grant compliance, donor relationships, board reporting, data management, and team coordination. Templates help because the outputs - grant reports, acknowledgment letters, compliance checklists - follow consistent structures, and consistent structures are worth building once rather than recreating repeatedly.

This list is organized by function. For each template, there is a brief description of what it should contain and where to find it or start building it. GrantPipe's free resource library has templates specifically built for nonprofits; those are linked directly.

## Grant Compliance Templates

### 1. Grant Compliance Checklist

What it contains: a step-by-step checklist for each phase of grant management - pre-award setup, ongoing compliance monitoring, reporting, and closeout. Covers the key requirements for both private foundation and federal awards. Essential for organizations that manage multiple simultaneous grants.

Where to find it: [GrantPipe's grant compliance checklist](/free/nonprofit-crm-evaluation-scorecard) - also available through the [audit trail features](/features/audit-trail-activity-log) documentation.

### 2. Grant Closeout Checklist

What it contains: a complete task list for closing out a grant award - final report submission, budget reconciliation, documentation filing, equipment disposition, and funder correspondence. Closeout is where compliance errors compound; this checklist prevents them.

For federal awards, closeout requirements under 2 CFR 200.344 include specific deadlines for final financial and performance reports. A closeout checklist should include those deadlines explicitly.

Where to find it: Build from the [grant management best practices guide](/resources/guides/grant-management-best-practices). GrantPipe's grant closeout workflow tracks these tasks inside the grant record.

### 3. Grant Reporting Calendar

What it contains: a master calendar of all grant reporting deadlines for active awards - interim reports, annual reports, financial reports, and final reports. Should include the award name, funder, report type, due date, and assigned staff.

The calendar should live somewhere the full development team can see it. In GrantPipe, reporting deadlines are tracked in [grant pipeline management](/features/grant-pipeline-management).

### 4. Grant Kickoff Meeting Agenda

What it contains: a structured agenda for the internal meeting that should happen immediately after receiving a grant award. Covers: award terms review, budget walkthrough, eligible expenses discussion, reporting schedule, data collection plan, and assigned responsibilities.

Organizations that skip the kickoff meeting often discover compliance problems six months later. A standard agenda makes the meeting more efficient and ensures nothing is missed.

### 5. Award Setup Worksheet

What it contains: a data entry worksheet for recording all relevant information when setting up a new grant award - award number, funder contact, award period, approved budget by line item, reporting schedule, special conditions, and any cost match requirements.

This worksheet feeds the data that goes into your grant management system. In GrantPipe, this information is entered when creating a new grant record.

### 6. Time and Effort Certification Template

What it contains: a form for employees whose salaries are charged to federal grants to certify that their time was actually spent on the funded program. Required under 2 CFR 200.430 for federal recipients.

The template should include: employee name, title, award name, period covered, percentage of time on the award, and employee signature. Must be retained as documentation for federal auditors.

### 7. Subrecipient Monitoring Checklist

What it contains: a checklist for monitoring organizations that receive pass-through federal funds from your organization. Covers risk assessment, monitoring plan development, documentation of monitoring activities, and corrective action follow-up. Required if you sub-award federal funds to another organization.

### 8. SF-425 Federal Financial Report Checklist

What it contains: a preparation checklist for completing the Federal Financial Report (Form SF-425), which most federal awards require on a quarterly, semi-annual, or annual basis. Should include data gathering steps: federal expenditures by CFDA number, non-federal expenditures, cash receipts, unliquidated obligations, and program income.

The checklist should also include submission steps: logging into the federal system (Grants.gov or a specific agency portal), completing each field, and retaining a copy of the submitted form.

### 9. Single Audit Prep Timeline

What it contains: a 12-month reverse timeline working backward from your fiscal year-end to identify when to engage your auditor, when to complete SEFA preparation, when to gather major program documentation, and when to expect the audit window.

Organizations approaching the $1,000,000 federal expenditure threshold (for fiscal years ending on or after September 30, 2025; previously lower) should be working toward this timeline before they cross it.

### 10. Grant Budget Tracking Template

What it contains: a spreadsheet template for tracking actual expenditures versus approved budget by line item for each active grant. Should update monthly from your accounting records. This is the core document for grant compliance monitoring.

Most accounting systems can export this data. GrantPipe's [restricted fund tracking](/features/restricted-fund-tracking) maintains this tracking automatically.

## Donor Stewardship Templates

### 11. Donor Thank-You Letter Template

What it contains: a letter template for acknowledging charitable contributions that meets IRS requirements: organization name, date of contribution, amount, and a statement on whether goods or services were provided. Written for human beings, not legal review.

The best thank-you letters are specific enough to feel personal and brief enough to be read. A template should have variable fields for donor name, gift amount, fund designation, and a two-line program update that can be customized per mailing.

For the broader stewardship strategy, see [GrantPipe's donor retention playbook](/free/donor-retention-playbook).

### 12. Donor Acknowledgment Letter (Formal)

What it contains: a more formal acknowledgment letter for major gifts ($1,000+) that includes all IRS requirements plus a more personal expression of gratitude appropriate for significant contributors. Should include the executive director's signature (actual, not printed).

### 13. Major Donor Cultivation Plan

What it contains: a structured template for planning the relationship-building steps before making a major gift ask. Typically covers: discovery meeting, program experience (site visit or event), proposal stage, and stewardship post-gift. Should track meeting notes, ask amount, and timeline.

In GrantPipe, major donor cultivation notes live in the [donor segmentation](/features/donor-segmentation) record alongside giving history.

### 14. Matching Gift Tracking Template

What it contains: a spreadsheet for tracking which donors have indicated their employer offers matching gifts, the status of each match submission, and the total matching gift revenue attributed to each campaign or fiscal year.

Double the Donation (covered in the prospect research tools guide) automates much of this tracking for organizations with high donation volume.

## Reporting and Board Communication Templates

### 15. Funder Report Template

What it contains: a narrative and financial report template for submitting required progress reports to grant funders. Should include sections for: program narrative, outcome data, financial summary (actual vs. budget), and any required attachments. Most funders have their own format requirements - a flexible template that maps to common formats saves time.

GrantPipe's [funder reporting templates](/features/funder-reporting-templates) generate the financial data section automatically from fund records.

### 16. Board Financial Report Template

What it contains: a board-facing summary of the organization's financial position - revenue vs. budget, expenses vs. budget, cash position, and restricted fund summary. Should be readable by board members who are not finance specialists.

The data for this report should come from your accounting system and CRM. GrantPipe provides the grant and donor data; your accounting system provides the full P&L.

### 17. Impact Report Template

What it contains: an annual document summarizing your organization's program outputs, outcomes, and financial stewardship for donors, funders, and community stakeholders. Should include: mission statement, key program metrics, donor giving statistics, grant compliance summary, and forward-looking narrative.

The best impact reports are honest about both results and challenges. Funders have seen enough reports to notice when outcomes are cherry-picked.

### 18. Development Plan Template

What it contains: a 12-month development plan documenting fundraising goals by revenue source (individual donors, major gifts, grants, events, corporate), key strategies, staffing assignments, and milestone timeline. Should align with your organizational budget.

This template is particularly useful for boards that want to understand the development strategy before approving a budget that depends on it.

## CRM and Data Management Templates

### 19. Nonprofit CRM Evaluation Scorecard

What it contains: a structured comparison framework for evaluating CRM options. Includes criteria for donor management, grant tracking, reporting, integration, pricing, and support. Designed to make vendor comparison objective rather than based on sales demonstrations alone.

GrantPipe offers this free: [nonprofit CRM evaluation scorecard](/free/nonprofit-crm-evaluation-scorecard).

### 20. CRM Migration Data Map

What it contains: a worksheet for mapping data fields from your current CRM (or spreadsheets) to your new system's data model. Covers: contact fields, gift history, relationship codes, custom fields, notes, and documents. Essential for migrations that need to preserve historical data.

Ask your new CRM vendor for their standard data import template and use it as the destination map.

### 21. Prospect Research Profile Template

What it contains: a standardized form for documenting research findings on major gift prospects. Fields include: wealth indicators, philanthropic history, connection to your organization, suggested ask range, relationship stage, and next steps. Structured consistently across all prospects so major gift officer notes are comparable.

## Grant Prospecting and Pipeline Templates

### 22. Grant Proposal Budget Template

What it contains: a detailed line-item budget template for grant applications, with column formulas for calculating personnel costs (salary - FTE - percent time), fringe benefits, direct costs by category, indirect costs, and match requirements. Should export cleanly to Excel for submission.

Federal grant applications typically require a specific budget format (SF-424A for many federal programs). The template should either follow that format or be adaptable to it.

### 23. Grant Rejection Tracking Log

What it contains: a log of declined grant applications with fields for: funder name, program area, amount requested, decision date, reason given (if any), and decision on whether to reapply. Most development programs lose more applications than they win - tracking declines builds institutional memory about which funders are worth re-approaching.

### 24. Funder Pipeline Tracker

What it contains: a spreadsheet or database view tracking all grant opportunities at each stage: research, LOI submitted, application in progress, submitted and pending, awarded, declined, and closed. Should include amount, deadline, probability of success (your estimate), and staff owner.

In GrantPipe, this is the [grant pipeline management](/features/grant-pipeline-management) view.

### 25. Volunteer In-Kind Valuation Worksheet

What it contains: a worksheet for calculating and documenting the value of volunteer time donated to programs, which may count toward cost match requirements on grants. Uses the Independent Sector's published value of volunteer time as the standard rate. Includes fields for: volunteer name, dates, hours, role, and calculated value.

Required documentation if you are claiming volunteer time as matching funds on a federal or state grant. The worksheet must be retained as audit documentation.

## Putting the Templates to Work

Templates are worth less than the systems they feed into. A grant budget template that never connects to your fund tracking system produces numbers that drift from reality within weeks of award. A prospect research profile template that lives in a folder nobody checks stops being useful by the second quarterly review.

The most productive use of these templates is as inputs to your CRM and grant management system.
