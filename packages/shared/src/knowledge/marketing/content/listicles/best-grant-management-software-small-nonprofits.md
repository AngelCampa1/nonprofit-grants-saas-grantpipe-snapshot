Small nonprofits looking for grant management software run into the same wall fast: most tools are too expensive, too complex, or built for the wrong user.

Enterprise platforms like Salesforce require $30,000+ in implementation costs. Mid-market CRMs like Bloomerang handle donors but ignore grants entirely. Dedicated grant tools like Instrumentl focus on finding grants, not managing the compliance obligations after you win them. And most "grant management software" in search results is actually built for foundations awarding grants, not nonprofits receiving them.

The result: most small nonprofits manage grants in spreadsheets alongside whatever donor CRM they can afford. This works until it creates a problem: usually an incomplete audit trail, a missed funder report, or restricted funds that got commingled with general revenue.

This list evaluates 7 options for small nonprofits under $1M in annual budget based on three criteria: **affordability**, **ease of use without a dedicated admin**, and **actual grant compliance capability**.

## What Small Nonprofits Need vs. What the Market Sells

Before evaluating specific tools, it helps to separate what small nonprofits actually need from the features vendors push:

**What you need:**

- A way to track which dollars are restricted and which are not
- Deadline alerts so funder reports do not slip through cracks
- Basic templates for quarterly and annual funder reporting
- Donor management (most small nonprofits cannot afford separate systems)
- Something your existing staff can use without a training budget

**What vendors sell you that you probably do not need yet:**

- AI-powered analytics dashboards
- Multi-program allocation engines
- External auditor portals
- Broad private funder database search (unless finding non-federal funders is your bottleneck)
- Advanced workflow automation

The mismatch between what the market sells and what small nonprofits need is why spreadsheets remain the default. The available software either does too much at too high a price, or handles only half the problem.

## 1. GrantPipe

**Pricing:** Published self-serve pricing

**Best for:** Small nonprofits that need donor CRM and grant compliance in one system at the lowest possible price point.

We built GrantPipe because the gap between "spreadsheets" and "$30,000 Salesforce implementation" had nothing in the middle for small and mid-sized nonprofits. The Starter tier includes donor CRM, grant pipeline tracking, restricted fund tagging, compliance deadline alerts, and basic funder reporting templates.

**Why it matters for small nonprofits:** You do not have to buy a $125/month CRM and then add a $50-$150/month grant tracker on top. One system, one login, one data set.

**The practical limitation:** GrantPipe is strongest when your team wants Grants.gov search, manual/imported non-federal opportunity tracking, donor CRM, and post-award grant operations in one system. If your main requirement is a broad marketplace of third-party apps, AI matching, or private foundation prospect research, verify that fit before you switch.

## 2. ZoomGrants

**Pricing:** Free for grant applicants

**Best for:** Submitting applications to funders that specifically use ZoomGrants as their application platform.

ZoomGrants is not grant management software for nonprofits. It is an application management platform that funders purchase to manage their grant review process. As an applicant, you can use it for free to submit to ZoomGrants-using funders.

This distinction matters because ZoomGrants appears in "grant management software" searches, and some small nonprofits confuse it with a portfolio management tool. It does not track your grants across funders, manage restricted funds, or handle compliance reporting.

**Use case:** If you apply to several funders that use ZoomGrants, it is a useful (free) part of your workflow. It is not a substitute for grant management.

## 3. Foundant GrantHub

**Pricing:** Approximately $50-$150/month (pricing varies by organization size)

**Best for:** Grant-seeking nonprofits that need to organize applications, track deadlines, and research funders.

GrantHub is one of the few tools actually designed for grant-seeking nonprofits rather than grantmakers. The application tracking, deadline management, and funder research features are practical for development teams juggling multiple grant opportunities.

**The gap for small nonprofits:** GrantHub does not include a donor CRM. Organizations using GrantHub still need Bloomerang, Little Green Light, or another CRM for individual donor management. At $50-$150 (GrantHub) plus $45-$135 (CRM), the combined monthly cost reaches $95-$285 for two disconnected systems.

Post-award compliance features exist but are basic. GrantHub tracks that a grant was awarded and when reports are due, but restricted fund allocation, expenditure documentation, and compliance reporting are not part of the platform.

## 4. Bloomerang

**Pricing:** $125/mo (Standard) to $249/mo (Pro); scales with database size

**Best for:** Small nonprofits that rely on individual giving and do not manage grants.

Bloomerang does donor management well. The retention analytics dashboard, clean interface, and straightforward onboarding make it a popular choice for small teams. For organizations funded primarily by individual donors, it is a solid platform.

For grant-receiving nonprofits, Bloomerang has a clear gap: no grant tracking, no compliance features, no restricted fund management. Small nonprofits using Bloomerang for grants run spreadsheets alongside it, and the $125/month starting price still leaves you without the grant compliance features GrantPipe Starter includes at published pricing.

If grants represent less than 10% of your revenue and you have no restricted fund obligations, Bloomerang handles the donor side well. If grants are a meaningful part of your funding, the missing functionality becomes a real operational problem.

## 5. Little Green Light + Foundant GrantHub (Two-Tool Approach)

**Pricing:** $45-$135/mo (LGL) + ~$50-$150/mo (GrantHub) = $95-$285/mo total

**Best for:** Budget-conscious organizations comfortable managing two separate systems.

Little Green Light is one of the most affordable standalone donor CRMs. Pairing it with GrantHub gives you basic donor management plus grant application tracking. For small nonprofits that want dedicated tools for each function at reasonable prices, this is the budget two-tool stack.

**The tradeoff:** Two systems means two logins, two data stores, and no integration between them. When a funder asks for a report that combines program data with grant expenditures and donor contributions to the same program, you are pulling from two platforms and reconciling in a spreadsheet.

For organizations with simple needs (a few hundred donors, 2-3 foundation grants), this works. The friction increases with scale.

## 6. Instrumentl

**Pricing:** Published plans from $299/month annually ($349 monthly) to $1,159/month monthly, with Enterprise pricing on request

**Best for:** Development teams that spend significant time searching for grant opportunities.

Instrumentl's AI funder matching is genuinely useful for organizations that need to find more grants. The platform scans grant databases and surfaces opportunities matched to your nonprofit's mission, programs, and location.

**The problem for small nonprofits:** The entry plan is still a meaningful budget line for a small team, and Instrumentl only handles the pre-award phase: finding grants and tracking applications. Post-award compliance, donor management, and restricted fund tracking are not part of the platform.

If finding grants is your bottleneck, Instrumentl may be worth the investment. If managing the grants you already have is the bigger challenge, Instrumentl does not address that.

## 7. Spreadsheets (Excel / Google Sheets)

**Pricing:** $0

**Best for:** Organizations with 1-2 simple foundation grants and no restricted fund requirements.

This is the honest baseline. Most small nonprofits track grants in spreadsheets, and for very simple situations, it works.

**Where spreadsheets break:** Restricted funds, multiple concurrent grants, federal reporting requirements, staff turnover (the spreadsheet lives in one person's head), and audit preparation. The failure is usually invisible until a compliance review or audit reveals the documentation gaps.

**A practical rule:** If you manage 3+ grants or any grant with restricted fund requirements, the time you spend on manual tracking and reconciliation likely exceeds the cost of a $99/month tool. If you manage 1-2 simple grants with annual reporting, spreadsheets are fine.

## Scenario-Based Recommendations

**You manage 1-2 simple foundation grants with annual reports:**
Spreadsheets are adequate. Spend your limited software budget on a donor CRM instead.

**You manage 3+ grants and need to track restricted funds:**
GrantPipe at published Starter pricing handles donor CRM and grant compliance in one system. This is the most cost-effective path for small nonprofits facing real compliance obligations.

**Your biggest problem is finding grants, not managing them:**
Instrumentl or GrantHub for discovery. But plan for a separate CRM investment.

**You already have a donor CRM you like and just need grant tracking:**
GrantHub adds grant application tracking alongside your existing CRM. Accept the two-system limitation and manual reconciliation.

**You have federal grants with detailed compliance requirements:**
Do not use spreadsheets. The compliance documentation requirements under 2 CFR 200 demand audit trails that spreadsheets cannot provide. GrantPipe's compliance automation or a configured Salesforce instance (at much higher cost) are the realistic options.

For Audit-Ready and Enterprise plans, GrantPipe includes an [Auditor & Funder Portal](/features/auditor-funder-portal) that gives external reviewers scoped, time-limited access to specific grants, funds, and documents - no GrantPipe account required, no emailed ZIP files. Every view is logged in an audit trail.
