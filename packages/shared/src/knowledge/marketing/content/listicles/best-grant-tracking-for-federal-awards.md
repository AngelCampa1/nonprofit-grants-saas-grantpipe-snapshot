# Best Grant Tracking Software for Federal Awards (2026)

Federal grant compliance is not a feature most grant tracking software was built to handle. The platforms designed to help nonprofits find grants, manage pipelines, and track deadlines were optimized for the pre-award workflow - not for the documentation trail that begins the moment a federal award is executed.

The compliance obligations that accompany a federal award are specific: Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA) for single audit, SF-425 Federal Financial Reports on quarterly or semi-annual schedules, time-and-effort certification for any staff whose salaries are charged to federal programs, and indirect cost rate application under a Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement or the de minimis rate. Most grant trackers record grant amounts and deadlines. They do not organize expenditures by Assistance Listing Number, store NICRA rates per award, or support time-and-effort certification workflows.

The result: nonprofits with active federal awards frequently run two systems - a grant tracker for the pipeline, and a combination of spreadsheets, paper logs, and accounting workarounds for the compliance layer. The compliance layer is where single-audit findings originate.

## What Federal Award Compliance Actually Requires

Before evaluating software, it helps to understand what "federal grant tracking" means in a compliance context:

**SF-425 Federal Financial Reports:** Required quarterly or semi-annually for most federal awards. Captures cumulative expenditures, recipient share (match), program income, and indirect costs charged against the award. The data must reconcile to the general ledger.

**Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA):** Required for any organization that undergoes a single audit (federal expenditures ‰¥ $1,000,000 for fiscal years ending on or after September 30, 2025; previously lower). Lists every federal program expended during the year with the Assistance Listing Number (formerly CFDA number), federal awards received, expenditures, and pass-through entity information for sub-awards received. The SEFA must reconcile to the audited financial statements.

**Time-and-effort certification:** Required when employee salaries are charged to federal awards. Documents what percentage of each person's time was spent on federally-funded activities. The certification is signed by the employee or supervisor and retained for at least three years. Missing or incomplete certifications are a frequent single-audit finding.

**NICRA indirect cost rate:** Nonprofits with a Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement must apply their approved rate to federal awards, track cumulative indirect costs against the approved budget, and support the rate calculation in audit. Errors in indirect cost tracking result in either under-recovery (money left on the table) or disallowances (repayment required).

**Budget-to-actual monitoring:** Federal awards are awarded against an approved budget by object class (personnel, fringe, supplies, travel, contractual, indirect). Expenditures must stay within approved categories. Reallocation between categories above defined thresholds requires prior approval from the federal program officer.

Software that does not address these five areas is not a federal grant compliance tool - it is a pipeline tracker.

## How These Platforms Compare on Federal Compliance

### GrantPipe

GrantPipe is the only platform in this review that covers all five federal compliance requirements in a single system without requiring custom configuration or a consulting layer.

SF-425 reporting is supported through real-time expenditure tracking per award with cumulative totals updated as costs are posted. SEFA preparation works because every award in GrantPipe stores the Assistance Listing Number, cluster information, and pass-through entity fields - the exact data structure the SEFA requires. Time-and-effort certification is built into the platform as a workflow: staff submit certifications, supervisors approve, and the system maintains a timestamped audit trail. NICRA indirect cost rates are stored per award and applied automatically to each draw, eliminating the manual rate-lookup errors that occur when organizations juggle multiple federal programs simultaneously.

The flat pricing model means costs do not increase as federal award volume grows - relevant for organizations that add federal programs incrementally.

### Amplifund

Amplifund is the closest purpose-built alternative for federal grantees. It covers SF-425 data organization, budget amendment tracking (important when program officers approve reallocations), and subrecipient monitoring for organizations that receive federal awards and pass portions through to community partners.

The gap relative to GrantPipe is in the compliance depth: time-and-effort certification is not a native workflow, and NICRA tracking requires manual application rather than automatic rate calculation. SEFA preparation is supported in the sense that Amplifund stores federal award data by program, but direct SEFA formatting requires export and manual assembly.

Amplifund is worth evaluating for larger nonprofits with complex federal portfolios and existing staff resources to fill the compliance gaps - and budgets that can absorb $8,000-$30,000 in annual software costs.

### eCivis

eCivis is designed for state and local government entities managing federal pass-through programs - CDBG, HUD CPD, USDA rural development - and government entities that sub-award to nonprofits. Its reporting templates and compliance workflows reflect the needs of a government program officer, not a nonprofit sub-recipient.

Nonprofit organizations operating as sub-recipients of federal pass-through dollars may find eCivis relevant if their primary funder (a county agency or state department) uses eCivis to manage the program and requires reporting through its portal. As standalone nonprofit grant management software, the pricing and design orientation make it a poor fit for most organizations.

### SmartSimple Cloud

SmartSimple's strength is configurability. Any federal compliance workflow can be built within it - but "can be built" is different from "is included." An organization that commits to SmartSimple is committing to an implementation project of three to six months minimum and ongoing platform administration thereafter. For large enterprises with IT staff and compliance teams, this is manageable. For a mid-sized nonprofit with a two-person grants department, the total cost of ownership is prohibitive.

### Foundant GLM

Foundant GLM covers the grant lifecycle well for organizations managing private foundation grants. Federal compliance is not its design center. SF-425 data requires manual assembly, SEFA preparation is not supported, and time-and-effort certification has no native workflow. Organizations primarily funded by private foundations that also have one or two smaller federal awards may find GLM adequate - supplemented by manual compliance processes - but organizations with significant federal portfolios will find the gaps grow over time.

## The Spreadsheet Default

The majority of nonprofits with federal awards currently manage compliance in spreadsheets. Award tracking lives in one spreadsheet, time-and-effort tracking in another, indirect cost calculations in a third. The SF-425 is assembled manually from accounting exports. The SEFA is built from memory and prior-year templates.

This approach fails incrementally. A single federal award with annual reporting is manageable. Two awards with quarterly reporting and time-and-effort requirements is where errors start appearing. Three or more concurrent federal programs with a pending single audit is where the manual approach generates the findings that damage relationships with federal program officers.

The cost of a single-audit finding - not in dollars but in staff time, corrective action plan preparation, and the reputational impact on future award applications - typically exceeds the annual cost of dedicated compliance software.

## How to Choose

**If you have active federal awards and need full compliance coverage:** GrantPipe covers SF-425, SEFA, time-and-effort, and NICRA in one platform without custom implementation. For most mid-sized nonprofits with two to twenty federal awards, it is the most direct path to compliant operations.

**If you manage a complex federal portfolio with subrecipient responsibilities:** Amplifund is worth evaluating, with the understanding that time-and-effort and NICRA tracking will require supplemental process.

**If you are primarily a state or local government entity managing federal pass-through programs:** eCivis is designed for your use case, though the pricing reflects a government rather than nonprofit budget.

**If you need complete configurability and have IT resources:** SmartSimple Cloud can be built to match any federal compliance requirement. The question is whether the implementation and administration cost is justified against simpler alternatives.

**If your federal exposure is minimal:** One simple federal award with annual reporting, no time-and-effort requirement, and de minimis indirect cost rate may not justify dedicated software. The compliance obligations grow quickly, however - plan before the next award rather than after.

The test is not whether software can track your grants. It is whether software can produce the documentation an auditor needs when they arrive. That documentation - SEFA, SF-425 reconciliation, time-and-effort logs, indirect cost workpapers - comes from process built during the grant year, not from a reconciliation sprint in the weeks before audit fieldwork begins.
