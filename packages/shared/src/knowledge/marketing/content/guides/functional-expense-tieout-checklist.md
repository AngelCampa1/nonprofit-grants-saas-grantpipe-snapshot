Functional expense reporting shows how a nonprofit uses resources by purpose. It is not the same as natural expense reporting. Natural expense says what the cost was, such as salary, rent, or supplies. Functional expense says why the cost was incurred, such as program, management and general, or fundraising.

A tie-out proves that those functions agree across systems and reports.

## Start with the ledger

Begin with the general ledger for the period. Export expenses by natural account and function. If your accounting system uses dimensions, include program, grant, department, and fund codes.

The total expenses in the functional expense schedule should match total expenses in the trial balance and statement of activities. If the totals do not tie, stop and fix the base report before reviewing allocations.

For financial statement context, link to [FASB ASC 958 nonprofit reporting](/resources/guides/fasb-asc-958-nonprofit-reporting).

## Check the function map

Each expense account should have a default function or a clear allocation rule. Payroll may split across functions based on time records. Rent may split based on square feet or headcount. Software may split based on users or purpose.

Keep a map with:

- Natural account
- Default function
- Allocation method, if shared
- Source support
- Reviewer

Do not let staff choose functions from memory each month. A map makes coding more consistent.

## Review payroll first

Payroll is often the largest expense and the largest source of functional reporting risk. Review salaries, benefits, payroll taxes, and related costs together.

Check whether time records support staff charged to multiple functions or grants. If an employee works on both program and fundraising, the split should be based on records, not a rough guess made at year-end.

If payroll feeds the grant ledger, the functional split should also agree with grant reports. A salary charged to a restricted grant should still be reviewed for proper function.

## Test shared cost allocations

Shared costs need a written method. Common shared costs include rent, utilities, insurance, information technology, finance staff, executive time, and office supplies.

For each allocation, keep:

- Cost pool
- Allocation basis
- Calculation
- Period covered
- Approval
- Reason the method fits the cost

For more detail, link to [cost allocation plan nonprofit guide](/resources/guides/cost-allocation-plan-nonprofit-guide).

Do not use one allocation method for every cost just because it is easy. Rent may fit square footage. Software may fit user counts. Finance staff may fit time or transaction volume.

## Tie to Form 990 Part IX

IRS Form 990 Part IX reports expenses by type and function. It includes columns for program service, management and general, fundraising, and total.

Your financial statement functional expense schedule and Form 990 may not look identical, but they should be reconcilable. Build a crosswalk from ledger accounts to Form 990 lines.

Review common problem areas:

Compensation lines should tie to payroll reports and W-2 or 1099 records where relevant.

Professional fees should be split between legal, accounting, lobbying, investment management, and other categories as required.

Grants and assistance paid should not be mixed with internal program expenses.

Fundraising event costs should be reviewed carefully.

## Tie to grant reports

Grant reports often use funder budget categories, not GAAP natural accounts or Form 990 lines. The tie-out should explain how grant costs map to both.

A grant cost may be program for functional expense reporting, salary for natural expense reporting, and personnel for the funder budget. Those can all be true if the mapping is documented.

If your grant reports show costs that are not in the ledger, or ledger costs that never reach the grant report, investigate before submission.

## Review management and general

Some nonprofits understate management and general because they fear overhead ratios. That creates risk. Management and general costs are real and should be reported correctly.

The Council of Nonprofits has long pushed back on simplistic overhead thinking. The goal is not to make overhead disappear. The goal is honest reporting.

Finance, governance, human resources, and general administration often belong in management and general unless they are directly tied to a program or fundraising activity.

## Keep the final package

At year-end, save the tie-out package:

- Trial balance
- Functional expense schedule
- Allocation workbooks
- Payroll support
- Form 990 crosswalk
- Grant report crosswalks
- Reviewer signoff

This file helps with audit, Form 990 preparation, and board questions.
