Federal cash is not normal operating cash. It needs a clearer trail.

A written cash policy helps staff know when to draw, what to attach, who reviews the request, and how to prove the money was used.

## Name the draw owner and reviewer

The policy should say who prepares draw requests and who approves them. Those should be separate people when staffing allows.

The preparer gathers support. The reviewer checks timing, award fit, and math before the request is submitted.

## Define the support packet

Each draw or reimbursement request should include:

- award and budget reference
- ledger detail
- invoice or payroll support
- payment status
- prior draw or reimbursement history
- bank deposit support after funds arrive

Use the [grant reimbursement backup guide](/resources/guides/grant-reimbursement-backup-documentation-guide) when the award pays after costs are incurred.

## Set a timing rule

The policy should ban early draws that are not tied to real payment need. It should also explain how close the request should be to the planned payment date.

For reimbursement grants, the rule is simpler. Request only costs already incurred and supported.

For advance grants, document why cash is needed and when it will be disbursed.

## Reconcile cash after receipt

After funds arrive, match the payment system, bank deposit, ledger entry, and award record. Save the support.

If the deposit differs from the request, write down why. Differences can come from agency adjustments, timing, or rejected costs.

## Review interest and stale cash

The policy should include a review for interest and stale federal cash. If federal cash sits unused, the team should know who reviews it and what action is required.

This is also a cash flow warning sign. See the [grant cash flow warning signs guide](/resources/guides/nonprofit-cash-flow-warning-signs-grants).

## Policy language to include

A cash policy should be specific enough for daily use. Avoid a sentence that only says the organization will follow federal rules. Staff need a working standard.

Use language that covers these decisions:

| Policy area       | Practical rule                                                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Request authority | Name the role that can prepare and submit draws.                 |
| Review authority  | Require a separate reviewer when staffing allows.                |
| Draw basis        | State whether the award is reimbursement, advance, or mixed.     |
| Support           | Require ledger detail, invoices, payroll, or other cost support. |
| Timing            | Connect the request date to actual payment need.                 |
| Reconciliation    | Require bank and ledger match after receipt.                     |
| Exceptions        | Require written approval for unusual draw timing.                |

The policy should also say where the support packet is stored. A policy that names a folder, system, or award record is easier to follow than one that only names the rule.

## Reimbursement and advance grants

Reimbursement grants are simpler. The organization spends approved costs first, then requests payment. The main risk is weak backup or claiming a cost before it is supported.

Advance grants need more care. 2 CFR 200.305 focuses on minimizing the time between transfer of federal funds and disbursement by the recipient. That means the draw should be close to real payment need.

Some awards use both methods. For example, payroll may be advanced while vendor costs are reimbursed. The policy should allow the award setup to record which method applies to each cost stream.

## Monthly cash review checklist

Add a monthly review to catch stale cash and weak records:

- Compare draw requests to bank deposits.
- Match deposits to ledger entries.
- Review federal cash still on hand.
- Check whether draws were spent near the request date.
- Review any interest earned on advances.
- Save explanations for timing differences.
- Confirm the next report uses the same cash record.

This review should happen before the SF-425 or other financial report is prepared. A cash mismatch at report time is harder to fix.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.305 sets cash management requirements for federal awards. Award terms and agency systems can add more detail. Some pass-through entities also require their own draw forms or reimbursement worksheets.

Interest rules matter when advances are used. Do not treat interest as program income. Review the federal cash management rule and the award terms before deciding what to do with interest.

If the organization receives state or local pass-through funds, confirm whether the money is federal. A city reimbursement grant may still carry federal cash rules if the source is a federal program.
