# CDBG reimbursement request checklist

CDBG reimbursement requests are where program work, finance records, and federal rules meet. The city or county needs enough support to prove the cost is eligible. Your nonprofit needs the packet to be clear enough that a monitor can follow it months later.

Most nonprofits do not draw CDBG cash directly from HUD. They submit requests to a city, county, or state. The pass-through agreement controls the exact form and timing, but the support usually follows the same pattern.

## Confirm the activity first

Before collecting invoices, confirm the cost belongs to the approved CDBG activity. CDBG funds must meet a national objective and fit an eligible activity. Most nonprofit public service work qualifies through benefit to low- and moderate-income persons, but the file still has to show that link.

Use the [CDBG grants guide](/resources/guides/cdbg-community-development-block-grants-nonprofits) to review the basic funding flow. Then keep the approved activity description with every reimbursement packet.

## Check the agreement dates

The cost should fall within the approved period. If the invoice date, service date, payroll period, or purchase date sits outside the agreement period, stop and review before submitting. Some costs may be allowed only after environmental review or other clearance is complete.

For facility work, ask the pass-through entity what proof is needed before costs can be reimbursed. Environmental review rules can affect when work may begin.

## Pull source documents

Each packet should include:

- Reimbursement form or cover sheet
- Grant ledger detail for the request period
- Invoices, payroll records, or other source documents
- Proof of payment if required by the agreement
- Approval under your policy
- Procurement support for covered purchases
- Activity or client support tied to the national objective

Do not submit a general ledger alone. The ledger shows the cost was recorded. It does not prove the cost was eligible, approved, or tied to the CDBG activity.

## Support payroll carefully

If staff time is charged to CDBG, include payroll records and time allocation support. The record should show the employee, pay period, hours or percentage charged, activity, and approval. If staff worked on more than one program, the allocation method should be clear.

The [time and effort certification guide](/resources/guides/time-and-effort-certification-federal-grants) can help when CDBG-funded staff also work on other grants.

## Add procurement records

CDBG-funded purchases may need quotes, bids, conflict checks, and selection notes. The threshold and method depend on federal rules and local policy. If the city has stricter rules, follow the city rule.

Use the [2 CFR 200 procurement file checklist](/resources/guides/2-cfr-200-procurement-file-checklist) before submitting costs for vendors, equipment, supplies, or facility work. A reimbursement can be delayed if the purchase support is missing.

## Prove the national objective

For direct service work, keep the documentation that supports low- and moderate-income benefit. This may include income forms, presumed benefit category records, service logs, or area benefit support. The reimbursement packet does not need every client file, but it should point to the source records used.

Avoid putting unnecessary personal data in the packet. If the city needs client-level backup, ask for a secure method and use the minimum data needed.

## Separate program income and refunds

If the CDBG activity earns program income, follow the pass-through entity's rule for reporting and use. Do not net it quietly against the next request unless the agreement says to do that. Keep the income record, deposit, use approval, and ledger entry.

Refunds and credits need the same care. A returned supply, payroll correction, or vendor credit should reduce the reimbursed cost or be returned as the agreement requires. Keep the correction with the original packet so the city can see the full trail.

## Tie out the math

The request amount should match the ledger detail and the support. If the invoice total is higher than the CDBG amount, mark the portion charged. If part of a payroll period is charged, show the calculation. If a credit reduced the cost, show the net amount.

Add a short tieout page for larger requests. It can be a table with source document, total cost, CDBG share, other funding share, and notes.

## Review before submission

Have someone other than the preparer review the packet. The reviewer should check agreement dates, eligible activity, source support, procurement support, math, and approval. Keep the review date in the packet.

After submission, save the sent packet, city response, payment record, and any correction. If the city reduces the request, keep the reason and update the ledger or future request.

Set a monthly due date that gives finance time to fix missing support. Reimbursement grants create cash pressure when packets are late. A simple calendar with packet owner, reviewer, and due date protects cash flow and reduces rushed submissions.
