# Nonprofit CRM data cleanup before grant season

Grant season exposes weak CRM data fast. A missing funder contact may delay a renewal call. A stale report date may make the team late. A duplicate foundation record may hide past giving and confuse the proposal owner.

The goal is not to make every record perfect. The goal is to clean the records that affect grants, restrictions, reports, and funder follow-up. Start with active grants, likely renewals, open proposals, and funders tied to restricted money.

Use this with the [nonprofit CRM data hygiene guide](/resources/guides/nonprofit-crm-data-hygiene-guide) and the [CRM grant tracking requirements guide](/resources/guides/nonprofit-crm-grant-tracking-requirements).

## Start with a safe export

Export a backup before staff change records. Include organizations, people, gifts, pledges, grants, notes, tasks, tags, custom fields, files, and user lists. Keep the export read-only.

This is basic data discipline. It also protects audit history and Form 990 support. If cleanup breaks a field or merges the wrong records, the team can trace what changed.

## Pick the records that matter now

Do not start with the full CRM if time is short. Start with records tied to current grant work:

- active funders
- open proposals
- renewal targets
- grants with reports due in the next 120 days
- restricted gifts and grants
- funders with missing contacts
- records used in board reports

This keeps cleanup close to real risk. A misspelled old event attendee may matter later. A wrong grant report date matters now.

## Merge duplicate funders

Foundations often appear under several names. One record may hold the gift. Another may hold the grant. A third may hold the program officer.

Pick a naming rule before merging. Keep the legal name, common name, former name, and tax ID when available. Add a note when two names refer to the same funder.

After the merge, check that history moved correctly. Gifts, proposals, reports, notes, and documents should land on the right organization. Do not merge records if the team is unsure. Mark them for review.

## Confirm current contacts

Grant season depends on reaching the right person. Review funder contacts for role, email, phone, title, and relationship owner. Remove former staff from active outreach lists.

Use plain roles. "Program officer," "finance contact," "portal admin," and "signer" are clearer than custom labels that only one person knows.

If a funder portal uses a shared login, record who controls it and where access is approved. Do not store passwords in notes.

## Clean grant history fields

Each active or likely renewal grant should have a clean record. At minimum, check funder, award amount, award period, program, owner, status, approved budget, report dates, and closeout status.

Federal grants add more detail. 2 CFR 200.302 requires financial records that identify awards and funding sources. If the grant uses a federal award number or assistance listing, the CRM or linked grant record should preserve that detail.

## Review restrictions with finance

Development and grants teams may know why money was given. Finance knows how it is coded. Both views matter.

Before grant season, review restriction fields for current grants and major gifts. Check purpose, time period, program, fund code, release trigger, and balance source. If the CRM cannot show the balance, it should at least link to the finance record or reconciliation file.

Do not let old labels carry forward without review. "Restricted" is not enough. Staff need to know what the money can pay for and when it can be released.

## Fix report dates and owners

2 CFR 200.329 covers federal performance reporting. Foundation grants may have their own portal rules. In either case, a CRM record should show the next report date, internal review date, owner, and status.

Add missing internal dates. The funder due date is not the work date. Finance numbers, program data, and leadership review often need time before submission.

Every report should have one owner. Other staff can help, but one person needs to move the task.

## Link core documents

Clean records should point to the signed agreement, approved budget, amendments, reports, support files, and closeout letters. If documents live in a shared drive, use a stable folder link and a clear file naming rule.

Record retention matters. 2 CFR 200.334 generally requires federal award records to be kept for three years after final report submission, with exceptions. Your CRM cleanup should not delete closed grant support just because it is old.

## Remove stale tags and fields

Old tags create bad lists. Review tags used for funder type, proposal status, priority, geography, and restriction type. Merge tags that mean the same thing. Retire tags no one can define.

Custom fields need the same review. If a field no longer drives a report, workflow, or decision, archive it or stop using it. Too many fields slow staff down and invite bad data.

## Build a short cleanup log

Keep one cleanup log with issue, owner, decision, and status. Record duplicate rules, missing contacts, disputed restrictions, unclear report dates, and document gaps.

The log prevents repeat debates. It also gives leadership a clear view of what still creates grant season risk.

## Test the cleanup

Pick three funders after cleanup: one active grant, one renewal target, and one restricted gift. Ask staff to open each record and answer basic questions.

Can they find the right contact? Can they see the grant history? Can they find the next report date? Can they tell whether money is restricted? Can they open the support file?

If the answer is no, cleanup is not done.
