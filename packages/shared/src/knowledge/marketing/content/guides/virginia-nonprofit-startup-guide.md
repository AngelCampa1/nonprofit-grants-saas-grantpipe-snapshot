Starting a Virginia nonprofit is a sequence problem, not a complexity problem. Each filing depends on the one before it, and each agency has its own clock. Skip a step and the rest of the timeline slips.

This guide walks the formation flow end-to-end: SCC incorporation, EIN, governing documents, federal 501(c)(3) application, VDACS charitable registration, and the ongoing filings that keep the organization current.

## Step 1: Choose the Entity Type

Almost every Virginia nonprofit incorporates as a **nonstock corporation** under Title 13.1, Chapter 10 of the Code of Virginia. That structure gives the organization legal personhood, limits director and officer liability, and is what the IRS expects when it reviews a 501(c)(3) application.

Trusts and unincorporated associations exist as alternatives but rarely make sense for an operating charity. If the organization will hold real estate, hire staff, or pursue federal grants, incorporate.

## Step 2: Reserve and Register the Name

Search the SCC's Clerk's Information System (cis.scc.virginia.gov) for name availability before drafting Articles. Virginia requires the corporate name to be distinguishable from existing entities. Reserve a name for 120 days for $10 if you need time before filing.

The name must not include words implying the organization is something it is not - "bank," "trust," and certain regulated terms are restricted.

## Step 3: File Articles of Incorporation with the SCC

Form **SCC819** (Articles of Incorporation of a Virginia Nonstock Corporation) is the foundational filing. Required content:

- Corporate name
- Whether the corporation will have members
- Names and addresses of the initial directors (minimum of one)
- Registered agent name and Virginia street address
- Purpose statement - for 501(c)(3) eligibility, use the IRS sample purpose language limiting activities to exempt purposes
- Dissolution clause - assets distribute to another 501(c)(3) or to government on dissolution
- Incorporator signature

The fee is $75. Online filing through CIS processes in 1-3 business days. Mail filings take longer.

### Required IRS Language

The IRS will reject Form 1023 if the purpose and dissolution clauses don't meet 501(c)(3) requirements. Use the IRS sample language verbatim or run the language by a nonprofit attorney. Vague purpose statements ("to do good in the community") are a frequent rejection reason.

## Step 4: Get an EIN

Apply at IRS.gov for an Employer Identification Number. Online applications issue immediately for entities with a US-based responsible party. There is no fee.

The EIN is required for the bank account, the IRS Form 1023, and VDACS Form 102. Without it, nothing downstream can proceed.

## Step 5: Adopt Bylaws and Hold the Organizational Meeting

Bylaws are the internal rulebook. Virginia doesn't file them with the SCC, but the IRS requires them on Form 1023, and grant funders ask for current bylaws.

Cover at minimum:

- Board structure, size, term limits, and election procedures
- Officer roles and appointment
- Meeting frequency, quorum, and notice requirements
- Conflict of interest policy and procedure
- Indemnification provisions
- Amendment procedure
- Fiscal year and dissolution clause (consistent with the Articles)

Hold the **organizational meeting** to adopt the bylaws, elect officers, authorize the EIN application, authorize bank account opening, and approve the IRS Form 1023 filing. Keep the minutes - they're foundational governance records.

## Step 6: File for Federal 501(c)(3) Status

Two forms exist:

- **Form 1023-EZ** - short-form online application, $275 fee, 4-6 weeks. Eligibility caps at projected gross receipts of $50,000 or less for each of the next three years and total assets of $250,000 or less.
- **Form 1023** - full application, $600 fee, 6-12 months processing. Required for organizations exceeding the EZ thresholds, churches, schools, hospitals, and certain other categories.

File at pay.gov. The IRS issues a determination letter on approval. The exemption is retroactive to the date of incorporation if the application is filed within 27 months.

For the full walkthrough, see the [501(c)(3) application step-by-step guide](/resources/guides/501c3-application-step-by-step-guide).

## Step 7: Register with VDACS Before Soliciting

This is the step Virginia founders most often miss. The Virginia Solicitation of Contributions law (Va. Code §57-48 et seq.) requires registration with the Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services before soliciting any charitable contributions in the Commonwealth.

File **Form 102** (Initial Registration Statement) with VDACS, Office of Charitable and Regulatory Programs. The fee is $100. Required attachments include:

- Articles of Incorporation (file-stamped copy)
- Bylaws
- IRS determination letter, if issued - pending Form 1023 is acceptable
- IRS Form 990 (or first-year budget if no 990 exists yet)
- List of officers, directors, and key employees with addresses
- Audited or reviewed financial statements if revenue thresholds met

Processing typically takes 30-60 days. The full mechanics - including renewal, audit thresholds, and edge cases - are in the [Virginia charitable registration workflow](/workflows/virginia-charitable-registration-workflow).

### When Registration is Required

The trigger is _solicitation_, not just incorporation. A Virginia nonprofit that hasn't yet asked for any contributions can wait. The moment fundraising begins - including a donation page that accepts Virginia gifts - Form 102 must be on file. Solicitation without registration is a Class 1 misdemeanor.

### Exemptions

Va. Code §57-60 exempts certain organizations from registration: religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, hospitals, government entities, and a few other categories. Most operating charities are not exempt and must register.

## Step 8: Apply for Virginia Sales Tax Exemption

Federal 501(c)(3) status does not exempt the organization from Virginia retail sales and use tax. File **Form NP-1** with the Virginia Department of Taxation to apply for nonprofit sales and use tax exemption.

The application is online through the Department's Nonprofit Online portal. Approval takes 30-90 days. The exemption certificate is valid for five years and renewable.

## Step 9: Open a Bank Account

Most Virginia banks require:

- IRS-issued EIN letter
- Articles of Incorporation
- Bylaws
- Board resolution authorizing the account and listing signers
- Photo ID for each signer

Some banks delay opening until the IRS determination letter arrives. If your bank does, ask whether they'll open with the application receipt while approval is pending - most will, with restrictions.

## Step 10: Calendar the Recurring Filings

Once formed, Virginia nonprofits maintain three independent compliance calendars:

- **SCC annual report** - due each year on the anniversary month of incorporation. $25 fee. Filed online through CIS.
- **VDACS Form 102** annual renewal - due 4½ months after fiscal year end. Sliding fee from $30 to $325 based on gross contributions.
- **IRS Form 990** (or 990-EZ, 990-N) - due 4½ months after fiscal year end. Three consecutive missed filings trigger automatic revocation of 501(c)(3) status.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Virginia Nonprofits Down

**Soliciting before VDACS registration.** Virginia takes this seriously. Don't launch the donation page before Form 102 is on file.

**Wrong purpose language in the Articles.** The IRS will reject Form 1023 if the purpose clause is too broad or the dissolution clause is missing required language. Use IRS sample language verbatim.

**Skipping the conflict of interest policy.** The IRS asks for it on Form 1023. Adopt it at the organizational meeting and record adoption in the minutes.

**Confusing federal and state exemptions.** Federal 501(c)(3) approval doesn't waive state sales tax - Form NP-1 is a separate filing.

**Letting the SCC annual report lapse.** Two missed annual reports lead to administrative dissolution. Reinstatement is possible but costs more than the original filings.
