Small nonprofit finance teams have a real constraint: the same person often touches too many parts of the money cycle.

One staff member may enter bills, prepare deposits, post payroll, run reports, and answer auditor questions. That does not automatically mean the organization has weak controls. It does mean the organization needs compensating controls that fit its size.

The goal is reasonable assurance, not theater. A control that nobody can run every month is not a control. A small team needs a short list of checks that actually happen.

## Start with the money cycle

Map the money cycle before writing a policy. For each transaction type, name who does the work and who reviews it.

Use four columns:

- authorization
- custody or access
- recording
- review

For example, a vendor payment may be authorized by a program lead, prepared by finance, paid by the executive director, recorded by finance, and reviewed by the treasurer through the bank statement and monthly financial packet.

If the same person owns all four columns, add a compensating control.

## Control 1: Independent bank review

Bank reconciliation is the first control to fix. The person who records transactions can prepare the reconciliation, but someone else should review it.

The reviewer should see:

- bank statement
- reconciliation report
- outstanding checks
- deposits in transit
- unusual withdrawals or transfers
- bank fees and electronic payments
- evidence that questions were resolved

For a small nonprofit, the reviewer may be the executive director or board treasurer. The review should be dated. Email approval is better than nothing, but a signed or system-recorded review is easier to support later.

## Control 2: Approval before spending

Grant-funded spending needs approval before the cost hits the grant. Approval should answer:

1. Is the cost in the approved budget?
2. Is the cost allowable under the award?
3. Is the vendor or employee cost supported?
4. Does procurement policy apply?
5. Which fund, grant, or cost center should be charged?

Do not rely on month-end cleanup as the main control. If staff charge costs first and ask questions later, the organization will eventually submit a reimbursement or report with weak support.

GrantPipe helps here by tying approvals, budgets, documents, and grant restrictions to the award record. That gives reviewers context before the cost becomes a report problem.

## Control 3: Restricted fund review

Restricted funds need a monthly check. The review should compare:

- restricted beginning balance
- new restricted revenue
- releases from restriction
- spending by grant or purpose
- ending restricted balance
- support for unusual releases or transfers

The board does not need every line item, but the treasurer should see exception notes. Negative restricted balances, stale balances, and unexplained releases need follow-up.

## Control 4: Payroll and time support

Payroll is high-risk because it is large and recurring. For grant-funded staff, payroll also connects to time-and-effort support.

At minimum:

- one person reviews payroll changes
- one person reviews payroll reports after processing
- grant-funded salary charges tie to approved allocations or time records
- benefit allocations follow the same basis as wages when required
- corrections are documented

Federal awards require personnel charges to be supported by records that accurately reflect work performed and by a system of internal controls. A flat estimate with no review is not enough.

## Control 5: Procurement and conflicts

Small nonprofits often buy quickly because staff are stretched. Federal and restricted grants may still require documented procurement steps.

For purchases above your internal threshold, keep:

- purchase request
- budget check
- conflict check
- quotes or procurement method support
- approval record
- contract or invoice
- proof of delivery or service

The person choosing the vendor should not be the only person approving the payment. If a conflict exists, document it before the decision.

## Control 6: Report review before submission

Every financial report or reimbursement request should get a second review before submission.

The reviewer should check:

- report totals tie to the general ledger
- costs are within the award period
- costs are within approved budget categories
- payroll support exists
- procurement support exists where required
- prior advances or reimbursements are reflected correctly
- the report matches the narrative period

This review can be short. It should be visible. A dated checklist attached to the report is often enough.

## What the board should see

The board or finance committee should not run daily controls. It should receive a small control dashboard:

- bank reconciliations completed and reviewed
- late reconciliations
- restricted fund exceptions
- reimbursement receivables over expected timing
- grant reports submitted late
- audit or monitoring findings
- unresolved control issues

This gives the board oversight without turning it into staff.

## Keep the system small

A small team control system should fit on one page per cycle. If the policy is too complex, staff will work around it.

Start with bank review, approval before spending, restricted fund review, payroll support, procurement support, and report review. Run those monthly. Keep proof that they happened.

Then improve one control at a time.
