A grant spenddown dashboard helps staff see trouble early. It shows whether each grant is being used on time, within budget, and with support. Without it, teams often learn about problems at closeout, when the choices are limited.

The dashboard does not need to be complex. It needs to be current, tied to the ledger, and easy for program staff to read.

## Start with the grant list

Begin with one row per active award. Include the grant name, funder, grant period start date, end date, total budget, and staff owner.

Then add the main spenddown fields:

- Actual costs to date
- Remaining budget
- Percent spent
- Percent of grant period elapsed
- Days left
- Pending reimbursement or draw amount
- Missing support count
- Risk status

The side-by-side view matters. A grant that is 70% spent and 70% through the period may be fine. A grant that is 20% spent and 80% through the period needs a plan.

For the basic report layout, link staff to [how to build a grant spend-down report](/resources/guides/how-to-build-a-grant-spend-down-report).

## Use both time and money

Spenddown is about pace. Budget alone does not show pace. Time alone does not show cost.

Use two simple percentages:

Percent spent equals actual costs divided by approved budget.

Percent elapsed equals days used divided by total grant days.

Compare them. If percent spent is far below percent elapsed, ask whether hiring, purchasing, or program work is behind. If percent spent is far above percent elapsed, ask whether costs were front-loaded, mis-coded, or need funder approval.

Do not treat every variance as bad. Some grants pay for a large event, equipment buy, or seasonal program. The dashboard should allow notes so staff can explain real timing.

## Add cost allowability checks

Federal awards have rules under 2 CFR Part 200 for costs, procurement, documentation, and subrecipient monitoring. Foundation grants also have award terms. A spenddown dashboard should show whether costs are not just posted, but supportable.

Add fields for:

- Missing invoice or receipt
- Missing payroll support
- Missing time record
- Cost outside approved budget line
- Cost needing funder approval
- Match or cost share status, if required

This turns the dashboard from a spending report into a compliance report.

For budget setup, link to [grant budget tracking](/resources/guides/grant-budget-tracking). For federal cash work, link to [how to track federal grant draws and reimbursements](/resources/guides/how-to-track-federal-grant-draws-and-reimbursements).

## Track reimbursement timing

Many nonprofits spend grant money before cash arrives. A spenddown dashboard should show the cash gap.

Add these fields for reimbursement grants:

- Costs incurred but not requested
- Reimbursement requested but not received
- Date of last request
- Days since request
- Next request due date

This helps the executive director and board treasurer understand why unrestricted cash may be tight even when grant revenue is strong. It also helps finance avoid late requests.

Cash timing is not the same as release from restriction. A cost may support a release from restriction before the reimbursement cash arrives. Keep both records clear.

## Use risk flags that lead to action

Use plain status labels:

On track means no action is needed.

Watch means staff should review the grant before the next close.

Action needed means a decision is required now.

Common action items include moving staff time, requesting a budget change, submitting a reimbursement, asking for an extension, correcting coding, or gathering missing support.

Do not use vague notes like "needs review." Say what needs review. For example: "Payroll support missing for May" or "Travel line is 112% spent and needs funder approval."

## Review monthly with the right people

The dashboard should not live only in finance. Review it monthly with grants and program owners. Finance can explain the ledger. Program staff can explain delivery. Grants staff can explain funder terms and reporting deadlines.

The executive director should see grants with cash risk, deadline risk, or board-level decisions. The finance committee should see a summary of major awards and any fund with a material variance.

At closeout, connect the dashboard to your [grant closeout checklist](/resources/guides/grant-closeout-checklist). A clean dashboard makes closeout faster because support, draws, spending, and risk notes are already organized.
