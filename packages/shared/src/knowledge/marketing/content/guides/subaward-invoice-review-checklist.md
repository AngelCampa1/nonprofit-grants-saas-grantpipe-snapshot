A subaward invoice is not just a bill. It is also a monitoring checkpoint.

Before payment, the pass through organization should confirm that the invoice matches the subaward agreement, fits the approved budget, falls inside the period, reflects actual work, includes required support, and has no unresolved monitoring issue.

This checklist is for nonprofit teams that manage subrecipients under federal grants, foundation grants, or local government contracts. Always follow the signed agreement and funder rules first.

## Keep the agreement nearby

Review starts with the signed subaward agreement. The invoice should match the agreement's payment terms, budget, reporting duties, supporting document requirements, and deliverables.

Do not approve an invoice from memory. Staff turnover, amendments, budget revisions, and special conditions can change the rules.

GrantPipe can help keep subaward terms, budgets, files, deadlines, and review notes tied to the grant record. That matters when finance and program staff need to answer the same question before payment.

## Check the invoice basics

Confirm the invoice number, subrecipient name, award name, period covered, amount requested, payment address, and authorized signer.

The period covered should be clear. If the invoice only lists the date it was created, ask for the service period. The service period drives budget and grant period review.

Also check whether the invoice follows the required format. Some agreements require cost categories, cumulative amounts, matching support, certification language, or backup schedules.

## Match the budget

Compare the invoice to the approved subaward budget. Check each cost category, current period amount, cumulative billed amount, remaining balance, and any line item limits.

If the invoice exceeds a line or shifts costs between lines, look for an approved budget revision. Do not treat a budget change as approved because the subrecipient submitted it on an invoice.

For cost reimbursement subawards, the invoice should support actual costs. For milestone payments, the invoice should support the completed milestone. The agreement should make that difference clear.

## Check the period

Confirm that the billed work happened during the subaward period and the parent award period. Late invoices can still be valid if the work happened during the allowed period, but they may affect closeout timing.

If costs fall before the start date or after the end date, pause the review. Pre-award and closeout costs need special treatment and may need written approval.

Also check whether the invoice period overlaps a prior invoice. Duplicate billing can hide inside date ranges.

## Review performance before payment

Finance can check numbers, but program staff usually need to confirm work.

Ask whether the subrecipient submitted required reports, completed deliverables, met service volume expectations, and addressed prior findings. If the invoice covers participant services, compare the claim to attendance, case, unit, or output records required by the agreement.

Payment should not be the only enforcement tool. Still, repeated late reports or unsupported claims should trigger the monitoring plan.

## Check required support

Support requirements should match risk. A low risk subrecipient with a fixed milestone agreement may need less backup. A high risk subrecipient or cost reimbursement agreement may need payroll registers, invoices, receipts, general ledger detail, participant records, or match support.

The reviewer should not ask for every record by habit. Ask for the records required by the agreement, monitoring plan, funder terms, and risk level.

If support is sampled, save the sample request, records reviewed, exceptions found, and conclusion.

## Review allowability

Costs still need to be allowable. For federal awards, 2 CFR 200.403 includes standards such as necessary, reasonable, allocable, consistent, and documented. A subrecipient invoice does not bypass those standards.

Look for common issues:

- costs outside the period
- unapproved equipment
- unsupported payroll
- entertainment or lobbying
- duplicate charges
- costs that do not benefit the award
- budget shifts without approval
- missing match support

Escalate unclear items before payment.

## Check risk and monitoring status

Before approval, check the subrecipient risk file. Are there open monitoring findings? Late reports? Audit findings? Corrective action items? Prior questioned costs? Missing insurance or certifications?

Open issues do not always mean the invoice cannot be paid. They do mean the reviewer should follow the monitoring plan and document the decision.

For high risk subrecipients, payment may require extra support, extra approval, or closer review.

## Approve with clear evidence

Approval evidence should show who reviewed the invoice, what they checked, what exceptions they found, and what amount was approved.

If the approved amount differs from the invoice amount, document the reason. Partial payments should be easy to trace later.

Save the approval with the invoice, support, and payment record. The file should let someone follow the review without calling the reviewer.

## Checklist

Before payment, confirm:

- signed subaward agreement is active
- invoice format matches agreement
- invoice period is clear
- costs fit the subaward period
- costs fit the parent grant period
- budget lines and cumulative amounts are checked
- required reports are current
- deliverables or units are supported
- backup meets the agreement and risk level
- allowability issues are resolved
- monitoring findings are checked
- approval is dated and saved

This review protects both the pass through organization and the subrecipient. It catches issues before cash moves and creates the evidence needed for later monitoring, audit, and closeout.
