A restricted gift can start with one sentence in an email. It can also start with a pledge form, grant agreement, check memo, campaign page, or board conversation. If those details are not captured early, finance may record the gift without the full donor intent.

A donor restriction intake form gives development and finance one shared record. It does not replace accounting judgment. It collects the facts so finance can review the gift before it is coded, receipted, tracked, and reported.

This guide explains what to include in the form and how to use it without adding busy work.

## Start with the donor and gift facts

The first section should identify the gift. Keep it simple.

Include:

- Donor name or organization
- Donor CRM record link
- Gift amount or pledge amount
- Gift date
- Payment method
- Campaign or appeal
- Staff owner
- Source document

The donor CRM link matters. Restricted fund questions often start in finance, but the context may live with development. GrantPipe can help keep donor CRM records, restricted funds, deadlines, reporting notes, and audit evidence connected so staff do not chase the same facts twice.

## Capture the exact restriction

The form should include the donor's exact words. Do not summarize too early. A short phrase like "for youth tutoring in 2026" has both purpose and time details.

Add fields for:

- Purpose restriction
- Time restriction
- Location restriction
- Program or project named
- Spending deadline
- Reporting promise
- Unclear terms

If the source says "where needed most," it may be unrestricted. If it says "for the food pantry," it may be restricted by purpose. Finance should make the final call.

## Separate restrictions from conditions

Some agreements include barriers that must be met before revenue is recognized. FASB ASU 2018-08 discusses how conditions affect contribution accounting. A condition is not the same thing as a donor restriction.

The intake form should ask:

- Does the donor require a specific action before the gift is earned?
- Can the donor take back funds if the action is not met?
- Is there a matching requirement?
- Is the promise conditional on another event?

Development does not need to decide the accounting answer. The goal is to alert finance.

## Add receipt wording notes

The IRS has written acknowledgment rules for charitable contributions. Your form should not give legal advice, but it should help staff send accurate acknowledgments.

Include a field for receipt notes. If the donor received goods or services, finance or development should review the acknowledgment language. If the gift is restricted, the receipt or thank you note should not broaden the promise beyond what the organization can do.

Avoid adding claims such as guaranteed results or tax advice. Keep the thank you clear and factual.

## Require finance review

Every restricted gift should have a finance review before final coding when practical. The review can answer:

- Is the gift restricted?
- Is the restriction purpose, time, or both?
- Is there a condition?
- What fund, project, or class should be used?
- What evidence should be saved?
- Who owns reporting?

The reviewer should add a date and approval note. This creates audit evidence for the accounting treatment.

## Link to fund setup

If finance decides the gift is restricted, the form should start the setup work. Add fields for fund code, project code, release method, report deadline, and owner.

This helps prevent a common issue: development records a restricted gift, but finance does not learn about it until month end. By then, the receipt may be sent and the deposit posted.

## Keep unclear gifts out of autopilot

Some gifts need a pause. Examples include donor wording that conflicts with program plans, a pledge tied to future events, a restriction the organization cannot meet, or a gift that may need legal review.

Use a status field:

- Ready for deposit
- Needs finance review
- Needs donor clarification
- Needs leadership review
- Declined

Declining a gift may be rare, but the form should allow it. A gift with terms the organization cannot honor can create more risk than value.

## Save the evidence

Attach the donor letter, email, pledge form, grant agreement, campaign page, check memo, and review notes. Store them with the donor record and the restricted fund record.

At year end, finance may need to prove why a balance is reported with donor restrictions. The intake form should make that proof easier to find.

A good intake form is not long. It asks for the facts that matter before the gift is coded. It gives development a clear handoff and gives finance the evidence needed to protect donor intent.
