## What Pennsylvania Nonprofits Actually Need

Three local realities shape the Pennsylvania software decision:

1. **Two-region foundation landscape** - Greater Philadelphia funders (William Penn, Pew, Independence) and Western Pennsylvania funders (Heinz, Pittsburgh Foundation, Hillman) operate as near-independent networks. Restricted grants from each region come with funder-specific reporting expectations. The full list is profiled in the [Pennsylvania top foundation grants](/resources/best/pennsylvania-top-foundation-grants) listicle.
2. **Federal pass-through grants** - HUD, HHS, DOJ, and USDA dollars routed through Pennsylvania state agencies (DCED, DHS, DOH) inherit 2 CFR 200 compliance. Single Audit applies at $1,000,000 in federal expenditures for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later.
3. **BCO-10 charitable registration** - the Pennsylvania Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations within the Department of State requires annual filing for charities soliciting in the Commonwealth. Audit threshold sits at $1,000,000 in gross national contributions.

A grant management platform that handles all three is unusual. Most platforms handle one well and the other two as an afterthought.

## The Buyer Categories

Pennsylvania nonprofits typically fall into one of four software-buyer profiles:

### 1. Donor-led organizations under $1M with light grant activity

Two or three small grants per year, mostly local family foundations. The donor CRM is the central system. Grant tracking can live in a basic deadline calendar.

**Realistic options:** Little Green Light ($49-$159/month), supplemented by a spreadsheet for grants. Total annual software cost: under $3,000.

### 2. Mid-market organizations $1M-$5M with active grant compliance

Five to fifteen active grants, mix of foundation and federal pass-through. Multiple restricted funds. BCO-10 review or audit may apply.

**Realistic options:** GrantPipe (published self-serve pricing) for unified donor and grant management, or Bloomerang plus a separate grant tool. Total annual software cost: $3,000-$7,000.

### 3. Federal grant-heavy organizations $2M-$10M

Federal pass-through dollars dominate revenue. 2 CFR 200 compliance is the central operational requirement. Single Audit applies. Restricted fund tracking is non-negotiable.

**Realistic options:** GrantPipe or a federal-specific platform like Fluxx. Salesforce NPSP with a grants module is viable with $20,000+ implementation.

### 4. Large institutions $10M+

Enterprise scale. Salesforce ecosystem typical. Implementation $50,000-$200,000+.

**Realistic options:** Salesforce NPSP + grants module, Fluxx, or Blackbaud's Grants Management.

## Federal Pass-Through Considerations

Pennsylvania state agencies pass through federal funds under 2 CFR 200 and the FAR. The compliance requirements travel with the money. A grant management platform supporting federal pass-through must:

- Track restricted fund balances per award
- Document allowable cost determinations
- Maintain time-and-effort certifications
- Produce expenditure reports aligned with the funder's program-specific reporting schedule
- Generate the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA) for Single Audit

If federal grants are not a current concern, this checklist is overkill. If federal grants are or will be part of revenue, this is the buying criteria.

## BCO-10 Alignment

BCO-10 is filed annually with the Pennsylvania Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations. Audit and review thresholds depend on gross national contributions:

- Under $100,000 - internally prepared financial statements
- $100,000 to $250,000 - compiled financial statements
- $250,000 to $1,000,000 - CPA-reviewed financial statements
- $1,000,000 and above - independent CPA audit

A grant management system does not file BCO-10 for you. But the gross contribution data, restricted fund balances, and program expenditure detail it tracks feed into the BCO-10 schedules. Pulling this data from a clean system at year end is the difference between a 4-hour BCO-10 and a 40-hour BCO-10.

## Implementation Realities

Realistic ranges:

- **Little Green Light** - 1 to 2 weeks self-service
- **Bloomerang** - 2 to 4 weeks with vendor support
- **GrantPipe** - 2 to 4 weeks for unified donor and grant setup
- **DonorPerfect** - 4 to 8 weeks
- **Salesforce NPSP with grants** - 3 to 9 months with implementation partner

Implementation cost for Salesforce NPSP starts at $20,000 and routinely exceeds $100,000 for the grant compliance configuration. For a Pennsylvania nonprofit under $5M, that figure is the entire annual software budget for five years on a mid-market platform.

## Decision Framework

Three questions to settle the choice:

1. **How many active grants will you manage at once?** Under three, donor-CRM-plus-spreadsheet is workable. Three or more, the unified platform pays back within the first audit cycle.
2. **Will you cross $1,000,000 in federal expenditures?** If yes, Single Audit support is non-negotiable.
3. **What is your gross national contributions for the BCO-10 threshold?** Above $1,000,000 you need audit-ready financials. Above $250,000 you need CPA-reviewed financials.

For most mid-market Pennsylvania nonprofits, the GrantPipe unified-platform model is the realistic fit. For organizations with no current grant activity, a focused donor CRM is the lower-cost path.
