## San Antonio's Nonprofit Financial Landscape

San Antonio's nonprofit accounting environment is unlike any other Texas city. The military presence at Joint Base San Antonio creates a funding channel - DoD pass-through contracts and grants - that most community-based nonprofits in Austin or Houston never encounter. The Kronkosky Charitable Foundation distributes more than $20 million annually in the immediate region. H-E-B and USAA anchor a corporate giving landscape tied to companies whose headquarters and employee bases are concentrated locally. And the city's lower cost structure compared to other Texas metros creates genuine competitive advantages in grant proposals that evaluate cost-effectiveness.

But this landscape also creates accounting complexity. Managing a DoD subcontract alongside a Kronkosky foundation grant, an H-E-B corporate sponsorship, and a City of San Antonio service contract means tracking four different compliance frameworks, four different reporting calendars, and four different cost allocation methodologies simultaneously.

## Military Base Contracting and DoD Pass-Through Grants

Joint Base San Antonio employs over 80,000 military and civilian personnel across Fort Sam Houston, Lackland Air Force Base, and Randolph Air Force Base. This concentration creates demand for nonprofit services - family support, behavioral health, veteran transition, childcare, and community integration programs - and a corresponding flow of DoD funding to organizations that deliver those services.

### How DoD Funding Reaches Nonprofits

DoD funding typically reaches San Antonio nonprofits through two channels: pass-through grants from military-affiliated programs (Office of the Secretary of Defense family programs, Defense Health Agency behavioral health initiatives) and subcontracts under prime contractors providing services to JBSA installations.

Both channels carry the full weight of federal compliance requirements under [2 CFR 200 (Uniform Guidance)](/resources/guides/nonprofit-financial-statements-guide). For nonprofits accustomed to foundation grants with narrative reporting, the jump to federal compliance is significant.

### Accounting Requirements for Federal Military Funding

Organizations holding DoD pass-through awards must maintain several accounting capabilities that foundation-funded nonprofits often lack:

**Cost allocation plans.** You need a documented, defensible methodology for allocating shared costs (rent, utilities, executive salaries, IT) across multiple funding sources. The plan must be consistent, written, and available for audit review. If your executive director splits time between a Kronkosky-funded youth program and a DoD-funded family resilience contract, you need time-and-effort documentation for every pay period.

**Indirect cost rate.** Federal awards allow recovery of indirect costs using either a negotiated indirect cost rate agreement (NICRA) or the de minimis 15% rate. Choosing the right approach depends on your cost structure. Organizations with significant overhead may benefit from negotiating a rate that reflects actual costs, while smaller organizations may prefer the simplicity of the 15% de minimis rate.

**Single audit.** Nonprofits that expend $1,000,000 or more in federal awards for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later in a fiscal year must undergo a single audit under the Single Audit Act. This is a more extensive and expensive audit than a standard financial statement audit. Budget for it - and build the cost into your federal grant budgets as an allowable direct cost.

**Procurement standards.** Federal awards require documented procurement procedures for purchases above micro-purchase thresholds. Informal quotes, competitive bids, and full competition requirements apply at different dollar levels. Your purchasing process must be documented and followed consistently.

## Kronkosky Charitable Foundation

The Kronkosky Charitable Foundation is one of the largest private foundations in the San Antonio region. Founded by Albert and Bessie Mae Kronkosky, the foundation distributes more than $20 million annually across four counties: Bexar, Comal, Guadalupe, and Kendall.

### Funding Priorities

Kronkosky focuses on youth development, elderly services and aging, wildlife conservation, and community-based programs. The foundation favors established organizations with demonstrated community impact over startup initiatives. Grant sizes vary but frequently fall in the $25,000 to $250,000 range for programmatic support.

### Accounting Implications

Kronkosky grants are private foundation awards - they carry donor-imposed restrictions that must be tracked in your accounting system as temporarily restricted (or, under ASC 958, net assets with donor restrictions). Your [financial statements](/resources/guides/nonprofit-financial-statements-guide) must show Kronkosky funds as restricted until you meet the conditions (typically programmatic milestones or time-based release).

The foundation requires financial reporting on use of funds, and site visits are common. Maintain clean expense tracking by funding source from the first dollar spent. Reconstructing grant expenditure reports from comingled accounts at year-end is a compliance risk that Kronkosky's program staff will notice.

## Corporate Giving: H-E-B and USAA

### H-E-B

H-E-B is one of the largest privately held companies in the United States, headquartered in San Antonio. The company's community giving includes direct corporate donations, the annual H-E-B Tournament of Champions (which directs millions to Texas nonprofits), food bank partnerships, disaster response support, and employee volunteer engagement.

For San Antonio nonprofits, H-E-B giving often comes as corporate sponsorships and in-kind donations rather than traditional grants. The accounting treatment differs: sponsorships may be exchange transactions (requiring revenue recognition under ASC 606) if the company receives substantial return benefits (logo placement, naming rights, marketing exposure). Pure donations without substantial return benefits are contributions under ASC 958.

Correctly classifying H-E-B support as either a contribution or an exchange transaction affects your financial statements, your Form 990 reporting, and potentially your public support test. Get the classification right at the time of the gift, not at year-end.

### USAA Foundation

USAA, headquartered in San Antonio, focuses its philanthropic work on military family resiliency, financial readiness, and San Antonio community programs through the USAA Foundation. Similar accounting considerations apply - classify USAA support correctly as contributions or exchange transactions based on the substance of each arrangement.

## San Antonio Area Foundation

The San Antonio Area Foundation functions as the community foundation for the region, managing donor-advised funds and running competitive grant programs. Unlike the Kronkosky Foundation (a private foundation with a single endowment), the Area Foundation pools donations from many donors and serves as a civic leadership organization.

Competitive grants from the Area Foundation typically range from $5,000 to $50,000. The application process is accessible to smaller and mid-sized nonprofits. Track [grant deadlines](/free/san-antonio-grant-deadline-calendar-2026) for the Area Foundation's various funding cycles alongside your other San Antonio funding sources.

DAF grants through the Area Foundation arrive as contributions from the foundation (not from the individual donor). Report them as foundation grants on your Form 990 Schedule B and in your donor records. The individual DAF advisor's identity may be disclosed to you, but the legal donor is the community foundation.

## Texas Comptroller Requirements

Texas has no state income tax, but nonprofits still face state-level compliance requirements through the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts.

### Sales Tax Exemption

Apply for state sales tax exemption using Form AP-204 after receiving your IRS determination letter. The exemption covers purchases of tangible personal property used in your exempt function. You will receive an exemption letter and number to provide to vendors. Not all purchases qualify - items used for unrelated business activities or employee personal use are not exempt.

### Franchise Tax Exemption

Texas imposes a franchise (margin) tax on entities doing business in the state. Nonprofits with 501(c)(3) status are generally exempt, but you must apply for the exemption through the Comptroller's office. File the exemption request along with your IRS determination letter.

### Annual Reporting

File annual public information reports with the Texas Secretary of State. This is a simple filing confirming your organization's current registered agent, principal office address, and directors/officers. The filing is separate from your IRS Form 990 obligation. For a full overview of Texas requirements, see the [Texas charitable registration guide](/resources/guides/texas-charitable-registration-houston-dallas-austin-guide).

## San Antonio's Cost-Structure Advantage

San Antonio's cost of living runs approximately 15-20% below Austin and 10-15% below Houston. For nonprofits, this translates directly to financial competitiveness.

### Salary Benchmarks

Staff costs - typically 60-75% of a nonprofit's budget - are meaningfully lower in San Antonio. A program manager position that pays $55,000 to $65,000 in San Antonio might command $65,000 to $80,000 in Austin for equivalent experience. This difference compounds across an entire staff.

### Facility Costs

Office and program space rental in San Antonio runs well below Austin and Houston rates. Organizations that own facilities benefit from lower property values and (with exemption) eliminated property tax burdens.

### Grant Competitiveness

In competitive federal grants that evaluate cost-effectiveness - cost per participant served, cost per outcome achieved - San Antonio nonprofits can demonstrate more impact per dollar than organizations in higher-cost metros. Smart grant writers make this explicit in budget narratives rather than leaving reviewers to infer it.

## Building Your Accounting Infrastructure

San Antonio nonprofits managing multiple funding streams - DoD contracts, foundation grants, corporate sponsorships, government contracts - need accounting infrastructure that goes beyond basic bookkeeping.

### Fund Accounting

Every restricted funding source needs its own tracking mechanism. Your accounting system must produce reports showing revenue and expenses by funding source, fund balance by restriction category, and compliance with each funder's budget categories. [Nonprofit accounting software](/resources/guides/nonprofit-accounting-software-guide) built for fund accounting handles this natively; general-purpose tools like QuickBooks require extensive customization.

### Grant Tracking Integration

The compliance calendar for a San Antonio nonprofit holding four to five active grants from different sources is dense. Kronkosky reports quarterly, DoD contracts require monthly financial status reports, the Area Foundation wants an annual narrative, and H-E-B expects sponsorship fulfillment documentation on its own timeline. A [grant tracking system](/resources/best/best-grant-tracking-software-san-antonio) that integrates with your accounting software eliminates the manual reconciliation that causes missed deadlines and reporting errors.

### Audit Readiness

If your federal expenditures cross the $1,000,000 single audit threshold for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later, you need audit-ready financials year-round - not a scramble at fiscal year-end. Maintain your chart of accounts, reconcile monthly, document cost allocation consistently, and keep procurement records organized from the start. The cost of audit preparation after the fact always exceeds the cost of maintaining clean records continuously.
