Tampa Bay's population growth over the past decade has been among the fastest of any major metro area in the United States. That growth is driving demand for new nonprofits in housing, workforce development, immigrant services, youth programs, and health access - particularly in Hillsborough County, where the gap between population growth and social service capacity is widening.

Starting a nonprofit in Tampa follows the same fundamental sequence as anywhere in Florida - state incorporation, federal tax exemption, state charitable solicitation registration - but Tampa-specific factors including the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's process, the Tampa Bay Community Foundation's role as a local funding gateway, and the region's demographic trajectory all shape how the startup process plays out in practice.

This guide walks through each step in order, with the specific forms, fees, and filing requirements current as of 2026.

## Step 1: Florida Incorporation Through Sunbiz

Florida nonprofit incorporation is handled online through Sunbiz, the Division of Corporations portal. The filing fee is $35 for online submission.

Your Articles of Incorporation must include:

- The corporation's name (must include "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Company," or an abbreviation, and cannot be confusingly similar to an existing Florida entity)
- A statement that the corporation is organized exclusively for one or more exempt purposes under Section 501(c)(3)
- The registered agent's name and Florida street address
- The incorporator's name and address
- A dissolution clause directing assets to another 501(c)(3) upon dissolution (the IRS requires this language for tax-exempt status)

Florida does not require minimum board size in the Articles, but your Bylaws should establish a board of at least three directors - the IRS expects this for 501(c)(3) applications and most funders require it.

For the full Florida incorporation process beyond Tampa-specific details, see the [Florida nonprofit startup guide](/resources/guides/florida-nonprofit-startup-guide).

## Step 2: Obtain an EIN

After incorporation, apply for an Employer Identification Number from the IRS. This is free and can be completed online at IRS.gov for organizations with a responsible party who has an SSN or ITIN. The EIN is required before opening a bank account, applying for 501(c)(3) status, or entering into contracts.

## Step 3: Apply for 501(c)(3) Status

The federal tax exemption application is the most substantial step in the startup process. You will file either Form 1023 (the full application, $600 filing fee) or Form 1023-EZ (the streamlined version, $275 filing fee).

**Form 1023-EZ eligibility:** Projected annual gross receipts of $50,000 or less for each of the next three years, and total assets of $250,000 or less. If your Tampa nonprofit is starting small and expects modest early revenue, 1023-EZ saves time and money. However, the abbreviated application provides less documentation of your exempt purpose, which can be a disadvantage if your activities are later questioned.

**Form 1023 (full application):** Required for organizations above the 1023-EZ thresholds. The full application requires detailed narrative descriptions of activities, financial projections, governance documentation, and compensation information. Processing times vary but typically range from three to six months.

Both applications require the dissolution clause in your Articles of Incorporation. If your Articles do not include this language, you will need to amend them before the IRS will approve your application.

For a detailed walkthrough of the 501(c)(3) application, see the [501(c)(3) step-by-step guide](/resources/guides/501c3-application-step-by-step-guide).

## Step 4: Register with FDACS Before Fundraising

This step is non-negotiable and frequently overlooked by new Tampa nonprofits. The [Florida Solicitation of Contributions Act](/resources/guides/florida-solicitation-of-contributions-act-guide) requires registration with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services before soliciting any contributions from Florida residents. This includes:

- Online donation pages
- Email fundraising appeals
- Direct mail solicitations
- Fundraising events
- In-person solicitation
- Grant applications (in some interpretations)

Registration fees are tiered based on the amount of contributions received:

- Under $25,000: $10
- $25,000 to $100,000: $75
- $100,000 to $200,000: $125
- Higher tiers scale accordingly

FDACS registration also triggers financial statement requirements. Organizations soliciting above certain thresholds must submit audited or reviewed financial statements. The specific thresholds and requirements are detailed in the Florida Solicitation of Contributions Act guide linked above.

Do not launch a fundraising campaign, set up a donation page, or send a fundraising email before completing FDACS registration. Violations carry penalties and can damage credibility with funders who check registration status.

## Step 5: Florida Sales Tax Exemption (Form DR-5)

After receiving your IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter, apply for a Florida Consumer's Certificate of Exemption using Form DR-5, filed with the Florida Department of Revenue. This exempts the organization from paying sales tax on eligible purchases made for exempt purposes.

Key points:

- The exemption applies to purchases the organization makes, not to sales the organization conducts (fundraising sales may still be taxable)
- You must present the Consumer's Certificate of Exemption to vendors at the time of purchase
- Not all purchases qualify - the DR-5 instructions specify exclusions
- The certificate must be renewed periodically

For detailed coverage of the Florida sales tax exemption process, see the [DR-5 guide](/resources/guides/florida-dr-5-sales-tax-exemption-guide).

## Step 6: Hillsborough County Property Tax Exemption

If your Tampa nonprofit owns real property used exclusively for exempt purposes, you can apply for an ad valorem property tax exemption through the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's office. The application deadline is March 1 of each year for the following tax year.

Requirements:

- The property must be owned by the nonprofit (leased property generally does not qualify)
- The property must be used exclusively for the organization's exempt purpose
- The organization must have current 501(c)(3) status
- The application must be filed annually - the exemption does not automatically renew in all cases

For new nonprofits that lease space, this step is not immediately relevant but becomes important if the organization acquires property. The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's website provides current application forms and filing instructions.

## Step 7: Ongoing Compliance

Starting the nonprofit is the beginning, not the end, of the regulatory relationship. Annual compliance requirements for a Tampa nonprofit include:

**Federal:**

- IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N depending on gross receipts
- Maintaining the organization's exempt purpose and governance requirements

**State:**

- Florida Sunbiz annual report ($61.25, filed through the Division of Corporations)
- FDACS annual registration renewal
- Financial statement submissions to FDACS based on solicitation thresholds

**County:**

- Hillsborough County property tax exemption renewal (if applicable)

**Grant-specific:**

- Individual reporting requirements per each grantor's terms

Missing any of these filings can result in loss of good standing, loss of solicitation authority, or loss of tax-exempt status. Build a compliance calendar during the startup phase and maintain it as a permanent operational discipline.

## Tampa Bay Community Foundation and Local Funding

The Tampa Bay Community Foundation is the region's primary community foundation, managing donor-advised funds and running competitive grant programs across Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco, Hernando, and Citrus counties. For new nonprofits, the foundation serves two purposes:

1. **Future funding source.** Most competitive grant programs require one to two years of operating history before an organization is eligible to apply. Plan for this ramp-up period in your initial fundraising strategy.
2. **Community connector.** The foundation's nonprofit directory and community programs provide visibility to donors and other funders in the Tampa Bay region.

Beyond the community foundation, Tampa Bay's funding landscape includes Hillsborough County's federal pass-through programs (CDBG, ESG, HOME) for housing and community development, the United Way Suncoast, and several family foundations with Tampa Bay focus areas.

For new organizations, the realistic fundraising sequence is: board giving and personal networks first, small community grants and individual donors second, foundation grants after establishing a track record. Attempting to secure major foundation or government grants before having twelve to eighteen months of operations and financial statements is generally unsuccessful and can consume startup capacity that would be better spent on program delivery.

## Tampa's Growth and Nonprofit Demand

Tampa Bay's population growth is not evenly distributed, and neither is the demand for new nonprofits. Hillsborough County's fastest-growing areas - including plant-city corridor communities and unincorporated areas south and east of Tampa - often lack the social service infrastructure that established urban neighborhoods have.

Areas of particularly high demand for new nonprofit services include affordable housing navigation, immigrant and refugee services, workforce development for displaced workers, mental health access, and youth programming in rapidly growing suburban areas that lack existing organizations.

New founders should research existing organizations in their proposed service area before incorporating. Duplication of services in an area already served by established nonprofits wastes resources and makes fundraising harder. Collaboration, fiscal sponsorship, or joining an existing organization's program may be more effective than starting from scratch.
