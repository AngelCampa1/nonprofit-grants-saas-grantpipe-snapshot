## What state grants are available to Pennsylvania nonprofits, and how does the funding cycle work?

Pennsylvania nonprofits draw from five funding channels that operate on overlapping but distinct calendars: state agency competitive grants funded by the legislature, federal pass-throughs administered through state departments under 2 CFR 200, private and community foundations headquartered in Pennsylvania, tribal and regional funders where applicable, and occasional capital appropriations routed through state facilities or infrastructure agencies.

The state funding cycle begins each year with the governor's budget proposal, moves through legislative appropriation during the session, and triggers Notices of Funding Availability (NOFAs) from individual agencies - typically clustered in the spring for awards effective at the start of the July 1-June 30 fiscal year. Federal pass-throughs follow the federal fiscal year (October 1-September 30), which means most Pennsylvania nonprofits manage two parallel grant calendars at once.

This guide maps the Pennsylvania state grant funder landscape: the major grantmaking agencies, federal pass-through context, top private and community foundations by assets, and tribal funders where relevant. ## Major Pennsylvania state grantmaking agencies

### Pennsylvania Department of Community and Economic Development (DCED)

**Programs:** Keystone Communities Program, CDBG, Neighborhood Assistance Program tax credits, Industrial Development Program.

**Typical award size:** $50,000-$5,000,000.

**Cycle:** Rolling and annual; state FY July 1-June 30.

**Eligibility:** Nonprofits, CDCs, local governments.

**Where to apply:** [https://dced.pa.gov](https://dced.pa.gov)

### Pennsylvania Council on the Arts (PCA)

**Programs:** Pennsylvania Partners in the Arts (PPA), Creative Communities, Project Stream, Entry to Arts Funding.

**Typical award size:** $1,000-$100,000.

**Cycle:** Annual; PPA deadlines vary by regional partner.

**Eligibility:** 501(c)(3) arts and cultural organizations.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.arts.pa.gov](https://www.arts.pa.gov)

### Pennsylvania Department of Human Services (DHS)

**Programs:** Human Services Block Grant subgrants, Homeless Assistance Program, child welfare contracts.

**Typical award size:** $100,000-$5,000,000.

**Cycle:** State FY; RFPs typically March-May.

**Eligibility:** Human services 501(c)(3) organizations.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.dhs.pa.gov](https://www.dhs.pa.gov)

### Pennsylvania Commission on Crime and Delinquency (PCCD)

**Programs:** VOCA subgrants, VAWA STOP grants, Byrne JAG, Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention.

**Typical award size:** $50,000-$1,000,000.

**Cycle:** Annual federal FY cycle.

**Eligibility:** Victim services, criminal justice nonprofits.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.pccd.pa.gov](https://www.pccd.pa.gov)

### Pennsylvania Department of Education (PDE)

**Programs:** 21st CCLC, Title pass-throughs, EITC and OSTC related programs.

**Typical award size:** $50,000-$1,500,000.

**Cycle:** Annual RFA; state FY.

**Eligibility:** Nonprofits partnering with LEAs.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.education.pa.gov](https://www.education.pa.gov)

## Federal pass-through context

Pennsylvania state agencies receive substantial federal funding that is then subgranted to nonprofits as pass-throughs. The major federal funding streams routed through Pennsylvania include:

- **CDBG (HUD Community Development Block Grant)** - administered through Pennsylvania Department of Community and Economic Development for non-entitlement areas. Subject to 2 CFR 200 Uniform Guidance and HUD-specific compliance.
- **HOME Investment Partnerships (HUD)** - affordable housing development and rehabilitation, administered through the state housing finance or community affairs agency.
- **Emergency Solutions Grants and Continuum of Care (HUD)** - homeless services. Subject to ESG and CoC regulations on top of 2 CFR 200.
- **Community Services Block Grant (HHS)** - anti-poverty programming through Community Action Agencies and partner nonprofits.
- **VOCA Victim Assistance and VAWA STOP grants (DOJ)** - victim services, administered through the state's criminal justice or attorney general's office.
- **Title V Maternal and Child Health Block Grant (HHS HRSA)** - public health pass-through through Pennsylvania's health department.
- **USDA Rural Development programs** - rural-area infrastructure, housing, and community facilities funded through state offices and Cooperative Extension partners.

Every federal pass-through carries the original federal compliance terms: Single Audit threshold ($1,000,000 in federal expenditures triggers a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later), procurement standards, conflict-of-interest rules, and time-and-effort documentation. State agencies typically add additional state-specific reporting on top.

## Top Pennsylvania-headquartered private and community foundations

The largest private and community foundations headquartered in Pennsylvania, by approximate assets:

- **The Pew Charitable Trusts** - Philadelphia, national and PA-focused.
- **The Heinz Endowments** - Pittsburgh-based.
- **Richard King Mellon Foundation** - Pittsburgh-based.
- **The Pittsburgh Foundation** - western PA community foundation.
- **The Philadelphia Foundation** - southeast PA community foundation.

These are drawn from publicly available IRS Form 990-PF filings and aggregated foundation data. Asset levels and giving patterns shift year over year - verify current figures against the foundation's most recent 990-PF before treating any number as authoritative. The top five typically represent a meaningful share of Pennsylvania-originated philanthropic capital, but smaller family and corporate foundations also fund mission-aligned work in specific regions or program areas.

## Tribal and regional funders

No federally recognized tribes are headquartered in Pennsylvania. Pennsylvania nonprofits serving Native communities partner with regional tribal governments and HHS ANA programs.

Where tribal-led programs apply, the major federal funding streams include HHS Administration for Native Americans (ANA) Social and Economic Development Strategies (SEDS) grants, HUD Indian Community Development Block Grant (ICDBG), and BIA Tribal Government Services. State-level tribal partnerships vary by state.

## How the calendar works

The recurring deadlines table in this guide's frontmatter shows the major Pennsylvania state grant submission windows. Three calendar realities shape how Pennsylvania nonprofits plan:

1. **Two parallel fiscal years.** State-funded programs follow July 1-June 30; federal pass-throughs follow October 1-September 30. Renewal and reporting deadlines collide.
2. **Spring-heavy NOFA cycle.** Most state agency RFAs and NOFAs publish March through May for awards effective at the start of the next state fiscal year. A development director who is not pipeline-ready by February misses the cluster.
3. **Federal pass-throughs lag federal allocation.** State agencies cannot release a CDBG or HOME NOFA until HUD finalizes the state's allocation, which can push pass-through cycles later than state-funded equivalents.

## What this means for grant management practice

A Pennsylvania nonprofit running a representative grant mix - one CDBG pass-through, one state arts council award, two foundation grants from in-state community foundations, and a federal direct grant - manages four reporting cadences, three audit perspectives (state, federal, foundation), and two fiscal year calendars. The compliance overhead is real and grows non-linearly with each new restricted fund.

Three practical implications:

- **Build the calendar before the funder list.** Mapping deadlines and report cycles is the work that catches the slips. A pipeline without a calendar is a wishlist.
- **Track restricted balances per grant, per fund, per FY.** GAAP-aligned restricted fund accounting under FASB ASC 958 is non-negotiable once federal expenditures cross $1,000,000 for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later and a Single Audit is triggered.
- **Document the audit trail before you need it.** Time and effort, procurement, conflict-of-interest, and subaward monitoring documentation must exist contemporaneously, not be reconstructed at audit time.
