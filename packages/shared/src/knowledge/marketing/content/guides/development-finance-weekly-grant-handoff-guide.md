Development and finance often see different parts of the same grant. Development sees the funder, proposal, relationship, and deadline. Finance sees the budget, cash, restrictions, coding, and support. A weekly handoff keeps those facts together.

The handoff does not need to be long. It does need to be regular. Waiting until award notice, report week, or audit prep can turn small gaps into bigger problems.

Use this guide to run a weekly development and finance grant handoff. It works for teams where one person wears several hats. It also works for larger teams with a grants manager between development and finance.

## Start with one shared list

Do not run the handoff from two separate spreadsheets. Use one list of grants and opportunities. Each item should show stage, owner, next date, budget status, restriction status, cash timing, and report status.

Stages can include research, qualified, writing, submitted, awarded, active, renewal, closeout, declined, and paused.

The shared list should answer a basic question: what changed since last week?

If the team spends half the meeting asking where things live, the system needs work. GrantPipe can help by keeping opportunities, awards, tasks, documents, reports, and notes in one shared record. The goal is not more software for its own sake. The goal is fewer missing facts.

## Development brings funder and deadline facts

Development should come ready with updates on funder conversations, proposal deadlines, letters of intent, submitted applications, expected award dates, renewal signals, and relationship concerns.

For each active opportunity, development should answer:

- What is due next?
- Who owns it?
- What changed with the funder?
- Is the request amount still right?
- Are there new restrictions or terms?
- Is any board or executive approval needed?

This helps finance see what is coming before a proposal turns into an award.

## Finance brings budget and cash facts

Finance should come ready with updates on budget fit, coding, receivables, reimbursement timing, restricted balances, match support, and any missing documentation.

For each active or pending award, finance should answer:

- Does the budget cover the real cost?
- Are staff and shared costs treated correctly?
- Will the award pay in advance or by reimbursement?
- Is any payment delayed?
- Are restricted funds tracked clearly?
- What support is missing for a report or audit file?

Finance should not be surprised by a submitted grant budget. Development should not be surprised by a cash issue. The handoff prevents both.

## Review proposals before submission

Before a proposal is submitted, finance should review the budget and payment terms. Development should review the funder fit and narrative promises. Program staff should confirm delivery capacity.

Use the [grant proposal budget review checklist](/resources/guides/grant-proposal-budget-review-checklist) for this step.

The handoff should flag grants where the budget does not match the work. It should also flag grants that require match, indirect cost choices, special reports, or board approval.

Do not wait for an award letter to discover the grant cannot cover the promised work.

## Review award notices before acceptance

When an award notice arrives, development should send it to finance before anyone treats the money as final.

The weekly handoff should check award amount, period, payment method, restriction, reporting dates, match, budget changes, insurance, procurement, prior approval terms, and closeout rules.

If the award differs from the proposal, name the difference. A lower award amount may require a smaller scope. A changed period may affect staffing. New payment terms may affect cash.

Use the [grant award acceptance checklist](/resources/guides/grant-award-acceptance-checklist) before signing or booking revenue.

## Keep reporting dates visible

Reports often need both teams. Development may manage funder communication. Finance may supply financial schedules. Program may supply outputs and outcomes.

The handoff should review reports due in the next 30 to 60 days. For each report, name the owner, data needed, finance support, program support, and submission date.

If a report controls payment, say so. If a late report could affect renewal, say so too.

Use the [grant reporting calendar template](/resources/guides/grant-reporting-calendar-template) to turn terms into dates.

## Watch restricted funds and reimbursement gaps

Development may know the funder intent. Finance must translate it into accounting and cash planning.

Each week, review any grant with restricted cash, delayed reimbursement, or unclear revenue treatment. This guide does not replace accounting advice. It prompts the team to ask the right question early and involve the right help.

If finance is waiting on a funder email, development can help. If development is promising a report date, finance can confirm whether the numbers will be ready.

## Use a short agenda

Keep the weekly handoff tight:

1. New opportunities and go or no-go choices.
2. Proposals due in the next 30 days.
3. Budgets needing finance review.
4. Award notices needing acceptance review.
5. Reports due in the next 60 days.
6. Reimbursement and receivable issues.
7. Restricted fund or coding questions.
8. Renewal and funder relationship notes.
9. Decisions and owners.

End by reading back decisions. Each decision should have one owner and one date.

## Close the loop after the meeting

After the handoff, update the shared list. File award notices, budget notes, funder emails, report receipts, and finance schedules in the grant record.

Do not leave key facts trapped in meeting notes. The next report, audit, or staff transition will need those facts.

A good weekly handoff does not add work. It moves work earlier, when the team can still fix problems.
