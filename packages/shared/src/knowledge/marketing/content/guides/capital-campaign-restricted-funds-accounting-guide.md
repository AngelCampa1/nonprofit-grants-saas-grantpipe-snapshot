Capital campaigns can create accounting complexity fast. A campaign may include cash gifts, multi-year pledges, naming agreements, donor restrictions, project costs, construction draws, financing, and board reports. If the tracking is weak at the start, the finance team may spend months trying to explain restricted balances.

This guide gives a practical setup path. It is not accounting advice. Capital campaigns can raise pledge, revenue recognition, construction, debt, and legal questions. Use a CPA or legal advisor for organization-specific decisions.

## Start with gift terms, not the campaign slogan

The campaign name is not enough for accounting. Finance needs the donor's actual terms.

For each gift or pledge, save:

- Signed agreement or pledge card
- Donor letter or email
- Campaign page that named the use
- Payment schedule
- Any matching terms
- Naming or recognition terms
- Reporting promise
- Donor change or release clause

The donor may restrict the gift to a building, a wing, equipment, land, endowment, or a time period. Some gifts may support the campaign broadly. Others may be tied to a specific part of the project.

## Separate pledges, receivables, and cash

Campaign teams often talk about "raised" dollars. Finance needs more detail.

Track at least four amounts:

- Pledges committed
- Receivables recorded
- Cash received
- Amount released or spent

These numbers will not always match. A donor may pledge today and pay over five years. Another donor may pay cash before the project starts. A third gift may be conditional on another event.

FASB ASU 2018-08 can matter when a promise includes conditions. Finance should review those terms before recording revenue.

## Build fund tracking before deposits grow

Do not wait until year end to decide how campaign gifts will be tracked. Set up fund, project, grant, class, or campaign codes before the first large deposit.

The tracking should show:

- Donor restriction
- Campaign purpose
- Gift date
- Payment status
- Project cost category
- Release status
- Report owner

GrantPipe can help connect donor CRM records, restricted funds, deadlines, reporting, and audit evidence. That is useful when development sees the donor promise and finance needs the fund balance.

## Tie project costs to the restriction

Restricted campaign gifts should be released based on the donor terms and accounting review. The team needs evidence that the restriction was met.

For a building campaign, support may include invoices, architect bills, construction draws, permits, board approvals, or placed in service dates. For equipment, support may include purchase orders, invoices, serial numbers, and location records.

Do not assume every campaign cost meets every donor restriction. A gift restricted to playground equipment may not pay for general campaign consulting unless the agreement allows it.

## Watch timing restrictions

Some gifts are restricted by time. Others are restricted by purpose. Some have both. A multi-year pledge can also create timing questions.

Add fields for:

- Gift period
- Payment due dates
- Project start date
- Project completion date
- Release trigger
- Review date

This helps finance explain why cash may be on hand while the related restricted balance remains.

## Review campaign reports before sending

Campaign reports often go to donors, boards, lenders, and auditors. The report should be checked before it is shared.

Development may report campaign progress. Finance may report restricted balances and cash. Those numbers can differ, and that is okay if the report explains them clearly.

Before sending a campaign report, compare gift totals, cash received, pledge balances, project spending, and restricted balances. Save the reviewed version.

## Handle changes with a record

Campaigns change. Costs rise. Timelines move. Donors change pledge schedules. The board may change the project scope.

When a change affects restricted funds, save the evidence. That may be a donor approval, board approval, revised agreement, or advisor note. Do not rely on a verbal update for a material change.

## Close the campaign cleanly

At closeout, reconcile the campaign. Tie donor records to cash, receivables, restricted balances, releases, and project costs. Identify open pledges, unused restricted amounts, and reporting promises.

Keep the closeout packet with the campaign file. It should help a new finance lead understand what was promised, what was received, what was spent, and what remains restricted.

A capital campaign is easier to manage when donor intent and accounting evidence stay connected from the first gift.
