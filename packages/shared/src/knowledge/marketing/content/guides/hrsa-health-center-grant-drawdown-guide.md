# HRSA health center grant drawdown guide

HRSA health center grant drawdowns are cash requests. They are not budget updates. They should match real grant costs and a short cash need. The file should show what was requested, why it was allowed, who checked it, when the money arrived, and how it cleared the ledger.

Start with the [federal grant drawdown controls checklist](/resources/guides/federal-grant-drawdown-controls-checklist). Then add the health center details that HRSA reviewers expect. HRSA's site visit protocol for financial management asks reviewers to look at procedures for drawdown, disbursement, and expenditure of federal award funds from PMS. That means your process needs to be written, used, and easy to prove.

## Build one award profile

Create a drawdown profile for each HRSA award. Include the award number, project period, PMS account access, authorized submitters, budget categories, report due dates, and any special terms in the notice of award. Keep the profile with your grant file.

Add a short note on the payment method. Some awards work as reimbursement. Others allow advance payment under 2 CFR 200.305. If the award allows advances, your file must still show that cash on hand stayed as low as feasible. Do not draw the full monthly budget unless the costs are ready to pay.

## Pull costs before cash

Each draw should start with cost support. Pull a grant ledger report for the period. Remove costs that are outside the project period, outside the approved scope, missing support, or coded to the wrong award. Payroll should tie to time records. Vendor costs should tie to invoices and approvals. Indirect costs should tie to the approved rate and base.

For a health center, also check scope. If a cost supports a site, service, or activity outside the approved Health Center Program scope, stop and review it before drawing HRSA funds. The [community health center HRSA 330 guide](/resources/guides/community-health-centers-hrsa-330-reporting-guide) explains why scope and finance records have to agree.

## Keep PMS, bank, and ledger in one file

The drawdown packet should include the PMS confirmation, the ledger detail, the approval record, and bank proof. After funds arrive, add the bank deposit and the ledger cash receipt. If you drew in advance, add the date the funds were paid out for grant costs.

This timing note matters. It shows the cash did not sit idle. It also helps reconcile the SF-425. HRSA recipients use PMS for Federal Financial Report work, so PMS cash records and financial reports should not tell different stories.

## Use a second-person review

The preparer should not be the only reviewer. The reviewer should check the award number, draw amount, grant period, ledger detail, and duplicate cost risk. If the center has a small finance team, the CFO, executive director, or board treasurer can review the packet.

Write the review down. A simple approval log is enough if it names the reviewer, date, amount, period, and records checked. A verbal review will not help during a site visit or audit.

## Watch high-risk draws

Add extra review when the draw is larger than normal, near closeout, tied to a cost transfer, or based on a late payroll adjustment. These draws raise more questions because they can hide weak coding or rushed spending.

For high-risk draws, compare the request to the approved budget and spending pace. If prior approval was needed for the cost, keep that approval in the packet. If a cost was moved from another grant, keep the reason and the records showing HRSA benefited from the cost.

## Reconcile monthly

At month end, compare total HRSA cash received to PMS, bank deposits, and the ledger. Then compare federal expenditures to the costs used for each draw. Small timing differences are normal. Unexplained differences are not.

Use the [SF-425 line item review guide](/resources/guides/sf-425-line-item-review-guide) before each report. The same work that keeps a drawdown file clean also keeps cash and expenditure lines from drifting.

## Train backup users

Do not let one PMS user become the whole control. Keep a list of authorized users, their roles, and the last access review date. Remove access when staff leave or change jobs. Train at least one backup person on the drawdown packet, but keep preparation and approval roles separate.

Access review is simple, but it prevents two common problems. First, a former staff member keeps system access. Second, the only person who knows the draw process is out when a report is due. Add the access list to the annual finance review.

## Close the file

When the project period ends, finish the drawdown file before closeout. Confirm all costs were incurred in the project period, liquidated within the required window, and included in the final report. Lock the final PMS, bank, ledger, and SF-425 support together.
