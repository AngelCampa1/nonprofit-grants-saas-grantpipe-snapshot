Many nonprofit teams still say "temporarily restricted funds." The phrase is familiar, and older reports may use it. Under current GAAP, ASU 2016-14 replaced the old three-class presentation with two classes: net assets with donor restrictions and net assets without donor restrictions.

The close process should use the current terms in financial statements. Staff can still map older internal labels to the new classes, but board reports and audit schedules should be clear.

## Build the close schedule

Start with a schedule of every active restricted fund. Each row should show:

- Fund or grant name
- Donor or grantor
- Restriction type
- Beginning balance
- New restricted revenue
- Releases this period
- Corrections or returns
- Ending balance
- Support location
- Owner

This schedule is the control document. It should tie to the general ledger and the board packet.

For a shorter operational list, use the [restricted fund month-end close checklist](/resources/guides/restricted-fund-month-end-close-checklist).

## Confirm new restricted revenue

Review new gifts, pledges, and grant awards. Decide whether each item has a donor restriction. Do not treat an internal budget note as a donor restriction. The restriction must come from the donor or grantor.

Keep the award letter, gift letter, pledge, grant agreement, or email that states the restriction. If the wording is unclear, ask for clarification before close when possible.

Common restricted revenue includes grants for a named program, gifts for a capital project, funds for a future fiscal year, and gifts limited to a population or place.

Common unrestricted revenue includes general donations, annual fund gifts without limits, and board-designated money. Board designations should not be reported as donor restrictions.

## Post valid releases

A release should happen when the restriction has been met. For purpose restrictions, that usually means qualifying expenses were incurred. For time restrictions, it means the specified period arrived. For combined restrictions, both tests matter.

Do not release based only on cash received. Do not release based only on budget. Do not release just because the funder paid the invoice.

At close, compare restricted expenses to releases posted. If expenses were coded to a restricted fund but no release was posted, investigate. If releases exceed supported expenses, investigate that too.

Use [net asset release workflow](/resources/guides/net-asset-release-workflow) as the accounting reference.

## Tie restricted balances to detail

Each ending balance should have a reason. It may represent unspent grant funds, a time restriction for a future period, a capital campaign balance, or an endowment-related amount.

Old balances need special review. Ask:

- Is the donor restriction still active?
- Was the release missed?
- Was revenue recorded twice?
- Was cash returned or due back?
- Is written donor or funder clarification needed?

Do not force old balances to zero without support. That can create a bigger audit issue than leaving the balance open with a note.

## Check cash and receivables

Restricted net assets do not always equal restricted cash. A grant may be receivable but not yet collected. A reimbursement grant may have expenses incurred before cash arrives. A donor may restrict a pledge for a future year.

Still, finance should explain the relationship between restricted balances, cash, receivables, and payables. This helps the board understand liquidity.

At year-end, prepare a cash availability bridge. Start with total cash and investments. Remove amounts restricted by donors. Remove other amounts not available for general use. This supports the liquidity disclosure required under ASU 2016-14.

## Prepare the audit support file

For each material restricted fund, keep:

- Donor or funder restriction language
- Grant budget, if relevant
- General ledger detail
- Expense support
- Release entries
- Receivable and cash support
- Correspondence for changes or approvals
- Ending balance explanation

Auditors may test whether restrictions were properly classified and released. The Council of Nonprofits notes that audits look at financial records and controls. A clean close file helps staff answer without rebuilding history.

## Update board reporting

After close, update the board report. Show total net assets with donor restrictions, releases this period, old balances, and any funds needing action.

If a board packet still says temporarily restricted, add a note that current financial statement presentation uses net assets with donor restrictions. Keep the explanation simple. The board needs to understand the restriction, not memorize accounting history.
