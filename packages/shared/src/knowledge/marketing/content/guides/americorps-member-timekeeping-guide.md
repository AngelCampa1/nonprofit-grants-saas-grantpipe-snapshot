# AmeriCorps member timekeeping guide

AmeriCorps member time records are more than attendance logs. They support member service hours, program compliance, living allowance records, and education award eligibility. A weak timesheet process can create problems long after the service happened.

The AmeriCorps member timesheet outline says timesheets must be stored under grant recordkeeping rules and available for inspection. Treat that as the design goal. A reviewer should be able to open a member file and see what the member did, when they did it, who approved it, and how corrections were handled.

## Write the timekeeping rule first

Create a short written procedure before members start. Define the time period, due date, allowed categories, approval steps, correction process, and record location. Include what counts as service, training, fundraising if allowed, and other categories your program tracks.

Do not rely on informal site habits. If one site records daily hours and another site records weekly totals, monitoring becomes harder. The whole program should use the same core fields.

## Use clear categories

AmeriCorps programs often need to separate direct service, training, fundraising limits, and non-service time. Use categories that match the member service agreement and grant terms. Avoid vague labels like "program work." A reviewer needs to understand the activity without asking the member to remember it months later.

The entry does not need a long story. A good line can say: "June 10, food pantry client intake, direct service, 4 hours." That is clearer than a weekly total with no detail.

## Require two approvals

The member should certify the time. A supervisor should review and approve it. Both approvals should be dated. Electronic approvals are fine if the system preserves the signer, date, and record history.

The supervisor review should check reasonableness. Do the hours fit the service schedule? Did the member exceed a daily cap or miss a required break under program policy? Did the entry include prohibited activity? Approval should mean someone checked the record, not just clicked through.

## Handle corrections openly

Mistakes happen. A member may enter the wrong category or forget to submit a day. Corrections should keep the original record history or include a correction note. The note should show what changed, why, who requested it, who approved it, and when.

Do not erase old records without a trace. That makes the corrected sheet look cleaner, but it weakens the audit trail. A clear correction trail is better than a perfect-looking record with no history.

## Check hours before key points

Review member hours before midterm checks, living allowance milestones, exit, and education award certification. Compare the timesheet total to any member management system total. Differences should be resolved before closeout.

If a member is behind pace, document the plan. If a member is near the maximum allowed hours for a category, review entries before more time is approved. Small checks during service prevent larger disputes at exit.

## Keep payroll and member service separate

Member timekeeping is not the same as employee payroll timekeeping. Some programs also charge staff salaries to the grant. Staff salaries should follow the [time and effort certification guide](/resources/guides/time-and-effort-certification-federal-grants). Member hours should follow AmeriCorps member rules and the member service agreement.

Do not mix the two files. A member's service hours support service completion. Staff time records support grant salary charges. They answer different questions.

## Watch prohibited activities

The timekeeping process should help catch activities that cannot be counted. Train members and supervisors on prohibited activities before service begins. If a prohibited activity appears on a timesheet, remove it from counted service hours and document the correction.

This is another reason categories matter. Vague entries hide risk. Clear entries help supervisors catch issues before the record becomes final.

## Retain the full file

Keep timesheets, approvals, corrections, member agreements, exit hour support, and monitoring notes under the grant record retention rule and any AmeriCorps award terms. See the [federal award closeout retention guide](/resources/guides/federal-award-closeout-document-retention-guide) for a broader retention file.

For multi-site programs, keep site records in a central place or make sure the central office can retrieve them quickly. "The site has it" is not enough if AmeriCorps asks for records.

## Review the process each year

After each program year, review common errors. Did members miss approvals? Did supervisors approve late? Did categories confuse people? Did the system preserve corrections? Update training and forms before the next member cohort starts.
