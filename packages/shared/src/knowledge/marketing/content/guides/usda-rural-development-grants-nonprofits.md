USDA Rural Development is the largest single source of federal capital for rural community infrastructure, business development, and housing in the United States. For a rural-serving nonprofit, RD funding can finance a community health clinic, capitalize a small business revolving loan fund, preserve aging affordable housing stock, or extend broadband and telemedicine into underserved areas. The application processes are unusual within federal grantmaking - most program intake happens at the state RD office rather than through Grants.gov - and the eligibility tests are program-specific in ways that catch first-time applicants by surprise.

## What USDA Rural Development funds

Rural Development operates through three agencies, each with multiple programs relevant to nonprofits.

**Rural Housing Service (RHS).** The Community Facilities Direct Grant and Loan program is the workhorse for nonprofit-owned essential community facilities - health clinics, child care centers, fire and emergency response facilities, libraries, schools, and similar civic infrastructure. The program funds construction, renovation, and equipment purchases. The Housing Preservation Grant funds nonprofit and tribal housing organizations to repair and rehabilitate housing for low-income rural residents. The Multifamily Housing Preservation and Revitalization program restructures existing Section 515 properties to extend affordability.

**Rural Business-Cooperative Service (RBCS).** The Rural Business Development Grant (RBDG) supports small and emerging private business development through nonprofit and public intermediaries. The Rural Cooperative Development Grant funds nonprofit Cooperative Development Centers that incubate rural cooperatives. The Value-Added Producer Grant assists agricultural producers in entering value-added markets and is open to producer-led nonprofits. The Rural Microentrepreneur Assistance Program (RMAP) capitalizes microloan revolving funds operated by nonprofit Microenterprise Development Organizations.

**Rural Utilities Service (RUS).** The Distance Learning and Telemedicine Grant program funds rural healthcare and education organizations to deploy telecommunications equipment for distance learning and telemedicine. The ReConnect program funds rural broadband infrastructure deployment. The Solid Waste Management Grant supports nonprofits providing technical assistance to rural communities on solid waste reduction and water quality.

Award sizes vary widely. Community Facilities Direct Grants typically range from $30,000 to $50,000 per project (paired with direct loans for full project financing). Rural Business Development Grants typically range from $50,000 to $500,000. Distance Learning and Telemedicine Grants can reach $1 million per award. Housing Preservation Grants generally range from $50,000 to $200,000.

## Application requirements

Application logistics for USDA RD differ in several important ways from most other federal grantors.

**Where you apply.** Some programs (Distance Learning and Telemedicine, REAP, ReConnect) use Grants.gov. Many - including Community Facilities Direct Grants and Rural Business Development Grants - are submitted directly to the USDA RD state office serving the project location. Each state RD office maintains its own intake calendar and frequently runs its own pre-application conferences. Build a relationship with your state RD office well before you intend to apply.

**Prerequisites.** All RD applicants need an active SAM.gov registration with a valid Unique Entity Identifier. SAM registration must be renewed annually. The eligibility check on the Rural Development Property Eligibility map should happen first, because if the project address falls outside the eligible rural definition, the application is dead before you draft a single page.

**Population and income documentation.** Most RD programs require formal documentation of the population and median household income of the project area. Acceptable sources include the most recent decennial census, the American Community Survey 5-year estimates, or USDA-approved income surveys. Self-reported numbers are not accepted. For Community Facilities Direct Grants, the project's median household income relative to the state non-metropolitan MHI determines the grant-to-loan ratio.

**Environmental review.** Most RD construction projects require a National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) review under 7 CFR Part 1970. Categorical exclusions cover many small projects but require documentation. Larger projects require Environmental Assessments or Environmental Impact Statements. Build NEPA timelines into your project schedule from day one - the review can take 4-9 months for medium-sized projects.

**Davis-Bacon prevailing wages.** Construction-related RD projects are subject to Davis-Bacon Act prevailing wage requirements. Contractor selection, weekly certified payrolls, and wage interview documentation become part of your compliance file the moment the first laborer steps on site.

## Compliance specifics

USDA RD grants are governed by 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance) plus 2 CFR Part 400 (USDA's adoption of Uniform Guidance) plus program-specific regulations in 7 CFR. The interaction matters.

**Cost principles.** The Uniform Guidance allowable cost criteria apply - necessary, reasonable, allocable, conformant, consistent, documented. RD compliance officers are particularly focused on construction cost reasonableness and on the proper allocation of administrative time across multiple federal awards.

**Procurement.** 2 CFR 200 Subpart D applies. Construction procurement above the simplified acquisition threshold (currently $250,000) generally requires sealed bids with public opening. Architectural and engineering services use qualifications-based selection under 40 USC Chapter 11 (the Brooks Act). Sole-source justifications are scrutinized.

**Match documentation.** For programs requiring match (Housing Preservation Grant at 25%, several others), document in-kind contributions contemporaneously. Donated land must be supported by a qualified appraisal. Volunteer labor uses independentsector.org rates. Reconstructed match documentation rarely survives audit.

**Reporting cadence.** Most RD grants require an SF-425 Federal Financial Report annually with a final report due 90 days after the project completion date. Programmatic reports are program-specific - Community Facilities project reports are quarterly during construction, then annual after the facility opens. RBDG recipients submit annual program performance reports. Distance Learning and Telemedicine grant recipients submit semi-annual reports.

**Single Audit.** Nonprofits expending $1,000,000 or more in total federal awards in a fiscal year must obtain a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F. RD will request the audit reporting package via the Federal Audit Clearinghouse and will follow up on any findings affecting RD programs.

**RD-specific monitoring.** Independent of the Single Audit, RD state offices conduct routine compliance reviews - typically annual desk reviews and on-site reviews every 2-3 years for active grants. RD compliance reviews focus heavily on procurement files, match documentation, and continuing eligibility (in particular, that the funded facility continues to serve the rural community as proposed).

**Recapture and acceleration.** Community Facilities grants and loans include continuing eligibility covenants. If the funded facility ceases to serve the rural community within a defined recapture period, RD can accelerate the loan and require partial grant recapture. Track facility-use restrictions carefully - a sale or material change in use without RD pre-approval is a serious compliance event.

## Deadlines and NOFA cadence

USDA RD program calendars are largely predictable.

- **Community Facilities Direct Grant** - open year-round on a continuous-application basis at state RD offices, with funds awarded as available.
- **Rural Business Development Grant** - annual NOFA in late winter or spring; deadlines typically in spring.
- **Housing Preservation Grant** - annual NOFA in spring; deadlines typically in summer.
- **Distance Learning and Telemedicine** - annual NOFA in winter; deadlines typically in spring.
- **Value-Added Producer Grant** - annual NOFA in spring; deadlines in late spring.
- **Rural Cooperative Development Grant** - annual NOFA in summer; deadlines in late summer.

State RD offices also operate continuous-intake programs for the Community Facilities loan and grant program, with awards prioritized as funds become available. Building a relationship with your state RD office is the single highest-leverage activity for a rural-serving nonprofit pursuing this funding stream - many awards are shaped through pre-application discussions months before the formal package is assembled.

## Common mistakes

Five recurring failure modes account for most first-time RD compliance issues:

1. **Applying for a project at an ineligible address.** Always check the Rural Development Property Eligibility map for the specific program before drafting an application.
2. **Underestimating NEPA timelines.** Construction projects without an early NEPA strategy frequently miss their construction season and incur cost increases.
3. **Treating Davis-Bacon as paperwork.** Weekly certified payrolls, wage interviews, and posted wage decisions are inspected. Late or incomplete payrolls hold draws.
4. **Mismatching project scope to RD-approved scope.** Any material scope change requires an amendment processed through your state RD office. Charging costs against an unapproved scope is unallowable.
5. **Missing population and income documentation.** Census and ACS source citations are required; self-reported community population data is not accepted.
