Travel costs are easy to question because the business purpose can fade after the trip.

The file should show why the trip belonged to the grant and why the cost was reasonable.

## Approve the trip before booking

The travel request should name the grant, traveler, destination, dates, event or purpose, estimated cost, and budget line.

If the trip includes mixed funding, split the purpose and cost before approval.

## Keep purpose support

Save the agenda, meeting invite, site visit notice, training description, or funder request. A receipt only proves payment. It does not prove grant purpose.

Add a short note if the purpose is not clear from the document.

## Review lodging, meals, and mileage

Follow the nonprofit travel policy and the award terms. Check mileage logs, lodging receipts, meal rules, and per diem support.

If the organization uses per diem, keep the rate basis and trip dates.

## Watch personal costs

Personal days, guest costs, alcohol, upgrades, and entertainment should be removed unless the award and policy clearly allow them. Most grant files are stronger when these costs are excluded.

If one receipt includes both grant and personal costs, write the split on the support.

## Reconcile after the trip

After travel, compare approved estimate to actual costs. Check receipts, card charges, reimbursement forms, and ledger coding.

Use the [expense approval workflow](/workflows/approve-grant-expense-before-payment) before payment or reimbursement.

## Travel support table

Each trip should have a support packet. The packet does not need to be fancy. It needs to answer the audit questions.

| Cost type        | Support to keep                                      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Airfare or train | Receipt, itinerary, traveler, and trip purpose.      |
| Lodging          | Itemized hotel bill and approved travel dates.       |
| Meals            | Receipt or per diem basis, depending on policy.      |
| Mileage          | Date, origin, destination, miles, purpose, and rate. |
| Conference fee   | Registration receipt and agenda.                     |
| Local transport  | Receipt and link to grant activity.                  |
| Shared trip      | Allocation note and approval.                        |

Card slips are not enough. They show payment, not business purpose or allowability.

## Pre-trip approval checklist

Before booking, confirm:

- the trip supports the grant purpose
- the traveler is the right person
- dates fit the event or meeting
- costs fit the approved budget
- the award allows the cost
- any funder approval is saved
- shared costs have an allocation method

The approval should happen before the organization spends money. If that is not possible, require written approval as soon as the exception is known.

## After-trip reconciliation

After the trip, compare the approved plan to actual charges. Look for extra nights, personal expenses, upgrades, unused tickets, guest costs, and costs outside travel dates.

If the trip changed, write a note. A delayed flight or added funder meeting may be reasonable. A personal extension should not be charged to the grant.

If the trip was partly grant-funded and partly unrestricted, confirm the split before posting.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.475 covers travel costs for federal awards. It should be read with the general allowability and reasonableness rules in 2 CFR 200.403 and 200.404.

Some awards require prior approval for travel, foreign travel, conferences, or out-of-state trips. The award term is the first source to check.

If the organization uses per diem, the policy should say how rates are selected and how travel dates affect the amount. Keep the rate basis with the trip file.

## Mixed-purpose trips

Mixed-purpose trips need a written split. A staff member may attend a grant-funded training for 2 days, then stay for a separate donor meeting. The grant should only carry the part that fits the award.

Use a simple allocation note. Name the grant purpose, non-grant purpose, dates, and cost split. If airfare would have been the same for the grant trip alone, say that. If extra hotel nights were personal or tied to another program, remove them from the grant.

## Conference and training records

For conferences, save more than the registration receipt. Keep the agenda, session list, attendee name, travel approval, and a short note on why the event supports the award.

If the award pays for required training, keep the funder requirement or program guidance. If training is optional, explain why it is needed for the grant work.

## Mileage and local travel

Mileage logs should name the date, route, purpose, and miles. A calendar entry can help, but it should not be the only record. The reviewer should be able to see why the trip belongs to the grant.

For local travel, small costs add up. Parking, rideshare, tolls, and mileage should follow the same approval and purpose rules as larger trips.
