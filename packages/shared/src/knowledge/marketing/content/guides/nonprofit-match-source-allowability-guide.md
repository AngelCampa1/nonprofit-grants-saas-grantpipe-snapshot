Match can look easy in a proposal. It gets harder when the team has to prove it.

Before a match source appears in a report, test whether it is allowed, documented, and not counted somewhere else.

## Start with the award terms

The award should say whether match is required, how much is required, and what types of match are allowed.

Do not assume cash, volunteer time, donated space, or another grant can count. The award terms matter.

## Test for double counting

The same dollar or donated service should not be counted twice. A donation used as match for one grant should not also be used as match for another grant.

Create a match tracker with source, amount, date, valuation method, grant, and report status.

## Review valuation

Cash match is easier to value than in-kind match. In-kind match needs a fair and documented value.

For volunteer time, keep the date, service, hours, rate, and approval. For donated space or supplies, keep the donor record and valuation support.

Use the [in-kind match documentation guide](/resources/guides/in-kind-match-documentation-nonprofits) for the support file.

## Check timing

Match should happen during the period allowed by the award. A pledge may not be enough unless the award says pledges can count.

If the match happens after the report period, do not report it early.

## Review other grant funds carefully

Federal funds usually cannot be used as match for another federal award unless a law allows it. State, local, or foundation funds can also have restrictions.

If another grant is the match source, save both award terms and the approval note.

## Match source review table

Use a table before each report. It helps finance and grants staff agree on what can be reported.

| Match source   | Support to keep                               | Review question                               |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Cash donation  | Deposit record and donor restriction, if any. | Is the cash available for this grant purpose? |
| Staff time     | Payroll record and time support.              | Was the time worked during the award period?  |
| Volunteer time | Log, role, hours, and rate basis.             | Is the valuation reasonable and documented?   |
| Donated space  | Donor letter and value support.               | Is the space used for award activity?         |
| Supplies       | Donor record or purchase support.             | Are the supplies needed for the award?        |
| Other grant    | Both award terms.                             | Do both funders allow the use?                |

The table should live with the report packet. It can be short, but it should show the decision.

## Timing and report discipline

Match often fails because staff report what they expect to receive. A pledge, planned volunteer event, or budgeted staff allocation may not be enough.

Report match after it is earned, donated, paid, or otherwise supported. If a funder allows pledged match, keep that language in the file. If the award does not say that, use actual support.

This is especially important near closeout. A final report with unsupported match can create a questioned cost even when the program work was strong.

## Valuation caveats

In-kind match needs a fair value. Do not use a flat rate because it is easy. Use a rate tied to the service, item, or space.

Volunteer rates should fit the work performed. A lawyer donating legal review may have a different value than a volunteer packing boxes. A board member attending a normal board meeting may not count unless the award terms allow it and the service directly supports the grant.

Donated space should be valued with a reasonable basis, such as a lease rate or comparable rate. Keep the basis with the file.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.306 sets the federal standard for cost sharing or match. It requires match to be verifiable from records and not used for another federal award unless allowed by statute.

2 CFR 200.403 still matters. A match cost should be allowable under the award, even though the federal government is not paying that share.

Some awards set stricter match rules than the federal baseline. Read the notice of award, pass-through agreement, and program guidance before reporting match.
