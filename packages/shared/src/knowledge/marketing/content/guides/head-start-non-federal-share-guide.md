# Head Start non-federal share guide

Head Start non-federal share is often called match, but it is more than a target number. The file has to prove that cash and in-kind support were real, allowable, needed for the program, valued properly, and not counted somewhere else.

The Office of Head Start explains non-federal share as the grantee's contribution toward the total cost of the program. Federal regulations at 45 CFR 1303.4 cover the non-federal share requirement and waiver path. Your process should help the team see the match position before it becomes a closeout problem.

## Start with the required amount

Read the notice of award and budget. Calculate the non-federal share target and put it in a live tracker. Break the target into planned sources: volunteer time, donated space, donated goods, cash, parent activity, or other approved sources.

Do not use one annual line. A monthly view is safer. It shows whether the program is collecting enough support as the year moves.

## Check allowability

Non-federal share must meet the same basic cost tests as federal costs. It should be necessary and reasonable for the program, allowable under the award, and supported by records. 2 CFR 200.306 also says contributions must be verifiable from records and not counted as match for another federal award unless allowed by law.

Use the [allowable costs guide](/resources/guides/allowable-costs-federal-grants) before counting unusual items. If the donated item would not be a proper program cost, do not count it as non-federal share.

## Document volunteer time

Volunteer time is common in Head Start, but it needs real support. The log should show the volunteer name, date, activity, hours, site, and supervisor approval. The activity should connect to the program. A vague "helped at school" entry is weak. "Classroom reading activity, 1.5 hours" is clearer.

Value volunteer time using a rate that fits the service. Classroom help, professional services, and facilities work may use different rates. Keep the source for the rate in the file.

## Document donated space and goods

Donated space needs a supportable fair value. Keep the agreement, location, dates used, valuation method, and calculation. If only part of the space supports Head Start, document the allocation.

Donated goods need a receipt or donor record, description, date, condition if relevant, value, and program use. If the item was shared with another program, count only the Head Start share.

## Avoid double counting

Add a "used as match for" field to your tracker. This prevents one donation from being counted twice. It also helps during audit because the reviewer can see which award received the credit.

Double counting risk is higher for shared facilities, shared volunteer pools, and donations that support more than one program. Split the value based on a clear method and keep the math.

## Track waiver risk early

A grantee may request a waiver under Head Start rules in defined cases, such as lack of community resources or a disaster. A waiver should not be treated as a backup plan at the last minute. If the tracker shows a shortfall, brief leadership early and document outreach to possible sources.

If a waiver is requested, keep the request, reason, supporting facts, and approval or denial in the grant file.

## Reconcile to the budget

Non-federal share should be compared to both the required match amount and the approved budget. If a source supports classroom activity, facilities, or family engagement, mark the budget area it supports. This helps reviewers see that the contribution was needed for the Head Start program.

Budget tieout also protects against counting support that is helpful but outside the award. For example, a donation to a separate childcare program may not support Head Start unless the records show a clear program link.

## Review with finance and program staff

Finance can check rates, math, and ledger support. Program staff can confirm that the activity happened and supported Head Start. Review both sides each month. A finance-only match tracker can miss weak program support. A program-only tracker can miss valuation problems.

Use the [grant documentation checklist](/resources/guides/grant-documentation-checklist-audit-ready) to keep each source complete.

## Close the year cleanly

Before final reports, compare required non-federal share to documented support. Remove weak entries. Confirm rates, dates, and approvals. Keep the final tracker with source files and report support.

Add a short management note when the final amount is close to the required floor. The note should name the highest-risk sources and where the support is stored. That way, if a reviewer questions one source, leadership already knows the backup plan and possible waiver path.

GrantPipe can help teams track non-federal share sources, support status, reviews, and closeout tasks beside the grant budget. That gives program and finance staff the same view before the year ends.
