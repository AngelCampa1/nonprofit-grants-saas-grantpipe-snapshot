A nonprofit grants meeting should make grant work clearer. It should not become a long status round where everyone reads their task list. The best meetings focus on dates, risks, decisions, and handoffs.

Grant work touches development, finance, program, data, and leadership. A fixed agenda helps those teams use the same facts. It also keeps urgent deadlines from hiding behind less important updates.

Use this guide as a meeting agenda template. Adjust the timing to fit your grant load. The structure matters more than the exact minutes.

## Before the meeting

Send the agenda before the meeting. Include the current grant list, deadlines due before the next meeting, reports due in the next 60 days, proposals due in the next 30 days, and any decision needed.

Ask people to update their items before the meeting. The meeting should not be the first time the team opens the grant tracker.

Each agenda item should have an owner. If an item has no owner, assign one before discussing details.

GrantPipe can help by keeping the grant list, tasks, documents, notes, and report dates in one place. That lets the meeting focus on choices instead of file hunting.

## Agenda item 1: decisions from last meeting

Start with prior decisions. This should take five minutes.

Review what the team decided last time. Confirm whether each owner completed the next step. If not, decide whether the date moves, the owner changes, or the item escalates.

Do not shame people. Fix the work path. Missed decisions often mean the owner was unclear, the task was too broad, or the needed support was missing.

## Agenda item 2: deadlines due before the next meeting

Review deadlines before general updates. Deadlines create the meeting's order.

Include proposal due dates, report due dates, reimbursement requests, funder calls, board approvals, budget reviews, data locks, closeout dates, and renewal dates.

For each deadline, ask:

- Is the owner clear?
- What is missing?
- Is finance needed?
- Is program data needed?
- Is executive or board approval needed?
- Is the funder waiting on us?

The goal is to leave with a clear owner and next action for each near deadline.

## Agenda item 3: new opportunities and go or no-go choices

Review new grant opportunities quickly. Do not let every opportunity move forward because it sounds useful.

Ask:

- Does it fit the mission?
- Is the amount worth the work?
- Can the program deliver?
- Can finance support the budget and reporting?
- Does it require match?
- Does it create cash strain?
- Does it need executive or board review?

Use the [grant prospect research scorecard](/resources/guides/grant-prospect-research-scorecard-nonprofits) or a simple go or no-go checklist. End with a decision: pursue, pause, decline, or research more.

## Agenda item 4: proposals in progress

Review proposals due in the next 30 days. Focus on blockers.

For each proposal, confirm narrative owner, budget owner, program input, finance review, attachments, letters, approvals, and submission plan.

Finance should see the budget before final submission. Program should confirm that the narrative matches the work. Leadership should approve any large risk, match, or new commitment.

Use the [grant proposal budget review checklist](/resources/guides/grant-proposal-budget-review-checklist) before the final draft leaves the team.

## Agenda item 5: award notices and setup

When an award notice arrives, the meeting should not treat it as done. It should trigger setup.

Review award amount, period, payment method, restrictions, reporting dates, budget changes, match, grant code, document file, and kickoff needs.

If the award differs from the proposal, decide who will adjust scope, budget, and funder communication.

Use the [grant award acceptance checklist](/resources/guides/grant-award-acceptance-checklist) before signing or booking the award.

## Agenda item 6: active grant health

Review active grants by exception. Do not walk through every healthy grant in detail.

Focus on grants with cash issues, slow spending, overspending, missing support, late reports, staffing gaps, funder questions, or closeout risk.

For each issue, state the risk in one sentence. Then name owner, next date, and escalation path.

Use the [grant budget tracking guide](/resources/guides/grant-budget-tracking) and [grant compliance guide](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) for support.

## Agenda item 7: reports and reimbursement

Reports and reimbursement need a dedicated agenda block because they affect cash and funder trust.

Review reports due in the next 60 days. Confirm program data, finance schedules, narrative owner, review date, and submission date.

Review reimbursement requests that are pending, late, or blocked by missing support. If a report controls payment, mark it clearly.

Do not wait until the due date to discover that attendance data, receipts, or match support is missing.

## Agenda item 8: escalations

End the discussion by naming escalations. An escalation may go to an executive director, finance committee, board, auditor, legal advisor, accountant, or funder.

Examples include reserve use, high-risk award terms, budget amendments, late reports, suspected unallowable costs, conflicts of interest, or policy gaps.

Do not bury escalations in meeting notes. Put them in a decision log with owner and date.

## After the meeting

Update the grant records the same day. File decisions, update tasks, change dates, and move closed items out of the active agenda.

Send a short recap with only decisions, owners, dates, and escalations. Long recaps are less likely to be read.

The meeting is working when people leave knowing what changed, what is due next, and who owns each step.
