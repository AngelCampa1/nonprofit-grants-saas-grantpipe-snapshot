A grant expense approval workflow keeps a simple question from becoming an audit problem: should this cost be paid from this grant?

The answer should not depend on one person's memory. A reviewer needs the invoice, the grant terms, the approved budget, the service period, the account code, and the reason the cost benefits the grant. When those checks happen before payment, the team can stop errors early.

This guide is for nonprofit finance, grants, and program staff. It works for federal awards, foundation grants, restricted gifts, and reimbursable contracts. Use your award terms and accounting policy as the final rule.

## Start with the source record

Every review starts with the source record. That may be an invoice, receipt, purchase request, contract, employee reimbursement, payroll report, or card statement.

The reviewer should confirm the vendor, amount, date, service period, description, and requested payment account. If the source record is vague, ask for more detail before approval. A line that says "services" is weak support. A line that names the service, date range, program, and rate is much better.

Attach the source record to the grant or payment record. Do not rely on email search as the file system. If the cost matters, the support should be easy to find later.

GrantPipe can help teams keep expenses, grant budgets, restricted funds, approvals, and evidence near the grant record. The workflow still needs a human reviewer who understands the award.

## Check allowability first

Allowability means the cost fits the grant rules. For federal awards, 2 CFR 200.403 says costs must meet several standards, including being necessary, reasonable, allocable, consistent with policy, and properly documented. Many private funders use similar ideas, even when they do not cite that rule.

Ask these questions before budget review:

- Does the award allow this type of cost?
- Does our policy allow it?
- Is the cost needed for the approved work?
- Is the amount reasonable for the service or item?
- Does the cost benefit this grant?
- Does the cost need prior approval?

If any answer is unclear, pause the payment. A budget line does not make a cost allowable by itself.

## Check the grant budget

After allowability, compare the cost to the approved budget. Look at the budget category, remaining balance, spending pace, and any line item limits.

This check should happen before payment because it catches problems while the team can still choose a different funding source, ask for an amendment, or delay the purchase.

Do not approve a cost only because money is available. Budget room is one piece of the review. The cost still needs to be allowable, supported, and tied to the grant work.

## Check the period of performance

The service date matters more than the invoice date. A vendor may send an invoice in July for work done in June. The reviewer should know which grant period the work belongs to.

Check the start date, end date, closeout rules, and any restrictions on pre-award or post-award costs. If the cost falls near the end of an award, confirm whether the item was received and used during the grant period.

For reimbursement grants, this step also protects cash requests. A paid cost outside the grant period may be rejected later.

## Confirm benefit and allocation

Some expenses support more than one grant. Shared software, shared program staff, office space, supplies, and training may need an allocation.

Use an allocation basis that reflects benefit. Hours, units, square footage, user counts, or participant counts may work depending on the cost. Do not split a cost based only on which grant has room left.

When a cost is shared, the approval file should show the total cost, basis, calculation, grant codes, and reviewer approval. The [grant-funded program shared cost allocation guide](/resources/guides/grant-funded-program-shared-cost-allocation-guide) covers this in more depth.

## Review coding before payment

Grant expense approval should include coding review. The reviewer should check the grant code, fund code, department, natural account, restriction code, and reporting category.

Small coding errors can create large cleanup work. A cost posted to the wrong grant may appear in a report, drawdown, reimbursement request, board packet, or restricted fund balance. Fixing it later may require a journal entry and more review.

Make the approval form show the coding that will be posted. If the accounting system code differs from the funder report category, save the mapping.

## Separate requester and approver

The person requesting the purchase should not be the only approver. Program staff can confirm need and benefit. Finance or grants staff should confirm allowability, budget, period, support, and coding.

For larger costs, add a second approval. The threshold should be in policy. It may depend on amount, funder, cost type, or risk.

Approval evidence can be a system approval, signed form, dated email, or board action. It should show the approver, date, amount, grant, and decision.

## Build the workflow

A clean workflow has seven stages:

1. Request the purchase or payment.
2. Attach source support.
3. Confirm allowability and prior approval.
4. Check budget and period.
5. Confirm benefit, allocation, and coding.
6. Approve before payment.
7. File the approval with the payment record.

Use the same stages for card charges and reimbursements. Card charges often fail because approval happens after the fact. If staff must use cards, require a pre-approval record for grant charged purchases above a set threshold.

## Review exceptions monthly

Even a strong workflow will have exceptions. Track missing support, late approvals, miscoded costs, over budget lines, and costs that needed funder approval.

Review those exceptions each month. The goal is not blame. The goal is to fix the rule, form, training, or system setup that allowed the same error to repeat.

## Approval checklist

Before paying a grant expense, confirm:

- the cost is allowed by the award and policy
- the cost is reasonable for the work
- the cost benefits the grant
- the service date fits the grant period
- the budget line has room
- source support is attached
- coding is correct
- allocation support is attached when needed
- prior approval is saved when required
- approval is dated and tied to the payment

If the file can answer those points, the workflow is doing its job.
