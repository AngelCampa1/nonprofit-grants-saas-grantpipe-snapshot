Maryland nonprofit formation has more moving parts than most Mid-Atlantic states. Two distinct state agencies - SDAT for the corporate filing, the Secretary of State for the charitable registration - combine with the federal IRS process and the Comptroller's sales tax exemption.

This guide walks the formation flow end-to-end so the order of operations is clear.

## Step 1: Choose the Entity Type

Almost every Maryland nonprofit incorporates as a **nonstock corporation** under the Maryland Corporations and Associations Article §5-201 et seq. The structure provides legal personhood, limits director and officer liability, and is the standard the IRS expects on Form 1023.

## Step 2: Reserve and Confirm the Name

Search Maryland Business Express at businessexpress.maryland.gov for name availability. Maryland requires the corporate name to be distinguishable from existing entities. Reserve a name for 30 days for $25 if you need time before filing.

## Step 3: File Articles of Incorporation with SDAT

The Articles of Incorporation are the foundational filing. Required content:

- Corporate name
- Purpose statement - for 501(c)(3) eligibility, use IRS sample purpose language
- Whether the corporation will have members
- Names and addresses of the initial directors (minimum of three)
- Resident agent name and Maryland street address
- Dissolution clause distributing assets to another 501(c)(3) or to government on dissolution
- Incorporator signature

Standard fee is $100 plus a $20 expedited processing assessment for online filings. Expedited service ($50 additional) returns the filing in 1-2 business days.

### Required IRS Language

The IRS will reject Form 1023 if the purpose and dissolution clauses don't meet 501(c)(3) requirements. Use IRS sample language verbatim.

## Step 4: Get an EIN

Apply at IRS.gov for an Employer Identification Number. Online applications issue immediately for entities with a US-based responsible party. There is no fee.

## Step 5: Adopt Bylaws and Hold the Organizational Meeting

Bylaws are the internal rulebook. Maryland does not file them with SDAT, but the IRS requires them on Form 1023.

Cover at minimum:

- Board structure, size, term limits, and election procedures
- Officer roles and appointment
- Meeting frequency, quorum, and notice requirements
- Conflict of interest policy and procedure
- Indemnification provisions
- Amendment procedure
- Fiscal year and dissolution clause

Hold the **organizational meeting** to adopt bylaws, elect officers, authorize the EIN application, authorize the bank account, and authorize the IRS Form 1023 filing.

## Step 6: File for Federal 501(c)(3) Status

Two forms exist:

- **Form 1023-EZ** - short-form online application, $275 fee, 4-6 weeks. Eligibility caps at projected gross receipts of $50,000 or less for each of the next three years and total assets of $250,000 or less.
- **Form 1023** - full application, $600 fee, 6-12 months processing.

File at pay.gov. The IRS issues a determination letter on approval.

For the full walkthrough, see the [501(c)(3) application step-by-step guide](/resources/guides/501c3-application-step-by-step-guide).

## Step 7: Register with the Maryland Secretary of State Before Soliciting

Maryland's Solicitations Act (Md. Code Bus. Reg. §6-101 et seq.) requires registration with the Secretary of State's Charitable Organizations Division on **Form COR-92** before soliciting in the state. Required attachments:

- Articles of Incorporation (file-stamped copy)
- Bylaws
- IRS determination letter or pending Form 1023
- IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N (most recent), or first-year budget
- List of officers, directors, and key employees with addresses
- CPA review for $300,000-$1,000,000 in contributions; full audit above $1,000,000
- Filing fee scaled to charitable contributions (see fee schedule)

Processing takes 30-60 days. The full mechanics - including renewal, audit thresholds, and edge cases - are in the [Maryland charitable registration workflow](/workflows/maryland-charitable-registration-workflow).

### When Registration is Required

Solicitation triggers registration. The trigger is the _ask_ - a donation page accessible to Maryland residents counts.

### Exemptions

Md. Code Bus. Reg. §6-103 exempts religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, hospitals, and certain other categories. Most operating charities are not exempt and must register.

## Step 8: Apply for Maryland Sales Tax Exemption

Federal 501(c)(3) status does not exempt the organization from Maryland sales and use tax. Apply through the Comptroller of Maryland's nonprofit exemption process. Approval takes 30-90 days. The exemption certificate must be presented to vendors at point of sale.

## Step 9: Open a Bank Account

Maryland banks require:

- IRS-issued EIN letter
- Articles of Incorporation
- Bylaws
- Board resolution authorizing the account and listing signers
- Photo ID for each signer

Some banks delay opening until the IRS determination letter arrives.

## Step 10: Calendar the Recurring Filings

Once formed, Maryland nonprofits maintain four independent compliance calendars:

- **SDAT annual report** (Personal Property Return) - due April 15 each year. No fee for nonprofits without taxable personal property.
- **SOS Form COR-92 renewal** - annual, due 6 months after fiscal year end.
- **IRS Form 990** (or 990-EZ, 990-N) - due 4½ months after fiscal year end.
- **Sales tax exemption renewal** - every 5 years.

Build all deadlines into a single compliance calendar with 90, 60, and 30-day reminders.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Maryland Nonprofits Down

**Soliciting before COR-92 is on file.** Maryland's Solicitations Act is enforced. Don't launch the donation page before registration.

**Wrong purpose language in the Articles.** The IRS will reject Form 1023 if the purpose clause is too broad or the dissolution clause is missing. Use IRS sample language.

**Skipping the SDAT annual report.** The Personal Property Return is mandatory and easy to miss because most nonprofits owe nothing on it. Two consecutive misses lead to forfeiture of corporate charter.

**Crossing the $300,000 review threshold without engaging a CPA.** The COR-92 financial attachment requirement applies the year contributions cross the line. Engage the CPA before fiscal year end.
