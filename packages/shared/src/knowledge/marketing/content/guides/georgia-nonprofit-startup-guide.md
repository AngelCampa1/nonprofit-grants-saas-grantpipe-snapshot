## Why Georgia Is Workable for First-Time Founders

Georgia runs nonprofit formation through two divisions of the Secretary of State: the Corporations Division for incorporation and corporate annual filings, and the Charities Division for charitable solicitation registration. Both run online portals. Both publish their rules in plain language. The Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code (O.C.G.A. Title 14 Chapter 3) is readable. The Charitable Solicitations Act (O.C.G.A. Title 43 Chapter 17) is readable.

The complexity is in two places. First, Georgia's biennial registration cycle for charitable solicitation is unusual - most states are annual. The renewal date is set when the certificate is issued, which means it does not align with the federal Form 990 calendar. Second, Georgia's paid-solicitor and fundraising-counsel rules are stricter than many states; misclassifying a fundraising vendor exposes the charity.

This guide walks the seven concrete formation steps in order. It assumes a founding team willing to read instructions carefully and file on time.

## Founder Voice Disclosure

I write this from the perspective of someone building software for nonprofit operations, not from years of hands-on nonprofit-sector experience. The benefit is that the rules are recent in my own learning and I can describe them without assuming reader familiarity. The cost is that nothing here substitutes for advice from a Georgia nonprofit attorney or a CPA when stakes are high. Spend money on professional review when the organization will hold meaningful assets, hire staff in the first year, or solicit beyond a small founding circle.

## Step 1: Verify Name Availability

Georgia uses a distinguishability standard. Search the Secretary of State business name database. Names that are confusingly similar to existing entities will be rejected. Names that include restricted words (bank, insurance, trust, certain professional terms) require additional review.

If you are not ready to file articles immediately, reserve the name to lock it in. Do not commit to domain names, signage, or stationery until the reservation or the articles are accepted.

## Step 2: File Articles of Incorporation

Georgia's standard nonprofit articles form is the path of least resistance. The articles must include:

- The exact corporate name
- Charitable purpose language tracking IRC 501(c)(3) - typically "organized exclusively for charitable, educational, religious, scientific" purposes plus the limitation on private benefit and the prohibition on political campaign activity
- The registered agent and a Georgia street address
- A dissolution clause directing assets on wind-down to another 501(c)(3) or to a government for a public purpose
- The names and addresses of the incorporators

The dissolution clause is non-negotiable for IRS recognition. Get the language right the first time.

Confirm Georgia's current publication requirement before filing. Historically, Georgia required notice of intent to incorporate to be published in a newspaper of general circulation in the county of the registered office. Rules change; check the current Secretary of State guidance.

## Step 3: Adopt Bylaws and Seat the Board

Bylaws govern day-to-day operations. Cover at minimum:

- Board size, term length, succession
- Officer roles and duties (president, vice president, secretary, treasurer)
- Meeting cadence and quorum
- Conflict-of-interest policy
- Amendment procedures
- Indemnification within Georgia law
- Membership structure (member-governed or director-governed)

Georgia requires at least one director by statute. The IRS expects an independent, unrelated majority for 501(c)(3) recognition; plan for at least three unrelated directors before filing Form 1023.

Hold an organizing meeting. Adopt the bylaws. Elect officers. Approve the banking resolution. Document everything in minutes.

## Step 4: Obtain an Employer Identification Number

File Form SS-4 online through the IRS website. The EIN is free and immediate. Use it on the bank account, Form 1023, and the Charities Division Form C-100. The EIN belongs to the corporation, not the founder.

## Step 5: File IRS Form 1023 or 1023-EZ

Form 1023-EZ is available to small organizations meeting the eligibility worksheet - generally projected gross receipts under $50,000 annually for the first three years and assets under $250,000. If eligible, 1023-EZ processes in 2-4 weeks at $275.

Long-form Form 1023 is 30+ pages with attachments. Plan 3-9 months and a $600 user fee. The form requires:

- A detailed narrative of activities
- Three-year budget projections
- Compensation arrangements
- Conflict-of-interest policy attached
- Articles, bylaws, and amendments attached
- Specific schedules for unusual activities

Read the IRS instructions cover to cover before drafting. The instructions tell you what the reviewer is looking for.

## Step 6: File Form C-100 With the Charities Division

The Charities Division registration is the step founders most often miss. Solicitation in Georgia - including online giving pages reachable from Georgia residents - requires registration before fundraising begins.

Form C-100 requires:

- IRS determination letter (or evidence of pending application)
- Articles of incorporation
- Bylaws
- Most recent Form 990 if applicable
- Audited or reviewed financial statements if revenue tiers require them
- List of officers and directors
- Description of programs and geographic scope
- Federal EIN
- Any contracts with paid solicitors or fundraising counsel covering Georgia activity

Registration is valid for 24 months. Calendar the renewal the moment the certificate arrives.

## Step 7: Build the First-Year Compliance Calendar

Cover at minimum:

- IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N (due 4 months 15 days after fiscal year end)
- Georgia biennial Form C-100 renewal
- Georgia Secretary of State corporate annual registration
- Georgia Department of Revenue filings if applicable
- Any county-level business filings
- Out-of-state charitable registrations triggered by national solicitation

Assign a named owner to each deadline. Review the calendar quarterly. The first year sets the cadence for everything that follows.

## What to Do Next

If you are pre-incorporation, start with Step 1 and work in order. Do not skip the publication check or the dissolution-clause language; both come up later in unhappy ways.

If you are post-incorporation, focus on the Form 1023 filing. Read the IRS instructions in full, draft the narrative carefully, and have a second person review it before submission.

If you have a determination letter and you are about to launch fundraising, Form C-100 is the next step. Do not delay it until "after the campaign launches" - it is a prerequisite to the campaign.

Georgia rewards careful sequencing. Done in order, formation is a 3-9 month project. Done out of order, every step has to be redone.
