## What state grants are available to Michigan nonprofits, and how does the funding cycle work?

Michigan nonprofits draw from five funding channels that operate on overlapping but distinct calendars: state agency competitive grants funded by the legislature, federal pass-throughs administered through state departments under 2 CFR 200, private and community foundations headquartered in Michigan, tribal and regional funders where applicable, and occasional capital appropriations routed through state facilities or infrastructure agencies.

The state funding cycle begins each year with the governor's budget proposal, moves through legislative appropriation during the session, and triggers Notices of Funding Availability (NOFAs) from individual agencies - typically clustered in the spring for awards effective at the start of the October 1-September 30 fiscal year. Federal pass-throughs follow the federal fiscal year (October 1-September 30), which means most Michigan nonprofits manage two parallel grant calendars at once.

This guide maps the Michigan state grant funder landscape: the major grantmaking agencies, federal pass-through context, top private and community foundations by assets, and tribal funders where relevant. ## Major Michigan state grantmaking agencies

### Michigan Economic Development Corporation (MEDC)

**Programs:** Michigan Strategic Fund grants, Community Development Block Grant, Revitalization and Placemaking.

**Typical award size:** $100,000-$5,000,000.

**Cycle:** Rolling and annual; state FY October 1-September 30.

**Eligibility:** Nonprofits, CDCs, local governments.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.michiganbusiness.org](https://www.michiganbusiness.org)

### Michigan Arts and Culture Council (MACC)

**Programs:** Operational Support, Project Support, Capital Improvement, Services to the Field.

**Typical award size:** $1,500-$40,000.

**Cycle:** Annual; deadlines typically April-May.

**Eligibility:** 501(c)(3) arts organizations.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.michigan.gov/macc](https://www.michigan.gov/macc)

### Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS)

**Programs:** Community Services Block Grant, Strong Families/Safe Children, Maternal Infant Health Program subgrants.

**Typical award size:** $100,000-$3,000,000.

**Cycle:** State FY; RFPs typically May-July.

**Eligibility:** Health and human services nonprofits.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs](https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs)

### Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA)

**Programs:** HOME, ESG, CDBG-DR, Housing Trust Fund, MI Homeowner Assistance Fund.

**Typical award size:** $200,000-$10,000,000.

**Cycle:** Annual NOFA cycles.

**Eligibility:** Affordable housing nonprofits and developers.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.michigan.gov/mshda](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda)

### Michigan Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity (LEO)

**Programs:** Going PRO Talent Fund, workforce development passthroughs, adult education.

**Typical award size:** $50,000-$1,500,000.

**Cycle:** Annual; state FY.

**Eligibility:** Workforce nonprofits, training providers.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.michigan.gov/leo](https://www.michigan.gov/leo)

## Federal pass-through context

Michigan state agencies receive substantial federal funding that is then subgranted to nonprofits as pass-throughs. The major federal funding streams routed through Michigan include:

- **CDBG (HUD Community Development Block Grant)** - administered through Michigan Economic Development Corporation for non-entitlement areas. Subject to 2 CFR 200 Uniform Guidance and HUD-specific compliance.
- **HOME Investment Partnerships (HUD)** - affordable housing development and rehabilitation, administered through the state housing finance or community affairs agency.
- **Emergency Solutions Grants and Continuum of Care (HUD)** - homeless services. Subject to ESG and CoC regulations on top of 2 CFR 200.
- **Community Services Block Grant (HHS)** - anti-poverty programming through Community Action Agencies and partner nonprofits.
- **VOCA Victim Assistance and VAWA STOP grants (DOJ)** - victim services, administered through the state's criminal justice or attorney general's office.
- **Title V Maternal and Child Health Block Grant (HHS HRSA)** - public health pass-through through Michigan's health department.
- **USDA Rural Development programs** - rural-area infrastructure, housing, and community facilities funded through state offices and Cooperative Extension partners.

Every federal pass-through carries the original federal compliance terms: Single Audit threshold ($1,000,000 in federal expenditures triggers a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later), procurement standards, conflict-of-interest rules, and time-and-effort documentation. State agencies typically add additional state-specific reporting on top.

For nonprofits managing pass-through dollars, the [grant compliance 101 guide](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) covers the underlying Uniform Guidance framework that applies regardless of which state agency holds the contract.

## Top Michigan-headquartered private and community foundations

The largest private and community foundations headquartered in Michigan, by approximate assets:

- **Charles Stewart Mott Foundation** - Flint, national and Michigan focus.
- **W.K. Kellogg Foundation** - Battle Creek, national.
- **The Kresge Foundation** - Troy, national with Detroit focus.
- **Community Foundation for Southeast Michigan** - Detroit metro community foundation.
- **Grand Rapids Community Foundation** - west Michigan community foundation.

These are drawn from publicly available IRS Form 990-PF filings and aggregated foundation data. Asset levels and giving patterns shift year over year - verify current figures against the foundation's most recent 990-PF before treating any number as authoritative. The top five typically represent a meaningful share of Michigan-originated philanthropic capital, but smaller family and corporate foundations also fund mission-aligned work in specific regions or program areas.

## Tribal and regional funders

Twelve federally recognized tribes in Michigan, including Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa, Saginaw Chippewa, and Bay Mills Indian Community. Tribal-led nonprofits access HHS ANA SEDS, HUD ICDBG, and BIA programs.

Where tribal-led programs apply, the major federal funding streams include HHS Administration for Native Americans (ANA) Social and Economic Development Strategies (SEDS) grants, HUD Indian Community Development Block Grant (ICDBG), and BIA Tribal Government Services. State-level tribal partnerships vary by state.

## How the calendar works

The recurring deadlines table in this guide's frontmatter shows the major Michigan state grant submission windows. Three calendar realities shape how Michigan nonprofits plan:

1. **Two parallel fiscal years.** State-funded programs follow October 1-September 30; federal pass-throughs follow October 1-September 30. Renewal and reporting deadlines collide.
2. **Spring-heavy NOFA cycle.** Most state agency RFAs and NOFAs publish March through May for awards effective at the start of the next state fiscal year. A development director who is not pipeline-ready by February misses the cluster.
3. **Federal pass-throughs lag federal allocation.** State agencies cannot release a CDBG or HOME NOFA until HUD finalizes the state's allocation, which can push pass-through cycles later than state-funded equivalents.

## What this means for grant management practice

A Michigan nonprofit running a representative grant mix - one CDBG pass-through, one state arts council award, two foundation grants from in-state community foundations, and a federal direct grant - manages four reporting cadences, three audit perspectives (state, federal, foundation), and two fiscal year calendars. The compliance overhead is real and grows non-linearly with each new restricted fund.

Three practical implications:

- **Build the calendar before the funder list.** Mapping deadlines and report cycles is the work that catches the slips. A pipeline without a calendar is a wishlist.
- **Track restricted balances per grant, per fund, per FY.** GAAP-aligned restricted fund accounting under FASB ASC 958 is non-negotiable once federal expenditures cross $1,000,000 for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later and a Single Audit is triggered.
- **Document the audit trail before you need it.** Time and effort, procurement, conflict-of-interest, and subaward monitoring documentation must exist contemporaneously, not be reconstructed at audit time.
