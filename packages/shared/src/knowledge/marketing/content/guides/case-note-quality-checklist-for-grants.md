# Case note quality checklist for grants

Case notes can explain what happened in a program. They can also create problems when they are vague, late, or too private for a grant file.

Good case notes help staff serve people. Grant reports need a narrower use. They need records that support a required measure, show service delivery, or explain an outcome without exposing more participant detail than needed.

Use this checklist before relying on case notes for a grant report, renewal request, or compliance review.

## Check the date

Each note should show when the service happened and when the note was written. These dates answer different questions.

The service date shows whether the activity belongs in the report period. The entry date shows whether the record was timely. A note entered three months later may still be useful, but reviewers may ask why it was late.

Check for:

- Service date
- Entry date
- Report period match
- Staff name or role
- Program or grant link
- Correct participant or household record

If the note date is outside the report period, do not force it into the count. Save it for the correct period or explain the timing if the funder asks.

## Check the service link

The note should connect to the funded activity. A funder may pay for coaching, training, case management, or follow up. The note should show which service occurred.

Weak note: "Met with client. Went well."

Better note: "Held 45 minute job coaching session. Reviewed resume draft and set next interview practice date."

The better note is still simple, but it tells a reviewer what happened. It also avoids broad claims.

## Check for facts before opinions

Case notes should avoid labels and guesses. A grant report should not turn staff opinion into evidence.

Use facts:

- What service was provided
- What the participant chose to share
- What next step was agreed
- What document or referral was completed
- What outcome was observed or measured

Avoid unsupported claims:

- "Participant is not motivated."
- "Family is doing great."
- "Client will succeed."

If a judgment matters for service, write the basis. If it does not support the report, leave it out of the grant evidence summary.

## Check privacy before sharing

Raw case notes may include sensitive details. A grant file may not need those details.

Before copying a note into a report packet, ask:

- Does the funder require this detail?
- Can a summary support the same point?
- Is the participant identifiable?
- Does the note include health, legal, school, housing, or family details?
- Who can access the grant file?
- Does policy allow this use?

Use the minimum detail needed. If the report needs a story, remove names and details that identify the person unless you have clear permission.

## Check outcome support

A case note may show progress, but it may not prove an outcome. If the report claims an outcome, look for a clear source.

For example, a note can support: "Staff helped the participant submit a job application."

It may not support: "The participant gained stable employment" unless the note or another record confirms the job and date.

Pair case notes with stronger records when available:

- Assessment results
- Survey data
- Attendance logs
- Partner confirmations
- Case status changes
- Follow up records

## Check consistency

If several staff write notes, the team needs shared rules. Otherwise, one person may write detailed notes and another may write only short reminders. That makes reporting uneven.

Create a short case note standard:

- Required fields
- Timing rule
- Service types
- Outcome terms
- Privacy limits
- Review owner
- Correction process

The standard should be easy to use. A long policy that staff ignore will not improve data.

## Save the review evidence

When case notes support a grant report, save the review trail.

Save:

- Report measure or narrative claim
- Case note source location
- Summary used in the report
- Reviewer name
- Review date
- Privacy check
- Final approved wording

Do not export all notes into the grant folder just because one sentence supports the report. Point to the approved system and save the summary.

## Use a final checklist

Before submission, confirm:

- The note belongs to the report period.
- The service matches the grant activity.
- The wording is factual.
- Private details are limited.
- The outcome claim has support.
- The program owner approved the meaning.
- The final report does not quote more than needed.

Case notes are useful when they are clear and careful. They should help a reviewer understand the work, not expose a participant or stretch a claim.
