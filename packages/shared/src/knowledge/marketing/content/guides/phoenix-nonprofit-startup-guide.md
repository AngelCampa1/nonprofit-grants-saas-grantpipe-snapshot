## Why Phoenix for a Nonprofit

Phoenix sits in one of the most business-friendly regulatory environments in the country for starting a nonprofit. Arizona charges $10 for online incorporation - compared to $75 to $200+ in states like California, New York, and Illinois. The state does not require mandatory charitable solicitation registration for most nonprofits, eliminating an entire compliance layer that organizations in other states must maintain. And the annual reporting requirement is simply filing a copy of your IRS Form 990 with the state.

None of this means starting a nonprofit in Phoenix is effortless. You still need to incorporate properly, obtain IRS tax-exempt status, register with the Attorney General, and secure local tax exemptions. But the process here has fewer steps, lower costs, and less bureaucratic friction than in most major metro areas.

## Step 1: Choose a Name and Check Availability

Search the Arizona Corporation Commission's online database to confirm your desired name is available. Arizona requires that your name include a corporate designator - "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Association," "Company," or their abbreviations. The name cannot be deceptively similar to an existing Arizona entity.

Pick a name that clearly communicates your mission. Foundation program officers, government agencies, and donors form first impressions from your organizational name. Avoid names that could be confused with government agencies or existing well-known nonprofits.

## Step 2: Recruit Your Board of Directors

Arizona requires a minimum of three directors for a nonprofit corporation. Directors do not need to be Arizona residents, though having local board members matters for community credibility and access to Phoenix-area funders.

Your initial board should include people with complementary skills - financial management, legal knowledge, programmatic expertise, and community connections. Avoid filling the board entirely with friends and family; IRS reviewers scrutinize boards that lack independence when evaluating your [501(c)(3) application](/resources/guides/501c3-application-step-by-step-guide).

## Step 3: File Articles of Incorporation

File your Articles of Incorporation with the Arizona Corporation Commission online. The filing fee is $10 - one of the lowest in the country. Your articles must include the required IRS language for 501(c)(3) organizations: a statement of exempt purpose, a prohibition on private benefit, and a dissolution clause directing assets to another 501(c)(3) upon dissolution.

Get the IRS language right in your articles. If your articles lack the required purpose and dissolution clauses, the IRS will reject your 501(c)(3) application and you will need to file amended articles with the ACC before resubmitting. The [Arizona nonprofit startup guide](/resources/guides/arizona-nonprofit-startup-guide) covers the specific article language in detail.

## Step 4: Adopt Bylaws and Hold Organizational Meeting

Your board must adopt bylaws governing how the organization operates - board meeting frequency, officer roles, quorum requirements, fiscal year, conflict-of-interest policy, and amendment procedures. Arizona law provides flexibility in bylaw structure, but your bylaws must be consistent with your articles and with Arizona's nonprofit corporation statute (Title 10, Chapter 24).

Hold an organizational meeting to adopt bylaws, elect officers, authorize a bank account, and record these actions in formal minutes. The IRS requires bylaws and organizational minutes as part of the 501(c)(3) application.

## Step 5: Obtain an EIN

Apply for an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS online at no cost. The EIN is your organization's federal tax ID and is required for opening a bank account, filing tax returns, and applying for 501(c)(3) status. Online applications receive the EIN immediately.

## Step 6: Apply for IRS 501(c)(3) Status

File Form 1023 (full application, $600 filing fee) or Form 1023-EZ (streamlined application, $275 filing fee) with the IRS. Form 1023-EZ is available to organizations expecting annual gross receipts of $50,000 or less and total assets of $250,000 or less.

The full Form 1023 requires a detailed narrative describing your programs, a two-year projected budget, financial statements if you have been operating, board member information, and copies of your articles and bylaws. Processing typically takes three to six months, though times vary.

You can begin operations while the application is pending. If you file within 27 months of your formation date, the IRS determination letter is effective retroactively to your date of incorporation. Donations made during the pending period are tax-deductible for donors once the determination letter is issued.

A thorough walkthrough of the full application is available in the [501(c)(3) application guide](/resources/guides/501c3-application-step-by-step-guide).

## Step 7: Register with the Arizona Attorney General

Arizona does not require mandatory charitable solicitation registration for most nonprofits. However, you should register with the Arizona Attorney General's Charitable Organizations office voluntarily. Registration is free and establishes your organization's standing with the AG's office.

If you use paid professional fundraisers or fundraising counsel, registration with the AG becomes mandatory. And if you solicit donations in other states - including through a website accessible nationwide - you must comply with those states' registration requirements separately.

This is a meaningful advantage of operating from Phoenix. Organizations in states like California, New York, and Pennsylvania face registration fees, annual renewal filings, and state-specific financial reporting requirements that Arizona simply does not impose. But do not confuse Arizona's light-touch approach with no obligations - federal reporting (Form 990) and IRS compliance apply everywhere.

## Step 8: Apply for Transaction Privilege Tax Exemption

Arizona's Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT) is the state's version of sales tax. Once you receive your IRS determination letter, apply to the Arizona Department of Revenue for TPT exemption. This exempts your organization from most state and city sales tax obligations on purchases made for charitable purposes.

The TPT exemption application requires a copy of your IRS determination letter, your articles of incorporation, and a description of your activities. Processing typically takes four to six weeks. Until the exemption is approved, you will pay TPT on taxable purchases.

## Step 9: Maricopa County Property Tax Exemption

If your nonprofit owns or leases real property in Maricopa County, apply to the Maricopa County Assessor for property tax exemption. The exemption covers property used exclusively for charitable, educational, or religious purposes.

Applications must be filed before June 1 for the following tax year. You must reapply annually. The Assessor's office may conduct site visits to verify that the property is used exclusively for exempt purposes - property used partly for unrelated business activities may not qualify for full exemption.

## Timeline and Cost Summary

Here is a realistic timeline for the full Phoenix nonprofit startup process:

**Week 1-2**: Choose name, recruit initial board, draft articles and bylaws. Cost: $0 (plus legal fees if using an attorney).

**Week 2-3**: File Articles of Incorporation ($10), hold organizational meeting, obtain EIN (free). Cost: $10.

**Week 3-6**: Prepare and file Form 1023 ($600) or Form 1023-EZ ($275). Cost: $275-$600.

**Months 2-8**: IRS processing period. Begin operations, register with AZ AG (free), open bank account.

**Upon IRS determination**: Apply for TPT exemption (free), apply for property tax exemption if applicable (free).

**Total hard costs**: $285 to $610, depending on which IRS form you use. This is substantially less than the $500 to $1,500+ total startup cost in states like California or New York.

## What to Do After Formation

Receiving your IRS determination letter is the beginning, not the end. Phoenix nonprofits need to establish accounting systems that properly track restricted and unrestricted funds from the start. The [nonprofit accounting guide](/resources/best/best-nonprofit-accounting-software-phoenix) covers software options appropriate for Phoenix organizations at various stages of growth.

File your annual IRS Form 990 (or 990-EZ or 990-N) and send a copy to the Arizona Corporation Commission. Maintain your corporate records - minutes, bylaws amendments, financial statements - in an organized system. These records will be required when you apply for your first foundation grant, government contract, or [grant compliance review](/free/grant-compliance-checklist).

If you plan to solicit donations beyond Arizona, research [state charitable registration requirements](/resources/faq/faq-phoenix-nonprofit-registration) for each state where you will fundraise. Arizona's lack of a registration requirement is an exception, not the rule - most states require registration before you solicit their residents.

## Arizona's Regulatory Advantage

Phoenix nonprofits operate in a regulatory environment that is genuinely lighter than most major cities. No mandatory charitable solicitation registration. No state annual report (just a 990 copy). A $10 incorporation fee. No franchise tax. These savings are real and compound over years of operation.

But regulatory simplicity is not a substitute for organizational discipline. The IRS, your funders, and your donors expect the same governance standards regardless of what state you operate in - board independence, financial controls, program accountability, and transparent reporting. Arizona just does not charge you extra fees for the privilege of meeting those standards.
