Fluxx, Submittable, and Foundant are grant administration platforms - but they are built for the foundation, not the nonprofit. They manage the grantmaking process from the funder's side: application intake, reviewer assignment, award decisions, grantee reporting portals.

If your organization receives grants rather than awards them, you need software designed for the other side of that relationship: the compliance, documentation, and reporting obligations that begin the day the award letter arrives.

### Grant Administration vs. Grant Discovery: Two Different Software Categories

The market for "grant software" contains two fundamentally different product categories that serve different needs at different points in the grant lifecycle.

**Grant discovery software** helps organizations find grant opportunities and track applications before awards are made. Platforms in this category: Instrumentl, GrantStation, Candid GrantSpace. The core features are grant database search, saved searches and deadline alerts, and application pipeline tracking. This software addresses the question: what should we apply for?

**Grant administration software** manages everything that happens after an award is made. Core features: award record management, budget tracking against approved line items, expenditure documentation, compliance deadline calendar, report submission tracking, document storage, and closeout procedures. This software addresses the question: are we managing this award in compliance with its requirements?

Organizations that confuse the two categories often end up with grant discovery software managing their post-award compliance - typically in a spreadsheet tagged onto the subscription - or with grant administration software that lacks the funder database they actually need.

A mid-sized nonprofit with 8 active grants at any time and a grant calendar that includes 3-5 new applications per year likely needs both, but the administration platform is the more compliance-critical purchase because the consequences of failure are audit findings and disallowed costs, not missed application deadlines.

### What Grant Administration Software Manages Post-Award

Post-award grant administration involves four categories of work that software can systematize:

**Budget tracking:** Monitoring actual expenditures against each grant's approved budget by line item, flagging variances before they create compliance problems, and generating the expenditure reports that go to funders. Without software, this is typically done by printing reports from QuickBooks and manually comparing them to the grant budget spreadsheet - an error-prone process that breaks down when an organization manages more than three or four simultaneous awards.

**Compliance documentation:** Storing and organizing the documentation required to support every expenditure: timesheets allocating personnel hours to specific grants, vendor invoices referencing approved budget categories, purchase order approvals, matching fund documentation. Federal awards under 2 CFR 200 require documentation retained for 3 years after the final expenditure report. Software that organizes documents by grant and expense creates the audit file automatically rather than retroactively.

**Reporting deadlines:** Managing the calendar of financial and programmatic report due dates across all active awards. Most organizations with 5 or more active grants have at least one report due every 30 days. A compliance calendar that exists only in a shared spreadsheet fails when the staff member who maintains it is unavailable.

**Closeout procedures:** Federal grants must be closed out within 120 days of the period of performance end under 2 CFR 200.344. Closeout requires final financial reports, final programmatic reports, property disposition reports if applicable, and return of any unspent federal funds within the same 120-day window. Software-based closeout checklists prevent the late closeout findings that appear in Single Audits.

### The Five Modules in a Complete Grant Administration Platform

A complete grant administration platform for a grant-receiving organization contains five functional modules:

**1. Award registry:** A database of all active and historical grant awards with key fields: funder name, award number, CFDA number (for federal grants), award amount, period of performance start and end, budget categories, matching requirement, reporting schedule, and responsible staff. The award registry is the source of truth for all downstream compliance activities.

**2. Budget management:** Award-level budget tracking that shows approved amounts, encumbrances, actual expenditures, and remaining balance by line item. Integration with the accounting system (QuickBooks, Sage Intacct, or the organization's ERP) eliminates the dual-entry that creates reconciliation errors. Budget variance alerts trigger before cumulative reallocations cross the funder's modification approval threshold.

**3. Document management:** Organized digital file storage for every document in the grant file - award letter, grant agreement, approved budget, all report submissions, all funder correspondence, all expenditure documentation. Document management that organizes by grant and expense type creates an audit-ready file without the manual filing that most organizations defer until a finding forces them to catch up.

**4. Compliance calendar:** All reporting deadlines, budget modification windows, drawdown schedules, and closeout deadlines for all active awards in a unified view, with responsible staff assigned, automated reminders, and escalation when deadlines are approaching without assigned completion status.

**5. Reporting:** Report generation for funder submissions and internal management - expenditure summaries by grant, budget variance reports, compliance status dashboards, and the consolidated multi-grant financial summaries that finance directors and boards need to understand overall restricted fund positions.

### Integration Requirements: Accounting, CRM, Document Storage

The value of grant administration software depends heavily on how well it integrates with the systems that hold the data it needs.

**Accounting integration:** The grant administration platform needs expenditure data from the accounting system in near-real time. Platforms that integrate with QuickBooks Online, QuickBooks Desktop, Sage Intacct, or MIP Fund Accounting via API eliminate the export-import cycle that creates reconciliation errors. Platforms that require manual data entry from accounting reports are replacing one spreadsheet problem with a more expensive one.

**Donor CRM:** For organizations that manage individual donors alongside grants, grant administration data - active awards, fund restrictions, expenditure positions - needs to be accessible to development staff tracking donor intent and gift designation. Platforms that integrate with Bloomerang, Salesforce Nonprofit, or similar CRMs prevent the information siloing that causes donor stewardship errors when a restricted gift creates compliance obligations development staff don't know exist.

**Document storage:** Integration with Google Drive, SharePoint, or Box for document management is preferable to a proprietary document storage module in most cases - organizations already have file organization conventions and staff familiarity with existing tools.

### How to Evaluate Grant Administration Software

The evaluation process for grant administration software should focus on five criteria:

1. **Accounting integration depth:** Does it sync with your accounting system bidirectionally, or does it require manual exports? Who does the reconciliation when numbers differ?
2. **Federal compliance support:** Does the platform understand 2 CFR 200 cost allowability categories, MTDC indirect cost calculation, and Single Audit thresholds? Or is it designed for foundation grant management only?
3. **Reporting flexibility:** Can it generate the specific report formats your funders require, or only its own standard formats? Federal program officers require specific formats (SF-425 Federal Financial Report, for example) - can the platform produce them?
4. **Multi-award view:** Can staff see compliance status, expenditure pace, and approaching deadlines across all active awards simultaneously, or only one award at a time?
5. **Implementation support:** What does the vendor provide for data migration, staff training, and configuration? A platform that requires IT staff to configure is not appropriate for a 20-person nonprofit without dedicated IT.

### The Build-vs-Buy Question for Organizations With IT Staff

Organizations with dedicated IT staff occasionally evaluate building internal grant administration systems on platforms like Airtable, Notion, or custom databases.

The build option is viable for organizations with: fewer than 5 active grants, no federal awards requiring 2 CFR 200 compliance, and IT staff who can maintain the system when personnel changes. It fails for organizations that add federal awards - the compliance specificity of 2 CFR 200 (MTDC calculation, subrecipient monitoring requirements, indirect cost rate application) is not something a generic database handles without custom development.

The buy option is appropriate when: the organization has more than 5 active grants, any awards are federal or have matching requirements, reporting schedules create deadline management complexity, or finance staff are spending more than 4 hours per week on grant reconciliation that software could automate.

The break-even calculation is straightforward: multiply the hourly cost of finance staff time spent on manual grant reconciliation and reporting by annual hours. If that number exceeds $6,000-$10,000, software at $300-$800/month pays for itself within the first year.
