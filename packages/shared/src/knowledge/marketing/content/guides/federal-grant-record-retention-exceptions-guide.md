Record retention is easy to understate. Teams often remember the 3-year federal baseline and miss the exceptions.

Those exceptions matter. They can keep a file open long after the final report is submitted.

## Start with the final expenditure report

For many federal grants, the basic retention period runs 3 years from the date the final expenditure report is submitted. That date should be recorded in the closeout file.

Do not start the clock from the award end date unless the award terms say so. The final report date is often later.

## Flag audit, claim, and litigation holds

If an audit, claim, or litigation starts before the retention period ends, keep the records until the issue is resolved. This includes related correspondence, workpapers, questioned cost support, and final decisions.

The safest file label is simple: "hold until resolved." Add the issue owner and review date.

## Treat property records separately

Equipment and real property records can have a longer practical life. Keep acquisition records, title or ownership support, inventory records, disposition instructions, and sale proceeds support until the property matter is closed.

If a grant funded equipment, connect this guide to the [equipment tagging and inventory guide](/resources/guides/federal-grant-equipment-tagging-inventory-guide).

## Watch indirect cost support

Indirect cost proposals, cost allocation plans, and related support can have their own retention timing. Keep the filed proposal, rate agreement, base calculation, and support used to build the rate.

This matters when a later grant uses an approved rate built from older records.

## Build an exception field into the closeout checklist

Every closeout checklist should ask:

- Was there an audit, claim, or open dispute?
- Did the award buy equipment or property?
- Was indirect cost support used?
- Did the pass-through entity set a longer period?
- Did state law require a longer period?

If any answer is yes, the file needs a longer retention note.

## Build a retention decision log

Do not leave retention decisions inside email threads. Keep a simple log in the grant file. It should show the award, final expenditure report date, base retention date, exception review, final retention date, and approver.

| Review item           | What to record                                           |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Final report date     | The date the final expenditure report was submitted.     |
| Base retention end    | The normal 3-year date when federal rules apply.         |
| Open audit or claim   | Yes, no, and the issue owner if yes.                     |
| Property records      | Whether equipment or real property rules apply.          |
| Indirect cost records | Whether rate or allocation support affects the file.     |
| Longer rule           | State, pass-through, contract, or insurance requirement. |
| Disposal approval     | Person and date approving archive or disposal.           |

This log does not replace legal advice. It gives staff a way to document the review. If leadership decides to keep records longer, note that too.

## Common exception examples

A monitoring letter can extend the practical retention period. So can a questioned cost, a pending management decision, or a pass-through request for more support. Keep the file open until the matter is closed.

Equipment can also stretch the timeline. If the award bought a vehicle, freezer, server, or other equipment, the organization may need the purchase record, tag record, use record, and disposition record after the grant itself closes.

Indirect cost files are easy to miss. A rate proposal may support several grants. Deleting the older rate support can weaken a later audit response, even when the grant that produced the support is closed.

## What belongs in the closeout packet

At closeout, collect these records before the team moves on:

- final financial report
- final performance report
- submission receipts
- award agreement and amendments
- budget and approved revisions
- ledger detail
- draw or reimbursement support
- procurement records
- equipment records
- subrecipient records
- retention decision log

The goal is not to keep every file forever. The goal is to know which files carry legal, audit, or funder weight.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.334 sets the federal retention baseline and exceptions. It does not cancel longer state rules, funder rules, or legal holds. It also does not mean every file can be deleted exactly 3 years after the award ends.

2 CFR 200.335 covers access to records. That matters when an agency, pass-through entity, or auditor requests records during the retention period.

2 CFR 200.344 covers closeout. Closeout is a good time to set the retention date because final reports, refunds, property issues, and open questions should be visible then.
