## What it actually takes to form a Minnesota nonprofit

Minnesota has one of the more developed nonprofit statutes in the country. Chapter 317A gives founders flexibility on board composition, member structures, and dissolution paths - but the same flexibility creates places to misfile if you use a generic template.

This guide walks through the steps that produce a Minnesota nonprofit corporation, a federal 501(c)(3), and a clean Minnesota Attorney General charitable registration so you can solicit donations legally.

### Step 1: Choose and clear a name

Minnesota requires the name be distinguishable from other entities on file with the Secretary of State. Search the MN SOS business name database before drafting. Names containing "Bank," "Insurance," or other regulated terms require additional approvals.

Optional name reservation: $35 (mail) or $55 (online), good for 12 months.

### Step 2: Recruit at least three directors

Chapter 317A requires a minimum of three directors. They do not have to be Minnesota residents. For 501(c)(3) eligibility the IRS expects independence - three directors from one household is a red flag on the Form 1023.

### Step 3: Prepare and file Articles of Incorporation

The Articles of Incorporation must include:

- Name of the corporation
- Registered office (Minnesota street address) and registered agent (optional)
- Names and addresses of incorporators
- Statement of purpose limiting activities to one or more 501(c)(3) exempt purposes
- IRS-required dissolution clause directing assets to another 501(c)(3) or government entity
- Whether the corporation has members
- Optional provisions on director liability limitations

Filing fee: $70 by mail or $90 through the MN SOS online filing system. Online filings are typically processed within 1 to 2 business days.

### Step 4: Hold the organizational meeting

At the initial board meeting:

- Adopt bylaws
- Elect officers (president, treasurer, secretary at minimum)
- Approve a conflict-of-interest policy
- Authorize the EIN application and bank account
- Authorize Form 1023 or 1023-EZ filing

Document with signed minutes.

### Step 5: Get a federal EIN

Apply at IRS.gov. Free. Issued immediately for online applications.

### Step 6: Apply for 501(c)(3) recognition

- **Form 1023-EZ** ($275): under $50,000 projected annual gross receipts, under $250,000 in assets, and not a school, church, hospital, or LLC. Processed in 4 to 6 weeks typically.
- **Form 1023** ($600): all others. Processing 6 to 12 months typical.

File within 27 months of incorporation to preserve retroactive recognition.

### Step 7: Register with the Minnesota Attorney General

Before soliciting Minnesota residents, file the Minnesota Charitable Organization Registration with the AG's Charities Division. Initial registration fee: $25. The package includes the Charitable Organization Initial Registration Statement, a copy of the IRS determination letter, articles, bylaws, conflict-of-interest policy, list of officers and directors, and the most recent financial statements.

Renewal is annual, due within the timeframe established by Minn. Stat. ch. 309 - typically within a fixed period after fiscal year end, with audited financials required above certain thresholds.

### Step 8: Apply for Minnesota tax exemptions

Minnesota nonprofits do not automatically receive state tax exemptions. Apply separately:

- **Sales tax exemption:** File Form ST16 with the Minnesota Department of Revenue.
- **Property tax exemption:** Apply with the county assessor for any real property used for exempt purposes.
- **Minnesota corporate franchise tax:** 501(c)(3) status generally exempts the organization from MN corporate franchise tax on exempt-purpose income, but unrelated business income remains taxable.

### Step 9: File Minnesota annual renewal

Every Minnesota nonprofit corporation must file an annual renewal with the Secretary of State by December 31. The filing has no fee but is required to maintain active status. Failure to file leads to administrative dissolution.

## Common Minnesota mistakes

- **Using boilerplate articles without IRS-required purpose and dissolution language.** The IRS rejects 1023s without it.
- **Skipping the AG charitable registration before soliciting.** Minnesota's Attorney General actively enforces Chapter 309.
- **Forgetting the December 31 SOS annual renewal.** Free, easy, and fatal if missed for two years.
- **Assuming federal exemption covers Minnesota sales tax.** It does not - Form ST16 is the separate filing.
