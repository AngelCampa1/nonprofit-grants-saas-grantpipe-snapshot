# SEFA footnote disclosure checklist

The SEFA is more than a table of federal awards. The notes explain how the table was prepared and which special items affect the numbers. Auditors use those notes to understand basis of accounting, indirect cost treatment, pass-through activity, and federal assistance that may not look like a normal grant payment.

Start with the [SEFA preparation checklist](/resources/guides/sefa-preparation-checklist). Then use this guide to review the note disclosures before the schedule goes to the auditor.

## Required note: basis of accounting

2 CFR 200.510 requires the SEFA notes to describe the significant accounting policies used to prepare the schedule. The most basic policy is the basis of accounting.

State whether the SEFA is prepared on the accrual basis, cash basis, or another basis. The note should match how expenditures were counted. If your financial statements are accrual basis but the SEFA uses a special basis, say so and explain enough for a reader to understand the difference.

Do not copy last year's note without checking it. A change in accounting software, year-end close process, or grant tracking method can change how the SEFA was actually built.

## Required note: de minimis indirect cost rate

The notes must state whether the organization elected to use the de minimis indirect cost rate under 2 CFR 200.414. This is required even if the organization did not use the rate.

A clear note can be short: "The Organization did not elect to use the de minimis indirect cost rate." If the organization did use it, state that it elected to use the rate and applied it according to 2 CFR 200.414.

If the organization has a negotiated indirect cost rate, keep the SEFA note aligned with the rate agreement and the [indirect cost rate guide](/resources/guides/indirect-cost-rate-explained).

## Pass-through awards and subrecipients

If your nonprofit receives federal funds through a state agency, county, city, school district, or another nonprofit, the SEFA should identify the pass-through entity and the pass-through award number when available.

If your nonprofit passes federal funds to subrecipients, 2 CFR 200.510 requires the SEFA to include the total amount provided to subrecipients from each federal program. This can appear as a SEFA column or a note. The reader should be able to see which program funded the subawards and how much went out.

Tie this note to your subrecipient records. The [subrecipient monitoring guide](/resources/guides/subrecipient-monitoring-requirements-uniform-guidance) explains the related monitoring file.

## Noncash assistance

Some federal assistance is not received as cash. Examples can include donated food, donated property, vaccines, equipment, or other noncash federal assistance. These items may still be federal awards for SEFA purposes.

Disclose the nature of the noncash assistance and the valuation basis used. If the value comes from the federal agency or pass-through entity, keep that support. If management estimates fair value, document the method and source.

## Loans and loan guarantees

Federal loans and loan guarantees can create SEFA reporting issues because the expenditure amount may not equal current-year cash received. 2 CFR 200.502 explains how to count loans and loan guarantees for single audit purposes.

If the organization has federal loans, confirm whether the outstanding balance at year end must be disclosed. The note should make the treatment clear, especially when the loan has a continuing compliance requirement.

## Clusters and major program support

Clusters are usually shown in the SEFA table, but notes can help when programs share a cluster name or when pass-through award labels differ from Assistance Listing names. Use the current OMB Compliance Supplement to identify clusters.

Wrong cluster treatment can change major program selection. If the cluster analysis is complex, keep a workpaper showing how each Assistance Listing number was grouped.

## Reconciliation note

Some organizations include a reconciliation from SEFA expenditures to federal grant revenue or receivables in the financial statements. This is not always required, but it can help when timing differences are material.

Common reconciling items include advances, deferred revenue, receivables, accrued costs, and indirect cost postings. Write the note in plain terms. The goal is to help a reader understand why the SEFA does not equal one revenue line.

## Final footnote review

Before sending the SEFA to the auditor, check:

- Basis of accounting is stated
- De minimis election is stated
- Pass-through entities are identified
- Amounts passed to subrecipients are disclosed
- Noncash assistance is described and valued
- Loans and guarantees are treated correctly
- Clusters agree to the current Compliance Supplement
- Notes agree to the SEFA table and ledger
