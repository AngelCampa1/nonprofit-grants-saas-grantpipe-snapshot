Donor restricted grants touch many records. The award letter says what the money is for. The ledger shows revenue and expenses. The bank shows cash. Receivables show money due. Release entries show restrictions met. The grant file holds support.

A reconciliation proves these pieces agree. Without it, a nonprofit can send a funder report that does not match the books, release restricted funds too early, or miss cash that should be collected.

## Start with the award terms

Before looking at numbers, read the grant agreement. Record the key terms in the reconciliation:

- Grant amount
- Grant period
- Purpose restriction
- Approved budget
- Payment terms
- Reimbursement rules
- Match or cost share rules
- Reporting dates
- Closeout rules

Do not rely on memory. Use the grant agreement and any written amendments. If a funder approved a budget change by email, save it with the file.

For setup basics, link to [restricted fund tracking for nonprofits](/resources/guides/restricted-fund-tracking-for-nonprofits).

## Tie revenue to the agreement

Next, compare recorded grant revenue to the award terms. Under ASC 958, a donor restricted grant may be recorded as contribution revenue with donor restrictions when it is unconditional. Conditional grants need more care. Conditions must be met before revenue is recognized.

The reconciliation should ask:

Was the grant recorded in the right period?

Was it recorded as with donor restrictions?

Was any receivable recorded for unpaid but earned amounts?

Was any conditional amount kept out of revenue until conditions were met?

If revenue was recorded wrong, fix it before closing the period or sending reports.

## Tie cash and receivables

Cash timing often differs from grant revenue. A funder may pay upfront, reimburse later, or pay in installments.

Build a cash table:

- Cash received to date
- Receivables billed
- Receivables not yet billed
- Reimbursements requested
- Reimbursements received
- Difference to explain

For reimbursement grants, this table helps finance manage cash. It also helps the executive director see why strong grant activity may still create a short-term cash strain.

For federal draw and reimbursement work, link to [how to track federal grant draws and reimbursements](/resources/guides/how-to-track-federal-grant-draws-and-reimbursements).

## Tie expenses to the budget

Compare actual expenses to the approved grant budget. Use the fund or grant code in the ledger, but do not stop there. Review whether each major cost fits the grant period and purpose.

For federal awards, 2 CFR Part 200 includes rules for cost allowability, documentation, procurement, and cost allocation. Foundation grants may use simpler terms, but the same discipline helps.

Group costs by budget line. Show approved budget, actual costs, remaining budget, and variance. Add notes for lines that are overspent, underspent, or missing support.

If staff time is charged to the grant, check payroll coding and time records. If shared costs are allocated, keep the allocation method.

## Tie releases to qualifying expenses

For purpose-restricted grants, releases from restriction should usually follow qualifying expenses. If the grant had $50,000 of allowed costs this period, the release should normally be $50,000, unless another restriction applies.

The reconciliation should compare:

Qualifying expenses to date.

Releases posted to date.

Remaining restricted balance.

If expenses exceed releases, the unrestricted column may be understated. If releases exceed expenses, the restricted balance may be understated.

Use [net asset release workflow](/resources/guides/net-asset-release-workflow) to review the accounting logic.

## Explain the ending balance

Every donor restricted grant should end the month with an explainable balance.

The ending restricted balance may be unspent upfront cash. It may be a future time restriction. It may be a receivable tied to work already performed. It may be a balance due back to the funder.

Do not leave unexplained differences in a "to review" column for months. Assign an owner and a due date.

## Review before reports go out

Reconcile before sending funder reports. The report should match the ledger or explain timing differences clearly. Reconcile before the board packet too, especially for large grants.

At year-end, save the reconciliation with the audit file. The Council of Nonprofits notes that audits review records and controls. A clean reconciliation shows both.

Also keep a short reviewer note. It should say who reviewed the grant, what reports were used, what differences remain, and when any open item will be cleared. That note saves time when the same question returns during close or audit.
