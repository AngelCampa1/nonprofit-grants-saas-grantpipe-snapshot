A p-card is useful for small purchases. It is also easy to misuse when grant rules are not checked until month end.

The control goal is simple: the purchase should be approved, allowed, supported, coded, and reviewed.

## Require approval before the charge

For grant-funded purchases, approval should happen before the card is used when possible.

The request should name the grant, budget line, vendor, amount, and business purpose. If the purchase is urgent, the policy should still require a written explanation after the fact.

## Keep the receipt and purpose together

A receipt alone is not enough. The file should explain why the cost was charged to the grant.

For example, a receipt for supplies should also say which grant activity used the supplies. A hotel receipt should tie to approved travel and the event purpose.

## Review allowability before coding

Finance should check whether the cost is necessary, reasonable, allocable, and allowed by the award.

Some costs may be normal for the organization but not allowed on a specific grant. The award terms control the decision.

## Reconcile monthly

Each monthly p-card review should match the statement, receipts, approvals, and ledger coding. Missing receipts should be cleared before the close.

Do not let a missing receipt sit for months. A stale missing receipt becomes an audit problem.

## Set limits by grant risk

Higher-risk awards may need lower limits, tighter purchase categories, or a second review. New staff may need a lower card limit until they understand award rules.

Use the [grant expense approval workflow](/workflows/approve-grant-expense-before-payment) for the review step.

## Control table for card charges

P-card control works best when each control has an owner. A vague policy says receipts are required. A useful policy says who collects them, who reviews them, and what happens when they are missing.

| Control                | Owner                   | Evidence                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pre-approval           | Grant owner             | Approved request with grant, vendor, amount, and purpose. |
| Spending limit         | Finance                 | Card limit and exception approval.                        |
| Receipt capture        | Cardholder              | Itemized receipt, not just a card slip.                   |
| Allowability review    | Finance and grant owner | Note that the cost fits award terms.                      |
| Monthly reconciliation | Finance                 | Statement matched to receipts and ledger.                 |
| Exception follow-up    | Supervisor              | Missing receipt note or repayment record.                 |

These controls are not meant to slow small purchases. They are meant to stop weak costs before they reach a report or reimbursement request.

## What a reviewer should look for

The reviewer should ask whether the cost was needed for the grant, whether the amount was reasonable, whether the cost can be assigned to the grant, and whether the award allows it.

Look closely at meals, travel, software, gift cards, supplies used across programs, and anything bought near the end of the award. These are common places where a normal operating cost becomes a weak grant cost.

If a receipt covers more than one program, split the cost before posting. The split should be written on the support or in the expense record.

## Missing receipts and exceptions

Every policy needs an exception path. Staff will lose receipts. Vendors will issue poor receipts. A grant file still needs a decision.

For a missing receipt, require a written statement with the vendor, date, amount, item, business purpose, and reason the receipt is missing. Then require supervisor approval. Do not let the exception become routine.

For an unallowable card charge, remove it from the grant. If the cardholder should repay the organization, document that separately.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.403 and 200.404 focus on allowable and reasonable costs. A p-card charge does not become allowable because it was convenient. The cost still needs to meet the award and cost principle tests.

2 CFR 200.302 and 200.303 require financial management and internal controls. For card spending, that means the organization should be able to trace approval, support, coding, review, and correction.

Some funders prohibit certain p-card categories or require purchase orders. Award terms should override the general card policy when they are stricter.
