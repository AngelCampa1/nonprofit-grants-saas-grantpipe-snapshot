# Grant contract review finance checklist

Finance should see a grant contract before it is signed. The contract may decide how cash is paid, what costs are allowed, what records must be kept, and what happens if the nonprofit misses a rule.

This checklist is for finance review. It is not legal advice. If terms affect liability, privacy, insurance, indemnity, intellectual property, or other legal rights, bring in qualified counsel.

## Confirm award basics

Start with the facts that will enter the accounting system.

Record:

- Funder
- Award name
- Award amount
- Contract period
- Budget period
- Purpose
- Restricted use
- Payment method
- Match requirement
- Reporting dates
- Signer

If the contract conflicts with the award letter, stop and ask the funder to confirm the correct term in writing.

## Review payment terms

Payment timing can change the risk of the grant.

Find out whether payment is:

- Advance
- Reimbursement
- Milestone based
- Installment based
- Final report based

For federal awards, payment rules appear in 2 CFR 200.305. Other funders may have their own rules. Finance should know whether cash arrives before costs, after costs, or only after a report is accepted.

If the grant is reimbursement based, estimate the cash gap. Ask how much the nonprofit may need to spend before payment arrives and how long approval may take.

## Check cost rules

Read the budget and cost terms together. Do not review the contract alone.

Ask:

- Are costs limited to approved lines?
- Are indirect costs allowed?
- Is there a cap on admin costs?
- Are staff costs allowed?
- Are equipment costs allowed?
- Are travel rules listed?
- Are pre-award costs allowed?
- Are costs after the end date banned?
- Are budget transfers allowed?

For federal awards, costs generally need to be necessary, reasonable, allocable, consistent, and supported. The contract may add stricter limits.

## Review match and cost share

If the contract requires match, finance should approve the tracking plan before signature.

Record:

- Match amount
- Match source
- Cash or in-kind
- Valuation method
- Timing
- Documentation
- Restrictions
- Owner

Do not accept match terms that rely on vague future donations or staff time no one can track.

## Check reporting and backup

Finance should identify each financial report and each backup file before the grant starts.

Look for:

- Reimbursement forms
- Financial status reports
- Budget to actual reports
- Payroll support
- Invoice support
- Proof of payment
- Match support
- Closeout forms

Add each requirement to the reporting calendar. GrantPipe can help connect contract terms to report tasks, budgets, and documents so finance does not have to search the contract each month.

## Review records and access

Contracts often say the funder can inspect records. Finance should know what that means.

Check:

- Record retention period
- Audit access
- Site visit rights
- Required file format
- Closeout records
- Equipment records
- Subrecipient records if any

For federal awards, 2 CFR 200.334 covers retention requirements. Award terms may extend or clarify those rules.

## Identify approval triggers

Some actions need funder approval before the nonprofit acts.

Common triggers:

- Budget revision
- Scope change
- No-cost extension
- Key staff change
- Equipment purchase
- Subaward
- Contracting change
- Carryforward

Put these triggers in plain language for program staff. A rule hidden in the contract will not help when a manager needs to make a fast decision.

## Create the finance setup note

Before signature, finance should write a short setup note.

Include:

- Accounting code needed
- Budget lines
- Billing or reimbursement process
- Match tracking
- Report schedule
- Approval limits
- Cash risk
- Records to save

Send the note to grants, program, and leadership. This makes the contract usable.

## Decide before signing

The final finance review should produce one of three results.

Approve means finance can support the terms.

Approve with actions means the contract can move forward after listed setup tasks are done.

Hold means a term needs funder clarification, leadership decision, or legal review.

A grant contract is easier to manage when finance reads it before acceptance. After signature, the team is already bound to the work.
