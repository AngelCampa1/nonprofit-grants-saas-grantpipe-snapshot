The last invoice is the easiest one to rush. It is also the one that closes the money trail.

Before submission, the team should prove that all final costs are supported, allowed, and inside the award period.

## Lock the grant ledger

Pull the grant ledger after final payroll, invoices, corrections, and indirect cost entries are posted.

If the accounting period is still open, label the invoice draft as draft. Do not submit final numbers from a moving ledger.

## Check the period of performance

Costs should fall inside the period of performance unless the award allows a closeout cost or late charge.

Review late invoices carefully. The service date matters more than the invoice date.

## Review indirect costs

If the grant allows indirect costs, calculate the final amount using the approved rate and base.

Save the calculation. If the rate changed during the award, keep the rate support and explain the split.

## Check refunds, credits, and advances

Look for vendor credits, payroll corrections, refunds, and unused advance cash. These can reduce the final claim.

Do not ignore small credits. A final closeout file should show how they were handled.

## Match the final invoice to final reports

The final invoice should agree with the final financial report or include a clear reconciliation. If the final SF-425 or funder report uses cumulative totals, check that the invoice does not create a mismatch.

Use the [final invoice workflow](/workflows/prepare-final-invoice-for-grant-closeout) for the step-by-step process.

## Final invoice review table

Use one worksheet for the final invoice. It should be narrow enough to review but detailed enough to defend.

| Review area    | What to check                                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Period         | Cost service dates are inside the allowed period.             |
| Budget         | Costs fit the approved budget or approved revision.           |
| Support        | Invoices, payroll, travel, or allocation records are saved.   |
| Indirect costs | Rate, base, and calculation are documented.                   |
| Match          | Required match is supported and reported.                     |
| Credits        | Refunds, rebates, and voids reduce the claim when required.   |
| Advances       | Unused cash is returned or handled under agency instructions. |
| Reports        | Final invoice ties to final financial report totals.          |

The reviewer should sign or approve the worksheet before submission. A short approval note is enough if it names the reviewer, date, and packet reviewed.

## Late costs and accruals

Late costs need care. A vendor invoice dated after the award end may still be allowable if the service happened during the period of performance and the award allows it. A cost dated before the award end may still be weak if the service happened later.

Use service dates, not only invoice dates. Save the proof that shows when the work happened.

Accruals should be documented. If the final invoice includes accrued costs, keep the later payment support with the closeout file when payment clears.

## Refunds, credits, and overdrawn cash

Closeout should include a search for credits. Review vendor statements, payroll corrections, returned supplies, and unspent advances.

If the organization drew federal cash in advance, compare the final claim to cash received. Any excess should be handled under agency instructions. Do not leave the issue for the next audit.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.344 covers closeout, but agencies can give more specific instructions. The final invoice, final SF-425, final performance report, and property reports may have different forms and portals.

2 CFR 200.403 still applies at closeout. A cost does not become allowable because budget remains. It must still be necessary, reasonable, allocable, documented, and allowed.

Some pass-through entities use shorter deadlines than the federal closeout rule. Follow the agreement and get written direction if the deadline is unclear.

## Who should sign off

The final invoice should have at least 2 views before submission. Finance should confirm the dollars. The grant owner should confirm award terms, period of performance, program status, and any special closeout conditions.

For higher-risk awards, add executive review. This is useful when the final invoice includes large accruals, unresolved credits, equipment, subrecipients, or match.

The approval note can be simple:

- final ledger reviewed
- support packet reviewed
- credits and refunds checked
- final report tie-out complete
- submission approved

Save the note with the packet. A final invoice without review proof is harder to defend later, even when the numbers are right.
