# Grant award letter intake checklist

A grant award letter is more than good news. It is the first operating record for the funded work. If the team treats it like a celebration email, important terms can get missed.

Use this checklist before anyone signs, spends, announces, or enters the award into the accounting system.

## Save the full award packet

Start by saving the complete packet in the grant file.

Include:

- Award letter
- Grant agreement or contract
- Approved budget
- Budget narrative
- Program scope
- Reporting instructions
- Payment instructions
- Funder contact list
- Portal messages
- Attachments

Do not save only the award letter if the terms live in another document. The grant file should hold the full record staff need later.

## Confirm the basic terms

Enter the award facts in one place.

Record:

- Funder name
- Grant title
- Award amount
- Award date
- Grant period
- Purpose
- Restricted use
- Payment type
- Match requirement
- Report dates
- Closeout date
- Record retention rule

If the award amount or period differs from the proposal, flag it. The budget and work plan may need a change before acceptance.

## Check the budget

Compare the approved budget to the submitted budget. Do not assume they match.

Ask:

- Did the funder cut the amount?
- Did the funder change any line item?
- Did the funder approve indirect costs?
- Did match change?
- Are any costs banned?
- Are any costs subject to prior approval?
- Does finance need a new fund or project code?

For federal awards, 2 CFR 200.302 expects financial records that identify the award and support source and use of funds. Intake is where that setup begins.

## Review payment terms

Payment type affects cash.

Identify whether the grant is:

- Paid up front
- Paid in installments
- Paid by reimbursement
- Paid after milestones
- Paid after final report

For reimbursement grants, finance needs to know how long the nonprofit may carry costs before payment. The team should also know what backup the funder requires before cash comes in.

## Capture reporting duties

Create reporting tasks during intake.

For each report, record:

- Report name
- Reporting period
- Due date
- Required form
- Data needed
- Financial backup
- Program backup
- Owner
- Reviewer
- Submission method

For federal awards, performance and financial reporting rules may apply. The funder may also require special forms or portal steps. Save those instructions beside the report task.

## Review signature and approval rules

Before signing, confirm who is allowed to accept the award.

Check:

- Authorized signer
- Board approval need
- Finance review
- Legal review if required
- Insurance or data terms
- Partner signatures
- Conflict of interest rules

This guide is not legal advice. If contract terms are unclear or create risk, ask qualified counsel or the funder's grants officer before signing.

## Hold a short intake meeting

Bring grants, finance, program, and leadership into one short meeting.

Cover:

- What was awarded
- What changed from the proposal
- What finance must set up
- What program must deliver
- What reports are due
- What evidence must be saved
- What risks need attention

GrantPipe can help connect the award record, budget, report dates, documents, and tasks so each team can see its part after intake.

## Build the first action list

End intake with a dated action list.

Good tasks include:

- Finance will create the project code.
- Grants will enter report dates.
- Program will confirm the service plan.
- Executive director will review signature terms.
- Finance will confirm reimbursement backup.
- Grants will save submission and award records together.

Each task should have one owner. Shared ownership often means no ownership.

## Keep the original version

If the award changes, keep the first award letter and every later revision. Label files by date. Save funder approvals for budget changes, period changes, or reporting changes.

Award intake is the bridge from proposal to management. A clean intake makes the first report easier, the first reimbursement cleaner, and the closeout less stressful.
