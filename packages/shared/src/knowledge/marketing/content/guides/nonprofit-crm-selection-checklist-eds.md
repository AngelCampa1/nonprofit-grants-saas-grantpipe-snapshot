CRM selection at nonprofits tends to follow a predictable pattern: someone attends a conference, watches three demos in a row, picks the one with the best slide deck, and signs an annual contract. Six months later, the development team is still maintaining a spreadsheet for grant tracking because the CRM they chose handles donors fine but cannot track restricted funds.

This checklist exists to prevent that outcome. It is structured for executive directors who need to make a CRM decision and want to do it once rather than twice.

## Step 1: Document Your Requirements Before You Talk to Vendors

Spend 30 minutes writing down every task your organization currently handles with technology (or without it). Include donor contact management, gift entry and acknowledgment, pledge tracking, grant award recording, restricted fund tracking, expenditure reporting by grant, funder report generation, board financial summaries, and communication history.

Divide that list into two categories:

**Must-haves:** Operations stop or audit risk increases if this feature is missing. For most grant-funded nonprofits, the must-have list includes restricted fund tracking, compliance reporting, donor management, and gift entry.

**Nice-to-haves:** Would improve things, but the organization survives without them. Peer-to-peer fundraising pages, event management, volunteer hour tracking, and marketing automation fall here for most mid-sized nonprofits.

Evaluate vendors only against must-haves. A platform that handles 15 nice-to-haves but cannot track restricted funds is not a fit for a grant-funded organization.

## Step 2: Compare Free Trial Availability and Feature Caps

Not all trials are created equal. Some platforms give you full access for a limited time. Others give you permanent access to a stripped-down version. The difference matters.

**Trial lengths by platform (as of 2026):**

| Platform             | Trial Length      | Self-Service?                    | Key Limitations                             |
| -------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| GrantPipe            | 30 days           | Yes                              | Selected plan, no contact caps              |
| Bloomerang           | 14 days           | Yes                              | Contact cap at 1,000 on starter tier        |
| Little Green Light   | 30 days           | Yes                              | Full access, per-record pricing after trial |
| Keela                | 14 days           | Yes                              | Some reporting locked to higher tiers       |
| Salesforce Nonprofit | No standard trial | No - requires sales rep          | Sandbox provisioning takes 1-2 weeks        |
| Neon CRM             | Demo only         | No                               | Must request access through sales           |
| DonorPerfect         | Varies            | No - requires sales conversation | Feature set depends on tier quoted          |

The trial is where you test your must-haves with real data. A 14-day trial is enough if you block off two full days to enter actual donor records, record a grant award, and try to generate a compliance report. GrantPipe's 1-month trial uses the plan you choose, so teams can test the tier they expect to buy. No credit card is required to start, and you can add billing later if the trial is a fit.

If a vendor does not offer a self-service trial, ask why. Platforms that require a sales conversation before you can test them are either under-investing in self-service or trying to control the evaluation experience.

## Step 3: Test Grant Management Specifically

Every vendor will demo donor management first. Contact records, gift history, acknowledgment letter generation - these are visually compelling and well-developed on every platform. Grant management is where the real differences appear.

During your trial or demo, ask to see these four specific workflows:

1. **Record a new grant award** with a restricted fund designation and approved budget categories
2. **Track three expenditures** against that grant, each coded to a different budget line
3. **Generate a compliance report** for a single funder showing expenditures by category against the approved budget
4. **Set a reporting deadline** and show how the system alerts staff when it approaches

If the vendor cannot walk through all four smoothly, the grant management capability is either underdeveloped, requires a consultant to configure, or lives in a separate add-on module. All three outcomes mean more cost and more risk for your organization.

## Step 4: Calculate Total Cost of Ownership

The demo price is not the operating price. Ask every vendor for a complete cost breakdown:

**Year one costs:**

- License or subscription fees (monthly or annual)
- Implementation or setup fees
- Data migration assistance
- Staff training
- Required add-on modules (grant management, reporting, integrations)
- Consulting hours (for platforms like Salesforce that require configuration)

**Year three costs:**

- Automatic price escalation terms
- Contact tier increases as your database grows
- Additional modules required as data volume scales
- Ongoing admin or consulting costs

For Salesforce Nonprofit, implementation commonly runs $30,000-$100,000+ beyond license fees for mid-sized nonprofit deployments. That cost covers custom object configuration, workflow automation, data migration, and training. Purpose-built platforms like Bloomerang, GrantPipe, and Keela are designed to be deployed without consulting.

The total cost gap between a purpose-built nonprofit CRM and a configured enterprise platform is significant enough that it should be part of the board-level decision, not discovered after contract signing.

## Step 5: Evaluate Data Export and Exit Terms

How easy it is to leave a vendor tells you something about their product confidence. Platforms that make data portability difficult are betting on switching costs rather than product quality.

Ask these questions:

- Can I export all donor data, gift history, and grant records in CSV or Excel format at any time?
- Is there a fee for data export?
- How long does the export process take for an organization our size?
- Does the export include custom fields, communication history, and grant documentation?
- What happens to my data after I cancel?

Prefer platforms with clear, no-fee data export and month-to-month contract options. Annual contracts without exit clauses make a wrong CRM decision significantly more expensive.

## Step 6: Check Accounting System Integration

Your CRM must connect to your accounting system - QuickBooks Nonprofit, Sage Intacct, or whatever manages your general ledger. Grant expenditure data needs to flow between systems without manual reconciliation at month-end.

Questions for vendors:

- Does the platform integrate natively with our accounting software, or does it require a third-party connector (Zapier, custom API work)?
- What does the monthly reconciliation process look like between CRM and accounting?
- Can the integration handle restricted fund tracking across both systems?

Failed integrations between CRM and accounting are the most common source of audit risk when nonprofits switch platforms. If the integration requires manual data transfer, you are replacing one spreadsheet problem with another.

## Step 7: Run a Live Trial With Real Data

Never make a final CRM decision based solely on a demo. Demos are presented under ideal conditions with sample data. Trials reveal limitations.

During your trial, complete at least these five tasks with actual organizational data:

1. Enter three current donors with full contact information and gift history
2. Record a grant award with restricted fund designation
3. Assign expenditures to the grant and verify the restricted fund balance updates
4. Generate a compliance report for the grant
5. Export the data you entered and verify it is complete

If you cannot complete your normal workflows during a trial, you will not complete them in production. The trial is not a formality - it is the evaluation.

## Step 8: Make the Decision in Writing

Document which requirements you evaluated against and how each vendor performed. Include the total cost calculation. Share this document with the decision stakeholders before signing anything.

Commit to a 90-day post-go-live review. At the 90-day mark, verify the system is meeting every must-have requirement in production, not just in trial. Check your contract for an exit provision if must-have requirements are not met within the first 90 days.

## Migration Considerations

If you are switching from an existing CRM, plan for a 2-4 month migration timeline:

**Month 1:** Data cleanup in your current system. Deduplicate records, standardize field formats, export and verify data completeness.

**Month 2:** Import data into the new platform. Run both systems in parallel for 30 days so staff can verify that records transferred correctly.

**Months 3-4:** Staff adoption and workflow adjustment. The hardest part of a CRM migration is not the technology. It is breaking habits built around the old system.

Clean your data before migrating. Deduplication and field standardization in the old system save significant time during import.

## The Checklist Summary

Before signing with any CRM vendor, confirm all of these:

- [ ] Requirements document written before vendor contact
- [ ] Must-haves and nice-to-haves separated
- [ ] Free trial completed with real organizational data
- [ ] Grant management tested with all four workflows (award, expenditure, report, deadline)
- [ ] Total cost of ownership calculated for year one and year three
- [ ] Data export terms verified (no fee, standard format, includes all records)
- [ ] Accounting system integration confirmed
- [ ] Contract terms reviewed (monthly vs. annual, exit clauses, price escalation)
- [ ] 90-day post-go-live review scheduled

Skip any of these steps and you increase the odds of choosing a platform that handles half your workflows and forces spreadsheet workarounds for the rest.
