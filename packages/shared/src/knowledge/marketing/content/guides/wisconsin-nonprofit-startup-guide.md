## Wisconsin's three-track formation

Wisconsin nonprofit formation has three regulatory tracks plus the federal IRS track: state incorporation with the DFI, state sales tax exemption with the DOR, and charitable organization registration with the DFI. The IRS sits at the center, gating the state-level tax and registration filings.

Wisconsin's structure is more involved than Indiana's (no general charitable registration) and less involved than New York's (multiple AG bureaus and trigger-based audit requirements). Plan on six months from incorporation to fully registered status, with the IRS review consuming most of the calendar.

## Step 1: Wisconsin nonstock corporation under Chapter 181

Wisconsin Statutes Chapter 181 governs nonstock corporations - the standard nonprofit corporate form. File Articles of Incorporation (Form 102) with the Department of Financial Institutions for $35.

The Articles must include:

- Corporate name distinguishable from existing entities
- A statement that the corporation is organized under Chapter 181
- Purpose clause (include IRS 501(c)(3) language)
- Registered agent and registered office
- Names and addresses of incorporators
- Whether the corporation will have members

Wisconsin requires at least three directors under Statute 181.0803. Recruit unrelated directors and document acceptance of board service.

### IRS-required language at the state level

The IRS will not grant 501(c)(3) status without the purpose, inurement, and dissolution clauses. Include them in the Articles at the state filing stage. IRS Publication 557 has model language. Putting it in later requires filing Articles of Amendment with another fee and another DFI review cycle.

## Step 2: Federal EIN and 501(c)(3) determination

Apply for the EIN on irs.gov immediately after incorporation. Free, instant.

Choose the 1023 path:

- **Form 1023-EZ** ($275): Under $50,000 projected annual receipts for three years and under $250,000 in total assets. Eligibility worksheet has 30 disqualifying questions. Decisions in 2-4 weeks.
- **Form 1023** ($600): Full application with narrative, three-year budgets, governance policies, and bylaws. Decisions in 3-10 months.

The IRS determination letter is the gating document for the Wisconsin CES and the multi-year audit-readiness posture.

## Step 3: Wisconsin Certificate of Exempt Status (CES)

Once the IRS letter arrives, apply to the Wisconsin Department of Revenue for the Certificate of Exempt Status. The CES is provided to vendors at the point of sale to exempt qualifying purchases from Wisconsin sales tax.

Not all purchases are exempt. Wisconsin has specific rules on what constitutes a qualifying use, particularly for purchases related to unrelated business activity. When in doubt, the DOR's nonprofit FAQ and Publication 206 are the authoritative references.

## Step 4: Form 308 charitable organization registration

Wisconsin requires charitable organizations soliciting contributions to register with the DFI before solicitation begins. Form 308 is the registration statement.

The fee is tiered by annual contributions:

| Annual contributions | Form 308 fee |
| -------------------- | ------------ |
| Under $50,000        | $15          |
| $50,000-$200,000     | $30          |
| Over $200,000        | $54          |

Certain organizations are exempt from Form 308 - small charities under $25,000 in contributions that do not use professional fundraisers, religious organizations, and political committees, among others. Confirm exemption status against the DFI's published exemption criteria; do not assume.

### Annual financial reporting

Registered charities file an annual financial report with the DFI within 12 months of fiscal year end. Wisconsin Statutes Chapter 202 sets two thresholds:

- **$300,000-$500,000 contributions**: Attach a CPA review report
- **Over $500,000 contributions**: Attach an audited financial statement

These thresholds are lower than the federal single audit threshold ($1M as of October 2024 single audit revisions). Founders planning around the federal threshold can be surprised by the Wisconsin requirement.

## Step 5: Ongoing Wisconsin compliance calendar

| Filing                               | Frequency    | Fee                 | Filed with                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------ | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Wisconsin annual corporate report    | Annual       | $10 online          | Wisconsin DFI (Corporations)             |
| Form 308 annual financial report     | Annual       | None (with renewal) | Wisconsin DFI (Charitable Organizations) |
| IRS Form 990 / 990-EZ / 990-N        | Annual       | $0                  | IRS                                      |
| Wisconsin DOR sales/use tax (if any) | As scheduled | $0                  | Wisconsin Department of Revenue          |
| CES renewal (if expiration)          | As issued    | $0                  | Wisconsin Department of Revenue          |

Calendar each filing the day the prior year's filing is accepted. The DFI is responsive and the fees are low, but missed deadlines compound - late annual reports can lead to administrative dissolution, and the cure path is more expensive than the original filing.

## When professional help pays back

Wisconsin Articles of Incorporation and Form 308 are commonly self-filed. Form 1023-EZ is also a self-file route for eligible organizations. The full Form 1023 and the audit/review preparation at the $300K and $500K thresholds are where founders most often see professional help return more than its cost. Budget $1,500-$5,000 for attorney-assisted full Form 1023 filings and $5,000-$15,000 for first-year audits depending on complexity.
