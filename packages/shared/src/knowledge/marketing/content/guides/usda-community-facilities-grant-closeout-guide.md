# USDA Community Facilities grant closeout guide

USDA Community Facilities grants often fund visible projects: clinics, fire stations, community centers, food access facilities, and other rural public service assets. Closeout should prove the same simple story from start to finish. The project was approved. The costs were allowed. The work was completed. The asset is recorded. The final reports were filed.

Use the [grant closeout checklist](/resources/guides/grant-closeout-checklist) for the general workflow. This guide adds the file details that matter for Community Facilities awards.

## Start with the grant agreement

Before final costs are submitted, read the grant agreement and any USDA Rural Development conditions. Confirm the project purpose, approved budget, procurement terms, construction or purchase conditions, reporting due dates, and any special approvals.

Do not close from the budget spreadsheet alone. The grant agreement is the controlling file. If the project changed, keep the USDA approval with the closeout packet.

## Confirm the project is complete

Closeout should include proof that the funded facility, equipment, or improvement was completed or placed in service. The proof may include final invoices, inspection records, board acceptance, photos, certificate of occupancy, or other records required by USDA or the pass-through process.

For equipment or vehicles, add serial numbers, location, condition, date placed in service, and the grant that paid for the item. The [equipment tagging guide](/resources/guides/federal-grant-equipment-tagging-inventory-guide) can help build that inventory.

## Reconcile final costs

Pull the grant ledger and compare it to the approved budget. Every cost should be incurred within the approved period, tied to the project, supported by an invoice or payroll record, and approved under your policy.

For construction or facility work, keep contracts, change orders, bids or procurement records, invoices, inspection records, and payment proof together. If a change order moved the project over a budget line, keep the approval that allowed it.

## Check payment and draw records

Final reimbursement or draw records should tie to the ledger and bank. If the award used advances, confirm cash timing and disbursement records. If the award reimbursed paid costs, keep proof that the costs were paid before reimbursement.

Do not leave small differences unresolved. A small retainage amount, credit, or unpaid invoice can delay closeout. Add a note for timing items and resolve them before final submission when possible.

## Review procurement files

Community Facilities projects often involve vendors, builders, architects, or equipment sellers. Review procurement support before closeout. The file should show the method used, bids or quotes received, selection reason, contract, conflict check, and approval.

If the project used a sole source or noncompetitive method, keep the written basis and approval. A final invoice is not enough to prove procurement was proper.

## Lock asset records

Assets bought or improved with federal funds need a clear record. Record the asset in the fixed asset register. Include funding source, acquisition date, cost, location, condition, and use. For shared facilities, add a note showing the grant-funded purpose is still being met.

The closeout file should also name who is responsible for future inventory checks. Closeout does not end asset stewardship.

## Prepare final reports

2 CFR 200.344 sets federal closeout timing. Recipients generally submit final financial, performance, and other required reports no later than 120 calendar days after the period of performance. Subrecipients submit to pass-through entities sooner if required.

Check USDA award terms for exact report names and dates. Keep final report copies, submission confirmations, and any USDA response in the file.

## Resolve liens, insurance, and use duties

Facility projects can carry duties after the last invoice is paid. Check whether the agreement mentions insurance, liens, security interests, useful life, or continued use for the approved purpose. If USDA or the lender requires proof, keep the certificate, release, or board action in the closeout file.

This step matters for executive directors because the asset may stay on the books for years. The finance file should tell future staff why the asset was funded, where it is, and what use limits still apply.

## Keep records after closeout

Federal records generally must be retained for three years from final financial report submission. Keep them longer if there is an open audit, claim, litigation, or award term that requires more time. Facility and equipment records may also need to stay active while the asset is used for the grant purpose.

Use the [federal award closeout retention guide](/resources/guides/federal-award-closeout-document-retention-guide) to set a retention date. Write the date on the file map.

## Brief the board

For a facility project, the board should see a short closeout memo. Include the final project cost, USDA funds used, other funds used, final asset location, remaining duties, and record retention date. This helps leaders understand that closeout is not just a finance task.
