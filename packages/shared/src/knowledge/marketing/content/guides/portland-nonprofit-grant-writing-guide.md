## Portland's Foundation Landscape: Concentrated and Relationship-Driven

Portland's grant writing environment is shaped by a simple structural fact: a small number of foundations control a large share of available funding. In larger metros, a development director can apply to dozens of foundations and treat each application independently. In Portland, the realistic pool of major institutional funders is smaller, and the program officers at those foundations know each other.

This concentration changes the grant writing calculus. Each application carries more weight. Each declined proposal is a data point that the funder remembers. Each grant report - delivered on time or late, thorough or thin - affects the relationship not just with that funder but with the broader Portland philanthropic community.

The strategy that works in Portland: fewer, better-aligned applications, honest communication, and long-term relationship investment.

## Meyer Memorial Trust: Oregon's Largest Private Foundation

Meyer Memorial Trust (MMT) is the largest private foundation based in Oregon, distributing approximately $40 million per year. Founded in 1982 from the estate of Fred G. Meyer (founder of Fred Meyer stores), it has been the single most influential funder in the state for decades.

Meyer's current strategy emphasizes racial equity and community power. Its primary funding areas are housing, education, and building the capacity of community-based organizations led by people most affected by systemic inequity. Over the past several years, Meyer has shifted toward trust-based philanthropy practices - multi-year general operating support with simplified applications and streamlined reporting.

### Writing for Meyer

Meyer uses an LOI-first process. The [letter of inquiry](/resources/guides/grant-loi-guide) should demonstrate:

- **Clear alignment with Meyer's current priorities.** Read their website, annual report, and recent grants list before writing anything. Meyer's priorities have shifted significantly in recent years - proposals that would have been funded five years ago may not align with current strategy.
- **Organizational identity, not project description.** Because Meyer increasingly funds general operations rather than specific projects, the LOI should convey who you are and what you do, not just what you'd do with Meyer money.
- **Evidence of community leadership.** Meyer prioritizes organizations led by the communities they serve. Proposals from organizations with community-representative leadership and governance are stronger.
- **Honest capacity assessment.** Meyer program officers appreciate candor about organizational challenges. Overpromising in the LOI and underdelivering in the grant period damages the relationship.

Meyer grants range widely - from $25,000 for smaller organizations to several hundred thousand for multi-year commitments to larger grantees. First-time Meyer applicants should expect the lower end of that range.

## Collins Foundation: Broad Funder, Straightforward Process

The Collins Foundation is one of the most prolific grantmakers in Oregon. It funds across a wide range of areas - arts, children and youth, community welfare, education, environment, health, and religion - and makes hundreds of grants per year.

Collins uses a direct application process rather than an LOI-first model, which makes it more accessible than Meyer for organizations submitting their first institutional grant request. The application asks for standard information: organizational history, program description, budget, and financial statements.

### Writing for Collins

Collins values organizational stability and track record. Proposals should emphasize:

- **Established operations.** Collins generally funds organizations with a demonstrated history of program delivery. Startups and new programs are harder sells unless they're housed within established organizations.
- **Clear, bounded requests.** Unlike Meyer's shift toward operating support, Collins still funds specific programs and projects. A well-defined request with a clear budget - rather than a vague general operating ask - is more competitive.
- **Oregon focus.** Collins funds throughout Oregon, not just Portland. If your work serves rural or underserved Oregon communities, highlight that geographic reach.
- **Financial health.** Collins reviews financial statements. Organizations with clean audits, adequate reserves, and diversified revenue are stronger applicants.

Collins grants typically range from $5,000 to $100,000+, with most grants in the $10,000 to $50,000 range. The foundation makes decisions relatively quickly compared to larger funders.

## Oregon Community Foundation: The Statewide Infrastructure

Oregon Community Foundation (OCF) operates as a statewide community foundation with more than $3 billion in assets. It functions differently from Meyer and Collins because it serves multiple roles: competitive grantmaker, donor-advised fund host, scholarship administrator, and community convener.

OCF's competitive grant programs focus on community priorities that shift over time. Recent priorities have included education equity, economic vitality, and civic engagement. The competitive programs are the most accessible institutional funding channel for smaller and newer Portland-area nonprofits.

### Writing for OCF

- **Check which program you're applying to.** OCF runs multiple competitive programs with different priorities, eligibility criteria, and deadlines. Applying to the wrong program wastes your time and the reviewers'.
- **Use OCF's language.** Each OCF program has published guidelines with specific vocabulary and priorities. Proposals that mirror that language - without being transparently templated - score higher.
- **Demonstrate community need with data.** OCF reviewers respond to evidence-based need statements more than emotional appeals. Use Census data, county health rankings, school district data, and other publicly available sources.
- **Budget realism.** OCF competitive grants are typically $10,000 to $50,000. Scale your request and your project accordingly. A $2M program requesting $15,000 from OCF raises questions about whether the OCF grant matters to the work.

Beyond competitive grants, OCF's value to Portland nonprofits lies in its donor-advised fund network. An organization that receives OCF competitive funding gains visibility with DAF holders who consult OCF staff for grant recommendations.

## Ford Family Foundation: Rural Oregon Focus

The Ford Family Foundation, based in Roseburg, Oregon, is one of the largest foundations in the state but focuses primarily on rural Oregon and Siskiyou County, California. Portland nonprofits with urban-only programs are generally outside its geographic priority.

The exception: Portland-based organizations whose programs serve rural communities. A Portland-headquartered workforce development organization with programming in rural Southern Oregon is a legitimate Ford Family applicant. A Portland-only after-school program is not.

Ford Family funds across education, community development, and scholarship programs. Their Visual Arts Program provides studio residencies for Oregon artists. Grant sizes range from modest project support to substantial multi-year commitments for community development initiatives.

## Murdock Charitable Trust: Pacific Northwest Scope

The M.J. Murdock Charitable Trust operates from Vancouver, Washington - just across the Columbia River from Portland - but funds across the entire Pacific Northwest region: Oregon, Washington, Idaho, Montana, and Alaska. This broader geography means Portland nonprofits compete with organizations from five states.

Murdock focuses on education, scientific research, arts and culture, and health. It is known for funding [capacity-building projects](/resources/guides/how-to-approach-a-foundation), capital campaigns, and programmatic work at established institutions. Grants can be substantial - $50,000 to $500,000 is common for capital and major program grants.

### Writing for Murdock

Murdock uses an inquiry-first process. The initial inquiry determines whether the trust invites a full proposal. Key considerations:

- **Institutional credibility matters.** Murdock tends to fund established organizations with strong governance, clean financials, and demonstrated capacity. Organizations with less than five years of operating history are harder sells.
- **Capital and capacity requests are strengths.** Murdock funds buildings, equipment, technology infrastructure, and organizational capacity-building. These tangible, measurable requests align with the trust's grantmaking style.
- **Scientific rigor.** For research-related proposals, Murdock expects methodological sophistication. The trust has deep expertise in evaluating scientific proposals.
- **Regional impact.** Because Murdock covers five states, proposals should articulate impact beyond a single neighborhood or city. Portland proposals that demonstrate metro-wide or statewide reach are stronger.

## The Portland Grant Writing Discipline

Portland's concentrated funder landscape demands a specific discipline from development directors and grant writers:

**Research before writing.** Read each funder's annual report, recent grants list, and published strategy documents before drafting anything. Portland funders update their priorities more frequently than their application guidelines suggest - the guidelines describe the process, but the strategy documents describe what they actually want to fund right now.

**Manage the portfolio.** Track which funders you've applied to, which are pending, which are active grants with reporting obligations, and which are prospects for future applications. A [grant tracking system](/free/grant-compliance-checklist) prevents the embarrassing and relationship-damaging error of submitting duplicate proposals or missing report deadlines.

**Coordinate messaging.** Because Portland program officers communicate with each other, your organizational narrative must be consistent across applications. If you tell Meyer you're focused on racial equity in housing and tell Collins you're focused on youth arts programming, and those are genuinely separate programs, that's fine. If you're positioning the same work differently to match different funders' priorities, program officers will notice.

**Report on time, every time.** Late or incomplete [grant reports](/resources/guides/nonprofit-financial-report-guide) damage your reputation with the reporting funder and create concern among other Portland funders. Set report deadlines in your calendar 30 days before the actual due date to allow for review and revision.

**Build relationships outside the application cycle.** Attend funder briefings, participate in community convenings organized by OCF or Meyer, and respond to program officer communications promptly. In Portland's small philanthropic community, the relationships built between grant cycles matter as much as the proposals submitted during them.

## Beyond the Big Five

Portland has additional funders worth pursuing based on your program area:

- **Spirit Mountain Community Fund** (Grande Ronde tribal community foundation)
- **Juan Young Trust** (youth-focused, Oregon)
- **Kinsman Foundation** (arts and culture in the Pacific Northwest)
- **Templeton Foundation** (Portland-based family foundation)

These smaller funders fill gaps that the five major foundations don't cover and often have simpler application processes. For a mid-sized Portland nonprofit, a diversified institutional funding strategy includes two or three major foundation relationships supplemented by several smaller funder relationships - all managed through a single [grant management workflow](/resources/guides/grant-prospecting-workflow-for-mid-sized-nonprofits) that keeps deadlines, relationships, and reporting obligations visible.
