## What state grants are available to Florida nonprofits, and how does the funding cycle work?

Florida nonprofits draw from five funding channels that operate on overlapping but distinct calendars: state agency competitive grants funded by the legislature, federal pass-throughs administered through state departments under 2 CFR 200, private and community foundations headquartered in Florida, tribal and regional funders where applicable, and occasional capital appropriations routed through state facilities or infrastructure agencies.

The state funding cycle begins each year with the governor's budget proposal, moves through legislative appropriation during the session, and triggers Notices of Funding Availability (NOFAs) from individual agencies - typically clustered in the spring for awards effective at the start of the July 1-June 30 fiscal year. Federal pass-throughs follow the federal fiscal year (October 1-September 30), which means most Florida nonprofits manage two parallel grant calendars at once.

This guide maps the Florida state grant funder landscape: the major grantmaking agencies, federal pass-through context, top private and community foundations by assets, and tribal funders where relevant. ## Major Florida state grantmaking agencies

### Florida Department of State, Division of Arts and Culture (DOS Arts)

**Programs:** General Program Support, Cultural and Museum Grants, Specific Cultural Project Support.

**Typical award size:** $1,000-$150,000.

**Cycle:** Annual; deadline June 1 for following FY.

**Eligibility:** 501(c)(3) cultural organizations with at least three years of operating history.

**Where to apply:** [https://dos.fl.gov/cultural](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural)

### Florida Department of Children and Families (DCF)

**Programs:** Domestic Violence Program subgrants, Substance Abuse and Mental Health subrecipient contracts, Refugee Services.

**Typical award size:** $100,000-$5,000,000.

**Cycle:** State FY July 1-June 30; RFPs typically March-May.

**Eligibility:** Human services 501(c)(3) organizations.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.myflfamilies.com](https://www.myflfamilies.com)

### Florida Department of Commerce (FloridaCommerce)

**Programs:** Small Cities CDBG, Community Services Block Grant, Rebuild Florida disaster recovery.

**Typical award size:** $200,000-$10,000,000.

**Cycle:** Annual NOFA; CDBG-DR follows federal allocation.

**Eligibility:** Local governments with nonprofit subrecipients.

**Where to apply:** [https://floridajobs.org](https://floridajobs.org)

### Florida Department of Health (DOH)

**Programs:** Closing the Gap, Reducing Racial and Ethnic Health Disparities, Maternal and Child Health subgrants.

**Typical award size:** $50,000-$500,000.

**Cycle:** Annual; state FY.

**Eligibility:** Community-based health organizations.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.floridahealth.gov](https://www.floridahealth.gov)

### Florida Housing Finance Corporation (FHFC)

**Programs:** State Apartment Incentive Loan, HOME, National Housing Trust Fund, Special Needs Housing.

**Typical award size:** $500,000-$15,000,000.

**Cycle:** Annual RFA cycle, multiple windows.

**Eligibility:** Affordable housing developers, including nonprofit.

**Where to apply:** [https://www.floridahousing.org](https://www.floridahousing.org)

## Federal pass-through context

Florida state agencies receive substantial federal funding that is then subgranted to nonprofits as pass-throughs. The major federal funding streams routed through Florida include:

- **CDBG (HUD Community Development Block Grant)** - administered through Florida Department of State, Division of Arts and Culture for non-entitlement areas. Subject to 2 CFR 200 Uniform Guidance and HUD-specific compliance.
- **HOME Investment Partnerships (HUD)** - affordable housing development and rehabilitation, administered through the state housing finance or community affairs agency.
- **Emergency Solutions Grants and Continuum of Care (HUD)** - homeless services. Subject to ESG and CoC regulations on top of 2 CFR 200.
- **Community Services Block Grant (HHS)** - anti-poverty programming through Community Action Agencies and partner nonprofits.
- **VOCA Victim Assistance and VAWA STOP grants (DOJ)** - victim services, administered through the state's criminal justice or attorney general's office.
- **Title V Maternal and Child Health Block Grant (HHS HRSA)** - public health pass-through through Florida's health department.
- **USDA Rural Development programs** - rural-area infrastructure, housing, and community facilities funded through state offices and Cooperative Extension partners.

Every federal pass-through carries the original federal compliance terms: Single Audit threshold ($1,000,000 in federal expenditures triggers a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later), procurement standards, conflict-of-interest rules, and time-and-effort documentation. State agencies typically add additional state-specific reporting on top.

For nonprofits managing pass-through dollars, the [grant compliance 101 guide](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) covers the underlying Uniform Guidance framework that applies regardless of which state agency holds the contract.

## Top Florida-headquartered private and community foundations

The largest private and community foundations headquartered in Florida, by approximate assets:

- **The Patterson Foundation** - Sarasota-based, statewide.
- **The John S. and James L. Knight Foundation** - Miami, national and Florida focus.
- **Community Foundation for Palm Beach and Martin Counties** - southeast Florida community foundation.
- **Community Foundation of Tampa Bay** - Tampa region community foundation.
- **The Helios Education Foundation** - Tampa-based, education focus across FL and AZ.

These are drawn from publicly available IRS Form 990-PF filings and aggregated foundation data. Asset levels and giving patterns shift year over year - verify current figures against the foundation's most recent 990-PF before treating any number as authoritative. The top five typically represent a meaningful share of Florida-originated philanthropic capital, but smaller family and corporate foundations also fund mission-aligned work in specific regions or program areas.

## Tribal and regional funders

Two federally recognized tribes - Seminole Tribe of Florida and Miccosukee Tribe of Indians. Tribal-led organizations can access HHS ANA, HUD ICDBG, and Indian Health Service funding.

Where tribal-led programs apply, the major federal funding streams include HHS Administration for Native Americans (ANA) Social and Economic Development Strategies (SEDS) grants, HUD Indian Community Development Block Grant (ICDBG), and BIA Tribal Government Services. State-level tribal partnerships vary by state.

## How the calendar works

The recurring deadlines table in this guide's frontmatter shows the major Florida state grant submission windows. Three calendar realities shape how Florida nonprofits plan:

1. **Two parallel fiscal years.** State-funded programs follow July 1-June 30; federal pass-throughs follow October 1-September 30. Renewal and reporting deadlines collide.
2. **Spring-heavy NOFA cycle.** Most state agency RFAs and NOFAs publish March through May for awards effective at the start of the next state fiscal year. A development director who is not pipeline-ready by February misses the cluster.
3. **Federal pass-throughs lag federal allocation.** State agencies cannot release a CDBG or HOME NOFA until HUD finalizes the state's allocation, which can push pass-through cycles later than state-funded equivalents.

## What this means for grant management practice

A Florida nonprofit running a representative grant mix - one CDBG pass-through, one state arts council award, two foundation grants from in-state community foundations, and a federal direct grant - manages four reporting cadences, three audit perspectives (state, federal, foundation), and two fiscal year calendars. The compliance overhead is real and grows non-linearly with each new restricted fund.

Three practical implications:

- **Build the calendar before the funder list.** Mapping deadlines and report cycles is the work that catches the slips. A pipeline without a calendar is a wishlist.
- **Track restricted balances per grant, per fund, per FY.** GAAP-aligned restricted fund accounting under FASB ASC 958 is non-negotiable once federal expenditures cross $1,000,000 for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later and a Single Audit is triggered.
- **Document the audit trail before you need it.** Time and effort, procurement, conflict-of-interest, and subaward monitoring documentation must exist contemporaneously, not be reconstructed at audit time.
