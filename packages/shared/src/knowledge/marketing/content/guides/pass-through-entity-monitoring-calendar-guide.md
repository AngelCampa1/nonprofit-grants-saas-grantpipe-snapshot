Subrecipient monitoring breaks when it lives in email. A calendar turns the monitoring plan into dated work with owners.

The calendar should start before the first invoice. It should continue until the subaward is closed and all findings are resolved.

## Start with the subaward terms

List each subrecipient and subaward. Add the period of performance, budget, report dates, invoice cadence, match terms, and special conditions.

Then add the risk rating. A new subrecipient, weak financial controls, late reports, or prior findings should change the monitoring cadence.

Use the [subrecipient risk assessment scoring guide](/resources/guides/subrecipient-risk-assessment-scoring-guide) before you set the dates.

## Add the recurring review points

Most calendars need these items:

- invoice review due date
- program report due date
- financial report due date
- desk review date
- site visit window, if needed
- Single Audit check, if the subrecipient must have one
- corrective action follow-up date

Each item needs an owner. "Finance" is not enough. Name the person or role.

## Tie monitoring to payment

Invoice review should happen before payment. The reviewer should confirm that costs are inside the period, fit the approved budget, and have support.

If the invoice includes costs that need more review, hold that part of the payment and write down the question. Do not let unclear costs become approved costs by habit.

Use the [review subaward invoice workflow](/workflows/review-subaward-invoice-before-payment) for the payment step.

## Track findings and follow-up

Monitoring is not complete when a concern is found. It is complete when the concern is resolved or escalated.

Create follow-up items for missing reports, unsupported costs, late match records, weak participant files, or audit findings. Add the due date, owner, decision, and evidence.

Keep the follow-up record in the grant file. A clean note is often enough: what was found, who reviewed it, what changed, and when it closed.

## Calendar fields to include

Build the calendar so another staff member can step in without asking for the story behind every date. A useful monitoring calendar has these fields:

| Field                               | Why it matters                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Subrecipient name                   | Keeps reports, invoices, and findings tied to the right organization. |
| Federal award or pass-through award | Shows which award terms apply to the subrecipient.                    |
| Risk rating                         | Explains why the monitoring cadence is light, normal, or heavy.       |
| Due date                            | Creates the control point for the task.                               |
| Internal review date                | Gives staff time to review before the external deadline.              |
| Owner                               | Makes the task accountable.                                           |
| Evidence required                   | Tells the owner what must be saved.                                   |
| Follow-up status                    | Shows whether a concern is open or closed.                            |

Do not build the calendar as a list of due dates only. The due date tells staff when something is late. The evidence field tells them what good looks like before the date arrives.

## Risk changes during the year

Risk is not fixed at award setup. Update the calendar when the subrecipient misses a report, changes finance staff, submits weak support, has a finding, or changes the program design.

The update does not need to be dramatic. It can be a note that the next 2 invoices need a deeper review, or that a desk review will be added before the next report. The point is to show that monitoring reacts to facts, not just the original risk score.

For example, a subrecipient may start as low risk. Then its second invoice arrives without payroll support and the program report is 3 weeks late. The calendar should change. Add a follow-up date, require missing support, and review the next invoice before payment.

## What to save for audit support

A monitoring calendar is useful only if it points to records. Save the calendar export or a dated snapshot at least quarterly. Keep these records with it:

- subrecipient risk review
- subaward agreement
- report submissions
- invoice review notes
- desk review notes
- site visit notes, if any
- findings and follow-up
- management decision or close note

If the auditor asks how the organization monitored subrecipients, the answer should not be a verbal summary. It should be a dated calendar and the support behind each completed item.

## Caveats for non-federal pass-through awards

Some pass-through awards do not include federal funds. Others include federal funds but arrive through a state, city, county, or another nonprofit. Read the agreement before choosing the rule set.

If the agreement identifies a federal award, Assistance Listing number, federal agency, or 2 CFR Part 200 terms, treat the monitoring file as a federal compliance file. If the agreement is silent, ask the funder in writing before assuming federal monitoring rules do not apply.

Foundation subgrants may have lighter formal rules, but the calendar still helps. It protects the relationship by making report dates, payment checks, and follow-up visible.
