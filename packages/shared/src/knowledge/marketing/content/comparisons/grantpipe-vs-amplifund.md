## BLUF

AmpliFund and GrantPipe are not competing for the same buyer. AmpliFund is an enterprise grant management platform for state agencies, large public universities, and enterprise recipients. GrantPipe is built for mid-sized nonprofits ($500K-$10M) where the same staff own donor, grant, and finance coordination. Fit is a function of organizational scale, not feature preference.

## TL;DR

- AmpliFund: enterprise GMS, quote-based pricing, vendor-led implementation, no donor CRM.
- GrantPipe: mid-sized nonprofit operating record, flat monthly SaaS, self-serve setup, donor CRM included.
- State agencies and R1 universities legitimately need AmpliFund.
- A $2M nonprofit with three staff legitimately cannot operate AmpliFund.
- The federal compliance obligations under 2 CFR Part 200 apply in both models; the delivery differs.

## Where AmpliFund still fits

AmpliFund is strongest at enterprise scale. A state agency distributing federal passthrough funds to hundreds of subrecipients has real portfolio complexity, multi-program budgeting, and reporting obligations that benefit from a deep enterprise module set. A large public university managing a research award portfolio has similar structural requirements.

For those profiles, AmpliFund's implementation investment is justified because the organization has the staff, budget, and procurement model to absorb it.

## Where GrantPipe wins

GrantPipe wins for the organization where the executive director, development director, and finance director are three people who also carry most of the operational work. That is the normal shape of a $500K-$10M nonprofit.

In that environment, the expensive problem is not the depth of an enterprise module. It is the recurring cost of splitting donor context, grant status, restricted balances, and compliance deadlines across separate tools and rebuilding the picture every month.

## Verdict

Choose AmpliFund when the organization is an enterprise recipient with a dedicated grants office and 20+ concurrent awards. Choose GrantPipe when the organization is a mid-sized nonprofit where the bottleneck is cross-functional coordination, not enterprise-grade portfolio depth.

## Scale matters more than feature lists

A common procurement mistake at mid-sized nonprofits is benchmarking against enterprise features the organization will never operate. A $3M nonprofit with two subrecipients does not need a 50-subrecipient monitoring module. It needs a clean way to track restricted balances, SF-425 cadence, and donor context together.

The opposite mistake happens too. A state agency cannot run its portfolio on a tool built for 5-10 concurrent awards because the subrecipient, performance, and budget modules are structurally different at scale.

## Federal compliance in both models

Both products sit on top of the same federal compliance obligations:

- SF-425 Federal Financial Reports due 30 days after each calendar quarter
- Final SF-425 due 90 days after the grant period ends
- Single Audit required at $1,000,000 in federal expenditures
- 3-year record retention under 2 CFR 200.334
- FFATA subaward reporting above $30,000 into USASpending.gov via FSRS
- 2 CFR 200.332 subrecipient monitoring requirements for prime recipients

What differs is how the obligations are carried in the product. GrantPipe treats SF-425 cadence and restricted balances as first-class objects in the same record as the donor. AmpliFund delivers these through configurable enterprise modules.

## Pricing reality

GrantPipe published self-serve pricing flat SaaS with no required implementation retainer. AmpliFund pricing is quote-based enterprise contracting; public procurement records and nonprofit reviews indicate typical annual totals of $15,000-$60,000 or more, plus implementation. These are not competing price points; they signal different target customers.
