## The Budget-Tier CRM Decision

Keela and Little Green Light occupy the affordable end of the nonprofit CRM market. Both are designed for organizations that need more structure than a spreadsheet but cannot justify Bloomerang, Blackbaud, or Salesforce pricing.

For executive directors at small nonprofits - annual revenue under $2M, development staff of one or two people - this comparison is often the practical one.

## Little Green Light: The Value Case

Little Green Light has been serving small nonprofits for over a decade. Its pricing is flat-rate, not contact-based: you pay the same whether you have 500 or 2,000 records. At $45-$119/mo, it is the most affordable option in this comparison that still provides a real donor database.

The interface is dated. It does not win usability awards. But for an ED who needs basic donor tracking, giving history, and simple reports, it does the job at a price that does not strain a tight operating budget.

There is no grant management module. That is a complete absence.

## Keela: The Modern Interface Case

Keela's interface is the most modern of the two. Email campaigns, donor profiles, and giving analysis are presented in a clean, accessible format that development staff tend to adopt quickly. The basic grant tracking module is a genuine differentiator against Little Green Light.

The limitation is that "basic grant tracking" means deadline reminders and status fields - not compliance workflows. For an organization managing government grants with restricted fund requirements, Keela's grant module is not sufficient for compliance accountability.

## The Price Reality

At $99-$299/mo, Keela is 2-3x the cost of Little Green Light. For a nonprofit running a $500,000 annual budget, that difference?$600-$2,400/yr - is material.

The question for the executive director is: does Keela's usability improvement and basic grant tracking justify the premium over Little Green Light?

If your development staff is technology-comfortable and the interface difference will not affect adoption, Little Green Light's lower price may be the right call. If you have turnover in development staff or anticipate training challenges, Keela's more intuitive interface may reduce the recurring cost of onboarding new staff.

## What Both Miss

Neither platform addresses the compliance requirements that accompany government grants, federal funding, or foundation grants with restricted use provisions. If grant compliance is part of your operational accountability as ED, both platforms leave that gap unaddressed.

## How to decide between these two options

Keela vs Little Green Light for Executive Directors: Budget CRM Comparison should be evaluated against the operating model of your team, not just a feature table. The first question is where complexity lives today. If the organization needs clean donor workflows with minimal setup, ease of use and deployment time matter more than theoretical customization. If the organization has complex approvals, long reporting cycles, and dedicated technical capacity, flexibility may justify the heavier setup burden.

A useful comparison frame is this: what does each platform require after purchase to become trustworthy for board packets, funder updates, and month-end reporting? Some tools look competitive until the team realizes every grant-related workflow still lives outside the system. Others offer broad customization but require paid administration before they become stable.

## What to verify in the demo

Ask each vendor to walk through the same sequence: create a record, update a restricted or grant-related status, generate a leadership-ready report, and explain what happens when staff responsibilities change. That workflow exposes whether the product supports a repeatable process or only stores data. If the answer relies on exports, consultant-built objects, or manual spreadsheet reconciliation, the total cost and execution risk are higher than the headline comparison suggests.
