## Definition

CharityEngine is an all-in-one nonprofit CRM and fundraising platform offering native multi-channel fundraising (online, direct mail integration, telefundraising, peer-to-peer, events) and marketing automation. GrantPipe is a unified donor + grant + restricted fund + compliance operating record for mid-sized nonprofits ($500K-$10M) with active grant work.

## BLUF

These products are both wider than a basic CRM, and both market to mid-market nonprofits. They diverge on which job they take seriously: CharityEngine puts fundraising channels and marketing automation at the center; GrantPipe puts grant compliance and unified operations at the center. The right choice tracks the revenue mix.

## Where CharityEngine still fits

CharityEngine is built for organizations where direct-response and multi-channel fundraising drive revenue. The platform has native online giving, direct mail file production, telefundraising support, peer-to-peer fundraising, advocacy, and events - all wired into the same CRM rather than stitched from third-party tools.

If the organization runs annual mail appeals, monthly email campaigns to large lists, peer-to-peer events, and advocacy actions, that integration matters. The volume of cross-channel attribution and segmentation work in those operations is real, and a generic CRM-plus-third-party-tool stack creates friction that CharityEngine eliminates.

## Where GrantPipe wins

GrantPipe wins for the mid-sized nonprofit where grants represent a substantial share of revenue and the operational pain is on the grant + compliance side. A $4M nonprofit running three federal grants and a foundation portfolio has a different daily workflow than a $4M direct-mail-driven nonprofit:

- Federal reports due quarterly, with cascading consequences for being late
- Restricted-fund balances that program managers need to read in real time
- Subrecipient monitoring under 2 CFR 200.332
- Single-audit prep at the $1M federal expenditure threshold
- Donor cultivation and grant compliance owned by overlapping staff

CharityEngine's marketing automation depth is irrelevant to that work. GrantPipe's grant lifecycle and compliance workflow is exactly the work.

## Revenue mix as the deciding factor

Use the actual revenue mix, not the website tour, to choose:

- 70%+ revenue from direct-response and individual giving with high contact volume †’ CharityEngine
- 50%+ revenue from grants (foundation, government, federal) with active compliance obligations †’ GrantPipe
- Balanced mix at substantial scale ($5M+) †’ most organizations end up with two tools and accept the integration cost

## Pricing

CharityEngine is quote-based enterprise contracting. Public review platforms and procurement records suggest annual contracts of $10,000-$60,000+ depending on contact volume, modules, and channels. Implementation is typically vendor-led.

GrantPipe is flat-rate SaaS at published self-serve pricing for self-serve plans. The price difference reflects scope difference, not product quality.

## What both miss

Neither product replaces full financial statement work. GrantPipe does not connect to outside accounting tools right now. Keep Aplos, QuickBooks, Sage Intacct, or NetSuite as a separate workflow.

CharityEngine's grant tracking, where present, is shallow. GrantPipe's marketing automation, where present, is light. Picking either product means accepting that the other side will be done with a complementary tool or done less.

## Verdict

This is one of the comparisons where the right answer is dictated by what the organization actually does for revenue. A direct-response nonprofit will be miserable in GrantPipe; a grants-heavy nonprofit will be miserable paying CharityEngine prices for fundraising channels it does not run.
