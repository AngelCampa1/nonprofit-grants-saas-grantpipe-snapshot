## What AmpliFund Does Well

AmpliFund built a product specifically for federal grant compliance. Budget versus actuals reporting tied to grant line items, Uniform Guidance awareness, and workflows that understand SF-425 are genuine strengths. Government agencies and large nonprofits managing $10M+ in federal funds get real value from that depth.

Since the Bonterra acquisition, AmpliFund is part of a larger nonprofit software portfolio. That has brought new integrations and also new sales and pricing dynamics.

## Who This Alternative Is For

You are probably evaluating AmpliFund because your finance team flagged federal grant compliance as a risk or because a funder asked for better documentation. If your organization has a $500K to $10M budget and federal grants are one slice of your funding mix (not all of it), AmpliFund is usually more platform than you need.

## Where AmpliFund Falls Short for Mid-Sized Nonprofits

**Enterprise pricing anchored to government agency budgets.** AmpliFund does not publish pricing. Government RFP responses and G2 estimates place annual contracts at $15,000 to $50,000+, with implementation fees adding $5,000 to $20,000. That sizing assumes a government or larger-nonprofit buyer.

**No donor CRM.** AmpliFund is pure grant compliance. You need a separate donor CRM for individual giving, which means another subscription and another integration.

**Implementation is a project.** Customers describe 60 to 120 day implementations with dedicated project management on both sides. For a small finance team, that is a real cost beyond the contract price.

**Parent-company dynamics.** Bonterra's portfolio includes multiple overlapping products (AmpliFund, EveryAction, Salsa, Network for Good). Buyers sometimes end up routed toward bundled packages that add cost without adding capability.

## When to Stay With AmpliFund

Stay with AmpliFund if you are a government agency distributing federal pass-through funds, a state grants office, or a nonprofit above $25M in budget with federal grants as your primary funding stream. Stay with AmpliFund if your IT team has already built integrations and workflows on the platform that would be expensive to rebuild.

Switch to GrantPipe if you are a mid-sized nonprofit with a mixed funding model (individual giving plus federal and foundation grants) and you need both donor CRM and compliance in one system.

## Migration Path

AmpliFund exports contact, opportunity, award, and expenditure data via CSV. GrantPipe's import templates accept those exports and map to donors, grants, funds, and expenditures. Because both tools model Uniform Guidance natively, the compliance configuration carries over cleanly: budget categories, match requirements, and reporting cadences.

Allocate two to four weeks for a full migration, including parallel running for one reporting cycle to confirm the numbers reconcile.

## Questions to Answer Before Switching

1. What percentage of your total revenue comes from federal grants? If under 40 percent, AmpliFund's depth is probably mismatched to your mix.
2. What do you pay today in combined AmpliFund plus donor CRM subscriptions? If over $15,000 per year, consolidating to GrantPipe is usually a net savings.
3. How long does your current federal financial reporting cycle take? If more than a day per report, a purpose-built workflow will recover meaningful staff time.
