Keela is a Canadian nonprofit CRM that has been growing its grant management feature set. The grant tracking functionality covers the pre-award development workflow competently: grant records, deadline reminders, pipeline status, and funder relationship tracking that links grants to the organization's donor records.

For development teams managing twenty or thirty grant applications per year, this is functional. It provides visibility into what is in the pipeline, when deadlines are due, and which grants are active versus closed.

The limitation is the post-award side. When a grant is awarded and the compliance clock starts - when a funder requires a financial report showing how restricted funds were spent, when a budget vs. actual comparison needs to go to an auditor, when a federal grant requires drawdown documentation - Keela's grant management tools are not designed for those workflows.

## What Keela's Grant Module Does

Keela's grant management centers on the development team's needs:

- Grant records connected to funder (donor) records
- Pipeline stages and deadline tracking
- Notes and document attachments on grant records
- Reporting on grant revenue and application success rates

This is useful for tracking the development cycle. It answers questions like: how many grants are in the application stage, which funders do we have relationships with, what deadlines are coming up this quarter.

## Where Keela Stops

The compliance workflow starts where Keela stops. Once a grant is awarded:

Post-award compliance requires tracking actual expenditures against the approved grant budget by line item - not just total spending, but whether personnel costs are within the personnel budget line, whether supplies spending matches the approved supplies budget, and whether any category is approaching its approved limit.

Restricted fund accounting requires maintaining a fund balance that updates with every posted expense - so that at any moment, finance staff can see how much of a specific restricted grant remains and whether the restriction is on track to be satisfied.

Funder reporting requires generating a financial report that shows the funder exactly how their money was spent, in the format they require. Most funders want budget vs. actual by budget category, for the reporting period and cumulative from award start. This report should come from the accounting system, not from manual assembly.

None of these functions are in Keela. Organizations managing multiple active restricted grants end up running Keela for development and a separate system - or spreadsheets - for compliance.

## The Case for Switching

The trigger for looking beyond Keela is almost always a compliance pressure that development-only grant tracking cannot absorb: a federal grant, an audit finding about restricted fund documentation, a funder that asks for a financial report format that requires pulling data from multiple sources.

GrantPipe's design addresses exactly this transition: the point where grant management expands from a development function to a cross-team compliance function involving finance, program staff, and leadership. The donor CRM side handles what development needs. The compliance and fund accounting side handles what finance needs. Both layers sit on the same record, eliminating the reconciliation step that Keela's two-system workflow makes unavoidable.
