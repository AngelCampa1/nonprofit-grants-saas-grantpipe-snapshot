Network for Good built its reputation as an accessible fundraising platform for small and mid-sized nonprofits. Its donation pages, peer-to-peer fundraising tools, and email outreach features were straightforward enough for teams without a dedicated technology person. That reputation came from the product as it existed before 2022.

In 2022, Bonterra acquired Network for Good as part of a multi-platform consolidation that also brought in EveryAction, CommunityForce, and Social Solutions. Post-acquisition, the product roadmap, support contracts, and pricing structure are managed by Bonterra. Published pricing disappeared. Reviews written before 2022 describe a different product structure than what current prospects encounter in a sales conversation.

## What Network for Good Does

The platform focuses on fundraising engagement. Donation pages, fundraising campaigns, peer-to-peer fundraising, and donor email tools are the core features. For small nonprofits running annual fund campaigns or event-based fundraising, this covers the main workflow.

Donor communication tools are reasonable. Email templates, automated acknowledgment emails, and basic donor records give smaller organizations a starting point for relationship management.

The connection to the broader Bonterra ecosystem means larger organizations can eventually access EveryAction's more advanced constituent management tools, though that represents a separate product and contract.

## What Network for Good Has Never Included

Grant management was not part of Network for Good before the acquisition and is not part of it now. The platform has no grant prospect tracking, no grant lifecycle stages, no restricted fund tracking, and no compliance reporting.

Nonprofits that receive restricted grants from foundations or government sources need to track how each restricted dollar is spent, compare expenditures against approved grant budgets, and produce reports showing funders that their funds were used as intended. Network for Good provides no mechanism for any of this.

Transaction fees on processed donations add another variable to the total cost. These are negotiated in the custom contract rather than disclosed in any public pricing, so organizations cannot compare the true cost of Network for Good against alternatives without going through a full sales process.

## How GrantPipe Compares

GrantPipe publishes pricing for Starter, Growth, and Audit-Ready. Starter covers donor management, grant pipeline tracking, deadline reminders, the compliance calendar, Program Allocation management, basic restricted-fund visibility, budget-vs-actual exports, and 990 export templates. Growth adds spend-down threshold alerts, the full restriction lifecycle, the compliance report pack, and outcome tracking. Audit-Ready adds the restriction evidence package output, advanced fund accounting, financial statements and board-ready outputs, and guided onboarding.

No transaction fees. No setup fees. No sales call required to see what you will pay.

Network for Good fits nonprofits whose primary activity is campaign-based fundraising with no grant compliance requirements. For organizations that manage restricted grants alongside donor relationships, GrantPipe handles both in one system at a predictable cost.

## Why teams start looking for an alternative

An alternatives search usually means the current system is not failing everywhere. It is failing at one repeated moment: implementation takes too long, reporting requires workarounds, or the product handles donor management but not the grant and compliance layer sitting beside it. That distinction matters because the replacement should be chosen based on the workflow gap, not on general dissatisfaction.

For nonprofit teams, the most common trigger is operational fragmentation. Staff can still enter data, but they cannot get from transaction to funder report without rebuilding context in another tool. When that happens, switching only makes sense if the next system reduces coordination work across development, finance, and leadership rather than moving the same problem into a different interface.

## Questions to answer before switching

Before replacing the incumbent, document the three reports or workflows that currently create the most delay. Then test whether the alternative handles them natively, how long migration will take, and what staff training is required after go-live. A credible alternative should lower reporting effort within the first quarter, not create another long implementation phase that postpones the benefit of switching.
