Domestic violence shelters operate at the intersection of victim services, housing, legal advocacy, and crisis response -- each funded by a different grant program with different compliance requirements. The result is one of the most complex compliance environments in the nonprofit sector, made more challenging by the confidentiality obligations that restrict how survivor data can be tracked and reported.

## VAWA Funding: Program Requirements and Confidentiality

VAWA formula grants flow from the Office on Violence Against Women through state administering agencies to local programs. VAWA-funded activities must fall within approved categories: direct services to survivors, training for service providers, coordination with legal and law enforcement systems, and prevention programming. Shelters with VAWA funding must document how each expense aligns with an approved activity.

The confidentiality provisions of VAWA are statutory requirements that apply to all VAWA grantees. These provisions prohibit shelters from disclosing personally identifying information about survivors without informed, written, reasonably time-limited consent. Critically, confidentiality extends to grant records -- shelters cannot maintain grant files in ways that link identifiable survivor information to service records accessible to outside reviewers.

This creates a data architecture requirement: program tracking systems must be designed to report aggregate service data to funders without exposing individual survivor records.

## VOCA Victim Assistance Grants

VOCA grants fund direct services to crime victims, with domestic violence shelters representing a major category of VOCA-funded providers. VOCA grantees must comply with state-level performance measurement requirements (each state sets its own metrics) plus OVC's national performance measurement framework.

Performance reporting typically requires aggregate data on: victims served by crime type, services provided by service category, demographic breakdown of clients, and outcome data on client safety and stability. These reports require aggregate counts and rates -- not individual case data -- which requires careful data collection practices throughout the year.

VOCA funds have restrictions on administrative costs (typically capped at 10% of the award) and require that a significant portion of funding support direct services. Shelters must track whether their expenditure patterns remain within these constraints.

## Managing Multiple Federal Programs Simultaneously

Most domestic violence shelters operate with a portfolio of VAWA, VOCA, FVPSA, and state funding simultaneously. Each has different period of performance dates, different allowable cost categories, and different reporting timelines. VAWA progress reports are semiannual through OVW's GMS. VOCA reports go to the state administering agency on a schedule set by the state. FVPSA reporting has its own requirements.

Managing this portfolio in a shared spreadsheet creates serious reconciliation risk. When a shelter director's salary is split across VAWA, VOCA, and general operating funds, three separate allocations must be tracked and documented. If those allocations are maintained in different places, assembling consistent documentation for a site visit becomes a crisis-level exercise.

Grant management software with fund-level accounting tracks each expenditure to its source award, generates the documentation required for each funder's reporting format, and maintains the audit trail that demonstrates compliance with each program's requirements.

## Restricted Fund Accounting for Victim Services

The restricted fund tracking challenges for domestic violence shelters parallel those of other federal grantees but are amplified by the confidentiality dimension. Standard fund accounting practice -- segregating each grant's receipts and expenditures -- applies here, but the tracking must be done in a way that does not create grant records linking individual survivors to specific funded services.

Organizations that use a single case management database for both service delivery and grant compliance tracking risk creating exactly this kind of linkage. Purpose-built grant compliance systems allow shelters to track program metrics for grant reporting purposes in a separate layer from case management records, satisfying both compliance reporting requirements and confidentiality obligations.
