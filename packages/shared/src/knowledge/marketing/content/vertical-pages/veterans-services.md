Veterans service organizations navigate a federal grant landscape that spans multiple agencies with distinct program requirements. A mid-sized organization providing housing assistance, employment services, and case management to veterans may simultaneously manage VA SSVF grants, DOL employment program funding, and HUD-VASH service contracts -- each subject to different compliance frameworks, different performance metrics, and different audit standards.

## VA SSVF: Housing Assistance and Rapid Rehousing

The VA Supportive Services for Veteran Families program is the primary federal grant program for nonprofits providing homeless prevention and rapid rehousing to veterans. SSVF grants are substantial awards -- often $500K to $3M+ for larger programs -- with detailed program requirements set out in the annual SSVF Program Guide.

SSVF compliance begins with eligibility determination. Each participant must meet SSVF veteran household eligibility criteria: at minimum one household member must be a veteran with military service verified by a DD-214 or VA records, and the household must meet income eligibility thresholds. Documentation of eligibility must be in the case file before SSVF funds are expended on behalf of that participant.

Financial compliance requires tracking SSVF expenditures separately from other program funding. SSVF has defined allowable cost categories -- financial assistance, case management, outreach, training -- each with its own limits. Organizations must document how staff time is allocated between SSVF-funded activities and other program activities.

## DOL Veterans Employment Programs

The Department of Labor funds veterans employment services through several programs, with Jobs for Veterans State Grants (JVSG) as the primary formula grant flowing to states, which then contract with local service providers. DOL also funds Homeless Veterans Reintegration Program (HVRP) grants directly to nonprofits for employment services to homeless veterans.

DOL performance measurement requirements focus on employment outcomes. Unlike VA grants that focus on housing stability, DOL grants require tracking job placements, wages at placement, and retention at both the 2nd and 4th quarter after placement. This requires an ongoing tracking relationship with program participants that extends months beyond their initial service period -- a data management challenge that spreadsheets handle poorly.

## Multi-Agency Program Coordination

Veterans service organizations often provide integrated services -- housing assistance funded by VA, employment services funded by DOL, and case management funded by HHS or state contracts -- to the same participant. This integration creates compliance complexity: each funding source applies its own eligibility standards, allowable cost rules, and reporting requirements.

When a case manager's time is split across VA-funded and DOL-funded activities, that cost must be allocated to the correct program. When a client simultaneously receives SSVF housing assistance and HVRP employment services, the organization must demonstrate that each program's funds supported that program's specific activities.

Grant management software that tracks funding source at the cost and service-delivery level -- not just at the program level -- provides the documentation infrastructure needed to manage multi-agency funding for the same participant population.

## Veteran Eligibility Documentation

Veterans grants have participant eligibility documentation requirements that other nonprofit grant programs typically do not. VA grants require veteran status verification (DD-214 for most veterans, alternative documentation for some service periods), income documentation, and in some programs, documentation of housing status (literal homelessness or at-risk of homelessness).

Organizations must collect and maintain this documentation before expending grant funds on behalf of a participant, and must retain it for the required period after the grant's period of performance closes. Eligibility documentation gaps are a primary audit finding in VA SSVF reviews.
