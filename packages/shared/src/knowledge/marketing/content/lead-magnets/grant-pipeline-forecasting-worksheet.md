## Why Grant Forecasting Is Hard

Grant revenue is different from individual donor revenue in ways that make standard forecasting approaches unreliable.

**Multi-year awards create false certainty.** When a foundation awards a three-year, $150,000 grant, organizations often book all $150,000 as "secured" revenue and plan accordingly. But years two and three of a multi-year grant are usually contingent on satisfactory performance, continued funder interest, and the funder's own financial situation - none of which is guaranteed. Booking future years at 100% probability is an error that can create a significant budget crisis when a funder doesn't renew.

**Renewal uncertainty compounds over time.** An organization's track record with a funder is the strongest predictor of renewal, but even strong relationships end. Foundation priorities shift. Staff change. Economic conditions affect endowment performance, which affects grantmaking capacity. A grant in year three of a multi-year award is not the same as a grant that has been reliably renewed for seven consecutive years.

**Application timing is unpredictable.** Grant cycles don't align with fiscal years. A grant applied for in November may not be decided until March. A prospect identified in January may have a next deadline in October. Managing grant revenue expectations requires translating application timelines into fiscal year projections - which introduces another layer of uncertainty.

**The "applied for" problem.** Many organizations report "grants applied for" as a meaningful pipeline metric. It isn't. An application is not a revenue forecast - it is an expression of interest. Without probability weighting, a pipeline of $1.2M in applications tells you almost nothing useful about what you'll actually receive.

A stage-weighted pipeline model solves all of these problems by making probability explicit, building multi-year discounting into the methodology, and giving your board a number they can actually use.

## Pipeline Stage Definitions and Probability Weights

The following stage definitions and probability weights reflect the typical grant decision lifecycle. Your organization may need to adjust weights based on your historical win rates - if you have three or more years of pipeline data, compare your actual award rate at each stage to these benchmarks and calibrate accordingly.

| Stage                     | Definition                                                                                                         | Probability Weight |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| **Prospect**              | Funder identified as potential fit; no research completed yet; no relationship established                         | 5%                 |
| **Research**              | Funder researched; confirmed alignment with your program areas; grant amount estimated; next deadline identified   | 15%                |
| **Concept / LOI**         | Concept paper or letter of inquiry submitted; awaiting invitation to apply or feedback                             | 30%                |
| **Application Submitted** | Full application submitted; awaiting decision                                                                      | 50%                |
| **Under Review**          | Application confirmed as under active review (program officer contact, site visit scheduled, references requested) | 70%                |
| **Renewal In Progress**   | Active grant in final year; renewal application submitted or in preparation; prior relationship with funder        | 80%                |
| **Awarded**               | Grant agreement received and executed                                                                              | 100%               |
| **Declined / Dead**       | Application declined or funder confirmed not a fit; remove from pipeline                                           | 0%                 |

**Calibrating to your organization's win rate:**

The 50% weight for "Application Submitted" reflects a rough sector average for nonprofits applying to funders with prior relationships and reasonable fit. If your organization's historical win rate at application stage is 35%, use 35%. If it's 65% (perhaps because you only apply when you have strong relationships), use 65%. The weights are calibrated anchors, not fixed constants.

**Why "Under Review" is higher than "Application Submitted":**

Being under active review - as evidenced by program officer outreach, site visits, or reference checks - is a meaningful positive signal. Funders that request a site visit or check references are investing in the relationship; they accept a minority of applications but site-visit an even smaller percentage. The 70% weight reflects this signal value.

## The Weighted Pipeline Formula

The core calculation is straightforward:

**Weighted pipeline value = Sum of (grant amount × probability weight) for all active opportunities**

Example pipeline calculation:

| Opportunity              | Funder                     | Stage                 | Grant Amount | Probability | Weighted Value |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------ | ----------- | -------------- |
| Youth Program Support    | Smith Family Foundation    | Awarded               | $40,000      | 100%        | $40,000        |
| Literacy Initiative      | State Arts Council         | Under Review          | $25,000      | 70%         | $17,500        |
| Workforce Dev. Grant     | Federal DOL (pass-through) | Application Submitted | $75,000      | 50%         | $37,500        |
| Capacity Building        | Community Foundation       | Concept / LOI         | $20,000      | 30%         | $6,000         |
| Capital Campaign Support | Anonymous Foundation       | Research              | $50,000      | 15%         | $7,500         |
| Housing Services         | Peterson Foundation        | Prospect              | $30,000      | 5%          | $1,500         |
| **Total**                |                            |                       | **$240,000** | -           | **$110,000**   |

In this example, the total pipeline of $240,000 has a weighted value of $110,000. This is the number to use for revenue planning, not $240,000.

**What this number means for your budget:**

The weighted pipeline value is a probability-adjusted estimate of what you will actually receive from your current active opportunities. It is not a guarantee. It is the expected value - the number you would expect to land on average across many similar pipelines.

For budgeting, conservative organizations use 80-90% of their weighted pipeline value as the budgeted grant revenue figure. This provides a buffer for cases where an award is delayed by a quarter or a funder surprises you with a smaller award than expected.

## Handling Multi-Year Awards

Multi-year grant awards require a specific treatment to avoid the false certainty problem described at the top of this guide.

**Year 1 of a multi-year award:**

Treat like any other grant in the "Awarded" stage - 100% probability, full year 1 amount in the current year's forecast.

**Year 2 of a multi-year award:**

Apply a renewal discount of 20-30% to reflect the risk that the second year is contingent on performance and funder circumstances. Treat as a separate pipeline entry at 75-80% probability.

**Year 3+ of a multi-year award:**

Apply a steeper discount - 35-45% - to reflect increasing uncertainty. Treat as a separate pipeline entry at 55-65% probability.

**Example multi-year treatment:**

A foundation awards a 3-year, $30,000/year grant ($90,000 total):

| Year             | Amount      | Stage / Probability         | Weighted Value |
| ---------------- | ----------- | --------------------------- | -------------- |
| Year 1 (current) | $30,000     | Awarded - 100%              | $30,000        |
| Year 2           | $30,000     | Multi-year contingent - 80% | $24,000        |
| Year 3           | $30,000     | Multi-year contingent - 65% | $19,500        |
| **Total**        | **$90,000** | -                           | **$73,500**    |

The $90,000 total award should appear in your grant records as context. The $73,500 weighted value should appear in your revenue forecast. The difference represents the risk you are carrying.

## Board Reporting Format

Boards need to understand your pipeline without wading through individual grant entries. The standard three-column format works well:

| Category                                                     | Total Amount | Weighted Value |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------ | -------------- |
| **Awarded** (executed agreements)                            | $\_\_\_\_    | $\_\_\_\_      |
| **Under Review / Submitted** (applications pending decision) | $\_\_\_\_    | $\_\_\_\_      |
| **Early Stage** (prospect, research, concept)                | $\_\_\_\_    | $\_\_\_\_      |
| **Renewals in Progress**                                     | $\_\_\_\_    | $\_\_\_\_      |
| **Total Active Pipeline**                                    | $\_\_\_\_    | $\_\_\_\_      |
| **Budgeted Grant Revenue**                                   |              | $\_\_\_\_      |
| **Pipeline vs. Budget Variance**                             |              | $\_\_\_\_      |

Provide this summary quarterly. When the weighted value drops below your budgeted grant revenue target, it is a signal to either accelerate cultivation of late-stage prospects or add new prospects to replenish the early-stage pipeline.

**Narrative guidance for the board:**

Include a brief pipeline narrative alongside the numbers: which major opportunities changed stage during the quarter, any significant wins or declines, and whether the pipeline composition (distribution across stages) looks healthy relative to the revenue target. A pipeline that is heavily weighted toward early-stage prospects is a warning sign - it means you may not have enough high-probability opportunities to meet your revenue target within the fiscal year.

## Common Mistakes in Grant Pipeline Management

**Counting the full multi-year award at 50% probability.**

If a funder is "considering" a three-year, $90,000 grant, the entry should not be $90,000 at 50%. It should be year 1 at $30,000 x 50% = $15,000 (current year), and no entry for years 2-3 until year 1 is awarded. Counting future years at partial probability inflates your pipeline and creates misleading revenue projections.

**Leaving stale opportunities in the pipeline.**

A prospect that has been in "Research" for eight months without movement is probably dead. Establish a rule: opportunities that have not progressed in 90 days require a documented reason to remain in the pipeline. Remove declined, stale, and inactive opportunities promptly - they inflate the headline number without adding forecast value.

**Using the pipeline for strategy instead of projection.**

The pipeline worksheet is a forecasting tool, not a strategic plan. It shows the current value of current opportunities. A good pipeline does not tell you whether your grant strategy is sound - for that, you need to look at which funders you are approaching, whether those funders are a genuine fit, and whether your proposal quality is aligned with your win rate targets.

**Forecasting without history.**

New organizations or organizations new to pipeline tracking often apply probability weights without historical calibration. After 2-3 years of pipeline data, compare your actual awards at each stage to the probability weights. If your organization wins only 30% of submitted applications (rather than 50%), adjust. If your under-review win rate is 85%, adjust up. The weights in this worksheet are starting points, not permanent fixtures.

## Quarterly Review Cadence

Grant pipelines need active management, not passive recording. Establish a quarterly review cadence:

**What to do every quarter:**

- Update stage for every active opportunity (has anything progressed, stalled, or been declined?)
- Remove declined and dead opportunities
- Add new prospects identified during the quarter
- Recalculate weighted pipeline value and compare to budget
- Prepare the three-column board summary
- Identify the top 3 opportunities by weighted value and confirm that cultivation activities are on track

**When to escalate:**

If the weighted pipeline value falls below your budgeted grant revenue target with less than six months remaining in the fiscal year, escalate to the Executive Director immediately. Closing a grant pipeline gap in six months requires accelerating applications already in progress (which is possible) or adding early-stage prospects that will mature in the next fiscal year (which helps next year but not this year). Understanding this timeline allows the board to make informed decisions about contingency planning.

**When to celebrate:**

Pipeline management tends to be framed around gaps and risks. It is worth naming wins explicitly in the board report: a high-probability opportunity that was awarded, a prospect that advanced two stages in a quarter, a renewal that came in above the prior year award. Grant development is a long-cycle activity and the feedback loops are slow; making wins visible keeps the development team motivated and keeps the board's confidence calibrated.
