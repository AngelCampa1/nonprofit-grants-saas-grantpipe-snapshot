## Why Minnesota Compliance Is Worth Calendaring Now

Minnesota AG actively pursues delinquent charities. Public registry status appears on the AG website and is checked by foundation grantmakers. Every Minnesota nonprofit that solicits contributions has the same three obligations: an initial registration filed before solicitation begins, a recurring Charitable Organization Annual Report renewal at the cadence the Minnesota Attorney General Charities Division sets, and the financial schedules and disclosures the statute requires. None of those are dramatic on their own. They become dramatic when one slips and a foundation grantmaker checks public registry status before approving an award.

The governing law in Minnesota is Minn. Stat. ch. 309 (Charitable Solicitation Act). The administrative portal is https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Charity/. Fees, deadlines, and forms can change between fiscal years - confirm against the source before each cycle.

This checklist is structured the way the work actually flows: confirm your registration trigger, complete the initial registration, calendar the recurring renewal, manage the audit threshold, and handle closeout. Each step links to the form name and authority so you can hand it to a colleague without re-explaining context. For broader grant compliance scope beyond charitable registration, see our [Grant Compliance 101 for Nonprofits](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) guide and the [Minnesota nonprofit software overview](/nonprofit-software/minnesota).

## Step 1: Confirm Whether You Must Register

- [ ] **Determine the registration trigger.** Most Minnesota 501(c)(3) charities that solicit contributions from Minnesota residents must register with the Minnesota Attorney General Charities Division under Minn. Stat. ch. 309 (Charitable Solicitation Act).
- [ ] **Check exemption categories.** Religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, certain government-affiliated entities, and small organizations under specific revenue thresholds may be exempt. Read the statute, do not rely on assumption.
- [ ] **Document any claimed exemption.** If you believe you are exempt, keep written documentation of the category and supporting facts. Some exemptions require an affirmative filing with the Minnesota Attorney General; others apply automatically but still require records if questioned.
- [ ] **Confirm out-of-state solicitation rules.** A nonprofit headquartered outside Minnesota that runs an email campaign or direct mail to Minnesota residents is generally soliciting in Minnesota for registration purposes.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration

- [ ] **Visit the Minnesota Attorney General portal at https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Charity/.**
- [ ] **Gather required attachments**:
- [ ] Articles of Incorporation (or equivalent founding document)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] List of officers, directors, and key employees with addresses
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $25 initial registration
- [ ] **Submit registration before any solicitation begins.** Many Minnesota statutes treat solicitation before registration as a separate violation from late renewal - registering after the fact does not cure the initial period.
- [ ] **Record the assigned registration number.** Use it on every subsequent filing and donor disclosure.

## Step 3: Calendar the Charitable Organization Annual Report

The recurring Charitable Organization Annual Report keeps registration active. Missing it is the most common cause of suspension across every state with a charitable registry.

- [ ] **Charitable Organization Annual Report due date:** July 15 (or 6.5 months after fiscal year end for non-calendar filers). Enter this date in a shared calendar - not just the ED's personal calendar.
- [ ] **Confirm the fee tier:** $25 annual report fee. Tiered fees scale with gross revenue or contributions; budget for the higher tier the year you cross a threshold.
- [ ] **Attach the correct financial schedule:**
- IRS Form 990 (or 990-EZ for smaller organizations; 990-PF for private foundations)
- Audited financial statement when revenue crosses the audit threshold
- State-specific schedules where required
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder** before the deadline. Late filings incur penalties under Minn. Stat. ch. 309 (Charitable Solicitation Act) and can suspend solicitation authority.
- [ ] **File even in years with no solicitation.** Many Minnesota statutes require renewal as long as the organization is registered, regardless of whether fundraising occurred.

## Step 4: Manage the Audit Threshold

$1,000,000 in total revenue (Minn. Stat. § 309.53, subd. 3 - independent CPA audit).

- [ ] **Calculate qualifying revenue or contributions.** Read the statute carefully - definitions of "gross revenue" or "contributions" can exclude government grants, in-kind contributions, or pass-through funds depending on the state.
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early.** A 990 audit takes 60-120 days. Start procurement before fiscal year end if you are within 10% of the threshold.
- [ ] **File the audited financial statements** with the Charitable Organization Annual Report.
- [ ] **Maintain audit committee structure** if required by statute or your bylaws. Some states require a board-appointed audit committee for organizations subject to audit; review your governing documents.
- [ ] **Plan for the audit cost.** Independent CPA audits for mid-sized nonprofits typically run $8,000-$25,000 depending on complexity and federal award activity.

## Step 5: Solicitation Disclosures

Every solicitation in Minnesota that the statute reaches must include the disclosures Minn. Stat. ch. 309 (Charitable Solicitation Act) requires. Verify the exact wording on the Minnesota Attorney General site.

- [ ] **Charity name as registered** with the Minnesota Attorney General Charities Division.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **The fact that financial information is available** by writing to the Minnesota Attorney General.
- [ ] **For commercial fundraisers and fundraising counsel:** additional registrations and contract disclosures apply. Verify any third-party fundraiser is registered before signing a contract - the charity remains responsible for the fundraiser's compliance under most state statutes.

## Step 6: Maintain the Public Registry Profile

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually** at https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Charity/. Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, fiscal year changes, and dissolution must usually be reported within 30 days. Use the amendment form on the Minnesota Attorney General site.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Foundations and government grantors check Minnesota registry status. A "Delinquent" or "Suspended" status will disqualify applications regardless of program fit.

## Step 7: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in Minnesota:

- [ ] **File a final Charitable Organization Annual Report** marked "Final" or otherwise indicate closeout per the form instructions.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Minnesota Secretary of State or other state authority responsible for corporate filings, along with the AG-required notices where applicable.
- [ ] **Distribute remaining charitable assets** consistent with your dissolution clause and Minn. Stat. ch. 309 (Charitable Solicitation Act).
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least four years after dissolution to respond to any post-closeout inquiries from the Minnesota Attorney General or IRS.

## Common Failure Points

- **Missing the initial registration window.** Solicitation begins the day you ask a Minnesota resident for money - including online appeals. Treating registration as a "after the first grant" task usually produces a violation.
- **Filing federal Form 990 only.** Federal filing does not satisfy Minnesota. The Charitable Organization Annual Report is a separate state requirement.
- **Treating the state audit threshold as a federal issue.** Minnesota's audit trigger ($1,000,000 in total revenue (Minn. Stat. § 309.53, subd. 3 - independent CPA audit)) is independent of the federal Single Audit threshold of $1,000,000 in federal awards for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later. The two can both apply.
- **Failing to register out-of-state organizations soliciting Minnesota residents.** A nonprofit headquartered elsewhere that emails Minnesota donors is soliciting in Minnesota.
- **Allowing status to lapse to "Delinquent."** Foundations and government grantors check public registry status. A delinquent status can cost a grant award worth far more than the renewal fee.

The Minnesota Attorney General Charities Division is administrative, not adversarial - agencies generally provide multiple notices before suspension. But suspension consequences are severe: loss of solicitation authority, ineligibility for state contracts and grants, and potential personal liability for directors and officers depending on the statute.
