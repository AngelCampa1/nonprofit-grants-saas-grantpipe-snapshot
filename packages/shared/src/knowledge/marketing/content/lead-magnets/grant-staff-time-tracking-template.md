## The Regulatory Basis: What 2 CFR 200.430 Requires

Before using any template, understand the standard it must meet.

2 CFR 200.430(i) establishes that charges to federal awards for salaries and wages must be based on records that accurately reflect the work performed. The records must:

- Be supported by a system of internal controls that provides reasonable assurance that charges are accurate, allowable, and properly allocated
- Reasonably reflect the total activity for which the employee is compensated - not just the grant-funded portion
- Conform to the organization's established accounting practices
- Support the distribution of the employee's salary or wages among specific activities or cost objectives if the employee works on more than one federal award or on multiple activities

The standard distinguishes between two categories of employees:

**Employees working solely on one federal program:** May use semi-annual certifications signed by the employee or supervisor confirming that the employee worked solely on the program during the period.

**Employees working on multiple activities or cost objectives:** Must maintain personnel activity reports (PARs) or equivalent records that: (1) reflect after-the-fact determination of actual activity, (2) account for the total activity for which the employee is compensated, (3) are prepared at least monthly and coincide with or follow one or more pay periods, and (4) are signed by the employee.

The critical word: "contemporaneous" - records prepared at or near the time the work is performed. Not reconstructed from memory.

## Template 1: Daily Time Log for Split-Funded Employees

Use this template for any employee whose salary is distributed across multiple grants, programs, or a combination of federal and non-federal activities.

**Instructions:** The employee completes this log daily or at least weekly. At the end of each pay period, the employee certifies the totals. The supervisor reviews and countersigns. The certified form is filed in the grant records.

| TOTALS |                         |                         |                             |                        |             |

**Column Labels:**

Replace [Grant A Name] and [Grant B Name] with the actual names of the grants. Add or remove columns as needed for the employee's specific funding sources. The total column must equal the employee's total compensated hours for the period.

**Completion instructions for employees:**

Record your actual hours for each day. Do not enter budgeted percentages as hours - record what you actually did. If a particular day was spent entirely on one grant, enter all hours in that column. If you split your time, record the actual split.

If you missed recording a day, record your best recollection as soon as possible. A record completed two days after the fact is acceptable. A record completed two months after the fact is not contemporaneous.

Use this template for employees whose salary is charged 100% to a single federal program. This is the less burdensome documentation standard under 2 CFR 200.430(i)(1)(i).

**Frequency:** Semi-annual (at minimum). Recommended: every six months, aligned with the organization's fiscal year (e.g., July 1-December 31 and January 1-June 30).

## Monthly Reconciliation Instructions

At the end of each month, the finance staff member responsible for grant accounting should complete a reconciliation of T&E records to payroll charges.

**Reconciliation steps:**

**Step 1: List all employees with salary charged to federal awards**
Pull the payroll register for the month. List each employee, the amount charged to each federal grant, and the percentage of total compensation each charge represents.

**Step 2: Pull the T&E records for the same employees**
Retrieve the signed time logs or semi-annual certifications for the period. For split-funded employees, calculate the percentage of total hours attributed to each funding source from the time log.

**Step 3: Compare percentages**
For each employee, compare: the percentage of salary charged to each grant in the payroll system vs. the percentage of time recorded on the T&E record.

If the percentages match (within a rounding tolerance of 1-2%): no action required. File the reconciliation.

If the percentages differ: investigate. Did the employee submit an amended time log? Was a payroll correction made? The discrepancy must be explained and, if it represents a charge that needs to be corrected, a payroll adjustment must be processed.

**Step 4: Document and file**
Complete the reconciliation worksheet and file it with the T&E records for the month. The reconciliation is the documented review that demonstrates internal controls are functioning.

## What Auditors Look For

When auditors test personnel costs charged to federal awards, they examine a sample of pay periods for a sample of grant-funded employees. For each sample item, they check:

1. **Does a signed T&E record exist for the pay period?** If no record exists, the salary charge is potentially unallowable.

2. **Is the record contemporaneous?** They compare the date the record was completed against the pay period it covers. If an annual certification covers a year of salary charges, it is not contemporaneous.

3. **Does the record account for 100% of compensated time?** Split-funded employees whose records only show grant-funded hours - not total hours - fail the standard.

4. **Does the T&E record match the payroll allocation?** If the record shows 60% on a grant but payroll charged 70%, the records are inconsistent. Auditors may question the entire period's charges.

5. **Are records signed by the employee and/or supervisor?** Unsigned records lack the accountability requirement.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

**Mistake: Using budgeted percentages instead of recording actual time**
An employee budgeted at 50% FTE on a grant enters "50%" on every time report without recording actual daily hours. This is not contemporaneous documentation of actual effort - it is documentation of the budget.

_Fix:_ Require daily or weekly time entry. Review time logs for employees who consistently record exactly their budgeted percentage - this pattern often indicates estimates rather than actual time.

**Mistake: Completing all time logs at year-end or before the auditor arrives**
A common pattern: an employee is reminded at year-end that time logs are needed. They complete all 12 months of certifications on a single afternoon. The records are dated in December. The audit examines them.

_Fix:_ Make time log completion a pay-period discipline, not an annual task. Supervisors should confirm receipt of time logs before approving payroll.

**Mistake: Single-program certifications signed by someone who could not verify the employee's effort**
A certification signed by the executive director for an employee the executive director does not supervise and does not directly observe.

_Fix:_ Certifications should be signed by the direct supervisor who can observe the employee's actual work. If the direct supervisor is unavailable, document who has supervisory knowledge and can certify.
