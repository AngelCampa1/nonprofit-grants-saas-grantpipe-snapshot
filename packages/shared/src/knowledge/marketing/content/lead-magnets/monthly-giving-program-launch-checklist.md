## Why Monthly Giving Programs Succeed or Fail in the First 90 Days

Monthly giving is the most valuable donor relationship a nonprofit can build. A monthly donor who gives $25/month contributes $300 per year - and if they stay for three years, that's $900 from a donor who may have given a one-time $75 gift if you hadn't offered the monthly option.

The math is clear. The execution is where most programs stumble.

Monthly giving programs fail for three reasons: poor technical setup that creates friction at sign-up, a weak first-90-days experience that fails to reinforce the donor's decision, and an absent failed payment recovery workflow that allows donors to churn passively rather than actively cancel.

This checklist covers all three. Work through it in sequence. Don't launch until every item in the preparation section is complete.

## Before-Launch Checklist

### Payment Processing Setup

- [ ] Payment processor is configured to accept recurring transactions (not just one-time)
- [ ] ACH/bank transfer is available as a payment option (lower processing fees than credit cards; reduces card expiration churn)
- [ ] Card updater service is enabled (automatically updates stored card details when a donor's card is reissued)
- [ ] Recurring gift amounts are stored as tokens (not raw card numbers) - confirm with processor
- [ ] Test a $1.00 recurring transaction end-to-end and verify it processes correctly
- [ ] Test a transaction cancellation end-to-end and verify it processes correctly
- [ ] Confirm that declined transactions generate automated notifications to your team

### Failed Payment Handling Policy

Before launch, document what happens when a monthly gift fails. This is not something to figure out after the first failure.

- [ ] Define retry schedule for failed payments: How many times? On what days after failure? (Standard: 3 attempts over 7-10 days)
- [ ] Define what triggers donor notification: First failure? Second failure?
- [ ] Write the failed payment email (see "Failed Payment Recovery" section below)
- [ ] Assign responsibility: Who monitors the failed payment queue? Daily or weekly?
- [ ] Define when a donor is considered lapsed: After how many consecutive failures?
- [ ] Document what happens to donor record status after lapse

### Landing Page Requirements

- [ ] Landing page is live and mobile-optimized
- [ ] Recurring gift option is prominent - not buried below one-time gift options
- [ ] Monthly amount options reflect your donor base (typical range: $10, $25, $50, $100, $250/month)
- [ ] Custom amount field is available
- [ ] Page clearly states what the recurring gift will support (program or general fund)
- [ ] Confirmation page or thank-you email is customized for monthly donors (not the generic one-time gift confirmation)
- [ ] Email confirmation includes: gift amount, processing date, how to update payment method, how to cancel

### Donor Communication Series (before-launch internal)

- [ ] Staff understand the program and can answer donor questions
- [ ] Donor services staff know how to: upgrade a monthly gift, update a payment method, change the processing date, cancel a recurring gift
- [ ] CRM/donor database is configured to flag monthly donors as a distinct segment

## Program Naming and Positioning

Naming your monthly giving program is optional but meaningful. Named programs ("Friends of [Organization]", "The [Mission] Society", "Sustaining Members") create identity and belonging. Anonymous programs ("Monthly Giving") are fine but miss the opportunity.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Program name is decided (or you're proceeding without one)
- [ ] Program name is consistent across: website, thank-you emails, donor communications, CRM record
- [ ] If named: program is described on website with a dedicated page (not just a form)

## The Upgrade Ask Sequence

The most cost-effective way to grow a monthly giving program is to convert existing annual fund donors.

**Target:** Annual donors who gave $120+ last year (equivalent to $10/month) are strong upgrade candidates. Present the math: "Your $200 annual gift as a monthly commitment would be $17/month - or bump it to twenty dollars per month and actually increase your annual impact."

**Upgrade ask checklist:**

- [ ] Pull list of annual donors who gave $120+ last year and are not already monthly donors
- [ ] Segment by giving tier: $120-$499 annual, $500-$999 annual, $1,000+ annual
- [ ] Develop upgrade ask for each tier (amount suggestion, framing, channel)
- [ ] Assign upgrade outreach by channel:
  - [ ] $1,000+ annual donors: personal phone call from Development Director or board member
  - [ ] $500-$999 annual: personalized letter or email from Executive Director
  - [ ] $120-$499 annual: email series with upgrade option and clear CTA
- [ ] Upgrade deadline is set (urgency matters; open-ended asks get deferred indefinitely)
- [ ] Tracking system is in place to record upgrade contacts and outcomes

## First-90-Days Retention Sequence

The first 90 days determine whether a monthly donor becomes a long-term supporter or cancels quietly after a few months. Most organizations send a thank-you email and then treat the monthly donor exactly like any other donor. That's a missed opportunity.

**Monthly donor first-90-days sequence:**

**Day 0 - Enrollment confirmation:**
Automated email confirming: gift amount, processing date, program supported, how to update payment method, how to cancel. No appeals. No additional asks. Just confirmation and gratitude.

**Day 3 - Welcome message:**
Brief email (not an appeal) introducing the monthly donor to the program. One concrete story of impact. No call to action except "reply if you have questions."

**Day 7 - Impact story:**
One specific story or update from the program they're supporting. This can be a case study, a photo with caption, a quote from a program participant (with permission). Keep it short - three paragraphs maximum.

**Day 30 - After first payment processes:**
Acknowledgment that their first monthly gift has been received and put to work. Reiterate impact. Optional: include a one-year projection ("Your $25/month commitment will contribute $300 to [program] this year").

**Day 60 - Mid-point check-in:**
Non-transactional communication. Could be a program update, a behind-the-scenes photo, or a short note from the Executive Director. The goal is connection, not solicitation.

**Day 90 - 90-day milestone:**
Acknowledge that they've been a monthly supporter for three months. This is a significant moment - most monthly donor churn happens in the first 90 days. Donors who reach 90 days are much more likely to remain active for a year or more. Celebrate the milestone with a brief, genuine note.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Day 0 confirmation email is written, tested, and automated
- [ ] Day 3 welcome email is written
- [ ] Day 7 impact story is written (or system is in place to populate with program updates)
- [ ] Day 30 email is written
- [ ] Day 60 content is identified
- [ ] Day 90 email is written
- [ ] All emails are loaded into email automation system
- [ ] Automation is tested with a test account
- [ ] All emails are reviewed for brand voice and IRS-compliant language (no benefit-to-tax-deductibility claims that aren't substantiated)

## Failed Payment Recovery: The Workflow That Saves Monthly Donors

When a monthly gift fails, most nonprofits do nothing. The payment fails, the donor's record shows a lapsed recurring gift, and the relationship quietly ends. The donor may not even know it happened - they assumed their gift was still processing.

A structured failed payment recovery workflow prevents this passive churn.

**The workflow:**

**Failure Day 0:** Payment processor attempts charge. Failure logged.

**Failure Day 1:** System sends donor "payment update needed" email. Tone: neutral, not accusatory. Subject: "Your monthly gift - action needed." Body: "Your [DATE] monthly gift of $[AMOUNT] was unable to process. Please update your payment information to keep your monthly support active." Include direct link to payment update page.

**Failure Day 4:** If still unresolved, second email. Slightly more urgent. "We want to make sure your support continues - your monthly gift failed to process [NUMBER] days ago. If you'd like to update your payment information, click here. If you'd like to cancel your recurring gift, click here." Giving the cancellation option reduces the sense of pressure and increases trust.

**Failure Day 7:** Final email (or phone call for donors giving $50/month or more). "Your monthly gift has been unable to process for the past 7 days. We'll attempt one more time on [DATE]. If you'd like to update your payment information before then, [LINK]."

**Failure Day 10:** Final retry attempt. If successful, send confirmation. If unsuccessful, mark as lapsed.

**After lapse:** Lapsed monthly donors should receive a reactivation email 30 days after lapse (not immediately - give them time). Frame it as an invitation, not a guilt trip: "We missed you - if you'd like to restart your monthly support, it only takes a minute."

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Failed payment email sequence is written (3 emails)
- [ ] Payment update page exists and is mobile-optimized
- [ ] Emails are automated from payment processor or CRM
- [ ] High-value donors ($50+/month) are flagged for personal outreach on failure
- [ ] Lapsed monthly donor reactivation email is written
- [ ] Failed payment rate is tracked monthly (goal: <5%)

## Monthly Donor Graduation: The Upgrade to Major Gift Prospect

Monthly donors who have given for 24+ consecutive months are your best major gift prospects. They have demonstrated sustained commitment, they understand your organization's work, and their aggregate giving has already placed them in a meaningful cumulative giving tier.

**Graduation checklist:**

- [ ] Pull list of monthly donors with 24+ consecutive months of active giving
- [ ] Calculate each donor's cumulative lifetime giving
- [ ] Identify donors whose cumulative giving meets or approaches your major gift threshold
- [ ] Assign a relationship owner for each identified donor (typically Development Director or assigned frontline fundraiser)
- [ ] Schedule a personal outreach call or visit within 60 days
- [ ] Goal of outreach: deepen relationship, not make an immediate major gift ask
- [ ] Document the conversation and next steps in CRM
- [ ] Build a 12-month cultivation plan for each donor before making a major gift ask

Monthly donors who graduate to major gift relationships have a higher close rate on major gift asks than cold-cultivated major gift prospects - they already understand your organization and have a track record of supporting it.
