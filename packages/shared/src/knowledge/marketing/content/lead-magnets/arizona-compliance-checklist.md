## Why Arizona Compliance Is Worth Calendaring Now

Arizona repealed its charitable solicitation registration in 2013. Compliance now focuses on the ACC corporate record, the Annual Report, and any Department of Revenue exemption documentation your organization relies on. None of those are dramatic on their own. They become dramatic when one slips and a foundation grantmaker checks public status before approving an award.

The governing law in Arizona is A.R.S. Title 10, ch. 24-40 (Arizona Nonprofit Corporation Act). The administrative portal is https://ecorp.azcc.gov. Fees, deadlines, and forms can change between fiscal years - confirm against the source before each cycle.

This checklist is structured the way the work actually flows: confirm entity status, keep the ACC record current, calendar the recurring Annual Report, monitor federal audit exposure, and handle closeout. Each step links to the form name and authority so you can hand it to a colleague without re-explaining context. For broader grant compliance scope, see our [Grant Compliance 101 for Nonprofits](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) guide and the [Arizona nonprofit software overview](/nonprofit-software/arizona).

## Step 1: Confirm Corporate Status

- [ ] **Confirm whether you are an Arizona nonprofit corporation or a foreign nonprofit corporation qualified in Arizona.**
- [ ] **Verify entity status in ACC eCorp.** Record the entity ID, status, statutory agent, principal address, and last Annual Report date.
- [ ] **Document tax exemption status separately.** Keep IRS determination and Arizona Department of Revenue exemption documentation with the corporate file.
- [ ] **Check specialized fundraising rules before hiring a paid fundraiser.** Ordinary nonprofit solicitation is not a standalone ACC charitable-registration filing, but third-party fundraising arrangements can create separate compliance work.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration

- [ ] **Visit the ACC portal at https://ecorp.azcc.gov.**
- [ ] **Gather required attachments**:
- [ ] Articles of Incorporation (or equivalent founding document)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] List of officers, directors, and key employees with addresses
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $40 (corporate filing); no separate charitable registration
- [ ] **Submit formation or foreign qualification before operating in Arizona.** Do not describe this as charitable solicitation registration.
- [ ] **Record the assigned entity number.** Use it on subsequent corporate filings and internal compliance checklists.

## Step 3: Calendar the Annual Corporation Report

The recurring Annual Corporation Report keeps the ACC corporate record active.

- [ ] **Annual Corporation Report due date:** Anniversary of incorporation date. Enter this date in a shared calendar - not just the ED's personal calendar.
- [ ] **Confirm the fee tier:** $10 annual report fee. Tiered fees scale with gross revenue or contributions; budget for the higher tier the year you cross a threshold.
- [ ] **Follow the current ACC Annual Report instructions.** Do not attach IRS Form 990 or audited financial statements unless the current instructions specifically require them.
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder** before the deadline. Late filings can affect entity status.
- [ ] **File even in quiet years.** The corporate filing cadence is separate from whether you ran a fundraising campaign.

## Step 4: Manage the Audit Threshold

Arizona does not require a state audit; federal Single Audit at $1M in federal awards applies for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later.

- [ ] **Calculate qualifying revenue or contributions.** Read the statute carefully - definitions of "gross revenue" or "contributions" can exclude government grants, in-kind contributions, or pass-through funds depending on the state.
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early.** A 990 audit takes 60-120 days. Start procurement before fiscal year end if you are within 10% of the threshold.
- [ ] **Keep audit documentation with the grant and finance file.** Arizona does not create a separate state audit filing just because the federal Single Audit threshold applies.
- [ ] **Maintain audit committee structure** if required by statute or your bylaws. Some states require a board-appointed audit committee for organizations subject to audit; review your governing documents.
- [ ] **Plan for the audit cost.** Independent CPA audits for mid-sized nonprofits typically run $8,000-$25,000 depending on complexity and federal award activity.

## Step 5: Donor Disclosures and Fundraising Vendors

Do not add a nonexistent Arizona charitable-registration number to donor materials. Keep disclosures factual and check whether paid fundraiser or fundraising counsel arrangements create separate obligations.

- [ ] **Use the charity name as registered** with the Arizona Corporation Commission.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **For commercial fundraisers and fundraising counsel:** review vendor-specific registration or contract rules before signing.

## Step 6: Maintain the Public Registry Profile

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually** at https://ecorp.azcc.gov. Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, fiscal year changes, and dissolution must usually be reported within 30 days. Use the amendment form on the ACC site.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Foundations and government grantors check Arizona registry status. A "Delinquent" or "Suspended" status will disqualify applications regardless of program fit.

## Step 7: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in Arizona:

- [ ] **File a final Annual Corporation Report** marked "Final" or otherwise indicate closeout per the form instructions.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Arizona Secretary of State or other state authority responsible for corporate filings, along with the AG-required notices where applicable.
- [ ] **Distribute remaining charitable assets** consistent with your dissolution clause and A.R.S. Title 10, ch. 24-40 (Arizona Nonprofit Corporation Act).
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least four years after dissolution to respond to any post-closeout inquiries from the ACC or IRS.

## Common Failure Points

- **Inventing an Arizona charitable-registration task.** Arizona does not have the standalone charitable solicitation registration this template used to assume.
- **Filing federal Form 990 only.** Federal filing does not satisfy Arizona. The Annual Corporation Report is a separate state requirement.
- **Treating the federal audit threshold as an ACC annual report attachment.** Keep Single Audit work in the grant and finance file unless the ACC instructions ask for it.
- **Ignoring foreign qualification.** A nonprofit headquartered elsewhere may need corporate qualification if it is doing business in Arizona, even though that is different from charitable solicitation registration.
- **Allowing status to lapse to "Delinquent."** Foundations and government grantors check public registry status. A delinquent status can cost a grant award worth far more than the renewal fee.

The Arizona Corporation Commission is administrative, not adversarial - agencies generally provide multiple notices before suspension. The consequence is still serious: a bad entity status can derail grant applications, contracts, and banking updates.
