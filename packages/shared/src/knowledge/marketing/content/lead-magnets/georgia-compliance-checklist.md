## Why Georgia Compliance Catches Nonprofits Off Guard

Georgia is one of a handful of states that use a biennial registration cycle. The renewal date is set when the registration is issued, which means it does not align with the IRS Form 990 calendar, with the state corporate annual registration, or with any standard federal compliance milestone. Organizations that treat compliance as an annual event miss Georgia regularly.

The Charities Division within the Secretary of State's office runs registration and supervision. It enforces the Georgia Charitable Solicitations Act (O.C.G.A. Title 43 Chapter 17), which governs charitable organizations, paid solicitors, and fundraising counsel.

This checklist covers the Georgia compliance lifecycle from initial filing through closeout.

## Step 1: Determine Whether Registration Is Required

Most charities soliciting in Georgia must register. The narrow questions:

- Are you soliciting contributions from Georgia residents in any form - direct mail, phone, email, online giving page, social media, in-person event, grant request to a Georgia private foundation?
- Are you above the $25,000 small-charity threshold?
- Is your organization within the bona fide religious exemption? Many think they qualify and do not.
- Are you a hospital, school, or other organization with a specific statutory exemption? Verify the exemption against the current code, not against memory.

Document the analysis. If you exempt out, save the file. Charities Division staff assume solicitation triggers registration.

## Step 2: Initial Registration on Form C-100

Form C-100 is the registration application. Items to assemble before starting:

- IRS determination letter granting 501(c)(3) status
- Articles of incorporation as filed with the Georgia Secretary of State (or home state)
- Bylaws
- Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N
- Audited or reviewed financial statements if revenue thresholds require them
- List of officers and directors with addresses
- Description of charitable programs and the geographic scope of solicitation
- Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)
- Any contracts with paid solicitors or fundraising counsel covering Georgia activity

Solicitation cannot begin before the registration is approved. Out-of-state organizations with online donate buttons should treat that as a registration trigger the moment the page is reachable from Georgia.

## Step 3: Calendar the Biennial Renewal

Registration is valid for 24 months from the date issued. The renewal is also accomplished on Form C-100. Set the renewal reminder for 90 days before expiration. The Secretary of State sends notices, but the obligation to renew on time sits with the charity regardless of whether the notice arrives.

A lapsed registration means solicitation must stop until the renewal is approved. Organizations that continue soliciting on a lapsed registration are exposed to cease-and-desist orders.

## Step 4: Match Financial Statements to Revenue Tier

Georgia uses a tiered approach to financial reporting:

- Under $500,000 in revenue: financial information from Form 990 is sufficient.
- $500,000 to under $1,000,000: reviewed financial statements prepared by an independent CPA.
- $1,000,000 and above: audited financial statements prepared by an independent CPA.

The first year an organization crosses each threshold, the deliverable changes. CPAs schedule audit and review work months in advance, so plan around the projected fiscal year, not around year-end actuals.

## Step 5: Manage Paid Solicitors and Fundraising Counsel

Georgia distinguishes between paid solicitors (who solicit and may handle funds) and fundraising counsel (who plan and manage but do not solicit or hold funds). Both must register separately with the Charities Division. Paid solicitors must:

- File a separate registration before any campaign begins
- Post a $10,000 surety bond
- File each campaign in advance with a written contract
- File a financial report within 90 days of the campaign's end
- Disclose during solicitation that they are a paid solicitor and identify the charity

For the charity, the operational rule is simple: never sign a fundraising vendor contract without verifying the vendor's Georgia registration and confirming which category they belong to. Misclassification exposes both parties.

## Step 6: Maintain Records

Georgia expects registered charities to maintain books and records sufficient to verify reported amounts. This includes:

- Donor records at the level of detail underlying Form 990 Schedule B
- Solicitation materials - direct mail, email campaigns, event invitations, web copy, social media posts
- Paid solicitor and fundraising counsel contracts and payment records
- Board minutes documenting fundraising approvals
- Bank records for any restricted-purpose contributions

Retention should align with the longer of the IRS retention requirement (generally three years) and any funder requirement. Many practitioners default to seven years.

## Step 7: Update the Registration When Material Facts Change

The Charities Division expects updates when:

- Officers or directors change
- Principal address or registered agent changes
- Legal name changes
- Fiscal year changes
- The organization merges, dissolves, or significantly changes scope

Filing the update at the time of the change prevents an out-of-date public record. Georgia's registration record is searchable, and major donors and grantmakers increasingly check it before committing.

## Step 8: Closeout

When a charity stops operating in Georgia, file:

- A final IRS Form 990 (with the final-return box checked)
- A final Form C-100 covering the partial period or a written notice of cessation
- Dissolution documents with the Secretary of State if the entity is dissolving
- Final paid-solicitor campaign reports if any campaigns were active

Retain records for at least three years after closeout, longer if a funder or auditor requires it. Until the Charities Division accepts the closeout, registration obligations continue.

## Common Failure Patterns

The Georgia issues that recur in compliance reviews:

- Renewal missed because the biennial cycle is off-calendar.
- Paid solicitor signed a contract before the firm registered with the Charities Division.
- Audit threshold crossed mid-year and no CPA engaged.
- Online giving page launched without registering for Georgia solicitation.
- Religious exemption claimed for an affiliated education or service entity that does not qualify.
- Material change (board, address, fiscal year) never updated.
- Out-of-state parent registered, but a local Georgia chapter solicited under its own name without separate registration.

## Operating Cadence

The pattern that works in Georgia is the same as elsewhere: treat the registration calendar as fixed, assign a single owner, and review compliance status quarterly. The renewal date goes on the board calendar. The financial-statement deliverable is scoped during budget season. The paid-solicitor pipeline is reviewed before contracts are signed.

The Charities Division portal is functional, the statute is readable, and the rules rarely change. Once the cadence is in place, Georgia is straightforward.
