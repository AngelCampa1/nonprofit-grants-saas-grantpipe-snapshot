## Why Pennsylvania Registration Has Four Tiers

Pennsylvania has one of the more granular charitable registration regimes in the country. The Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations within the Pennsylvania Department of State requires every charity soliciting in the Commonwealth to register and file BCO-10 annually. What sets Pennsylvania apart is the four-tier financial review structure: under $100,000 in gross national contributions requires only internally prepared statements, $100,000 to $250,000 requires compiled statements, $250,000 to $1,000,000 requires reviewed statements, and $1,000,000 and above requires an independent CPA audit.

If your organization is incorporated in Pennsylvania, raises money from Pennsylvania residents, or holds property in trust for charitable purposes within the Commonwealth, you must register before you solicit. This checklist walks through registration, annual BCO-10, the financial review tiers, professional fundraiser rules, and the closeout steps when you stop operating in Pennsylvania.

## Step 1: Determine Whether You Must Register

- [ ] **Confirm registration triggers.** You must register if your organization (a) is incorporated in Pennsylvania, (b) holds assets or property in Pennsylvania for charitable purposes, or (c) solicits contributions from Pennsylvania residents - including online solicitation that targets Pennsylvania donors.
- [ ] **Check exemption categories.** Religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, hospitals, certain veterans' organizations, and small organizations with under $25,000 in gross national contributions and no compensated solicitors are exempt under 10 P.S. § 162.6.
- [ ] **If exempt, file Form BCO-9** (Institution of Higher Education or Public Charity Exemption Statement) instead of BCO-10. Exemption status is not self-effectuating - the Bureau must accept the filing.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration on Form BCO-10

- [ ] **Download Form BCO-10** from the Pennsylvania Department of State website.
- [ ] **Gather required attachments:**
- [ ] Articles of Incorporation
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter (or evidence of pending Form 1023)
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] Financial statement appropriate to the tier (see Step 3)
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $15 to $250 based on gross national contributions
- [ ] **File Form BCO-10** by mail to the Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations.
- [ ] **Record the assigned charity registration number.** This number is required on every subsequent filing.

## Step 3: Determine the Financial Review Tier

The required financial statement depends on gross national contributions for the fiscal year being reported.

- [ ] **Calculate gross national contributions** - total contributions received from all sources, not just Pennsylvania.
- [ ] **Match to the correct tier:**
- Under $100,000 †’ internally prepared financial statements
- $100,000 to $250,000 †’ CPA-compiled financial statements
- $250,000 to $1,000,000 †’ CPA-reviewed financial statements
- $1,000,000 and above †’ independent CPA audit
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early** if you are at or near the review or audit threshold. CPA capacity tightens between January and April.

## Step 4: Calendar Annual Renewal Obligations

The annual BCO-10 is the recurring filing that keeps registration active.

- [ ] **BCO-10 due date:** 4.5 months after fiscal year end. Calendar-year filers: May 15.
- [ ] **Match the fee to the current gross contributions tier.**
- [ ] **Attach the IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF** for the same fiscal year.
- [ ] **Attach the financial statement appropriate to the review tier.**
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder.** Late BCO-10 filings incur $25 per month in late fees plus potential suspension after extended noncompliance.

## Step 5: Professional Fundraiser and Solicitor Rules

If your organization works with a professional fundraiser or solicitor:

- [ ] **Verify Bureau registration.** Professional fundraisers, professional solicitors, and fundraising counsel must register separately with the Bureau before soliciting in Pennsylvania.
- [ ] **Post the solicitation contract** with the Bureau before the campaign begins.
- [ ] **Maintain copies of all professional fundraiser contracts** for at least three years after the campaign ends.
- [ ] **Required disclosures.** Solicitations must include the charity's name and the fact that registration documents are available from the Pennsylvania Department of State.

## Step 6: Solicitation Disclosures

Every solicitation in Pennsylvania must include certain disclosures.

- [ ] **Charity name as registered** with the Bureau.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **The fact that the official registration and financial information** of the charity may be obtained from the Pennsylvania Department of State by calling the toll-free number listed on the Bureau's website.

## Step 7: Maintain the Charity Database Profile

Your organization's registration status is publicly searchable on the Department of State charity database. Donors, foundations, and government grantors check this before issuing grants.

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually.** Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent BCO-10 filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, and fiscal year changes must be reported to the Bureau.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Pennsylvania foundations and government grantors check Bureau status. 'Delinquent' or 'Suspended' status will disqualify applications.

## Step 8: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in Pennsylvania:

- [ ] **File final BCO-10** marked as final.
- [ ] **Notify the Bureau** of dissolution or cessation of solicitation.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Pennsylvania Department of State Corporation Bureau if the entity is incorporated in Pennsylvania.
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least seven years after dissolution.

## Common Failure Points

- **Treating Form 990 as sufficient.** Federal filing does not satisfy Pennsylvania. BCO-10 is a separate state filing.
- **Misreading the four-tier review structure.** Organizations sometimes attach an audit when only a review is required, or vice versa. Match the tier to gross national contributions exactly.
- **Treating the audit threshold as a federal issue.** Pennsylvania's $1,000,000 threshold uses gross national contributions, not federal awards. Single Audit and BCO-10 audit requirements are independent.
- **Failing to register out-of-state organizations soliciting Pennsylvania residents.** A nonprofit headquartered elsewhere that emails Pennsylvania donors is soliciting in Pennsylvania.
- **Allowing late fees to compound.** $25 per month in late fees adds up quickly and is in addition to suspension risk.
- **Not verifying professional fundraiser registration.** Hiring an unregistered solicitor is a separate violation, even if the charity is properly registered.

The Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations is administrative, not adversarial - it gives charities multiple notices before suspension. But the consequences of suspension are severe: loss of solicitation rights, ineligibility for state contracts and grants, and compounding late fees.
