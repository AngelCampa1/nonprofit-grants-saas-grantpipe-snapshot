## Why Colorado Compliance Is Worth Calendaring Now

Colorado is one of the lowest-fee states for charitable registration. The bigger risk is the periodic report with the SOS business division (separate from the charities filing). Every Colorado nonprofit that solicits contributions has the same three obligations: an initial registration filed before solicitation begins, a recurring Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal renewal at the cadence the Colorado Secretary of State sets, and the financial schedules and disclosures the statute requires. None of those are dramatic on their own. They become dramatic when one slips and a foundation grantmaker checks public registry status before approving an award.

The governing law in Colorado is C.R.S. § 6-16-101 et seq. (Charitable Solicitations Act). The administrative portal is https://www.coloradosos.gov/biz/Charities.do. Fees, deadlines, and forms can change between fiscal years - confirm against the source before each cycle.

This checklist is structured the way the work actually flows: confirm your registration trigger, complete the initial registration, calendar the recurring renewal, manage the audit threshold, and handle closeout. Each step links to the form name and authority so you can hand it to a colleague without re-explaining context. For broader grant compliance scope beyond charitable registration, see our [Grant Compliance 101 for Nonprofits](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) guide and the [Colorado nonprofit software overview](/nonprofit-software/colorado).

## Step 1: Confirm Whether You Must Register

- [ ] **Determine the registration trigger.** Most Colorado 501(c)(3) charities that solicit contributions from Colorado residents must register with the Colorado Secretary of State under C.R.S. § 6-16-101 et seq. (Charitable Solicitations Act).
- [ ] **Check exemption categories.** Religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, certain government-affiliated entities, and small organizations under specific revenue thresholds may be exempt. Read the statute, do not rely on assumption.
- [ ] **Document any claimed exemption.** If you believe you are exempt, keep written documentation of the category and supporting facts. Some exemptions require an affirmative filing with the Colorado SOS; others apply automatically but still require records if questioned.
- [ ] **Confirm out-of-state solicitation rules.** A nonprofit headquartered outside Colorado that runs an email campaign or direct mail to Colorado residents is generally soliciting in Colorado for registration purposes.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration

- [ ] **Visit the Colorado SOS portal at https://www.coloradosos.gov/biz/Charities.do.**
- [ ] **Gather required attachments**:
- [ ] Articles of Incorporation (or equivalent founding document)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] List of officers, directors, and key employees with addresses
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $10 initial registration
- [ ] **Submit registration before any solicitation begins.** Many Colorado statutes treat solicitation before registration as a separate violation from late renewal - registering after the fact does not cure the initial period.
- [ ] **Record the assigned registration number.** Use it on every subsequent filing and donor disclosure.

## Step 3: Calendar the Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal

The recurring Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal keeps registration active. Missing it is the most common cause of suspension across every state with a charitable registry.

- [ ] **Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal due date:** Last day of the 5th month after fiscal year end. Enter this date in a shared calendar - not just the ED's personal calendar.
- [ ] **Confirm the fee tier:** $10 annual renewal. Tiered fees scale with gross revenue or contributions; budget for the higher tier the year you cross a threshold.
- [ ] **Attach the correct financial schedule:**
- IRS Form 990 (or 990-EZ for smaller organizations; 990-PF for private foundations)
- Audited financial statement when revenue crosses the audit threshold
- State-specific schedules where required
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder** before the deadline. Late filings incur penalties under C.R.S. § 6-16-101 et seq. (Charitable Solicitations Act) and can suspend solicitation authority.
- [ ] **File even in years with no solicitation.** Many Colorado statutes require renewal as long as the organization is registered, regardless of whether fundraising occurred.

## Step 4: Manage the Audit Threshold

Colorado does not impose a state audit threshold; federal Single Audit at $1M in federal awards still applies.

- [ ] **Calculate qualifying revenue or contributions.** Read the statute carefully - definitions of "gross revenue" or "contributions" can exclude government grants, in-kind contributions, or pass-through funds depending on the state.
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early.** A 990 audit takes 60-120 days. Start procurement before fiscal year end if you are within 10% of the threshold.
- [ ] **File the audited financial statements** with the Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal.
- [ ] **Maintain audit committee structure** if required by statute or your bylaws. Some states require a board-appointed audit committee for organizations subject to audit; review your governing documents.
- [ ] **Plan for the audit cost.** Independent CPA audits for mid-sized nonprofits typically run $8,000-$25,000 depending on complexity and federal award activity.

## Step 5: Solicitation Disclosures

Every solicitation in Colorado that the statute reaches must include the disclosures C.R.S. § 6-16-101 et seq. (Charitable Solicitations Act) requires. Verify the exact wording on the Colorado SOS site.

- [ ] **Charity name as registered** with the Colorado Secretary of State.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **The fact that financial information is available** by writing to the Colorado SOS.
- [ ] **For commercial fundraisers and fundraising counsel:** additional registrations and contract disclosures apply. Verify any third-party fundraiser is registered before signing a contract - the charity remains responsible for the fundraiser's compliance under most state statutes.

## Step 6: Maintain the Public Registry Profile

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually** at https://www.coloradosos.gov/biz/Charities.do. Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, fiscal year changes, and dissolution must usually be reported within 30 days. Use the amendment form on the Colorado SOS site.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Foundations and government grantors check Colorado registry status. A "Delinquent" or "Suspended" status will disqualify applications regardless of program fit.

## Step 7: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in Colorado:

- [ ] **File a final Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal** marked "Final" or otherwise indicate closeout per the form instructions.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Colorado Secretary of State or other state authority responsible for corporate filings, along with the AG-required notices where applicable.
- [ ] **Distribute remaining charitable assets** consistent with your dissolution clause and C.R.S. § 6-16-101 et seq. (Charitable Solicitations Act).
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least four years after dissolution to respond to any post-closeout inquiries from the Colorado SOS or IRS.

## Common Failure Points

- **Missing the initial registration window.** Solicitation begins the day you ask a Colorado resident for money - including online appeals. Treating registration as a "after the first grant" task usually produces a violation.
- **Filing federal Form 990 only.** Federal filing does not satisfy Colorado. The Charitable Solicitations Annual Renewal is a separate state requirement.
- **Treating the state audit threshold as a federal issue.** Colorado's audit trigger (Colorado does not impose a state audit threshold; federal Single Audit at $1M in federal awards still applies) is independent of the federal Single Audit threshold of $1,000,000 in federal awards for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later. The two can both apply.
- **Failing to register out-of-state organizations soliciting Colorado residents.** A nonprofit headquartered elsewhere that emails Colorado donors is soliciting in Colorado.
- **Allowing status to lapse to "Delinquent."** Foundations and government grantors check public registry status. A delinquent status can cost a grant award worth far more than the renewal fee.

The Colorado Secretary of State is administrative, not adversarial - agencies generally provide multiple notices before suspension. But suspension consequences are severe: loss of solicitation authority, ineligibility for state contracts and grants, and potential personal liability for directors and officers depending on the statute.
