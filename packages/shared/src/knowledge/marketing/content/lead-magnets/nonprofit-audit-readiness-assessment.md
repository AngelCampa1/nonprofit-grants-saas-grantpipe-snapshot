## What This Assessment Reveals

Audit findings, year over year, cluster in the same handful of control areas. The federal Office of Inspector General reports and Single Audit Database flag the same families of issues again and again: missing time-and-effort certifications, undocumented indirect cost charges, unmonitored subrecipients, procurement files without selection memos, and SEFA schedules that don't reconcile to the general ledger.

This assessment scores your organization against twelve of those areas. It's designed to be answered by someone who has visibility into both finance and grants - typically a finance director, executive director, or experienced grant manager. The questions take about five minutes.

## How the Score Maps to Action

The score lands you in one of three bands:

- **Audit-ready (80%+):** Documentation, controls, and reporting cadence are in a defensible position. The remediation list focuses on file completeness and the procedural tightening that auditors flag as opportunities for improvement.

- **At risk (55-79%):** Most controls are in place but specific files or policies have gaps. Most organizations in this band can close the gaps in 60 days if they start now. The remediation list is targeted at the most common at-risk areas: written internal control documentation, subrecipient monitoring files, and time-and-effort coverage.

- **Not audit-ready (under 55%):** Multiple control areas are unaddressed. Plan on a 90-day remediation sprint with weekly updates to the board's finance or audit committee. The remediation list starts with the foundational pieces - SEFA, indirect cost basis, written procurement policy - that need to be in place before the rest matters.

## Why These Twelve Areas

Each question maps to a section of 2 CFR 200 (Uniform Guidance) where auditors test compliance. SEFA reconciliation tests subpart F single audit requirements. Time-and-effort tests 200.430 compensation rules. Indirect cost documentation tests 200.414. Subrecipient monitoring tests 200.331-332. Procurement files test 200.317-326. Internal control documentation tests 200.303. The other five areas - board financial review, prior findings resolution, segregation of duties, drawdown reconciliation, FFATA reporting - are recurring weak points in single audit findings reports.

Scoring is weighted toward documentation rather than intent. An auditor examining your file does not give partial credit for things you meant to do, planned to write up, or know in your head. The assessment reflects that bias: full credit goes to written, current, signed, archived evidence.

## How to Use the Result

If your organization has a single audit due within 12 months, the score gives you a prioritized to-do list. The remediation links lead to the GrantPipe templates and checklists that map to each area - for example, the SEFA Prep Worksheet for SEFA gaps, the 2 CFR 200 Audit Prep Checklist for broader Uniform Guidance gaps, and the Subrecipient Monitoring Checklist for organizations passing federal funds downstream.

If you're not facing a near-term audit, the score is a baseline. Save the result, work through the remediation guides over a quarter, and retake the assessment in six months. The score should move; if it doesn't, the remediation work hasn't been operationalized.

## What the assessment measures

The assessment scores readiness across the five control areas that produce most single-audit findings under 2 CFR 200: SEFA reconciliation, time-and-effort certification discipline, indirect cost rate documentation, subrecipient monitoring procedures, and procurement file completeness. Each area is weighted by audit-finding frequency drawn from public OMB data on Schedule of Findings and Questioned Costs reports across federal awarding agencies.

## How to use your score

A score above 80 indicates an organization that should pass single audit with minor or no findings. A score between 60 and 80 indicates one or two control areas that need shoring up before fieldwork begins - usually time-and-effort or SEFA reconciliation. A score below 60 indicates structural gaps that take a quarter or two of disciplined work to close. The questionnaire routes you to the specific remediation worksheet for each gap so you can act on the score immediately rather than scheduling a follow-up project.

## Why these five areas

Single-audit findings published by federal Inspectors General cluster heavily into the same five categories year after year. Late or missing time-and-effort certifications are the most common finding overall. SEFA reconciliation issues - where the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards disagrees with the general ledger - are the second most common. Indirect cost rate documentation and subrecipient monitoring round out the top five. Procurement files come up frequently in single audits of organizations subject to the federal procurement standards. The assessment is built around these realities so you can spend remediation effort where the audit risk actually is.

## After the assessment

Once you have your score, the next step is a remediation calendar with named owners and dates. The remediation worksheets the assessment routes you to are designed to be completed inside one or two work sessions, not as multi-month projects. The principle throughout: shift audit-related work from annual to monthly, document as you go, and the next single audit becomes a verification exercise rather than a reconstruction.
