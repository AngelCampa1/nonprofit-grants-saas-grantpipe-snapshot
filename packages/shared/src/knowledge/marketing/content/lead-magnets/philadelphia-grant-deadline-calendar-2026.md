## Why This Calendar Exists

Most Philadelphia nonprofits managing a portfolio of city, state, federal pass-through, and foundation funding work from three or four separate calendars at once. Each quarter, two or three deadlines land in the same week. One report gets reconstructed from incomplete data. The team takes a shortcut, the funder receives a weaker submission, and the cycle repeats.

A single planning view that captures each funder, report type, source URL, and data source is the simplest structural fix.

## What's Inside

- City of Philadelphia grant cycles (Department of Behavioral Health, Office of Children and Families, Department of Public Health, Office of Homeless Services)
- Pennsylvania state agency cycles (DHS, PDE, DCED, DOH) for FY2026
- Federal pass-through windows that flow through Pennsylvania (CDBG, CSBG, Title XX, IDEA-B)
- Foundation cycle fields for Philadelphia-area grantmakers your team verifies from current funder pages, award letters, or LOI portals
- Reporting deadlines paired with application deadlines so you can spot collision weeks
- A reconciliation column showing which reports share GL data sources

## How to Use This Calendar

For every grant you currently hold or plan to apply to in 2026, mark the application deadline, the interim report deadlines, and the final report. Then look for the weeks where three or more deadlines land together. Decide now what gets sequenced and what runs in parallel.

The calendar covers PA state FY2026 (July 2025 - June 2026) and federal FY2026 (October 2025 - September 2026), with most foundation cycles aligning to calendar 2026.

## Common Collision Weeks Philadelphia Nonprofits Should Plan For

The recurring pinch points each year:

- Early January: Pennsylvania state interim reports collide with year-end foundation reports and federal Q1 SF-425 work. Data sources overlap; formats do not.
- Mid-April: 990 filing window overlaps with several large Philadelphia foundation LOI deadlines. Finance and development compete for the same staff hours.
- Late June: PA state fiscal year closes. Every active state award has a final report due that week.
- Late September: Federal fiscal year closes. City of Philadelphia reporting for several programs lands the same week.

Plan staffing for those four weeks the year before, not the week of.

## How to Use the Calendar with Your Existing Tracker

Most Philadelphia nonprofits already maintain a grants tracker - sometimes in a spreadsheet, sometimes in a CRM. This calendar is a cross-check, not a replacement. Run your tracker against the calendar quarterly. The gaps fall into two categories:

- Deadlines you knew about but logged in the wrong week.
- Deadlines you did not know about because the award letter buried the reporting cadence in an attachment.

Both are fixable. Catching them at quarter-start prevents the last-minute reconstruction that weakens reports.

## Why Single-View Calendars Beat Per-Funder Trackers

Most Philadelphia development teams maintain separate trackers per funder family - one for foundation grants, one for city contracts, one for state awards, one for federal. The reason is historical: each funder type produces a different paperwork stream, and the trackers grew up around the paperwork rather than around the team's calendar.

The downside is that no single document shows the team where time will be tightest. A single consolidated view fixes that, and it is the only way to see the collision weeks far enough in advance to staff for them.

You do not need new software to do this. A spreadsheet that pulls from each per-funder tracker monthly is sufficient. The calendar in this download is the template; the maintenance discipline is what makes it work.
