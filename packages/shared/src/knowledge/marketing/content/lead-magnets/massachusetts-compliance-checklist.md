## Why Massachusetts Registration Is Centralized

Massachusetts has one of the most centralized charitable oversight regimes in the country. The Attorney General's Non-Profit Organizations/Public Charities Division is the single point of contact for charitable registration - there is no separate solicitation registry. The division registers every public charity holding assets or soliciting in the Commonwealth and maintains a public-facing database where every Form PC, audit report, and registration status is searchable. Foundations, donors, and government grantors check this database before issuing grants.

If your organization is incorporated in Massachusetts, raises money from Massachusetts residents, or holds property in trust for charitable purposes within the Commonwealth, you must register before you solicit a single dollar. This checklist walks through registration, annual Form PC, the financial review and audit thresholds, and the closeout steps when you stop operating in Massachusetts.

## Step 1: Determine Whether You Must Register

- [ ] **Confirm registration triggers.** You must register if your organization (a) is incorporated in Massachusetts, (b) holds assets or property in Massachusetts for charitable purposes, or (c) solicits contributions from Massachusetts residents - including online solicitation that targets Massachusetts donors.
- [ ] **Check exemption categories.** Religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, hospitals, and certain government-affiliated entities are exempt from registration. Most 501(c)(3) public charities are not.
- [ ] **Document your basis for any claimed exemption.** If you believe you are exempt, keep written documentation of the exemption category and the supporting facts.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration via the Short Form

- [ ] **Confirm Massachusetts corporate status.** If incorporated in Massachusetts, the organization should be listed in good standing with the Secretary of the Commonwealth's Corporations Division. Out-of-state corporations soliciting in Massachusetts may need to register as a foreign corporation if doing business in the Commonwealth.
- [ ] **Download the Short Form Public Charity Registration** from mass.gov.
- [ ] **Gather required attachments**:
- [ ] Articles of Organization (or trust instrument, or association agreement)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter (or evidence of pending Form 1023)
- [ ] List of officers and directors
- [ ] Most recent financial statement (if any)
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $100, payable by check to the Commonwealth of Massachusetts
- [ ] **File the Short Form** by mail to the AGO Non-Profit Organizations/Public Charities Division.
- [ ] **Record the assigned public charity registration number.** This number is required on every subsequent filing.

## Step 3: Calendar Annual Form PC Obligations

The Form PC is the recurring filing that keeps registration active. Missing it is the most common cause of delinquent status.

- [ ] **Form PC due date:** 4.5 months after fiscal year end. Calendar-year filers: May 15. June 30 fiscal-year filers: November 15.
- [ ] **Determine your fee tier** based on gross support and revenue. Fees range from $35 for the smallest filers to $2,000 for the largest organizations. Refer to the AGO fee schedule for the current tiers.
- [ ] **Determine the financial review tier:**
- Gross support and revenue under $200,000 †’ no review or audit required
- $200,000 to $500,000 †’ CPA-reviewed financial statements required
- $500,000 and above †’ independent CPA audit required
- [ ] **Attach the IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF** for the same fiscal year.
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder** before the deadline. Late Form PC filings shift status to 'Delinquent' on the AGO public database and may trigger enforcement action.

## Step 4: Audit Requirement Mechanics

For organizations crossing the $500,000 audit threshold:

- [ ] **Calculate gross support and revenue** based on the Form 990 line for total revenue, including contributions, program service revenue, investment income, and other income. Do not net out functional expenses.
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early.** A nonprofit audit takes 60 to 120 days. Start procurement before fiscal year end if you are at or near the $500,000 threshold.
- [ ] **File the audited financial statements** with Form PC.
- [ ] **Maintain board financial oversight.** While Massachusetts does not impose a specific audit committee structure at this threshold, board financial oversight should be documented in minutes.

## Step 5: Annual Report with the Secretary of the Commonwealth

Separate from Form PC, every Massachusetts nonprofit corporation files an Annual Report with the Secretary of the Commonwealth, Corporations Division.

- [ ] **Annual Report due:** November 1 each year.
- [ ] **Filing fee:** $15 online, $20 paper.
- [ ] **Confirm officer and director list is current.** The Annual Report updates the public corporate record.
- [ ] **Calendar this separately** from Form PC. The deadlines are different and the agencies are different.

## Step 6: Solicitation Disclosures

Massachusetts solicitations should include certain disclosures, particularly if a professional solicitor is involved.

- [ ] **Charity name as registered** with the AGO.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **The fact that financial information is available** by writing to the Attorney General's Non-Profit Organizations/Public Charities Division or through the AGO public charity database.
- [ ] **For professional solicitors and fundraising counsel:** additional disclosures and separate registration requirements apply. Verify any third-party fundraiser is registered with the AGO before signing a contract.

## Step 7: Maintain the AGO Public Charity Profile

Your organization's registration status is publicly searchable on the AGO public charity database. Donors, foundations, and government grantors check this before issuing grants.

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually.** Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent Form PC filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, fiscal year changes, and dissolution must be reported to the AGO.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Many Massachusetts foundations require an active 'Current' status. 'Delinquent' or 'Not Registered' will disqualify applications.

## Step 8: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in Massachusetts:

- [ ] **File final Form PC** marked 'Final.'
- [ ] **Submit notice of charitable asset distribution** before distributing remaining assets - Massachusetts requires AGO review of dissolution distributions.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Secretary of the Commonwealth only after the AGO confirms no objection.
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least seven years after dissolution to respond to any post-closeout inquiries.

## Common Failure Points

- **Missing the initial registration window.** Many organizations treat registration as something to handle 'after the first grant.' That is too late - solicitation is the trigger, not grant receipt.
- **Filing Form 990 only with the IRS.** Federal filing does not satisfy Massachusetts. Form PC is a separate state requirement with separate schedules.
- **Treating the audit threshold as a federal issue.** Massachusetts's $500,000 audit trigger is a state requirement, independent of federal Single Audit thresholds at $1,000,000.
- **Failing to register out-of-state organizations soliciting Massachusetts residents.** A nonprofit headquartered elsewhere that emails Massachusetts donors is soliciting in Massachusetts.
- **Forgetting the Annual Report.** This is a separate $15 filing with the Secretary of the Commonwealth, due November 1 - distinct from Form PC.
- **Allowing status to lapse to 'Delinquent.'** Foundations check the AGO database. Delinquent status can cost a grant award worth far more than the registration fee.

The AGO Non-Profit Organizations/Public Charities Division is administrative, not adversarial - it issues notices before suspension. But the consequences of suspension are severe: loss of solicitation rights, ineligibility for state contracts and grants, and potential personal liability for directors and officers under Massachusetts public charity law.
