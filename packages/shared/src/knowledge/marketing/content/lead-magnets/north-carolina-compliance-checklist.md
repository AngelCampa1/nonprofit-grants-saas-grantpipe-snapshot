## Why North Carolina Compliance Requires a Calendar

North Carolina runs an annual licensing cycle for charitable solicitation. The license is valid for 12 months from issuance, which means the renewal date is unique to each organization and rarely lines up with the IRS Form 990 cycle. Organizations that treat compliance as a federal-calendar event miss North Carolina regularly.

The Charities Division within the Department of the Secretary of State runs licensing and supervision. It enforces N.C.G.S. Chapter 131F, the Charitable Solicitation Act, which governs charitable organizations, paid solicitors, and fundraising consultants. Enforcement tools include cease-and-desist orders, civil penalties, and license revocation.

This checklist works through the North Carolina compliance lifecycle in the order most organizations encounter it.

## Step 1: Determine Whether Licensure Is Required

Most charities soliciting in North Carolina need a license. The narrower questions:

- Are you soliciting contributions from North Carolina residents in any form? Direct mail, phone, online giving page, social media, in-person, grant requests to North Carolina foundations - all of these can trigger licensure.
- Are you above the $25,000 small-charity threshold and do you compensate anyone primarily for fundraising?
- Do you fit a specific exemption under N.C.G.S. 131F-3 - religious institution, educational institution within the statutory definition, hospital, government unit?
- Are you an out-of-state nonprofit with an online donate page reachable from North Carolina?

Document the determination. The default Charities Division assumption is that solicitation triggers licensure.

## Step 2: Initial License Application

Items to assemble before starting the application:

- IRS determination letter granting 501(c)(3) status
- Articles of incorporation (North Carolina or home state)
- Bylaws
- Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N
- Audited or reviewed financial statements if revenue thresholds require them
- List of officers and directors with addresses
- Description of charitable programs and the geographic scope of solicitation
- Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)
- Contracts with any paid solicitors or fundraising consultants covering North Carolina activity

Solicitation cannot begin before the license is issued. The processing time depends on completeness - incomplete applications go to the back of the queue.

## Step 3: Calendar the Annual Renewal

The renewal is due before the license expires. Set a reminder 90 days before expiration. The Charities Division sends renewal notices, but the obligation sits with the licensee regardless.

A lapsed license means solicitation must pause until renewal is approved. Continued solicitation on a lapsed license exposes the organization to enforcement.

## Step 4: Match Financial Statements to Revenue Tier

North Carolina uses a tiered financial-reporting framework:

- Under $500,000 in contributions: Form 990 financial data is sufficient.
- $500,000 to under $1,000,000: reviewed financial statements prepared by an independent CPA.
- $1,000,000 and above: audited financial statements prepared by an independent CPA.

Plan around projected fiscal-year revenue, not actuals at year-end. CPA review and audit engagements book months in advance.

## Step 5: Paid Solicitors and Fundraising Consultants

North Carolina distinguishes between paid solicitors (who solicit and may handle funds) and fundraising consultants (who plan and advise but do not solicit or hold funds). Both must register separately. Paid solicitors must:

- File a separate registration before any campaign begins
- Post a $20,000 surety bond
- File each campaign before it begins, with a written contract
- File a financial report within 90 days of campaign end
- Make required disclosures during solicitation

For the charity, the rule is straightforward: verify the firm's North Carolina registration before signing any fundraising vendor contract, and confirm which category the firm belongs to. Misclassification exposes both parties.

## Step 6: Maintain Records

The Charities Division expects records sufficient to verify reported amounts:

- Donor records at the detail level underlying Form 990 Schedule B
- Solicitation materials - direct mail pieces, email campaigns, event invitations, online copy, social media posts
- Paid solicitor and fundraising consultant contracts and payments
- Board minutes covering fundraising approvals
- Bank records for restricted-purpose contributions

Default retention is generally three years, longer if a funder or auditor requires.

## Step 7: Update the License When Things Change

The Charities Division expects timely updates when:

- Officers or directors change
- Principal address or registered agent changes
- Legal name or fiscal year changes
- Mergers, dissolutions, or significant program changes occur

File the update at the time of the change. Stale information on the public record is a credibility problem before it becomes a regulatory one.

## Step 8: Closeout

When activity in North Carolina ends, file:

- A final IRS Form 990 with the final-return box checked
- A final license filing covering the partial period or a written notice of cessation
- Dissolution documents with the North Carolina Secretary of State if the entity is dissolving
- Final paid-solicitor campaign reports if any are open

Retain records for at least three years post-closeout. Until the Charities Division accepts the closeout, license obligations continue.

## Common Failure Patterns

Recurring North Carolina findings:

- Renewal missed because the expiration date is off the federal calendar.
- Audit threshold crossed without engaging a CPA early enough.
- Online giving page launched without obtaining a license.
- Religious exemption claimed for an affiliated education or service entity outside the carve-out.
- Paid solicitor contract signed before the firm registered.
- Material change to officers or address never updated.
- Out-of-state parent licensed, but a local affiliate solicited under its own name without separate licensure.

## Operating Cadence

The cadence that works in North Carolina is the cadence that works in every state: a fixed renewal date on the calendar, a single named owner, a quarterly compliance review, and a financial-statement deliverable scoped during budget season. The Charities Division portal is workable, the statute is readable, and the thresholds rarely change.
