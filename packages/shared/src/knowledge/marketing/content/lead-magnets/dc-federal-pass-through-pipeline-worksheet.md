## Why This Worksheet Exists

Federal pass-through funding moves through the DC region via dozens of city and federal agencies, and the compliance obligations move with it. Most nonprofits accepting their first pass-through award don't realize that the Uniform Guidance applies in full - even when funds arrive from DC government, not directly from a federal agency.

The single audit is where these gaps surface, and the findings are usually structural: missing CFDA/ALN identification, indirect cost rates not documented, subaward agreements missing the required flow-down language from 2 CFR 200.332(a). None of that is about programmatic performance. All of it is about the file you should have built when the award arrived.

This worksheet captures the structural fields you need to track for every active pass-through award, so the answers are ready when the auditor arrives.

## What's Inside

- A per-award tracking template (UEI, CFDA/ALN, federal awarding agency, pass-through entity, period of performance, federal share, indirect cost rate)
- Single audit trigger thresholds and the running cumulative federal expenditure tally
- Subrecipient flow-down checklist (2 CFR 200.332(a) provisions)
- Documentation requirements by award type (research, training, services, capital)
- The five most common audit findings in pass-through programs, mapped to the worksheet field that prevents each
- A reconciliation log for the pass-through entity's reporting cadence

## How to Use This Worksheet

Open it the day the award letter arrives - not the week the report is due. Fill in every field that the award documentation supports. The fields you can't fill in are the ones to chase down with the pass-through entity before you start drawing funds.

For organizations approaching the $1,000,000 single-audit threshold for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later, this worksheet doubles as a Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA) prep tool. The cumulative expenditure tally tells you in real time when the threshold is about to be crossed, so the audit RFP starts on schedule rather than three months late.

## Common Findings This Worksheet Prevents

The four pass-through findings that show up most often in DC-area single audits:

- Subaward agreements missing required 2 CFR 200.332(a) provisions. The pass-through entity (you) is responsible, not the subrecipient. The flow-down checklist in this worksheet tracks each provision per subaward.
- Indirect cost rate not documented. If you charge indirect to a federal pass-through award, the rate must be documented either as a federally negotiated rate or as the de minimis 15%. The worksheet tracks which rate applies per award.
- CFDA/ALN number missing or wrong on the SEFA. The worksheet captures it at award start, where it is easy, rather than at audit, where it is not.
- Period of performance overruns. Federal awards have specific start and end dates. Expenditures outside those dates are findings even if they would have been allowable inside them. The worksheet flags overruns before they happen.

## What to Set Up This Quarter

Before the next pass-through award arrives, decide three things:

- Which staff member maintains this worksheet for every active pass-through award. Typically the grants manager, sometimes a grants accountant.
- Where the worksheet lives so the auditor can find it in five minutes when the audit starts. A shared drive folder works; a CRM record works; an email thread does not.
- The trigger for opening a new worksheet: the day the award letter arrives, not the day the first draw is processed.

Doing this once at the start of the fiscal year, for every active pass-through award, is the difference between a clean single audit and one with structural findings.
