## Why Most Nonprofit Financial Reports Fail the Board

The typical nonprofit board financial report is a profit-and-loss statement reformatted for nonprofit terminology and emailed as a PDF attachment two days before the board meeting. Board members who aren't finance professionals scan it, don't know what questions to ask, and approve it without meaningful review.

This is not a governance failure - it's a design failure. Financial reports built for accountants, not for mission-driven decision-makers, produce exactly this outcome.

A useful board financial report answers four questions a board member should be able to answer after reading it:

1. Are we on track financially for the year?
2. Are our restricted funds being managed correctly?
3. Are our current grants on track to be spent appropriately?
4. Are there any financial risks the board should be aware of?

The templates in this pack are organized around those four questions, not around accounting conventions.

## Section 1: Statement of Financial Position Summary

The Statement of Financial Position (balance sheet) tells you what the organization owns, owes, and has accumulated as net assets. For a board financial report, you don't need the full statement every month - but you do need a summary that shows trend.

| | Current Month | Prior Month | Same Month Last Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assets** | | | |
| Cash and cash equivalents | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |
| Accounts receivable (grants) | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |
| Accounts receivable (pledges) | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |
| Prepaid expenses | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |
| Property and equipment (net) | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |
| **Total Assets** | **$_______** | **$_______** | **$_______** |
| **Liabilities** | | | |

| Deferred revenue | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |
| **Total Liabilities** | **$_______** | **$_______** | **$_______** |
| **Net Assets** | | | |

| With donor restrictions | $_______ | $_______ | $_______ |

**Notes instructions:** Below this table, include a brief narrative (3-5 sentences) highlighting any significant changes from prior month or prior year. Flag any trend that warrants board attention: a decline in unrestricted net assets, a large increase in accounts payable, or a deferred revenue balance that indicates timing differences in grant recognition.

| Special events (net) | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | ____% |

## Section 4: Grant Expenditure Schedule

The grant expenditure schedule is the most operationally important section for organizations with significant grant revenue. It shows, for every active grant, the award amount, spending to date, remaining balance, and burn rate relative to the grant period remaining.

**Template: Grant Expenditure Schedule**

| Grant Name | Funder | Award Amount | Period Start | Period End | Months Remaining | Cumulative Expenditures | Remaining Balance | Monthly Burn Required | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Grant A] | [Funder] | $______ | [Date] | [Date] | __ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ |
| [Grant B] | [Funder] | $______ | [Date] | [Date] | __ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ |
| [Grant C] | [Funder] | $______ | [Date] | [Date] | __ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ |
| **Total** | | **$______** | | | | **$______** | **$______** | | |

**Column definitions:**

- **Monthly Burn Required:** (Remaining Balance) · (Months Remaining). This is how much needs to be spent per month to expend the grant fully before it expires.
- **Variance:** (Actual Monthly Average Spend) ’ (Monthly Burn Required). A negative number means you're behind pace.

**Flag for board attention:** Any grant where variance is negative by more than 15%, or where remaining balance plus projected spending will not exhaust the award within the period of performance.

## Section 5: Restricted Fund Status Table

Separate from the grant expenditure schedule, the restricted fund status table shows all funds with donor restrictions - both time-restricted and purpose-restricted. This is the FASB ASC 958 compliance view.

**Template: Restricted Fund Status Table**

| Fund Name | Restriction Type | Beginning Balance | Contributions Received | Expenditures | Releases from Restriction | Ending Balance | Restriction Expires |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Fund 1] | Purpose | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | [Date or "Upon completion"] |
| [Fund 2] | Time | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | [Date] |
| [Fund 3] | Purpose | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | $______ | [Date or "Upon completion"] |
| **Total** | | **$______** | **$______** | **$______** | **$______** | **$______** | |

**Notes instructions:** Explain any restriction releases made during the period - what conditions were satisfied, what accounting entry was made. Note any funds that are nearing their restriction expiration date and have significant balances remaining.

## Section 6: Key Financial Ratios

Ratios give context that raw numbers don't. Include three or four ratios on the board dashboard, updated monthly.

**Template: Key Financial Ratios**

| Metric | Current Month | Prior Month | Target / Benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Months of operating reserve | ___ months | ___ months | 3-6 months |
| Program expense ratio | ___% | ___% | ‰¥65% |
| Fundraising efficiency | $______ per $1 raised | $______ per $1 raised | <$0.25 |
| Grant dependence ratio | ___% | ___% | <70% recommended |

**Ratio calculations:**

- **Months of operating reserve:** (Unrestricted net assets) · (Average monthly operating expenses)
- **Program expense ratio:** (Program expenses) · (Total expenses)
- **Fundraising efficiency:** (Fundraising expenses) · (Total contributions raised)
- **Grant dependence ratio:** (Grant revenue) · (Total revenue)

**Context note on benchmarks:** These are directional benchmarks, not standards. An organization in its first three years may have lower reserves and higher fundraising expenses. An organization with government contracts as a primary revenue source may have a higher grant dependence ratio by design. Trend matters more than any single month's ratio.

## The One-Page Board Financial Dashboard

For organizations that send financial reports to board members who have limited financial backgrounds, consider a one-page dashboard that summarizes the full report in a format designed for non-accountants.

**Template: One-Page Board Dashboard**

**Are we on track for the year?**

## Presenting the Report: Notes for Finance Staff

The financial report is a conversation tool, not a document to approve. A few practices that make board financial discussions more productive:

**Lead with exceptions.** Start the presentation with items that require board attention or decision. Don't walk through every line - board members can read. Use the meeting time for the items that need discussion.

**Translate accounting language.** "Deferred revenue increased by $80,000" means "We received grant funds we haven't yet earned under the grant agreement." Say the second version.

**Connect finances to programs.** Board members who serve for mission reasons respond better to "the after-school program is on track" than to "program expenses are at 47% of budget." Use both, but lead with the mission framing.

**Identify the ask.** If the board needs to make a decision based on the financial report (approve a budget modification, authorize a line of credit, etc.), state that explicitly at the beginning. Don't bury the ask at the end.
