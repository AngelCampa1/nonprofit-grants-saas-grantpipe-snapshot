## What Makes Texas Different

Most states require some form of charitable solicitation registration before a 501(c)(3) can ask Texas residents for money. Texas does not. There is no general registry, no annual financial filing with the Attorney General, no equivalent to California's RRF-1.

That makes Texas one of the easiest states for charitable fundraising - and also one of the most commonly misunderstood. The absence of a general registration requirement does not mean the absence of compliance obligations. It means the obligations live in three other places: corporate filings with the Secretary of State, tax filings with the Comptroller, and special-category registrations for law enforcement, public safety, and veteran organizations.

This checklist covers all three buckets. If your organization fits the special-category triggers, jump to Step 3 first.

## Step 1: Form and Maintain the Texas Nonprofit Corporation

- [ ] **File Certificate of Formation (Form 202)** with the Texas Secretary of State. The filing fee is $25.
- [ ] **Include required IRS language** in your Certificate of Formation: a 501(c)(3) purpose clause and a dissolution clause meeting Treasury Regulation 1.501(c)(3)-1(b)(4). Without this language, the IRS will reject your Form 1023 application.
- [ ] **Appoint a Texas registered agent.** The agent must have a physical Texas address (not a P.O. box) and be available during business hours.
- [ ] **Adopt bylaws.** Texas does not file bylaws with the state, but you must adopt them and produce them on request.
- [ ] **Hold the organizational meeting** of the initial board of directors. Document with signed minutes.
- [ ] **Calendar Form 802 periodic reporting.** Texas nonprofit corporations must file a Periodic Report when requested by the Secretary of State (typically every four years). Missing the report - even one cycle - leads to involuntary termination of the corporation.

## Step 2: Texas Tax Exemption (Comptroller)

Federal 501(c)(3) status does not automatically exempt your organization from Texas state taxes. You must apply separately.

- [ ] **File Form AP-204** (Application for Exemption - Federal and All Others) with the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts after receiving your IRS determination letter.
- [ ] **Attach** your IRS determination letter, certificate of formation, and bylaws.
- [ ] **Receive exemption letter** from the Comptroller. This is what vendors will request when you claim sales tax exemption.
- [ ] **Annual sales tax-free days** - Texas allows qualifying 501(c)(3) and 501(c)(4) organizations to hold two one-day sales tax-free events per year. Document them in your records.
- [ ] **Franchise tax** - Texas charges franchise tax on most business entities, but 501(c)(3) organizations holding a Comptroller exemption letter are exempt. Maintain the letter; produce it if challenged.

## Step 3: Special-Category Solicitation Registrations

These are the only Texas registrations charged with regulating charitable solicitation. If your organization solicits under any of these categories, you must register before soliciting - there is no informal grace period.

### Law Enforcement Solicitations (Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1803)

- [ ] **Determine applicability.** This applies to any organization soliciting on behalf of, or representing, law enforcement officers, peace officers, or any law enforcement organization.
- [ ] **Register with the Texas Secretary of State** before any solicitation occurs.
- [ ] **File annually** and keep registration current.
- [ ] **Provide statutory disclosures** in solicitations, including the percentage of contributions that go to the represented organization and how the funds are used.
- [ ] **Register any third-party fundraisers** separately under Chapter 1803.

### Public Safety Solicitations and Publications (Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1804)

- [ ] **Determine applicability.** This applies to public safety organizations (including firefighters and EMS) and to public safety publications soliciting advertising or contributions.
- [ ] **Register with the Texas Secretary of State** before solicitation.
- [ ] **File annual financial report.**
- [ ] **Statutory disclosures** required in all solicitations, including the percentage of net proceeds the soliciting party retains.

### Veteran Organization Solicitations (Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1806)

- [ ] **Determine applicability.** Applies to organizations soliciting in connection with veterans, veterans' programs, or veteran-related causes - except for organizations that are themselves chartered by Congress as veterans organizations under federal law.
- [ ] **Register with the Texas Secretary of State.**
- [ ] **File required disclosures** - the registration includes financial reporting and disclosure of professional solicitor relationships.

If none of these categories apply, you do not have a Texas charitable solicitation registration to file. Skip to Step 4.

## Step 4: Federal Filings That Apply Regardless of State

Texas's hands-off approach to charitable registration does not change federal law.

- [ ] **IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N** - annually, due 4 months and 15 days after fiscal year end (May 15 for calendar-year filers).
- [ ] **IRS Form 990-T** if you have unrelated business income of $1,000 or more.
- [ ] **Texas Workforce Commission registration** if you have employees.
- [ ] **Federal employment tax filings** (Form 941, Form 940, W-2/W-3) if you have employees.

## Step 5: Other-State Registration Triggers

Even though Texas does not require registration, your organization may need to register in other states if you solicit there.

- [ ] **Identify states where you actively solicit.** The threshold for "soliciting" varies by state but generally includes: direct mail, online donate buttons accessible to residents, in-person fundraising events, and grant applications to local funders.
- [ ] **Use the Unified Registration Statement (URS)** as a starting point for multi-state registration. About 36 states accept the URS, though most still require state-specific supplements.
- [ ] **Track registration status by state** and renewal deadlines centrally. Lapsed registrations in even one or two large states can disqualify foundation grant applications.

## Step 6: Records and Public Disclosure Obligations

- [ ] **Maintain books and records of account** at the principal office for at least three years (Business Organizations Code Chapter 22).
- [ ] **Members and directors meeting minutes** retained as long as the corporation exists.
- [ ] **Financial records, bank statements, contracts** - recommended retention of at least seven years to align with IRS audit lookback.
- [ ] **IRS Form 1023 application and three most recent Form 990s** must be available for public inspection at the principal office during business hours and copies provided on request, per IRC Section 6104.

## Step 7: Dissolution

When a Texas nonprofit corporation dissolves:

- [ ] **Distribute remaining assets** consistent with the dissolution clause in the Certificate of Formation - to another 501(c)(3) or to a government for public purpose.
- [ ] **File Articles of Dissolution** with the Secretary of State (Form 652). Filing fee: $40.
- [ ] **File a final IRS Form 990** marked "Final return."
- [ ] **Notify the Comptroller** to close out tax accounts.
- [ ] **Notify donors and grantors** if grant funds remain unspent - many grant agreements require return of unused funds at dissolution.

## Common Failure Points

- **Assuming "no registration required" means "no compliance required."** Texas has minimal charitable solicitation oversight but full corporate and tax oversight. Periodic Report 802 failures terminate corporations every year.
- **Soliciting under a special category without registering.** Police, firefighter, EMS, and veteran fundraising registrations are checked. Penalties include civil fines and injunctive relief from the Attorney General.
- **Forgetting Comptroller exemption.** New nonprofits often skip the AP-204 step and then pay sales tax on purchases for years.
- **Operating in Texas as an out-of-state nonprofit without registering as a foreign filing entity.** If your organization has a Texas office or employees, file Form 302 with the Secretary of State as a foreign nonprofit corporation.
- **Treating Texas as a free-pass state for multi-state campaigns.** Texas may not require registration, but the other states you solicit in still do.

The Texas approach reflects a policy preference for limited state intervention in charitable fundraising, paired with strict enforcement in the categories where fraud has historically been concentrated (law enforcement and veteran solicitations). For most 501(c)(3) public charities, that means lower compliance overhead in Texas itself - but no relief from federal obligations or the obligations of other states where they solicit.
