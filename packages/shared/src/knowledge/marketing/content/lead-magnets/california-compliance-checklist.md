## Why California Registration Is Stricter Than Most States

California has the most active charity oversight regime in the country. The Registry of Charitable Trusts, run by the Attorney General's Charitable Trusts Section, registers approximately 100,000 organizations and pursues enforcement actions against hundreds each year. Penalties for late filings, missed audits, and unregistered solicitation are real - and they compound quickly.

If your organization is incorporated in California, raises money from California residents, or holds property in trust for charitable purposes within the state, you are required to register before you solicit a single dollar. The 30-day initial registration window starts when you first receive property - including your initial board contributions or seed funding.

This checklist walks through registration, annual renewal, the financial reporting tiers, the $2 million audit trigger, and the closeout steps when you stop operating in California.

## Step 1: Determine Whether You Must Register

- [ ] **Confirm registration triggers.** You must register if your organization (a) is incorporated in California, (b) holds assets or property in California for charitable purposes, or (c) solicits contributions from California residents - including online solicitation that targets California donors.
- [ ] **Check exemption categories.** Review Government Code Section 12583. Religious organizations, accredited schools, hospitals, and certain government-affiliated entities are exempt. Most 501(c)(3) public charities are not.
- [ ] **Document your basis for any claimed exemption.** If you believe you are exempt, keep written documentation of the exemption category and the supporting facts. Exemption is not self-evident on its face - you may need to defend it.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration on Form CT-1

- [ ] **Obtain an Entity ID from the California Secretary of State** before filing Form CT-1. Out-of-state corporations must file a Statement of Foreign Corporation if doing business in California.
- [ ] **Download Form CT-1** from oag.ca.gov/charities/forms.
- [ ] **Gather required attachments**:
- [ ] Articles of Incorporation (or trust instrument, or association agreement)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter (or evidence of pending application)
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $50, payable by check to the Department of Justice
- [ ] **File Form CT-1 within 30 days of first receiving property.** Mail to Registry of Charitable Trusts, P.O. Box 903447, Sacramento, CA 94203-4470.
- [ ] **Record the assigned CT (charity) number.** This number is required on every subsequent filing.

## Step 3: Calendar Annual Renewal Obligations

The Annual Registration Renewal Fee Report (Form RRF-1) is the recurring filing that keeps your registration active. Missing it is the most common cause of suspension.

- [ ] **Form RRF-1 due date:** 4 months and 15 days after the end of your fiscal year. Calendar-year filers: May 15.
- [ ] **Determine your fee tier** based on gross annual revenue:
- Under $50,000: $0
- $50,000 to $100,000: $25
- $100,001 to $250,000: $50
- $250,001 to $1,000,000: $75
- $1,000,001 to $10,000,000: $150
- $10,000,001 to $50,000,000: $225
- Over $50,000,000: $300
- [ ] **Attach the correct financial schedule:**
- Gross revenue under $50,000 †’ Form CT-TR-1 (Annual Treasurer's Report)
- Gross revenue $50,000 or more †’ IRS Form 990 or 990-EZ
- Private foundations †’ IRS Form 990-PF
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder** before the deadline. Late RRF-1 filings incur a $25 late fee plus potential suspension after extended noncompliance.

## Step 4: Audit Requirement Under the Nonprofit Integrity Act

Government Code Section 12586(e) requires charitable organizations with gross revenue of $2 million or more (excluding government grants requiring program-specific accounting and the audit thereof) to obtain an independent CPA audit annually.

- [ ] **Calculate qualifying gross revenue.** Exclude government grants where the granting agency requires its own audited accounting of those funds.
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early.** A 990 audit takes 60-120 days. Start procurement before fiscal year end if you are at or near the $2 million threshold.
- [ ] **File the audited financial statements** with Form RRF-1.
- [ ] **Establish or maintain an audit committee** - required by Section 12586(e)(2) for organizations subject to the audit requirement. The committee must be appointed by the board, may include non-board members, must not include staff, and may not include the president, CEO, treasurer, or CFO.

## Step 5: Executive Compensation Review

The Nonprofit Integrity Act also requires the board (or a board-authorized committee) to review and approve compensation for the CEO/Executive Director and CFO/Treasurer.

- [ ] **Conduct compensation review** at hire, at any subsequent compensation change, and at minimum every two years.
- [ ] **Document the review process.** Minutes must reflect the comparability data reviewed (compensation surveys, similar organizations) and the board's determination that compensation is "just and reasonable."
- [ ] **Retain documentation** for the period of the executive's employment plus three years.

## Step 6: Solicitation Disclosures

Every solicitation in California must include certain disclosures.

- [ ] **Charity name as registered** with the Attorney General.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **The fact that financial information is available** by writing to the Attorney General's Registry of Charitable Trusts.
- [ ] **For commercial fundraisers and fundraising counsel:** additional disclosures and separate registration requirements apply. Verify any third-party fundraiser is registered before signing a contract.

## Step 7: Maintain the Registry of Charitable Trusts Search Profile

Your organization's registration status is publicly searchable at oag.ca.gov/charities/checkup. Donors, foundations, and government grantors check this before issuing grants.

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually.** Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, fiscal year changes, and dissolution must be reported to the Registry within 30 days using Form CT-2 or appropriate amendment forms.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Many California foundations require an active "Current" status. "Delinquent" or "Suspended" status will disqualify applications.

## Step 8: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in California:

- [ ] **File final RRF-1** marked "Final."
- [ ] **Submit Notice of Distribution of Charitable Assets** before distributing remaining assets - California law requires Attorney General review of dissolution distributions.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Secretary of State only after the Attorney General issues a letter of no objection.
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least four years after dissolution to respond to any post-closeout inquiries.

## Common Failure Points

- **Missing the 30-day initial registration window.** Many organizations treat registration as something to handle "after the first grant." That is too late.
- **Filing Form 990 only with the IRS.** Federal filing does not satisfy California. RRF-1 is a separate state requirement.
- **Treating the audit threshold as a federal issue.** California's $2 million audit trigger is a state requirement, independent of federal Single Audit thresholds.
- **Failing to register out-of-state organizations soliciting California residents.** A nonprofit headquartered in another state that runs an email campaign to California donors is soliciting in California.
- **Allowing status to lapse to "Delinquent."** Foundations and government grantors check Registry status. A delinquent status can cost you a grant award worth far more than the registration fee.

The Registry of Charitable Trusts is administrative, not adversarial - it gives charities multiple notices before suspension. But the consequences of suspension are severe: loss of tax-exempt status under California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 23703, ineligibility for state contracts and grants, and potential personal liability for directors and officers under Government Code Section 12598.
