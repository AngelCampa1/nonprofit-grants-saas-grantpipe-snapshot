## What This Template Covers

This spreadsheet template provides a working structure for tracking restricted fund balances outside of a formal accounting system. It is designed for organizations managing two to eight active restricted grants and needing a documented approach to maintaining fund balances and preparing for funder reports.

The template is not a substitute for fund accounting software. It is a practical tool for organizations that have not yet made that investment and need a disciplined tracking approach that will stand up to basic audit inquiry.

---

## The Spreadsheet Structure

The template is organized with one row per active restricted fund. Each row captures:

**Fund Name** - A clear, consistent name for the fund. Use the funder's name plus the grant cycle or award year when the same funder has multiple awards: "Smith Foundation - Literacy 2025" rather than just "Smith Foundation."

**Funder** - The granting organization or donor. Should match the name in your donor CRM so cross-referencing is possible.

**Award Date** - The date the grant agreement was executed or the contribution was received. Not the application date or the announcement date.

**Restriction Type** - Purpose restriction, time restriction, or perpetual restriction. Most active grants are purpose restrictions. Use the grant agreement's language where available: "restricted to after-school tutoring for youth ages 10-18 in the Riverside service area" is more useful than "purpose restriction."

**Award Amount** - The total amount from the grant agreement or contribution document. If the award will be distributed in installments, note the total award amount and track received-to-date separately.

**Received to Date** - For installment awards, track cumulative cash received separately from the total award amount. These are frequently different, and the distinction matters for cash flow management.

**Expenses by Period** - Monthly actual columns, one per reporting period. Pull these figures from the accounting system after month-end close, by grant fund code. Do not estimate. If expenses have not been posted in the accounting system yet, wait until they have before updating the spreadsheet.

**Cumulative Expenses to Date** - The running total of all expenses posted against this fund from the award start date. This number should match the accounting system's fund code total for the same period.

**Remaining Balance** - Award Amount Received minus Cumulative Expenses to Date. This is the fund balance. It should update automatically via formula when you add monthly actuals.

**Award End Date** - When the grant period expires. Funds not spent by this date generally must be returned. Sort the spreadsheet by this column so the most urgent expirations are at the top.

**Release Conditions** - A brief description of what must happen for the restriction to be released. For purpose restrictions: "qualifying expenditures for literacy program activities." For time restrictions: "receipt of installment on [date]."

**Documentation Reference** - Where the supporting documentation for this fund lives: file path, cabinet drawer, shared drive folder. During audit, the auditor will ask for the grant agreement, expense records, and proof of restriction release. Know where to find them.

**Status** - Active, Closed/Fully Expended, Returned, or Pending (award received, work not yet begun).

## Setting Up the Spreadsheet

Before entering data, create a separate tab for each fiscal year. Restricted grants often span multiple fiscal years; maintain continuous fund records rather than resetting at the fiscal year boundary.

Enter data from grant agreements and award letters - not from memory or email threads. Every field should be traceable to a source document.

After initial setup, run the spreadsheet total against your accounting system's fund code report for the same period. If the numbers match, the baseline is clean. If they do not match, investigate before proceeding. A spreadsheet that starts with incorrect data will compound errors with every update.

---

## Monthly Update Process

The monthly update should happen after the accounting system's month-end close, when all expenses for the period have been posted to the correct fund codes.

**Step 1: Pull the current period expenses from the GL**

Run an expenditure report from the accounting system for the month just closed, filtered by grant fund code. For each active grant, the report should show all expenses posted to that fund code during the period.

**Step 2: Enter the period actuals**

Enter the monthly totals into the corresponding period column on the spreadsheet. Do not round. Enter the exact figure from the GL report.

**Step 3: Verify the cumulative total**

After entering the period actuals, the cumulative expenses column updates via formula. Compare this figure to the cumulative total from the GL for the same period. They should match exactly. A discrepancy means either a prior period entry was incorrect in the spreadsheet or an adjustment was made in the GL that was not reflected in the spreadsheet.

**Step 4: Recalculate remaining balances**

After verifying the cumulative totals, the remaining balance column updates automatically. Review the list for funds with low remaining balances and funds with low burn rates relative to the award end date. Flag any that require attention for the spend-down monitoring process.

**Step 5: Update status and documentation**

Mark any funds that closed during the period as Closed. Update the documentation reference if new supporting materials were added. Note any restrictions that were satisfied during the period and document the qualifying expenditure that triggered the release.

**Step 6: Date and initial the spreadsheet**

Record the date of the update and who performed it. This establishes the documentation trail the auditor will want to see.

---

## Reconciling to the Accounting System

Monthly reconciliation between the spreadsheet and the accounting system is not optional. It is the control that catches errors before they compound.

The reconciliation should compare:

- Total receipts in the spreadsheet vs. total restricted revenue posted in the GL
- Total expenses by fund in the spreadsheet vs. total expenses by fund code in the GL
- Remaining balance by fund in the spreadsheet vs. fund balance in the GL

When these match, the spreadsheet is current and correct. When they do not match, investigate immediately. Common causes:

- An expense was posted to the wrong fund code in the GL
- A grant receipt was deposited and posted in the GL but not added to the spreadsheet
- An indirect cost charge was calculated and posted in the GL but not included in the spreadsheet actuals
- A prior period correction was made in the GL without a corresponding update to the spreadsheet

Document every discrepancy found and every correction made. This documentation is the reconciliation record.

## When the Spreadsheet Is No Longer Sufficient

A spreadsheet is a legitimate tool for restricted fund tracking up to a point. These are the signals that the spreadsheet has reached its limit:

**More than six to eight active restricted grants.** At this scale, the monthly update and reconciliation process takes several hours. The error rate increases. The institutional knowledge required to maintain the spreadsheet concentrates in one or two people whose departure would create a crisis.

**A federal grant arrives.** Federal grants require expenditure documentation that goes beyond what a balance sheet spreadsheet captures: allowable cost categories per 2 CFR 200, time-and-effort records for staff charges, indirect cost rate calculations, and drawdown reconciliation against the payment system. A spreadsheet cannot manage this reliably.

**An auditor makes a comment.** If an auditor notes that the restricted fund tracking methodology is manual and difficult to trace, that is a finding. Act on it before the next audit cycle.

**Staff turnover disrupts the tracking.** When the person who built and maintained the spreadsheet leaves and the methodology is not documented, the next person starts over. This is a data governance failure that a system can prevent.

**The Executive Director needs self-service access.** If leadership has to ask finance staff to run a report to see current restricted fund balances, the tracking system is not providing the management visibility the organization needs.

At any of these points, the transition to purpose-built fund accounting software eliminates the risk. The spreadsheet served its purpose; it has been outgrown.
