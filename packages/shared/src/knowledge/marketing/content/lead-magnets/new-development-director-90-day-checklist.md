## Why the First 90 Days Decide the Next Three Years

Development Director tenure averages around 18 to 24 months in mid-sized nonprofits, well below other leadership roles. The reasons are usually structural: unrealistic board expectations, underdeveloped infrastructure, donor relationships that did not transfer, an Executive Director who wants results without strategy. A new DD walks into all of this in week one.

The first 90 days are when the trajectory gets set. Move too fast and you alienate the staff and donors who built the program before you. Move too slow and the board loses patience. Spend the time wisely and you build the credibility, relationships, and information base that make the next two years possible.

This checklist structures the first 90 days into four phases: orientation (week 1), listening (weeks 2-4), assessment (weeks 5-8), and year-one planning (weeks 9-12). Each phase has specific deliverables. Use it whether you are stepping into a $1M shop or a $10M shop.

## Week 1: Orientation

The first week is logistics, introductions, and not making decisions.

### Day 1

- HR onboarding, equipment, accounts.
- Tour of the facility. Meet program staff.
- Lunch with the Executive Director. Discuss communication norms, recurring meetings, and decision authority.

### Day 2-3

- Read every internal document that bears on fundraising: strategic plan, prior annual reports, last three audits, last fiscal year's gift detail, fundraising plan (if any), donor database access and orientation.
- Meet each member of the development team for a 30-minute introduction.
- Meet the CFO or finance lead for an overview of the chart of accounts, restricted fund tracking, and grant accounting.

### Day 4-5

- Meet other senior staff (program director, communications, operations).
- Schedule first-month meetings with board chair, governance committee chair, development committee chair, and major-donor board members.
- Begin compiling questions for the listening tour. Resist the urge to make any structural decisions.

### End of week 1 deliverable

A two-page memo to the ED summarizing what you learned, what you will focus on in weeks 2-4, and any urgent items requiring immediate attention.

## Weeks 2-4: Listening

Weeks 2 through 4 are about understanding before acting.

### Donor listening (8-15 conversations)

Identify the top 15 donors by lifetime giving and request 30-minute introductory calls or coffees. Goals:

- Introduce yourself.
- Ask: how did you become involved? What is your relationship with the organization? What do you want from your relationship with us?
- Listen. Do not pitch.

The top 5 donors should get in-person visits if geography allows. The remaining 10 can be phone or video.

### Board listening (all members)

Meet every board member individually before week 4 ends. 30 minutes each. Ask:

- Why did you join the board?
- What is going well in fundraising?
- What is not going well?
- What would success look like for you in my role?
- Where do you see yourself contributing?

### Staff listening (development team)

For each member of the development team, schedule a 60-minute one-on-one. Ask:

- What does your role look like in practice?
- What do you spend most of your time on?
- What would you change if you could?
- Where do you feel most confident? Least?
- What support do you need from me?

### Cross-functional listening

- Program director: what fundraising messages and proof points work? What does the program need from development?
- Communications: how do we talk about the work externally? What is in the calendar?
- Finance: where do restricted funds live? How is reporting done? What is the audit cadence?
- Operations: what systems do we use? Where are the friction points?

### End of week 4 deliverable

A confidential memo to the ED documenting:

- Strengths of current operation
- Risks and concerns
- Open questions for assessment phase
- Recommendations on quick wins (week 5 or later)

## Weeks 5-8: Assessment

Use weeks 5 through 8 to convert listening into a structured assessment.

### Fundraising performance review

Pull the last 3 years of giving data. Build:

- Revenue by source (individual, foundation, corporate, government, events, earned)
- Donor count by year
- Donor retention by segment
- Average gift by segment
- Major donor pipeline (active, lapsed, prospect)
- Cost per dollar raised by source

For underlying retention guidance, see the [donor retention strategies guide](/resources/guides/donor-retention-strategies).

### Donor portfolio review

Review the major donor portfolio (top 50 to 100 donors):

- Who is being stewarded actively?
- Who has not been touched in 12+ months?
- Who is at risk of lapsing?
- Who is being under-cultivated?

For each top donor, document last touchpoint, current cultivation stage, and next move. See the [major gift cultivation guide](/resources/guides/major-gift-cultivation-practical-guide).

### Operations and infrastructure review

- CRM: what is it, how clean is the data, how is it used?
- Gift processing: speed, accuracy, internal controls.
- Acknowledgments: timing, content, IRS compliance.
- Reporting: what reports run, how often, who acts on them?
- Stewardship: documented or ad hoc?
- Grants: pipeline, deadlines, compliance.

### Team review

For each direct report:

- Documented job description?
- Last performance review?
- Strengths and growth areas?
- Workload reasonable?

If there is a documented performance issue, address it in the year-one plan, not week 5.

### Board fundraising review

- Give/get policy in place?
- Board giving participation rate?
- Last year's board fundraising contribution?
- Board fundraising committee active?

If the board is not currently fundraising, it will not start fundraising in your first 90 days. Plan for a structured engagement effort starting in month 4.

### End of week 8 deliverable

A 6 to 10 page assessment document covering:

- Current state across fundraising, operations, team, and board
- Key findings (3 to 5 strengths, 3 to 5 challenges)
- Recommended priorities for the year-one plan
- Quick wins identified for weeks 9-12
- Resource and capacity gaps
- Open questions for the ED and board

## Weeks 9-12: Year-One Planning and Quick Wins

The final phase converts assessment into an actionable plan.

### Year-one fundraising plan

Develop a written plan covering:

- Revenue goals by source (built from last year + growth assumptions)
- Strategic priorities (3 to 5)
- Major gift pipeline targets
- Annual fund and recurring giving plans
- Grants pipeline and submissions
- Stewardship plan (see the [donor stewardship plan 12-month guide](/resources/guides/donor-stewardship-plan-12-month-guide))
- Operational improvements
- Team development plan
- Board engagement plan

Present the draft to the ED in week 11. Refine based on feedback. Present to the development committee or board in week 12.

### Quick wins (weeks 9-12)

Pick 3 to 5 quick wins that demonstrate momentum without locking in long-term decisions. Common candidates:

- Cleaning up acknowledgment letter quality and timing
- Documenting major donor cultivation moves
- Calling top 20 donors to thank them for prior support
- Updating donor segments in the CRM
- Setting up a monthly metrics dashboard
- Reviewing and updating gift processing internal controls

Avoid quick wins that commit to long-term direction (CRM migration, vendor changes, major staffing changes). Those wait for year-one plan execution.

### Donor outreach scaling

Move from listening conversations (weeks 2-4) to cultivation moves (weeks 9-12):

- Substantive follow-up to each listening conversation
- Specific next moves documented in CRM
- Initial asks where appropriate for donors who are ready

### Board presentation (end of week 12)

Present the year-one fundraising plan to the board. Cover:

- Listening tour findings
- Assessment summary
- Year-one revenue goals and strategy
- Major operational priorities
- Quick wins delivered
- Board engagement asks

The presentation establishes you as the strategic leader of fundraising. Use it.

## Common First-90-Day Failures

- **Major changes in week 2 based on first impressions.** Listening is not yet complete; impressions are wrong as often as right.
- **No listening tour.** Diving into operations without understanding context. Loses staff and donor trust fast.
- **Public comparison to predecessor.** Whether positive or negative, ages badly.
- **No documentation.** Memory is unreliable; the assessment phase requires written records.
- **Over-promising on year-one revenue.** Underestimates the time to build pipeline. Sets up the role to fail at month 9.
- **Avoiding the ED.** Unscheduled, infrequent communication breeds misalignment.
- **Avoiding the board.** Boards that do not know the new DD do not advocate for resources or engagement.

## What Success Looks Like at Day 90

By day 90, you should have:

- Met every board member, every direct report, the ED, and at least 15 major donors
- Documented the current state of fundraising operations and team
- Delivered 3 to 5 quick wins
- Drafted and presented the year-one fundraising plan
- Established working norms with the ED and senior team
- Started visible cultivation moves with key donors

The first 90 days are not about results. They are about credibility, information, and relationships. The next 12 months produce results based on what you build now.

For the year-one plan elements, see the [annual fund guide](/resources/guides/annual-fund-what-it-is-how-to-build-one) and the [donor stewardship plan 12-month guide](/resources/guides/donor-stewardship-plan-12-month-guide). The 90-day checklist is the foundation; the year-one plan is what you build on it.
