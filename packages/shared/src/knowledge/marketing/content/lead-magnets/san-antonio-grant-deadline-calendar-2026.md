## Why This Calendar Exists

San Antonio nonprofits maintaining a portfolio across city, Bexar County, Texas state, federal pass-through, and foundation funding work from three or four separate calendars at once. Each quarter, two or three deadlines collide. One report gets reconstructed under pressure, and the funder receives a weaker submission than the team is capable of producing.

A single planning view that captures each funder, report type, source URL, and data source is the simplest structural fix.

## What's Inside

- City of San Antonio grant cycles (Metro Health, Department of Human Services, Neighborhood and Housing Services, Department of Arts and Culture)
- Bexar County grants and pass-through cycles (Public Works, Community Resources, Behavioral and Mental Health)
- Texas state agency cycles (HHSC, TWC, TEA, TJJD, DSHS) for FY2026
- Federal pass-through windows flowing through Texas (CDBG, CSBG, Title XX, IDEA-B)
- Foundation cycle fields for South Texas grantmakers your team verifies from current funder pages, award letters, or LOI portals
- Reporting deadlines paired with application deadlines so collision weeks are visible

## How to Use This Calendar

For every grant you currently hold or plan to apply to in 2026, mark each deadline. Then look for the weeks where three or more land together. Decide now what gets sequenced and what runs in parallel.

The calendar covers Texas state FY2026 (September 2025 - August 2026) and federal FY2026 (October 2025 - September 2026), with most foundation cycles on calendar 2026.

## Common Collision Weeks San Antonio Nonprofits Should Plan For

Recurring pinch points each year:

- Early January: Texas state interim reports collide with year-end foundation reports and federal Q1 SF-425 work.
- Late April: 990 filing window overlaps with several large South Texas foundation LOI deadlines.
- Mid-August: Texas state fiscal year close. Every active state award has a final report due in this window.
- Late September: Federal fiscal year close. City of San Antonio reporting for several programs lands the same week.

Plan staffing for those four weeks the year before, not the week of.

## How to Use the Calendar with Your Existing Tracker

Most San Antonio nonprofits already maintain a grants tracker. This calendar is a cross-check. Run your tracker against the calendar quarterly. The gaps fall into two categories:

- Deadlines logged in the wrong week (date-format mistakes are common for federal awards on October cycles).
- Deadlines you did not know about because the original award letter buried the reporting cadence in an attachment.

Both are fixable, and catching them at quarter-start prevents the last-minute reconstruction that weakens reports.

## Why Single-View Calendars Beat Per-Funder Trackers

Most San Antonio development teams maintain separate trackers per funder family - one for foundation grants, one for city contracts, one for Bexar County, one for state awards, one for federal pass-through. The reason is historical: each funder type produces a different paperwork stream, and the trackers grew up around the paperwork rather than around the team's actual calendar.

The downside is that no single document shows the team where time will be tightest. A single consolidated view is the only way to see collision weeks far enough in advance to staff for them.

You do not need new software to do this. A spreadsheet that pulls from each per-funder tracker monthly is sufficient. The calendar in this download is the template; the maintenance discipline - running the monthly pull, looking at the next 90 days every quarter - is what makes it useful in practice.
