## Why This Map Exists

Seattle's foundation landscape is unusual. The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation distributes more grant dollars from a single zip code than most entire states, but its grantmaking is largely closed to unsolicited proposals from mid-sized regional nonprofits. Microsoft Philanthropies funnels significant capital through employee matching rather than direct grantmaking. Boeing's regional giving has shifted as its corporate footprint has changed. The community foundation, the Seattle Foundation, manages a large donor-advised fund pool whose public grants list mixes discretionary and donor-directed distributions.

The result is a landscape where the top names on a Google search of "Seattle foundations" are mostly the wrong prospects for a mid-sized human-services or arts nonprofit, and the funders who will actually fund you are buried below them. This map surfaces the right ones.

## What's Inside

The map covers 25 Pacific Northwest funders ranked by 2024 distributions and weighted toward funders that are reachable for mid-sized nonprofits: Seattle Foundation, Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation Discovery Center grants, Microsoft Philanthropies, Norcliffe Foundation, Boeing Company Charitable Trust, Bullitt Foundation, Russell Family Foundation, the Stranahan Foundation, Raikes Foundation, Glaser Progress Foundation, Wyman Youth Trust, ArtsFund, Pride Foundation, Tulalip Tribes Charitable Fund, Boeing Employees Community Fund, Casey Family Programs, Puget Sound Energy Foundation, Empire Health Foundation, Premera Social Impact, Group Health Foundation, Rainier Valley Community Development Fund, Schultz Family Foundation, Allen Foundation for the Arts, Vulcan Productions philanthropic arm, and the Lake Forest Park Civic Club Foundation.

For each funder the map shows median grant size and range, typical grant count per cycle, program areas funded over the last three fiscal years (not the priorities page), the application intake mode, the realistic time-to-decision, and flags for program-officer turnover or strategic shifts.

## Who This Is For

Development directors and executive directors at Seattle, Tacoma, Everett, and broader Puget Sound nonprofits with operating budgets between $500K and $10 million. The map is particularly useful for arts and culture organizations adjusting to post-pandemic foundation behavior, environmental and climate nonprofits prospecting against the Bullitt Foundation's sunset, and human-services organizations rebuilding corporate-foundation strategy in the wake of major employer changes.

## How to Use This Map

Open it next to your current PNW prospect list. Cross out funders where the median grant size, geographic focus, or program area no longer matches your work. The remaining names get a 24-month giving pull from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer or Candid before any LOI is drafted.

For corporate foundations, also check whether your organization is currently in the employer's matching gift program. Microsoft, Boeing, and Amazon employee matching can deliver more capital with less effort than a corporate foundation grant for many mid-sized nonprofits.

## Common Failure Modes This Map Surfaces

Pacific Northwest foundations have several patterns that catch newer development staff:

- The Bullitt Foundation announced a planned spend-down. The map flags timing for organizations relying on Bullitt as part of a multi-year revenue plan.
- Microsoft Philanthropies' direct grantmaking is more concentrated by theme than the public website suggests; many regional human-services LOIs are misaligned at the topic level.
- Seattle Foundation's public grants list mixes discretionary and DAF-directed grants. Reading both as evidence of the foundation's priorities will mislead your prospect strategy.
- Several family foundations on the list have program-officer turnover patterns that reset relationships every 18-24 months. The map flags those so you do not over-invest in a relationship that will be handed off.

## What to Do Next

Download the map. Cross out the funders that no longer match. Pull 24-month giving for the rest. Update your tracking system before drafting another LOI. The first pass typically takes 45 minutes for an organization tracking 30-50 PNW prospects.
