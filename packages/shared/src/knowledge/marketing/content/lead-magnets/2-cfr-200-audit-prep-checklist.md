## Why This Checklist Exists

Single audits test a defined set of compliance requirements. The OMB Compliance Supplement identifies which requirements apply to each federal program and what auditors are expected to test. This checklist maps those requirements to the documentation you need to have in hand - not assembled from scratch when fieldwork begins, but maintained as a running record throughout the year.

The threshold reminder: your organization is subject to a single audit when it expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in a fiscal year. This threshold was updated in the 2024 revisions to 2 CFR Part 200, effective for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later.

---

## Section 1: Restricted Fund Traceability

Auditors test that federal funds were spent on allowable activities within the terms of the award. Every expenditure charged to a federal grant must trace back to a source document that demonstrates: what was purchased, that it benefited the funded program, and that it was allocable to the grant budget category.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Grant-specific expenditure reports are run monthly from the accounting system
      _Why auditors check this: They need to trace every expenditure from the general ledger to the underlying source document. If monthly reports do not exist, the audit trail is reconstructed from scratch._

- [ ] Each active grant has a budget-to-actual comparison updated at least monthly
      _Why auditors check this: Budget variances signal potential rebudgeting without prior approval. Overspent categories are a disallowance risk._

- [ ] Indirect costs are applied at the correct approved rate (NICRA or 15% de minimis) using the correct base
      _Why auditors check this: Incorrect indirect cost rate application - wrong rate, wrong base, or inconsistent application - is a common questioned cost source._

- [ ] All expenditures are coded to the correct grant and budget category at the time of entry
      _Why auditors check this: After-the-fact recoding of transactions is a red flag for compliance staff trying to make the books match the budget._

- [ ] Any budget modifications exceeding the allowable rebudgeting threshold were pre-approved by the funder in writing
      _Why auditors check this: Expenditures in categories that were modified without required prior approval are disallowable, even if the cost itself is allowable._

- [ ] Program income, if any, was reported correctly and applied per the method specified in the award
      _Why auditors check this: Program income is a testable compliance requirement. Unreported or misapplied program income is a finding._

- [ ] Equipment purchases (unit cost $10,000+) are in the equipment inventory with acquisition cost, location, and condition
      _Why auditors check this: Equipment management is tested in the Equipment and Real Property Management compliance requirement._

## Section 2: Time-and-Effort Documentation

Personnel costs are the largest compliance testing area in most single audits. 2 CFR 200.430 requires contemporaneous records demonstrating that salary charges match actual work performed.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] All employees whose salary is charged to a federal award have T&E documentation for the full grant period
      _Why auditors check this: Missing T&E records for a period cause the associated payroll costs to be questioned. Questioned costs can become disallowed costs._

- [ ] T&E records reflect actual effort, not budgeted percentages
      _Why auditors check this: Allocating salary based on the grant budget rather than actual activity is one of the most common findings. Budgeted effort and actual effort must be reconciled._

- [ ] T&E records are contemporaneous - prepared at or near the time of the work, not at year-end
      _Why auditors check this: Record dates are examined. Certifications signed on a single date covering months of activity are flagged as retroactive._

- [ ] Employees working solely on one federal program have semi-annual certifications signed by the employee or supervisor
      _Why auditors check this: The semi-annual certification is the minimum documentation standard for single-program employees under 2 CFR 200.430(i)(1)(i)._

- [ ] Employees with split funding have monthly personnel activity reports accounting for 100% of compensated time
      _Why auditors check this: Split-funded employees must account for all time, not just grant-funded time. Partial records fail the standard._

- [ ] T&E records are signed by the employee and/or supervisor
      _Why auditors check this: Unsigned records lack the accountability that the standard requires._

- [ ] Payroll charges to grants match the allocations shown in T&E records
      _Why auditors check this: Discrepancies between T&E records and actual payroll charges indicate the records are not controlling the allocation - one or the other is inaccurate._

## Section 3: Procurement Files

Federal procurement standards under 2 CFR 200.317-200.327 require that purchases above certain thresholds be made through documented competitive processes. Auditors test both whether competition occurred and whether it was documented.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Purchases above the organization's controlling micro-purchase threshold and at or below its simplified acquisition threshold were made using documented competitive procedures
      _Why auditors check this: Purchases above the controlling micro-purchase threshold require competitive solicitation. The 2026 FAR ceiling is generally $15,000 for micro-purchases and $350,000 for simplified acquisition, but your policy, award, or pass-through terms may set a lower threshold._

- [ ] Sole-source procurements have written justification meeting one of the four permissible exceptions under 2 CFR 200.320
      _Why auditors check this: Sole-source awards without justification are a procurement finding regardless of whether the vendor was reasonable._

- [ ] All vendors, contractors, and subcontractors are verified as not debarred on SAM.gov at the time of award
      _Why auditors check this: Making awards to debarred parties is a suspension and debarment compliance finding. SAM.gov verification records must be on file._

- [ ] Contract files include: the solicitation or justification, bids/proposals received, selection rationale, and the signed contract
      _Why auditors check this: Incomplete procurement files indicate the competitive process may not have occurred as documented._

- [ ] Conflict of interest review was completed for all contracts above threshold
      _Why auditors check this: Organizational conflict of interest is a procurement standards requirement. If a board member's organization received a contract, was that conflict disclosed and managed?_

## Section 4: Subrecipient Monitoring Records

For every organization receiving federal funds as a sub-award from your organization, you have monitoring obligations under 2 CFR 200.332. These obligations begin before the sub-award is made and continue through the subrecipient's closeout.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] A written subrecipient vs. contractor determination is on file for every downstream relationship
      _Why auditors check this: The classification determines which compliance framework applies. Auditors apply the 2 CFR 200.331 five-factor test substantively._

- [ ] A pre-award risk assessment is documented for each current-year sub-award
      _Why auditors check this: 2 CFR 200.332(b) requires risk assessment before the sub-award is made. Its absence is an automatic finding._

- [ ] Sub-award documents include all required provisions from 2 CFR 200.332(a): federal award information, CFDA number, applicable compliance requirements, period of performance
      _Why auditors check this: Sub-award agreements missing required provisions are a compliance finding even if the program runs well._

- [ ] Subrecipient financial reports were reviewed and discrepancies or delays were followed up
      _Why auditors check this: Receiving reports is not monitoring. Reviewing them and documenting the review is what 2 CFR 200.332(d) requires._

- [ ] For subrecipients expending $1,000,000+ in federal awards, a current single audit was obtained from fac.gov and reviewed
      _Why auditors check this: The pass-through entity must verify subrecipient audit status. Using fac.gov is the standard verification method._

- [ ] Any monitoring findings from site visits or desk reviews were documented and followed up
      _Why auditors check this: Monitoring that identifies problems but does not document follow-up is inadequate - finding resolution is part of the monitoring obligation._

## Section 5: SEFA Accuracy

The Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards is the foundation of the single audit. Inaccuracies in the SEFA affect major program selection and can create scope limitations in the audit.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] All federal awards active during the fiscal year appear on the SEFA, including sub-awards from state pass-through entities
      _Why auditors check this: Missing awards on the SEFA affect the major program calculation. An omitted award that would have been selected as major creates a scope deficiency._

- [ ] Each SEFA row includes the correct Assistance Listing (CFDA) number for the program
      _Why auditors check this: The Assistance Listing number identifies the program for testing purposes. Incorrect numbers cause misclassification._

- [ ] Sub-awards received include the pass-through entity name and the pass-through's identifying number
      _Why auditors check this: Required disclosures under 2 CFR 200.510(b). Missing pass-through information is an audit adjustment._

- [ ] Cluster programs are identified correctly per Part 5 of the OMB Compliance Supplement
      _Why auditors check this: Cluster misidentification affects major program selection and compliance testing scope._

- [ ] The SEFA total for each award reconciles to the grant-specific expenditure total in the general ledger
      _Why auditors check this: SEFA accuracy is tested by reconciling to accounting records. Discrepancies require explanation and correction._

- [ ] The SEFA note discloses the accounting basis, indirect cost rate method, and any significant accounting policies
      _Why auditors check this: The note is a required disclosure under 2 CFR 200.510(b)(6). Missing or incomplete notes are an audit adjustment._

## Section 6: SF-SAC Filing Status

The SF-SAC data collection form is submitted to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse as part of the single audit reporting package. Its submission is a compliance obligation.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] The SF-SAC was submitted to fac.gov within the required deadline: the earlier of 30 days after audit completion or 9 months after fiscal year end
      _Why auditors check this: Late submission is a compliance finding in the current year's audit. The submission date is publicly visible in fac.gov._

- [ ] The prior year's SF-SAC submission is accessible at fac.gov and the organization's FAC entity ID is known
      _Why auditors check this: Current-year auditors reference the prior year's FAC submission during planning. If prior submissions are missing or incomplete, that signals a compliance history issue._

- [ ] If the prior year's audit had findings, the corrective action plan was implemented and the Summary Schedule of Prior Audit Findings is prepared for the current year
      _Why auditors check this: Prior findings that are not resolved become repeat findings - which are weighted as higher risk in major program selection._

- [ ] If federal awards will cross the $1,000,000 threshold this fiscal year for the first time, a qualified single auditor has been engaged
      _Why auditors check this: Single audits require an auditor with appropriate government auditing standards credentials (GAGAS). General financial statement auditors may not have this qualification._

## Pre-Fieldwork Final Check

Before auditors begin fieldwork, confirm:

- [ ] The SEFA draft is reconciled to the general ledger
- [ ] T&E records for the full audit period are compiled and organized by employee
- [ ] Grant files for each major program are complete: award document, budget, all modifications, all reports, all expenditure documentation
- [ ] Subrecipient monitoring files are complete and current
- [ ] The corrective action plan from any prior-year findings has documented implementation evidence
- [ ] Key personnel available during fieldwork have been briefed on the audit areas where questions are likely

This checklist covers the core federal compliance areas tested in most single audits. Program-specific compliance requirements - eligibility, matching, period of performance, special tests - vary by program and are defined in the applicable OMB Compliance Supplement section. Review the Supplement section for each major program in scope before fieldwork begins.
