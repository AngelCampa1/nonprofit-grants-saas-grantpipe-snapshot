## Why This Calendar Exists

Most Houston nonprofits maintaining a portfolio of city, county, state, federal pass-through, and foundation funding work from three or four separate calendars at once. The result is predictable: each quarter, two or three deadlines collide, one gets reconstructed from incomplete data, and the report quality drops because the deadline pressure made the team take shortcuts.

A single planning view that captures each funder, report type, source URL, and data source is the simplest structural fix for that pattern. This is the worksheet structure for building that 2026 calendar from source documents.

## What's Inside

- City of Houston grant cycles (Houston Health Department, Department of Neighborhoods, Houston Public Library, Mayor's Office programs)
- Harris County grants and pass-through funding cycles (Public Health, Community Services, Juvenile Probation)
- Texas state agency cycles (HHSC, TWC, TEA, TJJD) for FY2026
- Federal pass-through windows that flow through Texas (CDBG, CSBG, Title XX, IDEA-B)
- Foundation cycle fields for Houston-area grantmakers your team verifies from current funder pages, award letters, or LOI portals
- Reporting deadlines paired with application deadlines so you can spot the months where reports and applications collide
- A reconciliation column showing which reports share data sources, so you can sequence them

## How to Use This Calendar

For every grant you currently hold or plan to apply to in 2026, mark the application deadline, the interim report deadlines, and the final report deadline. Then look for the months where three or more deadlines land in the same week.

For those weeks, decide now what gets sequenced and what gets parallelized. If two reports share the same GL data sources, one of them needs to be ready first, and the second one inherits its inputs. If they don't share sources, run them in parallel - but assign the work that week, not the week before.

The calendar covers the FY2026 cycle (October 2025 - September 2026 for federal awards; calendar 2026 for most foundations and state programs).

## Common Collision Weeks Houston Nonprofits Should Plan For

The recurring pinch points each year:

- Mid-January: Texas state agency interim reports collide with year-end foundation reports and the federal Q1 SF-425 cycle. The data sources overlap, but the formats do not - sequencing matters.
- Late April: 990 filing window overlaps with several major foundation LOI deadlines. Finance and development compete for the same staff time.
- Mid-July: Texas state FY closes August 31, but interim reports for the year's largest awards are usually due in mid-July. This is also when most reporting on city of Houston grants is due.
- Late September: The federal fiscal year closes. Everything else gets crowded.

Plan staffing for those four weeks the year before, not the week of. The calendar marks them explicitly so they appear on next year's planning cycle.

## How to Use the Calendar with Your Existing Tracker

Most Houston nonprofits already maintain a grants tracker - usually in a spreadsheet, sometimes in a CRM. The calendar is not a replacement; it is a cross-check. Run your tracker against this calendar quarterly. The gaps it surfaces fall into two categories:

- Deadlines you knew about but logged in the wrong week (date-format errors are surprisingly common for federal awards on October cycles).
- Deadlines you did not know about because the original award letter buried the reporting cadence in an attachment that nobody filed.

Both are fixable. Both prevent the kind of last-minute reconstruction that makes reports weaker than they should be.
