## Why This Map Exists

Chicago has roughly 32,000 registered nonprofits, and Cook County alone hosts a foundation community that distributes well over $1.5 billion annually. The giving is concentrated, but the patterns are not what most application strategies assume - Chicago foundations vary widely in how they treat unsolicited LOIs, application cycles, and program-officer continuity.

This map shows what each foundation has actually funded over the last three years, paired with their stated priorities, so you can decide which LOIs are worth your team's time before you send them.

## What's Inside

- A ranked Chicagoland funder table your team fills from current 990-PF and funder-page research
- Median grant size, range, and typical grant count per cycle
- The actual program areas funded over the last three years

- Which funders accept unsolicited LOIs and which only fund prior grantees
- Program-officer transitions and the signals they send

## How to Use This Map

Pick the three funders whose 24-month grant history most closely matches your program area, budget tier, and geographic focus within Cook County. Those are your real prospects. Use the median-grant-size column to right-size every ask before submission - over-asks are the second-most-common rejection reason in this region.

## Common Failure Modes This Map Surfaces

The patterns most Chicago nonprofits miss until the second or third declined LOI:

- Foundations whose stated priorities still list a program area they have not funded in three years. The priorities page is aspirational; the 990-PF is descriptive.
- Foundations whose typical grant size is significantly below what your program requires. The mismatch wastes their cycle and yours.
- Foundations that only fund prior grantees but still accept unsolicited LOIs. The LOI is read; it does not lead anywhere.
- Foundations where the 24-month giving has shifted. Strategic-plan changes show up first in the 990-PF, often a year before the website reflects them.

Each entry in the map flags these patterns where they appear so the prospect list you build is one you can act on.

## What to Do With This Map First

Open the map next to your current prospect list. Cross out funders where the median grant size, geographic focus, or program area no longer matches your work. The remaining names get a 24-month-grant-history pull from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer or Candid before any LOI is drafted. Either the match holds, or the gap surfaces - and either answer saves the time you would have spent on a doomed LOI.

Most Chicago organizations cut their tracked prospect list by 30-50% after one pass and redirect that capacity to deeper stewardship of the funders that survive the cut.
