## Why This Map Exists

New York City has a dense foundation market, but public priority pages rarely tell the whole story. Prospecting works better when your team compares current 990-PF grants, stated priorities, intake mode, and relationship path before drafting.

The most common mistake mid-sized nonprofits make is applying to a foundation based on its stated priorities without checking what it has actually funded over the last three years. The 990-PF tells a different story than the website. This map pulls the 990-PF data and pairs it with what the foundation says it cares about - so you can spot the gap before you spend two months on an LOI that was never going to land.

## What's Inside

- A ranked NYC-area funder table your team fills from current 990-PF and funder-page research
- Median grant size, range, and typical grant count per cycle
- The actual program areas funded over the last three years (not the website list)
- LOI windows, full proposal calendars, and the realistic time-to-decision
- Which funders actively review unsolicited LOIs and which only fund prior grantees
- Program-officer turnover patterns - the foundations where relationships reset every 18 months

## How to Use This Map

Pick the three funders whose 24-month grant history most closely matches your program area, budget size, and population served. Those are your real prospects. The rest of the list tells you who not to spend time on.

If your typical ask is in the $25K-$75K range, the map's "right-sized funder" filter will cut your prospect list in half - and roughly double your hit rate on the funders that remain.

The map updates annually. Each year's edition reflects the prior fiscal year's actual giving, not announced intentions. The current edition reflects FY2024 990-PF filings.

## Common Failure Modes This Map Surfaces

The patterns most NYC nonprofits miss until the second or third declined LOI:

- Funders whose website lists a program area they have not actually funded in three years. The priorities page is aspirational; the 990-PF is descriptive. Use the 990-PF.
- Funders whose typical grant size is one-third of what your program needs. The mismatch wastes their cycle and yours.
- Funders who only fund prior grantees but accept unsolicited LOIs anyway. The LOI gets read; it does not lead anywhere. Knowing this in advance lets you redirect the time.
- Funders whose 24-month giving has shifted. New trustees, new program officers, and new strategic plans show up first in the 990-PF, often a full year before the website is updated.

Each entry in the map flags these patterns where they appear, so the prospect list you build from this document is one you can act on, not one you have to verify.

## What to Do With This Map First

Open it next to your current prospect list. Cross out the funders where the median grant size, geographic focus, or program area no longer matches your work. The crossed-out names get removed from your tracking. The remaining names get a 24-month-grant-history pull from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer or Candid before any LOI is drafted. The pull either confirms the match or surfaces a gap that would have killed the LOI anyway - and either answer saves time.

Most organizations report cutting their tracked prospect list by 30-50% after one pass with this map, and reallocating that capacity to deeper relationship work with the funders that remain.
