## What the Scorecard Measures

Financial health for a nonprofit isn't a single number. It's the relationship between liquidity, revenue stability, structural assets, and governance discipline. The scorecard combines those four dimensions into ten questions and grades the result A through F.

**Liquidity (cash reserves, debt).** How long the organization can operate if revenue stops, and how much of the asset base is encumbered.

**Revenue stability (concentration, restricted balance).** How exposed the organization is to a single funder leaving, and how much of net assets are available for general operations.

**Structural soundness (audit, indirect, budget variance).** Whether the financial statements are credible, whether the organization recovers the full cost of its programs, and whether actuals track the budget.

**Governance discipline (board review, receivables aging, policy currency).** Whether oversight is working, whether the organization manages its receivables, and whether the rules of the road are current.

## How the Grades Are Distributed

The scorecard isn't graded on a curve. It's graded against absolute thresholds that map to nonprofit finance norms documented in audit literature and Form 990 analysis. In practice:

- **A (90%+):** Roughly the top 10% of mid-sized nonprofits. Strong on every dimension.
- **B (75-89%):** Solid posture with one or two specific gaps. The most common gap is revenue concentration.
- **C (55-74%):** Functioning, but with meaningful exposure. Reserves and indirect cost recovery are typical pressure points.
- **D (35-54%):** Multiple weaknesses compounding risk. Stabilization work needed before growth investment.
- **F (under 35%):** Structurally fragile. The scorecard recommends a one-time diagnostic engagement with a fractional CFO or auditor.

## Using the Result

The scorecard links to the GrantPipe guides that map to your weakest answers. The most common needs by grade band:

- **A and B grades:** Tactical refinements - Indirect Cost Rate Negotiation Worksheet for organizations on the de minimis rate considering a NICRA, Single Audit Prep Timeline for organizations approaching the $1M federal expenditure threshold.
- **C grade:** Structural work - Nonprofit Financial Report Template for tightening monthly reporting, Cost Allocation Plan Worksheet for organizations charging indirects without a formal basis.
- **D and F grades:** Stabilization - written cash forecasting, audit prep, written gift acceptance and procurement policies. The scorecard explicitly flags the case for a fractional CFO engagement when the structural picture warrants outside help.

The scorecard is a diagnostic, not a prescription. Use it to surface the questions to bring to your finance committee and your auditor. Retake it after a year of remediation work to validate that the changes translated into a higher grade.

## What the assessment measures

The assessment scores readiness across the control areas and operating practices that most reliably distinguish well-run mid-sized nonprofits from organizations operating in chronic catch-up mode. Each area is weighted by the operational and audit risk it carries, so the score reflects where remediation effort should go first.

## How to use your score

A score above 80 indicates strong fundamentals across the assessed dimensions. A score between 60 and 80 indicates one or two areas that need attention, usually addressable within a quarter of focused work. A score below 60 indicates structural gaps that benefit from a deliberate remediation calendar with named owners and dates. The questionnaire routes you to the specific remediation worksheet for each gap so you can act on the score immediately rather than scheduling a follow-up project.

## What separates strong programs from weak ones

The pattern across nonprofits that score well: they treat the relevant work as monthly discipline rather than annual scramble. They document as they go, they tie financial and program data together at the moment of entry rather than reconstructing it at report time, and they use systems that surface the relevant constraints and metrics on a regular cadence rather than only at deadline pressure. These are cultural and structural traits more than they are technology choices, but the technology either supports the discipline or fights it.

## After the assessment

Once you have your score, the next step is a remediation calendar with named owners and dates. The remediation worksheets the assessment routes you to are designed to be completed inside one or two work sessions, not as multi-month projects. The principle throughout: shift the work from annual to monthly, document as you go, and the underlying numbers improve on the same multi-year horizon as the underlying balance sheet and operating practices.
