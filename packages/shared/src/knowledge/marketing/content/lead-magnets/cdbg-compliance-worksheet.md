## Why CDBG Compliance Surprises Nonprofit Subrecipients

Many nonprofits accept Community Development Block Grant subrecipient awards thinking they are similar to other federal grants. They are not. CDBG carries a layered compliance regime: HUD's CDBG-specific rules at 24 CFR Part 570, the Uniform Guidance at [2 CFR 200](/resources/guides/uniform-guidance-2-cfr-200-practical-guide), the grantee's local policies, and program-specific cross-cutting requirements (environmental review, Davis-Bacon, fair housing, Section 3, lead-based paint). The HUD Office of Community Planning and Development monitors these layers, and findings cascade through the grantee to the subrecipient.

This worksheet covers the obligations most nonprofit subrecipients face. It is not a substitute for your subrecipient agreement, your grantee's policy manual, or HUD's notices. Use it as a checkpoint structure to make sure nothing is missed. For the introductory overview, see the [CDBG guide](/resources/guides/cdbg-community-development-block-grants-nonprofits).

## Section 1: National Objectives Documentation

Every CDBG-funded activity must meet a national objective under 24 CFR 570.208. The grantee assigns the objective in the agreement, but the subrecipient is responsible for documenting compliance.

### Three national objectives

1. **Low- and moderate-income (LMI) benefit.** Activities serving LMI persons by area, by limited clientele, by housing, or by job creation/retention.
2. **Prevention or elimination of slums and blight.** Activities addressing specific slum or blight conditions in defined areas or on a spot basis.
3. **Urgent need.** Activities responding to threats to public health or welfare; rare and tightly defined.

### LMI documentation requirements

Most nonprofit subrecipients meet the LMI national objective. Documentation depends on the specific subcategory:

- **LMI Area Benefit (LMA):** The activity benefits all residents of a primarily residential area where 51 percent or more are LMI. Document with current HUD LMI data for the service area census tracts or a HUD-approved income survey.
- **LMI Limited Clientele (LMC):** The activity serves a clientele that is at least 51 percent LMI or that consists exclusively of presumed-eligible groups (abused children, elderly persons, severely disabled adults, homeless persons, illiterate adults, persons with AIDS, migrant farmworkers). Document with individual income certifications or status verification.
- **LMI Housing (LMH):** Activity provides or improves permanent residential structures occupied by LMI households. Document with household income verification.
- **LMI Jobs (LMJ):** Activity creates or retains jobs, at least 51 percent of which are held by or made available to LMI persons. Document with employee income verification or area benefit standard for job availability.

### Documentation checkpoints

For every activity:

- Which national objective and subcategory?
- What documentation method?
- Who is responsible for collecting it?
- Where is it filed?
- How is it reviewed before reporting?

Insufficient national objective documentation is the most common CDBG finding. Build the documentation flow before the activity begins, not after.

## Section 2: Eligible Activities

CDBG funds can only support activities listed as eligible under 24 CFR 570.201-.207. Common eligible activities for nonprofit subrecipients:

- Public services (570.201(e)) - capped at 15 percent of grantee's annual allocation
- Acquisition of real property (570.201(a))
- Public facilities and improvements (570.201(c))
- Rehabilitation of residential and non-residential buildings (570.202)
- Special economic development activities (570.203)
- Microenterprise assistance (570.201(o))

Activities not on the eligible list cannot be supported with CDBG funds even if they are otherwise meritorious. Confirm eligibility before incurring any costs.

### The 15 percent public services cap

If your activity is classified as a public service, the grantee can only spend up to 15 percent of its annual CDBG allocation on public services collectively. This often constrains how much CDBG funding is available for nonprofit social service subrecipients regardless of their proposal quality.

## Section 3: Environmental Review

CDBG funds cannot be committed (and grant agreements cannot trigger physical activity) until environmental review is complete under 24 CFR Part 58. The grantee is the responsible entity, but the subrecipient often supplies information.

### Categories of review

- **Exempt activities** (570.604): Administrative, planning, and certain other activities that are exempt from environmental review.
- **Categorically excluded activities (CENST):** Subject to environmental review but generally minimal impact.
- **Environmental Assessment (EA):** Required for activities not exempt or excluded; documents potential impacts and findings.
- **Environmental Impact Statement (EIS):** Required for activities with significant environmental effects; rare for nonprofit subrecipient activities.

### Subrecipient checkpoints

- Is environmental review complete and on file with the grantee?
- Has Release of Funds been received from HUD (when required)?
- Is the activity start date after Release of Funds?
- Are any conditions (mitigation measures) documented and being followed?

Committing funds before environmental clearance is a compliance violation that voids CDBG eligibility. Wait for the grantee's written authorization to proceed.

## Section 4: Cross-Cutting Federal Requirements

CDBG triggers a set of federal cross-cutting requirements that apply to the activity regardless of the underlying program.

### Davis-Bacon and Related Acts

Applies to most non-residential construction over $2,000 funded with CDBG. Requires payment of locally prevailing wages and submission of certified payrolls. Most subrecipients without prior federal construction experience underestimate this burden. The grantee typically supplies wage determinations and reviews payrolls.

### Fair Housing and Civil Rights

Title VI, Section 504, Age Discrimination Act, and Fair Housing Act apply. Subrecipients must:

- Affirmatively further fair housing
- Maintain Section 504 self-evaluation and transition plans (for entities with 15+ employees receiving federal funds for facilities)
- Provide language access for limited English proficient persons
- Avoid discrimination in services, employment, and contracting

### Section 3

Applies to certain HUD-funded construction and rehabilitation. Requires economic opportunities for low-income residents and businesses in the project area. Grantees set thresholds; subrecipients on covered projects must report on Section 3 outcomes.

### Lead-Based Paint

Applies to housing rehabilitation in pre-1978 buildings. Subrecipients must follow specific notification, evaluation, and abatement requirements depending on funding amount and rehabilitation scope.

### Uniform Relocation Assistance (URA)

Applies if CDBG-funded activity displaces residents or businesses. URA requires advance notice, replacement housing assistance, and moving expense compensation.

### Procurement (2 CFR 200.317-.327)

CDBG procurement must follow Uniform Guidance procurement standards. Methods include micro-purchase, small purchase, sealed bids, competitive proposals, and noncompetitive (sole source). Document the method used and the rationale.

## Section 5: Beneficiary Tracking and Reporting

Most CDBG activities require beneficiary tracking by:

- Total persons served
- Race, ethnicity, and income (for LMI documentation)
- Female-headed household status (where applicable)
- Other demographic data per the grantee's reporting template

### IDIS reporting

The grantee enters subrecipient activity data into HUD's Integrated Disbursement and Information System (IDIS). The subrecipient typically supplies the data on a quarterly or monthly basis using the grantee's template.

### CAPER

The grantee submits the Consolidated Annual Performance and Evaluation Report (CAPER) within 90 days of program year end. Subrecipient performance feeds into the CAPER. Late or inaccurate subrecipient data delays the grantee's submission, which is a relationship-damaging outcome.

## Section 6: Financial Compliance

### Cost reasonableness and allocability

Costs charged to CDBG must be reasonable, allocable, and allowable under 2 CFR 200 Subpart E. Common errors:

- Charging unallowable costs (alcohol, fundraising, lobbying)
- Allocating costs to CDBG that primarily benefit other programs
- Lacking documentation for cost allocation methodology

### Time and effort documentation

Personnel charged to CDBG require time and effort documentation per 2 CFR 200.430. Semi-annual certifications for staff working solely on the funded activity; after-the-fact time records for staff splitting time.

### Drawdown timing

CDBG funds are drawn down from the grantee on a reimbursement basis. The subrecipient incurs costs first, then requests reimbursement with supporting documentation. Avoid lump-sum drawdowns that exceed actual incurred costs - the grantee will require return of funds.

### Single audit

If your organization spends $1,000,000 or more in federal funds in a fiscal year, you trigger a single audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F. CDBG expenditures count toward the threshold. For audit prep guidance, see [common single audit findings](/resources/guides/common-single-audit-findings).

## Section 7: Subrecipient Agreement Review

Before signing or accepting a CDBG subrecipient agreement, confirm it includes the elements required by 24 CFR 570.503:

- Statement of work
- Records and reports
- Program income
- Uniform administrative requirements (2 CFR 200)
- Other program requirements (Davis-Bacon, fair housing, etc., as applicable)
- Conditions for religious organizations (if applicable)
- Suspension and termination
- Reversion of assets

If any element is missing or unclear, raise it with the grantee before signing. Ambiguity in the agreement compounds during monitoring.

## Section 8: Monitoring Visits

The grantee will monitor subrecipient compliance, typically annually. Prepare:

- Activity files with national objective documentation
- Beneficiary records
- Financial records and drawdown documentation
- Procurement files
- Construction documentation (if applicable)
- Time and effort records
- Sub-subrecipient monitoring (if you pass funds further down)

For broader subrecipient monitoring guidance, see [subrecipient monitoring requirements](/resources/guides/subrecipient-monitoring-requirements-uniform-guidance).

## Common CDBG Compliance Failures

- **Insufficient national objective documentation.** Files do not show how the activity meets the assigned national objective.
- **Funds committed before environmental clearance.** Activity costs incurred before the grantee's Release of Funds.
- **Missing Davis-Bacon certified payrolls.** Construction activity without wage compliance documentation.
- **Procurement violations.** No competitive process or no documentation of method used.
- **Beneficiary income not verified.** LMI claims not backed by individual or area documentation.
- **Late or incomplete reporting.** Quarterly data missed or inconsistent with financial drawdowns.

The discipline that prevents these is simple: build documentation flows before the activity starts, file as you go, and treat the grantee's monitoring template as the running checklist. CDBG compliance is not unmanageable, but it is unforgiving of "we will document it later."
