## New York's Dual Statutory Framework

Two separate New York statutes regulate charitable organizations, and most charities are subject to both:

- **Executive Law Article 7-A** governs charitable solicitation. It applies to any organization that solicits contributions in New York.
- **Estates, Powers & Trusts Law (EPTL) Section 8-1.4** governs charitable trusts and most charitable corporations.

The Charities Bureau administers both. A single registration covers both statutes, and the CHAR410 / CHAR500 forms have separate sections for each. This dual framework is why New York is more demanding than states with a single charitable solicitation regime - even charities that do not solicit may still need to register under EPTL.

## Step 1: Confirm Registration Triggers

- [ ] **Article 7-A registration** is required if the organization solicits contributions from New York residents - including online appeals targeting NY donors, direct mail, telephone, and in-person solicitation.
- [ ] **EPTL registration** is required if the organization is incorporated under NY Not-for-Profit Corporation Law, holds assets in trust for charitable purposes in New York, or has its principal office in New York.
- [ ] **Identify exemptions.** Article 7-A exempts religious organizations, certain educational institutions registered with the NY Education Department, parent-teacher associations, and certain veteran groups. Most 501(c)(3) public charities are not exempt.
- [ ] **Out-of-state organizations** soliciting NY residents must register under Article 7-A regardless of where they are incorporated.

## Step 2: Initial Registration on CHAR410

- [ ] **Create an account at charitiesnys.com** for the e-filing portal.
- [ ] **File CHAR410** within the registration window - before solicitation begins for Article 7-A, or within six months of receiving property for EPTL.
- [ ] **Required attachments:**
- [ ] Certificate of incorporation (or trust instrument, or constitution)
- [ ] Bylaws (or equivalent governing document)
- [ ] IRS Form 1023 application and IRS determination letter
- [ ] List of officers and directors
- [ ] Schedule of professional fundraisers (if any)
- [ ] **Pay registration fee:** $25 baseline, plus $10 if registering for EPTL purposes.
- [ ] **Record assigned NY registration number** (often labeled "Reg. No.") for use on all subsequent filings and solicitation disclosures.

## Step 3: Calendar the CHAR500 Annual Filing

- [ ] **CHAR500 due date:** 4 months and 15 days after fiscal year end. Calendar-year filers: May 15.
- [ ] **Filing method:** Online through the Charities Bureau e-filing portal at charitiesnys.com.
- [ ] **Filing fee tiers** (Article 7-A fee + EPTL fee):
- Article 7-A: $25 if revenue ‰¤ $250,000; tiered up to $750 for very large organizations
- EPTL: $25 to $1,500 depending on net worth
- [ ] **Required attachments:**
- [ ] IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (full unredacted version, including Schedule B donor list - Schedule B is non-public but required by NY)
- [ ] CPA review or audit if revenue triggers apply (see Step 4)
- [ ] Schedule 4a (Government Grants), Schedule 4b (Government Contracts) if applicable
- [ ] **Extension:** File CHAR500 marked "Extension Request" before the due date for a 180-day extension.

## Step 4: Financial Reporting Tier - Review or Audit

The CPA review/audit threshold under Article 7-A:

- [ ] **Gross revenue under $250,000:** No CPA review or audit required.
- [ ] **Gross revenue $250,001 to $1,000,000:** CPA-reviewed financial statements required.
- [ ] **Gross revenue over $1,000,000:** CPA-audited financial statements required.

Note: Thresholds reference "gross revenue" as defined in Executive Law Article 7-A - generally aligning with IRS Form 990 line 12 total revenue. Organizations near a threshold should reconcile gross revenue under both definitions.

- [ ] **Engage CPA firm at least 90 days before due date** for review or audit work.
- [ ] **Confirm CPA independence** - the firm cannot have prepared internal financial records or otherwise compromised independence.
- [ ] **Submit reviewed/audited financials** with CHAR500.

## Step 5: Nonprofit Revitalization Act Governance Requirements

Organizations subject to NY oversight must comply with NPRA governance standards beyond financial filings:

- [ ] **Written conflict-of-interest policy** adopted by the board, with annual disclosure statements signed by directors, officers, and key employees.
- [ ] **Independent board chair** - the board chair generally cannot also serve as an employee of the organization (subject to limited exceptions requiring board approval).
- [ ] **Audit oversight committee** for organizations subject to the audit requirement. The committee must consist of independent directors and oversee the auditor selection and audit findings.
- [ ] **Whistleblower policy** required for organizations with 20 or more employees AND annual revenue over $1,000,000.
- [ ] **Related-party transaction review and approval** - transactions with directors, officers, key employees, or their family members must be approved by the board with full disclosure of the conflict.

## Step 6: Solicitation Disclosures

Every solicitation in New York must include:

- [ ] **The exact name of the charity** as registered with the Charities Bureau.
- [ ] **A statement that financial information is available** from the Charities Bureau or by writing to the charity directly.
- [ ] **For commercial co-venturers and professional fund raisers:** additional registration and contract filings under Article 7-A. Verify any third-party fundraiser is registered with the NY Charities Bureau before signing.

## Step 7: Public Registry and Status Maintenance

- [ ] **Search your registration** at charitiesnys.com/RegistrySearch.
- [ ] **Confirm status is "Current"** - "Past Due" or "Delinquent" status disqualifies grant applications and triggers public-facing flags.
- [ ] **Update changes promptly:** address, fiscal year, officers, dissolution.
- [ ] **Foundations frequently check NY status** before issuing grants to NY-based or NY-soliciting charities.

## Step 8: Closeout

When a New York nonprofit dissolves or stops soliciting in NY:

- [ ] **Article 7-A organizations:** File a final CHAR500 marked "Final."
- [ ] **EPTL organizations:** Petition the Attorney General for approval of the dissolution plan and asset distribution. Many dissolutions also require court approval.
- [ ] **Distribute remaining charitable assets** only after Charities Bureau (and where required, Supreme Court) approval.
- [ ] **File Certificate of Dissolution** with the NY Department of State.
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least seven years after dissolution.

## Common Failure Points

- **Filing federal Form 990 only.** New York requires both the IRS form AND the CHAR500. Missing the state filing is the most common mistake.
- **Submitting redacted Schedule B.** New York requires the full Schedule B with donor names; Schedule B is non-public but must be on file with the Charities Bureau.
- **Underestimating the audit threshold trigger.** $1,000,000 in NY-defined gross revenue triggers a full CPA audit. Plan budgets for $15,000-$40,000 in audit fees.
- **Allowing status to lapse to "Past Due."** Foundations check the public registry. A past-due status will disqualify a grant application.
- **Missing NPRA governance requirements.** The conflict-of-interest, audit committee, and whistleblower policies are not optional once the thresholds are met.

The Charities Bureau enforces actively. Investigations are public, settlements are published, and the Attorney General has broad cy pr¨s authority over misused charitable assets. The cost of NY compliance - typically $5,000-$50,000 per year depending on size - is far less than the cost of an enforcement action or a grant disqualification.
