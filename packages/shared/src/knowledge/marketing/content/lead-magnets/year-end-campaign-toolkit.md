## Why Year-End Deserves a Real Plan

For most US nonprofits, the calendar year-end is the single largest fundraising window. Network for Good and similar industry data consistently show that the majority of online giving concentrates in December, with December 30 and 31 alone often generating 10 percent or more of annual online revenue. Donors are responding to tax deadlines, end-of-year reflection, and the cultural rhythm of giving in December.

Most year-end campaigns underperform their potential because planning starts too late. By December 1, the ability to segment, build differentiated creative, and coordinate across channels is constrained. By December 15, you are reacting rather than executing. The organizations that consistently grow year-end revenue have a written campaign plan by mid-October.

This toolkit covers the full campaign cycle: goal-setting, segmentation, content, channel coordination, send cadence, and post-campaign analysis. For deeper context on annual giving programs, see the [annual fund guide](/resources/guides/annual-fund-what-it-is-how-to-build-one).

## Phase 1: October - Strategy and Goal-Setting

### Build the goal from segments, not from a round number

Pull last year's year-end revenue (October 15 through December 31) by segment:

- New donors acquired during the campaign
- Returning donors (gave in prior year-end)
- Lapsed donors (gave in any prior year, not last year)
- Monthly givers (added or upgraded during the campaign)
- Major donors ($1,000+ gifts)

For each segment, calculate gift count, average gift, and total. Then apply assumptions for the upcoming campaign:

- New donors: How will you acquire them? Realistic acquisition rate based on this year's list growth and ad budget.
- Returning donors: Apply your retention rate. The Fundraising Effectiveness Project pegs sector-wide donor retention around 42 to 45 percent, but year-end retention is typically higher (60 to 70 percent) because year-end donors are more committed.
- Lapsed donors: Reactivation rates are typically 5 to 10 percent. Set a target list size and apply that rate.
- Monthly givers: A target number of new monthly gifts and average monthly value.
- Major donors: Pipeline list with named prospects, expected ask, and probability.

Sum the segments. That is your goal. If it is lower than last year, decide whether to compensate with new acquisition spend or accept it. If it is higher, document where the growth comes from.

### Identify the campaign frame

Every year-end campaign needs a single organizing message. Common frames:

- **Match challenge.** A board or major donor commits to match all gifts up to a cap. Match challenges typically lift response rate 20 to 30 percent.
- **Specific program need.** "Help us serve 200 more families this winter." Concrete, measurable, time-bound.
- **Urgency around a deadline.** Year-end tax deduction is the implicit deadline; explicit campaign deadlines (December 31 at midnight) reinforce it.
- **Gratitude and impact.** A reflection on what donors made possible this year.

Pick one frame and use it consistently across all channels.

## Phase 2: November - Segmentation and Asset Development

### Segment the file

At minimum, segment into:

1. Active donors (gave in the last 12 months)
2. Lapsed donors (gave in the last 13-36 months)
3. Long-lapsed and prospects (older than 36 months or never gave)
4. Monthly givers (active recurring)
5. Major donors ($1,000+ lifetime or recent)
6. Board members and key volunteers

Each segment receives different creative, different ask amounts, and different cadence. Sending the same email to a $25 first-year donor and a $5,000 board member is the most common year-end mistake.

### Develop the asset library

Plan to need:

- 6 to 10 emails per segment
- 2 to 3 direct mail pieces (large gift, mid-level, broad appeal)
- Social posts for organic and paid
- Landing page with compelling copy and a single CTA
- Donation form optimized for desktop and mobile
- Thank-you email and acknowledgment letter
- Stewardship sequence for January

Build everything by November 15. December is for sending, not creating.

## Phase 3: November 15 to December 31 - Execution

### Recommended send cadence

| Date                        | Audience              | Email type                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Tuesday before Thanksgiving | All active            | Soft opener: gratitude and preview |
| #GivingTuesday              | All active and lapsed | First ask, often with match        |
| December 5                  | All active            | Follow-up to #GivingTuesday        |
| December 10                 | Lapsed                | Reactivation appeal                |
| December 15                 | All segments          | Mid-month push                     |
| December 20                 | All segments          | Story-led appeal                   |
| December 27                 | All active            | Post-Christmas urgency             |
| December 30                 | All segments          | Final push, urgency                |
| December 31 (morning)       | All segments          | Last day                           |
| December 31 (evening)       | All segments          | Last hours, deadline-focused       |

Adjust frequency for major donors (fewer emails, more personal outreach) and monthly givers (focus on upgrade rather than new gift).

### Channel coordination

- **Email:** Primary acquisition and conversion channel.
- **Direct mail:** Mid-level and major donors. Drop pieces by December 5 to land before mid-month.
- **Phone:** Major donors and lapsed major donors. Personal calls in the last week of December.
- **Social:** Organic posts and paid retargeting. Paid retargeting from your donation page is one of the highest-ROI year-end ad spends.
- **SMS:** Optional for donors who have opted in. High open rates but easy to over-use.

### Daily rhythm during the campaign

By December 1, set up a daily 15-minute campaign standup:

- Yesterday's revenue and gift count by segment
- Today's planned sends and asks
- Issues blocking execution

Real-time visibility into segment performance is what enables mid-campaign adjustments.

## Phase 4: January - Stewardship and Analysis

The campaign is not over on December 31. The next 60 days determine retention.

### Stewardship sequence

Within 48 hours of the gift:

- Acknowledgment letter with IRS-required language for gifts over $250
- Personal email or call for gifts over $500
- Handwritten note from the Executive Director for gifts over $1,000

Within 30 days:

- Impact email referencing the campaign frame
- For first-time donors: a welcome sequence onboarding them to the organization

Within 60 days:

- Update on what their gift made possible
- Invitation to monthly giving (for one-time donors who gave $50+)

For deeper guidance, see the [donor stewardship plan 12-month guide](/resources/guides/donor-stewardship-plan-12-month-guide).

### Campaign analysis

Within two weeks of December 31, complete a written post-mortem covering:

- Total revenue versus goal, by segment
- Gift count and average gift, by segment
- New donor acquisition cost (if you ran paid)
- Email performance: open rate, click rate, conversion rate by send
- Channel attribution: revenue by channel
- Three things that worked and should be repeated
- Three things that did not work and should change
- Three open questions for next year

File the post-mortem somewhere accessible. The next year's plan starts there.

## Common Year-End Failure Modes

- **Starting in December.** Strategy work compresses, segmentation gets skipped, creative gets reused.
- **One-size-fits-all email.** A first-year $25 donor and a $5,000 monthly giver receive the same message. Both feel it.
- **No mid-campaign adjustment.** The campaign runs on autopilot regardless of what the data shows.
- **Weak December 30-31 push.** The biggest revenue days get the same treatment as November 28.
- **No stewardship plan.** January arrives, the team is exhausted, and the new donors who just gave receive nothing for 90 days.
- **Goal set top-down.** Leadership picks a number that has no relationship to segment-level capacity.

The pattern across all of these is the same: year-end gets treated as one big push instead of a structured campaign with segmented audiences and deliberate cadence. The toolkit's job is to make the structure visible so you can run it.

## Goal-Setting Worksheet (Use Inline)

For each segment, fill in:

- Segment size (count of records)
- Last year revenue from this segment
- Last year gift count and average gift
- This year retention assumption (%)
- This year revenue target

Sum the segment targets. Compare against last year's total. If the difference is greater than 15 percent in either direction, stress-test the assumptions before locking the goal.

## Channel Mix Worksheet (Use Inline)

For each channel, decide:

- Send count and dates
- Audience for each send
- Estimated revenue contribution
- Owner

Verify that no day has more than two emails to the same person and that the major donor segment has at least one personal touchpoint outside email.
