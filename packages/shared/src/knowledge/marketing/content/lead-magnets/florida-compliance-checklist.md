## Florida's Solicitation of Contributions Act

Chapter 496 of the Florida Statutes - the Solicitation of Contributions Act - governs charitable fundraising in Florida. FDACS administers the law through its Division of Consumer Services. The public-facing tool is Check-A-Charity, where donors can verify any registered organization at csapp.fdacs.gov.

Florida's approach is consumer-protection-driven: registration, financial disclosure, professional solicitor regulation, and aggressive enforcement of disclosure rules. Unlike states that focus on governance (e.g., New York's NPRA), Florida focuses on transparency at the point of solicitation.

This checklist covers initial registration on Form CH-14, the small organization exemption, annual renewal, professional solicitor rules, and the recordkeeping needed to avoid the most common enforcement triggers.

## Step 1: Determine Whether Registration Is Required

- [ ] **Confirm "solicitation" applies.** Florida defines solicitation broadly: any oral or written request for a contribution, including direct mail, online appeals targeting Florida residents, telephone, in-person, and event-based fundraising.
- [ ] **Identify if the small charitable organization exemption applies:**
- Gross contributions $25,000 or less for the fiscal year, AND
- No compensation paid to any officer, employee, fundraiser, or solicitor (including independent contractors).
- Both conditions must be met.
- [ ] **Identify other statutory exemptions** in Section 496.403, F.S., including: religious organizations, certain educational institutions, organizations that solicit only from members, and federally chartered organizations such as the Red Cross.
- [ ] **No exemption is automatic.** Even exempt organizations should file the annual exemption statement to maintain a clean record.

## Step 2: Initial Registration on Form CH-14

- [ ] **Download Form CH-14** from fdacs.gov.
- [ ] **Complete required sections:**
- Organization information (legal name, DBA, FEIN, address)
- Contact persons and registered agent
- Officers and directors
- Purpose and programs description
- Methods of solicitation
- Professional solicitor / fundraising counsel relationships (if any)
- Financial information (most recent fiscal year)
- [ ] **Required attachments:**
- [ ] IRS determination letter (or evidence that 1023 has been filed)
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (or pro forma if newly formed)
- [ ] Articles of incorporation
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] **Calculate registration fee** based on prior year contributions:
- Less than $5,000 - $10
- $5,000 to $100,000 - $75
- $100,001 to $200,000 - $125
- $200,001 to $500,000 - $200
- $500,001 to $1,000,000 - $300
- $1,000,001 to $10,000,000 - $350
- Over $10,000,000 - $400
- [ ] **Submit application** by mail or online via the FDACS portal.
- [ ] **Receive SOLE-FL registration number** - record this on all subsequent filings and where required in solicitations.

## Step 3: Annual Renewal

- [ ] **Calendar renewal date** - registration expires on the anniversary of initial registration. Renewal must be filed before expiration.
- [ ] **File renewal Form CH-14** with updated financial information.
- [ ] **Pay renewal fee** based on the current fiscal year's contribution tier.
- [ ] **Update officer / director list** if there have been changes.
- [ ] **Update professional solicitor information** if any commercial relationships have changed.
- [ ] **Track expiration carefully.** Expired registration means unlawful solicitation until renewed, even if the lapse is administrative.

## Step 4: Professional Solicitors and Fundraising Consultants

If you contract with a paid third party to solicit on your behalf, additional rules apply:

- [ ] **Verify the solicitor or consultant is registered** with FDACS before signing a contract. Use the Check-A-Charity portal.
- [ ] **Contracts with professional solicitors must be filed** with FDACS at least 15 days before solicitation begins.
- [ ] **Maintain records** of all funds received through the solicitor, copies of solicitation scripts, and any consumer complaints.
- [ ] **Disclosure requirement:** professional solicitors must disclose to potential donors, before requesting a contribution, the percentage of the contribution that will go to the charitable purpose and that the solicitor is a paid fundraiser.

## Step 5: Solicitation Disclosures

Section 496.411, F.S. requires specific disclosures in every solicitation in Florida.

- [ ] **Registration number disclosure:** "A copy of the official registration and financial information may be obtained from the Division of Consumer Services by calling toll-free 1-800-HELP-FLA (435-7352) within the state. Registration does not imply endorsement, approval, or recommendation by the state."
- [ ] **Include the disclosure in:** print solicitations, websites, donation forms, telephone scripts (read verbatim), and confirmation receipts.
- [ ] **Out-of-state organizations soliciting Florida residents** must include this disclosure even if their main solicitation is national.

## Step 6: Records to Maintain

- [ ] **Books of account** for all contributions received and expenses paid, retained for at least three years.
- [ ] **Contracts** with professional solicitors and fundraising consultants for at least three years after contract end.
- [ ] **Solicitation scripts and templates** used in any campaign.
- [ ] **Donor records** sufficient to respond to refund requests or complaints.
- [ ] **IRS Form 990 and audited financials** retained for at least seven years.

## Step 7: Federal and Other-State Obligations

Florida registration does not satisfy other obligations.

- [ ] **IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N** - annually, due 4 months and 15 days after fiscal year end.
- [ ] **Florida Department of Revenue** - apply for sales and use tax exemption on Form DR-5 if you expect to make tax-exempt purchases.
- [ ] **Multi-state registration:** if you solicit residents in other states, separate registrations apply. Florida does not currently accept the Unified Registration Statement (URS) - Form CH-14 is required.

## Step 8: Closeout

If the organization dissolves or stops soliciting in Florida:

- [ ] **Notify FDACS** in writing of intent to terminate Florida registration.
- [ ] **File final Form CH-14** marked "Final" with year-of-cessation financials.
- [ ] **Retain Florida-specific records** for at least three years after termination.
- [ ] **For dissolution:** distribute remaining charitable assets per Florida Not For Profit Corporation Act (Chapter 617) and the dissolution clause in the articles of incorporation.

## Common Failure Points

- **Operating without registering.** FDACS issues cease-and-desist orders weekly. The Check-A-Charity public portal makes it trivially easy for donors to verify status before giving.
- **Missing the disclosure language.** The 1-800-HELP-FLA disclosure is required verbatim. Web pages, donation buttons, and event materials all need it.
- **Using an unregistered professional solicitor.** Both the charity and the solicitor face penalties.
- **Missing the renewal date.** Registration is not auto-renewed. Build a 60-day reminder before expiration.
- **Misusing the small organization exemption.** The exemption requires zero compensated workers - including a single contractor paid to design fundraising materials may disqualify it.

Florida's enforcement is consumer-protection-oriented and visible. FDACS publishes annual enforcement reports, settlements are searchable, and the Check-A-Charity portal flags problems. The administrative burden of compliance is moderate; the cost of being caught noncompliant is high.
