## How to Use This Checklist

This checklist is designed for one grant at a time. Run it quarterly as a completeness check, and again at closeout to confirm the file is complete before archiving.

A well-organized grant file serves two purposes: it makes day-to-day management faster (you know where everything is), and it makes audit response straightforward (you can produce any document without a multi-day search).

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Award and Award Setup

Documents that belong in the file before the first expenditure is made.

**Application and Award Documents**

- [ ] Submitted grant application or proposal (final version as submitted)
      _Include the narrative, budget, and all attachments. If the award terms differ from what was proposed, the application is the baseline for understanding what was approved._

- [ ] Notice of award or grant agreement
      _The official document from the funder establishing the award. For federal grants, this is typically a Notice of Award from the agency's grants management system._

- [ ] All amendments, modifications, and no-cost extensions
      _Every change to the original award - period of performance extensions, budget modifications, scope changes - must be in the file with the date of approval._

- [ ] Funder's prior approval letters for any approved modifications
      _If a budget reallocation, scope change, or period extension required funder approval, the approval letter belongs in the file adjacent to the modification._

**Budget and Financial Setup**

- [ ] Approved budget as awarded (line-item detail)
      _The budget as approved in the notice of award, not the budget as proposed if the funder modified it._

- [ ] All subsequently approved budget modifications
      _Every approved rebudgeting. If a category was increased and another reduced with funder approval, the approval documentation belongs here._

- [ ] Indirect cost rate documentation (NICRA or de minimis election)
      _The rate agreement or the documented election of the 15% de minimis rate. Without this, indirect cost charges are unverifiable._

- [ ] Organizational chart or role assignments for grant-funded positions
      _Documents who is responsible for program delivery, financial management, and compliance for this award._

---

## Phase 2: Active Grant Period - Financial Documentation

Documents that accumulate throughout the grant period as expenditures are made.

**Expenditure Documentation**

- [ ] All invoices and receipts for direct cost purchases, organized by budget category
      _Every non-personnel direct expenditure needs a source document. Invoices should be filed in the grant file, not just in accounts payable._

- [ ] Vendor contracts for professional services, consulting, and subcontracts
      _Signed agreements for any services engaged under the grant. For federal grants, competitive bidding documentation should accompany contracts above threshold._

- [ ] Competitive bidding documentation for procurements above the micro-purchase threshold
      _Solicitation documents, bids or proposals received, evaluation records, and award rationale. For sole-source procurements, the written justification._

- [ ] SAM.gov debarment checks for all contractors and subcontractors
      _Date-stamped verification that vendors were not debarred at the time of contract award. Required for federal procurement._

- [ ] Travel documentation: pre-approvals, receipts, trip reports, mileage logs
      _All travel charged to the grant needs documentation of the business purpose, connection to the funded program, and actual expense receipts._

**Personnel Cost Documentation**

- [ ] Payroll registers or salary reports showing charges to this grant by pay period
      _The connection from the grant's personnel budget to actual payroll. These should be filed by pay period for the full grant term._

- [ ] Time-and-effort records for all grant-funded employees
      _For single-program employees: semi-annual certifications. For split-funded employees: monthly personnel activity reports accounting for 100% of compensated time._

- [ ] Fringe benefit rate documentation and calculations
      _The methodology for how fringe benefits were allocated to the grant. If a composite rate was used, the rate calculation documentation._

---

## Phase 3: Active Grant Period - Program and Compliance Documentation

Non-financial documentation that belongs in the grant file.

**Program Records**

- [ ] All progress reports and narrative reports submitted to the funder
      _Copies of all submissions, with dates. These document what was accomplished with the funds._

- [ ] Correspondence with the program officer
      _Significant correspondence about program performance, scope questions, or compliance issues. Routine check-ins do not need to be filed, but substantive communications do._

- [ ] Documentation of eligibility determinations for program participants (if applicable)
      _For programs with eligibility requirements, the records showing that participants met the criteria._

**Subrecipient Files (if applicable)**

- [ ] Subrecipient vs. contractor determination memo
      _A documented conclusion applying the 2 CFR 200.331 five-factor test to each downstream relationship._

- [ ] Pre-award risk assessment for each subrecipient
      _Required before the sub-award is made under 2 CFR 200.332(b). Must document risk factors considered and overall risk determination._

- [ ] Executed sub-award agreement with all required provisions
      _The signed sub-award document including the federal award information, CFDA number, period of performance, and compliance requirements required under 2 CFR 200.332(a)._

- [ ] Subrecipient financial reports received (all periods)
      _Every financial report submitted by the subrecipient. Include the review date - receiving reports without documented review is inadequate monitoring._

- [ ] Monitoring visit or desk review documentation
      _Evidence of monitoring activities conducted, findings noted, and any technical assistance provided._

- [ ] Single audit verification for subrecipients above the $1,000,000 threshold
      _FAC.gov search documentation confirming the subrecipient has a current single audit on file, and a note of any findings relevant to your program._

---

## Phase 4: Financial Reporting

Reports submitted to the funder during the grant period.

**SF-425 and Financial Reports**

- [ ] Copies of all interim SF-425 reports submitted, with submission dates
      _For federal grants, every quarterly or semi-annual SF-425 filed during the grant period. Include the system confirmation of submission._

- [ ] General ledger reconciliation supporting each SF-425 submission
      _The pre-submission reconciliation showing that SF-425 figures matched the grant-specific expenditure report in the accounting system._

- [ ] Budget-to-actual report for each SF-425 period
      _A snapshot of spending against the approved budget at each reporting point. Documents the monitoring that occurred throughout the grant._

---

## Phase 5: Closeout Documentation

Documents that must be in the file before the grant is archived.

**Financial Closeout**

- [ ] Final SF-425 (federal financial report), with submission date and confirmation
      _Must be submitted within 120 days of the period-of-performance end date for federal awards. The confirmation receipt belongs in the file._

- [ ] Final general ledger reconciliation: total expenditures reconciled to the final SF-425
      _The reconciliation confirming that the final SF-425 figures match the accounting records exactly._

- [ ] Documentation of return of unspent funds (if applicable)
      _If any federal funds were unspent and returned to the awarding agency, the return documentation and the agency's acknowledgment._

- [ ] Equipment inventory and disposition records for federally funded equipment above the applicable capitalization threshold, including current fair market value review when disposition is needed
      _Disposition determination for each qualifying item, and any agency correspondence about equipment disposition._

- [ ] Subrecipient closeout confirmations
      _For each subrecipient: confirmation that final reports were received, unspent funds returned, and the subrecipient's file is closed._

**Program Closeout**

- [ ] Final programmatic report, with submission date
      _The final narrative report submitted to the funder documenting outcomes, activities, and participants served._

- [ ] Final funder correspondence and closeout letter
      _When the funder formally accepts the final reports and closes the grant, the closeout letter belongs in the file._

---

## Phase 6: Record Retention

The retention record for the closed grant.

- [ ] Retention schedule entry: grant name, funder, award period, final expenditure report submission date, retention expiration date, archive location
      _The three-year retention clock under 2 CFR 200.334 starts from the final expenditure report submission date - not the grant end date._

- [ ] Note of any pending audit, litigation, or claim that extends the retention period beyond three years
      _If a matter is pending at the three-year mark, the retention period extends until the matter is fully resolved._

- [ ] Program-specific retention confirmation (USDA, HUD, and some DOE programs require seven years)
      _Check the grant agreement and the applicable OMB Compliance Supplement for program-specific retention requirements longer than the three-year baseline._

## Quick-Reference: What Auditors Ask For Most Often

Based on common single audit testing patterns, auditors most frequently request:

1. T&E records for a sample of payroll periods for grant-funded employees
2. Source documents (invoices, receipts) for a sample of direct cost expenditures
3. Competitive bidding or sole-source justification documentation for above-threshold procurement
4. Subrecipient risk assessments and sub-award agreements
5. The general ledger reconciliation underlying the final SF-425

If you have these five categories organized and retrievable, you are in strong shape for the most common audit requests. The rest of this checklist ensures completeness for the full scope of what a single audit may examine.
