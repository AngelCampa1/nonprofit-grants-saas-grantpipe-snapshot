## Why Negotiating Indirect Cost Rates Is Worth the Effort

Most mid-sized nonprofits with federal funding either accept the 15 percent de minimis rate under 2 CFR 200.414(f) or do not charge indirect costs at all. Both choices leave money on the table. Organizations with significant federal funding and substantial shared costs often have actual indirect rates of 18 to 35 percent. The difference between 10 percent and 25 percent on $2 million in federal direct costs is $300,000 of unrecovered shared cost annually.

The negotiation process is bureaucratic but tractable. The output is a Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement (NICRA) with your federal cognizant agency. The NICRA establishes the rate other federal agencies must accept. Once in place, NICRAs are renewed annually with relatively modest effort.

This worksheet walks through the negotiation: identifying your cognizant agency, building cost pools, selecting bases, calculating rates, and assembling the submission package. For overview context, see the [cognizant agency guide](/resources/guides/cognizant-agency-for-indirect-costs).

## Stage 1: Decide Whether to Negotiate

Before investing months of work, decide whether negotiation is worth the effort.

### Calculate your actual indirect rate

Pull your most recent audited financial statements. Identify:

- Total direct program costs (your direct cost base)
- Total general and administrative (G&A) costs that would be in your indirect pool
- Total fundraising costs (excluded from federal indirect rate)

Calculate: indirect rate = G&A costs / direct cost base.

If the result is materially above 10 percent, negotiation likely produces benefit. Below 12 percent, the administrative effort may exceed the marginal recovery.

### Federal funding scale check

The benefit of a negotiated rate scales with federal direct cost volume:

- Below $500K federal direct costs annually: de minimis is usually sufficient.
- $500K-$2M annually: negotiation often pays back, but evaluate carefully.
- Above $2M annually: negotiation almost always pays back.

### Capacity check

The negotiation process requires:

- Strong cost accounting (chart of accounts mapped to direct/indirect)
- Clean audited financials
- Staff time (60-100 hours typically)
- Patience (6-12 months for the negotiation)

If any of these is missing, fix the prerequisites first.

## Stage 2: Identify Your Cognizant Agency

Per 2 CFR 200.19 and Appendix IV, your cognizant agency is generally the federal agency that provides the most direct federal funding to your organization. Common cognizant agencies:

- HHS (PSC) - for nonprofits with HRSA, SAMHSA, NIH, ACF, CDC funding
- Department of Education
- USDA
- HUD
- Department of Justice
- USAID - for international NGOs

If your largest funder agency is unclear, sum federal direct costs by agency for the most recent fiscal year. The largest is your cognizant agency.

For HHS-cognizant organizations, negotiations go through PSC's Cost Allocation Services. PSC maintains a portal at rates.psc.gov.

## Stage 3: Choose the Rate Structure

Decide which rate(s) to negotiate.

### Single rate vs multiple rates

- **Single rate.** Most nonprofits negotiate a single indirect cost rate applied to all federal awards. Simplest to administer.
- **Multiple rates.** Larger organizations may negotiate separate rates for on-site and off-site activities, or by program type. More complex, more accurate.

Most mid-sized nonprofits use a single rate.

### Provisional and final rates

Most NICRAs include:

- **Provisional rate** for current and one or two future fiscal years. Used during the year on awards.
- **Final rate** applied retroactively after fiscal year audit, with adjustment to actual.

Or organizations may elect:

- **Fixed rate** with carry-forward of variance. The fixed rate is held; variance from actual carries forward to a future period.
- **Predetermined rate** for a defined future period without adjustment.

The choice depends on cost stability and administrative preference. Discuss with your cognizant agency negotiator.

## Stage 4: Build Cost Pools

Two pools are required for a standard nonprofit indirect cost rate proposal.

### Direct cost base

The base is the denominator. Most common: modified total direct costs (MTDC).

MTDC = Total direct costs - equipment - capital expenditures - rental costs - tuition remission - scholarships - subaward amounts above $50,000 per subaward.

Pull the most recent audited fiscal year. Identify direct costs and exclude the items above to compute MTDC.

### Indirect cost pool

The pool is the numerator. Includes general and administrative costs that benefit multiple cost objectives:

- Executive Director and senior leadership salaries (allocated portion)
- Finance and accounting staff
- HR
- IT
- General office expense
- Facilities (allocated portion)
- General insurance
- Audit fees
- Board governance costs

Exclude:

- Direct program costs
- Fundraising costs (separately tracked)
- Unallowable costs (lobbying, alcohol, bad debt, etc.)

For each cost type in the indirect pool, document the methodology for inclusion.

### Calculation

Indirect cost rate = Indirect cost pool / Direct cost base.

Example:
- Direct cost base (MTDC): $4,000,000
- Indirect cost pool: $880,000
- Calculated rate: 22.0%

## Stage 5: Document the Methodology

The submission package must document how the calculation was built.

### Required components

- Most recent audited financial statements
- Reconciliation of audited financials to the rate calculation
- Statement of total costs by direct, indirect, fundraising, unallowable
- Personnel listing with salaries and indirect/direct allocation
- Description of cost allocation methodology
- Description of accounting system and chart of accounts
- IRS Form 990 most recent
- Schedule of federal awards
- Statement of treatment of fringe benefits
- Lobbying certification

### Cost allocation methodology

Document how costs were classified and how shared costs were allocated. The methodology must be:

- Reasonable on its face
- Consistent with audited financials
- Applied consistently across cost objectives
- Causally related to costs being allocated

For deeper allocation methodology guidance, see related cost allocation worksheets and the [Uniform Guidance practical guide](/resources/guides/uniform-guidance-2-cfr-200-practical-guide).

## Stage 6: Submit the Package

Submission process varies by cognizant agency.

### HHS PSC submission

Submit through rates.psc.gov:

- Create organization account if first-time submitter
- Upload the rate proposal package
- Respond to negotiator questions during review
- Receive negotiated NICRA upon agreement

### Other agency submissions

Each agency has its own process. Request submission instructions from the agency's indirect cost rate office.

## Stage 7: Negotiation

Once submitted, expect 4 to 8 months for review and questions. Common negotiator questions:

- Why are specific costs in the indirect pool rather than direct?
- How are personnel allocated between direct and indirect?
- Are unallowable costs properly excluded?
- Does the rate reconcile to audited financials?
- Are fringe benefits handled correctly?
- Are subaward exclusions calculated correctly?

Respond promptly. Lengthy delays in response extend the negotiation timeline.

## Stage 8: Apply the Rate

Once executed, the NICRA defines:

- Approved rate(s)
- Base (typically MTDC) with definition
- Period of agreement
- Special remarks (treatment of specific costs)

Apply the rate on federal grant proposals and post-award invoicing:

- On proposals: apply the approved rate to the proposed direct cost base.
- On post-award draws: apply the rate to the cumulative direct cost base for the period.

If using provisional/final structure, expect to true up after fiscal year-end audit.

## Stage 9: Annual Renewal

Most NICRAs require annual rate renewal:

- Submit updated audited financials within the timeframe specified in the NICRA
- Submit updated rate proposal showing actual costs
- Reach final rate determination for completed fiscal year
- Establish provisional rate for upcoming fiscal year

Annual renewal effort is typically 30-60 percent of initial negotiation effort. Maintain a clean fiscal year-by-year record so the renewal package is straightforward.

## Common NICRA Negotiation Failures

- **Submission inconsistent with audit.** Numbers in the rate proposal do not reconcile to audited financials. Negotiator rejects.
- **Direct/indirect classification inconsistent.** Same cost type classified differently in different programs without explanation.
- **Unallowable costs included in pool.** Lobbying or fundraising in the indirect pool. Negotiator removes; rate drops.
- **Subaward exclusions incorrect.** Failing to exclude subaward amounts above $50,000 inflates the base, lowering the rate.
- **Personnel allocation undocumented.** Time and effort records do not support the allocation in the proposal.
- **Slow response to negotiator questions.** Delays compound.

## When to Use the De Minimis Instead

The 15 percent de minimis rate under 2 CFR 200.414(f) is appropriate when:

- Total federal direct costs are below $500K annually
- Internal cost accounting cannot reliably support the negotiation
- Indirect cost pool is small relative to direct costs
- Negotiation effort exceeds expected benefit
- Organization is new to federal funding

The de minimis rate is permanent for organizations that elect it. It can be used indefinitely. Switching from de minimis to a negotiated rate is allowed but requires a formal proposal.

## Cost-Benefit Snapshot

For a $2.5M federal direct cost organization:
- De minimis (10%): $250,000 indirect recovery
- Negotiated (22%): $550,000 indirect recovery
- Annual benefit: $300,000
- Negotiation effort: 60-100 staff hours initially, 30-60 hours annually for renewal

The math typically favors negotiation for organizations above $1M in federal direct costs and with material shared cost structures. The worksheet's job is to make the path concrete so the decision is made on numbers, not on the perceived complexity of the process.
