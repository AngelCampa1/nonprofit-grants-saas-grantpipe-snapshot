## What This Tracker Does

The grant spend-down tracker monitors budget burn for each active grant. It answers one question: given what we have spent and when the award ends, are we on track?

That question is deceptively simple. The answer has compliance implications. A grant that projects $40,000 unspent with two months remaining is not a financial planning problem - it is a compliance problem that requires action now.

---

## The Tracker Structure

The tracker covers one grant per worksheet tab. For each grant:

**Grant Summary Block**

- Grant name and funder
- Grant agreement number
- Award amount
- Award start date and end date
- Total months in award period
- Current month (for calculating months elapsed and months remaining)
- Indirect cost rate (if applicable)

**Budget vs. Actual Table**

One row per budget line as approved in the grant agreement. Columns:

| Budget Line | Approved Budget | Month 1 | Month 2 | … | Cumulative Actuals | Remaining Budget | % Spent | Variance Flag |

The budget lines should match the grant agreement exactly - not your internal budget structure, but the categories the funder approved. Common federal grant budget categories: Personnel, Fringe Benefits, Travel, Equipment, Supplies, Contractual, Other Direct Costs, Indirect Costs.

Enter monthly actuals from the accounting system, by grant fund code, after each month-end close. Do not estimate. Pull the exact figure from the GL.

**Burn Rate and Projection Block**

- Months elapsed since award start
- Total cumulative actuals
- Average monthly actuals (cumulative actuals ÷ months elapsed)
- Required monthly actuals to reach $0 by award end (remaining budget ÷ months remaining)
- Projected end-of-award balance (remaining budget − (average monthly actuals × months remaining))
- Alert: if projected end-of-award balance exceeds 10% of total award, flag red

**Variance Flag Logic**

For each budget line, calculate expected-to-date spending: (approved budget ÷ total award months) × months elapsed. Compare to actual cumulative spending.

Flag lines where actual spending is more than 20% below expected-to-date. These are the lines that will drive underspend if the pattern continues.

Flag lines where actual spending exceeds the approved budget amount. This is a compliance issue - over-expenditure on a budget line requires a modification request or reclassification.

## Monthly Use Instructions

**Step 1: Update the current month**

Change the "Current Month" cell to the month just closed. All date-based calculations update automatically.

**Step 2: Enter actuals**

For each budget line, enter the month's actuals from the GL. Do not enter estimates. If payroll for the period has not yet been posted, wait until it has before updating the tracker.

**Step 3: Review the burn rate projection**

Check the projected end-of-award balance. If it is positive and above 10% of total award, the grant is flagged. Investigate the cause:

- Is a position vacant that was budgeted in Personnel?
- Was a program activity rescheduled, delaying Contractual or Supplies spending?
- Has the indirect cost calculation been applied correctly?

**Step 4: Review line-level variance flags**

Check each flagged budget line. Determine whether the underspend reflects a timing issue (expenses will catch up next month) or a structural problem (the planned activity is not happening and the budget is not being used).

**Step 5: Take action on structural problems**

If a line has been flagged for two or more consecutive months with no reasonable timing explanation, escalate to the program director responsible for the grant activities. The tracker surfaces the number; the program director explains what is happening.

**Step 6: Document the review**

Note the date, reviewer, flags identified, and any actions taken. This becomes part of the grant file.

## When Burn Rate Is Too Low: Decision Framework

Low burn rate requires a decision, not just observation. The options:

**Option 1: Re-programming**

If the organization can spend more on other approved budget lines - adding approved program activities, accelerating existing work, or shifting budget to lines where legitimate spending can increase - request a budget modification from the funder. Re-programming requires prior written approval for most modifications above the threshold specified in the grant agreement. Check your specific award terms, as thresholds vary by funder and award type.

Re-programming is the preferred option when legitimate program activity can be expanded within the grant's approved scope.

**Option 2: No-Cost Extension**

If the program activities are incomplete and additional time would allow them to be finished, request a no-cost extension (NCE). Request as early as possible - at least 60 days before award end for federal grants, and well in advance for private funders. Include a specific activity completion plan, not just a general explanation of delays.

An NCE is appropriate when the work is real and incomplete. It is not appropriate when the work has effectively ended and the organization is looking for time to spend remaining funds without corresponding activity.

**Option 3: Return Funds**

If neither re-programming nor an NCE is viable, plan the final report to show accurate underspending and return the unspent balance at closeout. This is better than a spending rush in the final weeks that produces inadequate documentation or potentially unallowable charges.

Funders understand underspending when it is communicated honestly and early. What they do not forgive is discovering it without warning at closeout.

## Alert Thresholds

The tracker uses three alert thresholds. Adjust these to match your organization's funder requirements and risk tolerance.

**Yellow alert: Projected end-of-award balance is 10-20% of total award.** Review is needed. Investigate whether timing alone explains the underspend or whether structural action is required.

**Red alert: Projected end-of-award balance exceeds 20% of total award.** Action required. Escalate to program director and finance director. Initiate re-programming or NCE conversation with the funder now - not at month-end.

**Line-level flag: Any budget line where actuals exceed approved budget.** Immediate review required. Stop additional charges to that line until the situation is resolved (modification, reclassification, or correction of a coding error).

## Using the Tracker Across Multiple Grants

Create one tab per active grant. Use a summary tab that pulls key metrics from each grant tab:

- Grant name
- Award end date
- Remaining balance
- Projected end-of-award balance
- Red/yellow/green status

Sort the summary tab by award end date (soonest first). This gives the finance director and Executive Director a portfolio-level view in one glance. The grants that need attention are at the top.

Review the summary tab weekly. The grant-level detail tabs can be reviewed monthly as part of the close process.
