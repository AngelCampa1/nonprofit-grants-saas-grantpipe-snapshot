## Who Needs This Timeline

Organizations that expend $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in a fiscal year are required to have a single audit under 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart F. The threshold was updated - organizations should verify which threshold applies based on their fiscal year end date (new threshold effective for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later).

This timeline is designed for the finance director, grants manager, or operations lead who coordinates audit preparation for an organization without a dedicated audit readiness staff. The tasks are specific, not aspirational.

---

## Day 90 Before Fiscal Year End: SEFA Review and Reconciliation

**Goal:** Produce a draft SEFA that is reconciled and accurate before the fiscal year closes.

**Tasks:**

**Update the SEFA tracking record with current cumulative expenditures for every active award**
Pull grant-specific expenditure reports from the accounting system for each federal award. Update the SEFA tracking record with year-to-date totals. Note any awards where actual expenditures may differ from the award amount in ways that could affect the SEFA total.

**Verify Assistance Listing (CFDA) numbers for every award**
Cross-check each SEFA row against the notice of award and SAM.gov to confirm the Assistance Listing number is correct. Incorrect numbers are an audit adjustment and can affect major program selection.

**Identify all sub-awards received from pass-through entities**
For every award received from a state agency or other pass-through entity, confirm the pass-through entity name and the pass-through's identifying number are recorded in the SEFA. These are required disclosures under 2 CFR 200.510(b).

**Confirm cluster program designations**
Check Part 5 of the current OMB Compliance Supplement to verify that any awards belonging to cluster programs are correctly identified. Cluster misidentification affects major program selection.

**Estimate whether any programs will cross the Type A major program threshold**
The Type A threshold is the larger of $1,000,000 or 3% of total federal expenditures. Programs above this threshold receive a different risk classification in major program selection. If a program is close to crossing the threshold, project the year-end expenditure.

---

## Day 60 Before Fiscal Year End: T&E Completeness Check

**Goal:** Confirm that time-and-effort records are complete and contemporaneous for all grant-funded personnel, and identify any gaps while they can still be addressed.

**Tasks:**

**List every employee whose salary was charged to a federal award during the fiscal year**
Pull payroll records for each federal award. List every employee with payroll charges to the award and the total charged by pay period.

**Verify that T&E documentation exists for each employee and each pay period**
For each employee on the list, confirm that: (a) semi-annual certifications exist if they work solely on one program, or (b) monthly personnel activity reports exist for each month if they are split-funded across multiple activities.

**Check record dates - confirm records are contemporaneous**
Review the dates on T&E records against the payroll periods they cover. Records dated more than 30 days after the period they cover should be flagged. Certifications covering multiple months signed on a single date are retroactive.

**Address gaps while documentation is still possible**
If T&E records are missing for recent periods, work with employees to complete contemporaneous documentation now. Do not wait until after fiscal year end - records for periods in the current year can still be completed. Records for prior fiscal years that are missing cannot be recovered.

**Confirm payroll allocations match T&E records**
For a sample of pay periods, verify that the effort percentages on the T&E records match the payroll allocation to the grant. If the T&E record shows 50% on a grant but payroll charged 60%, the records are inconsistent.

---

## Day 45 Before Fiscal Year End: Subrecipient Monitoring Documentation

**Goal:** Confirm that subrecipient monitoring records are complete and that any subrecipients above the $1,000,000 threshold have been verified in fac.gov.

**Tasks:**

**List every sub-award made during the fiscal year**
Include the subrecipient name, award amount, period of performance, and the monitoring actions documented for each.

**Confirm pre-award risk assessments are on file for each sub-award**
2 CFR 200.332(b) requires a risk assessment before the sub-award is made. Verify that a documented assessment exists for every sub-award issued during the year.

**Confirm sub-award agreements include all required provisions**
Check each executed sub-award agreement against the requirements of 2 CFR 200.332(a): federal award information (FAIN, CFDA number, federal agency), period of performance, approved budget, applicable compliance requirements, and access-to-records provisions.

**Verify single audit status for subrecipients with $1,000,000+ in federal expenditures**
Search fac.gov for each subrecipient that may have expended $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in its most recent fiscal year. Document the search date, the submission found (or not found), and any findings reviewed. This verification must be documented in the monitoring file.

**Review financial reports received from subrecipients for the full grant period**
Confirm that financial reports from each subrecipient cover all required reporting periods. Document your review of each report - what was checked, whether there were concerns, and any follow-up conducted.

---

## Day 30 Before Fiscal Year End: Restricted Fund Reconciliation

**Goal:** Confirm that all active grant expenditures reconcile to the approved budgets and that no category overruns exist that require action before year-end.

**Tasks:**

**Run final budget-to-actual reports for each active federal award**
For each award, produce a report showing cumulative expenditures against the approved budget by category. Review for: overspent categories that may require a budget modification request, underspent categories that may result in unspent funds, and any expenditure codes that appear inconsistent with the budget category.

**Confirm all invoices and expenses incurred during the period have been coded and recorded**
Accrual accounting requires that expenses are recorded when incurred, not when paid. Confirm that all outstanding invoices for goods or services received before fiscal year end are accrued in the accounting system.

**Identify any costs that may be questioned at audit**
Review expenditures for any that lack adequate documentation, were not pre-approved when required, or fall outside approved budget categories. Address these before year-end if possible - an unresolved documentation gap at year-end becomes a questioned cost in the audit.

**Confirm that all SF-425 reports for the fiscal year have been submitted on time**
Review the reporting schedule for each federal award and confirm all required SF-425 submissions during the year were made by the deadline. Late submissions are noted as compliance deficiencies.

---

## Day 14 Before Fiscal Year End: Audit Evidence Binder

**Goal:** Compile the documentation that auditors will request in a format that allows rapid response during fieldwork.

**Tasks:**

**Compile the grant file for each major program expected to be in scope**
Using the Grant File Audit Checklist, verify completeness for each program. Major programs are likely to be tested - having a complete, organized grant file for each dramatically reduces audit fieldwork time.

**Prepare the SEFA draft for auditor review**
The draft SEFA should be reconciled to the general ledger, with a note explaining the accounting basis, indirect cost rate method, and any significant policies. Provide a supporting reconciliation schedule that auditors can trace.

**Prepare the prior audit findings summary**
If the organization had findings in prior single audits, the corrective action plan must be current. Prepare the Summary Schedule of Prior Audit Findings showing each prior finding, its current status, and the evidence of corrective action if it has been implemented.

**Brief key staff on audit areas where questions are expected**
Identify which staff members auditors will want to meet with - typically the CFO or Finance Director, the Grants Manager, and possibly program directors for specific major programs. Brief them on what to expect and how to respond.

---

## Day 0: Fiscal Year End

**Immediate priorities after fiscal year close:**

**Final accruals:** Ensure all expenses incurred before year-end are recorded in the accounting system. Close the books in a reasonable timeframe - most nonprofits target 30 to 45 days after fiscal year end.

**Final payroll reconciliation:** Confirm that all payroll charges for the fiscal year, including any year-end adjustments, are correctly coded to the appropriate grants.

**Equipment inventory update:** Conduct the year-end physical inventory count for equipment purchased with federal funds. Update the inventory records.

---

## After Fiscal Year End: Audit Scheduling and Submission

**Engage the auditor early.** Single audit fieldwork should be scheduled as soon as the books are closed. The nine-month submission deadline works backward from fiscal year end - for June 30 year-ends, the deadline is March 31 of the following year. Fieldwork typically requires six to eight weeks between fieldwork start and audit completion.

**Submission deadline reminder:** The single audit reporting package must be submitted to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse (fac.gov) within the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report is issued or nine months after fiscal year end. Late submission is a compliance finding in the current year's audit.
