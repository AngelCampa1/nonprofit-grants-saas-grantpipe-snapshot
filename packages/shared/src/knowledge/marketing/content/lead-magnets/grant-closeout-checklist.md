## Why Closeout Starts 90 Days Before the End Date

Organizations that treat grant closeout as a single event - something that happens after the period of performance ends - consistently produce late final reports, incomplete documentation, and unresolved compliance issues that extend the administrative life of the grant by months.

Closeout is a process. It starts long before the grant ends and continues well after. The checklist below organizes every step by when it should happen.

## Phase 1: 90 Days Before Period End

### Expenditure and Budget Review

- [ ] Run a full budget-vs-actual report for the grant. Review every line item.
- [ ] Identify any categories that are underspent relative to time elapsed. Determine whether the underspend reflects a genuine program delivery gap or a timing issue.
- [ ] Identify any categories approaching or exceeding the approved amount. Initiate a budget modification request if prior approval is required.
- [ ] Confirm that cumulative budget modifications are within the approved rebudgeting threshold for this award.
- [ ] Review all pending obligations (purchase orders, open invoices, consultant work in progress). Confirm all are within the period of performance.

### Subrecipient Management (if applicable)

- [ ] Notify all subrecipients of the prime recipient's closeout timeline. Remind them of their reporting obligations.
- [ ] Confirm what documentation each subrecipient must submit before your final report can be filed.
- [ ] Review subrecipient monitoring files for completeness - all desk reviews and site visits documented.

### Staffing and Time Records

- [ ] Confirm that time and effort documentation is current for all grant-funded personnel.
- [ ] Identify any gaps in time records and remediate them - do not leave this for the final weeks.
- [ ] If any 100%-funded staff will need final semi-annual certifications, schedule them now.

### No-Cost Extension Decision

- [ ] Assess whether the funded activities will be completed within the current period of performance.
- [ ] If an extension is needed, submit the NCE request now - at 90 days, you have adequate lead time for review and approval.
- [ ] Do not wait until 30 days before end to initiate an NCE request.

---

## Phase 2: 30 Days Before Period End

### Final Expenditure Planning

- [ ] Confirm that all authorized expenditures planned for the grant period have been committed (purchase orders issued, contracts signed, employment commitments made).
- [ ] Verify that no new obligations will be incurred after the period of performance end date.
- [ ] Confirm any pending invoices from vendors or subrecipients will be received and processed before the end date (or within the liquidation period if the award includes one).

### Equipment Review

- [ ] Pull the equipment inventory for all items purchased with grant funds at or above the capitalization threshold set in your award (verify the applicable threshold in 2 CFR 200.313 and your Notice of Award, confirm the current figure with official sources before applying).
- [ ] Determine the current fair market value of each item.
- [ ] If fair market value exceeds the applicable per-unit threshold under 2 CFR 200.313, notify the awarding agency and await disposition instructions.
- [ ] Document the inventory and disposition decision in the grant file.

**Federal vs. private funders:** Federal grants require formal equipment disposition procedures under 2 CFR 200.313. Private foundation grants vary - check the grant agreement. Some foundation agreements include equipment disposition provisions; many do not address equipment explicitly.

### Unspent Funds Calculation

- [ ] Calculate the projected unspent balance as of the period end date.
- [ ] Confirm the grant agreement's provision for unspent funds: return required, carry forward permitted, or other treatment.
- [ ] For federal grants: unspent federal funds must be returned to the awarding agency through the federal payment system (PMS, G5, or agency portal). Do not retain unspent federal funds without explicit authorization.
- [ ] For private foundation grants: the grant agreement specifies the treatment. If it requires return, initiate the return process. If it permits retention for related purposes, document the basis.

### Subrecipient Final Reports

- [ ] Follow up with all subrecipients on the status of their final reports and unspent fund returns.
- [ ] Do not proceed to your own final report submission until all subrecipient obligations are resolved.

---

## Phase 3: Final Week

### Expenditure Lock

- [ ] Confirm with finance that no new expenditures will be coded to the grant after the period end date.
- [ ] Review any last-minute invoices or payroll charges for timing - costs must be incurred within the period of performance to be allowable.

### Final Reconciliation

- [ ] Reconcile all grant expenditures to the accounting system. The total federal expenditures on the final SF-425 must match the accounting records to the dollar.
- [ ] Verify that every expenditure has adequate supporting documentation in the grant file.
- [ ] Confirm that indirect cost calculations are correct per the approved rate.

### Final Report Preparation

- [ ] Begin drafting the final SF-425 (for federal grants) or equivalent financial report.
- [ ] Begin drafting the final programmatic/narrative report.
- [ ] Confirm the submission deadline: for federal grants, the final SF-425 is due within 120 calendar days of the period end date. Some agencies impose shorter deadlines - check the Notice of Award.
- [ ] Confirm the submission portal and that your login credentials are current.

---

## Phase 4: Post-Period Submission (confirm your deadline in the Notice of Award)

### Final SF-425 Submission

- [ ] Complete the SF-425 with cumulative expenditures for the full period of performance.
- [ ] Verify that the cumulative federal expenditures match the accounting system.
- [ ] Verify that the indirect cost figures are calculated at the approved rate applied to the correct base.
- [ ] Submit through the designated portal. Save the submission confirmation.
- [ ] File the submission confirmation in the grant file.

**Federal SF-425 deadlines by agency:**

Most federal awards require the final SF-425 within 120 calendar days after the period end date, but some agencies impose shorter deadlines. Always confirm the exact deadline in your Notice of Award. Do not rely on a general rule.

_Note: Agency-specific deadlines (NIH, NSF, HUD, and others) are subject to change. Verify current requirements directly with the awarding agency or in your grant agreement before submitting._

### Final Programmatic/Narrative Report

- [ ] Complete the final programmatic report covering activities, outputs, and outcomes for the full period of performance.
- [ ] Use data from program records maintained during the grant period - not reconstructed from memory.
- [ ] Submit per the funder's instructions. Save the submission confirmation.
- [ ] File in the grant file.

### Final Drawdown (if applicable)

- [ ] For federal grants with a federal payment system: ensure the final drawdown matches the final SF-425 expenditures.
- [ ] Return any excess drawdown to the awarding agency through the appropriate mechanism.

---

## Phase 5: Post-Closeout

### Closeout Letter

- [ ] When the awarding agency accepts your final reports, request a formal closeout letter if one is not automatically issued.
- [ ] File the closeout letter in the grant file. This is the official confirmation that the grant is closed.

### Record Retention

Federal grant record retention under 2 CFR 200.334:

| Record Type                                | Minimum Retention Period                              |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Financial records and supporting documents | 3 years from final expenditure report submission date |
| Personnel records (time and effort)        | 3 years from final expenditure report submission date |
| Equipment records                          | 3 years after final disposition                       |
| Subrecipient records                       | 3 years from your final expenditure report            |
| Programmatic records                       | 3 years from final report submission                  |

Extended retention requirements (verify with your program officer or award terms, these vary by program and are subject to change):

- USDA programs: often longer than the baseline 3-year minimum, confirm in your award
- HUD programs: often longer than the baseline 3-year minimum, confirm in your award
- Any record involved in a pending audit, litigation, or claim: retain until matter is resolved

**The retention clock starts from the date you submit the final expenditure report - not the grant period end date.**

### File Archiving

- [ ] Compile the complete grant file: award letter and all amendments, all submitted reports with confirmations, all expenditure documentation, all time records, equipment records, subrecipient monitoring records, and the closeout letter.
- [ ] Archive in a location accessible to authorized staff for the retention period.
- [ ] Document the archive location in your grant management system.
- [ ] Update the grant record status to "Closed" with the closeout letter date.

### Staff Debrief

- [ ] Schedule a brief internal debrief: what compliance systems worked, what was difficult, what would be done differently for the next grant of this type.
- [ ] Document lessons learned in a format that will be accessible for future grants from the same funder or in the same program area.
- [ ] Identify any staffing, system, or process improvements to make before the next grant period begins.

## Federal vs. Private Funder Differences Summary

| Closeout Element          | Federal Grants                                                                                                          | Private Foundation Grants                                   |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Final financial report    | SF-425, deadline per Notice of Award (verify with awarding agency)                                                      | Varies; often annual report format, due per grant agreement |
| Final programmatic report | Required; deadline varies by agency, confirm in Notice of Award                                                         | Required; format and deadline vary widely                   |
| Unspent funds             | Must be returned to awarding agency                                                                                     | Per grant agreement; varies                                 |
| Equipment disposition     | 2 CFR 200.313 procedures (verify current thresholds)                                                                    | Rarely specified; check grant agreement                     |
| Record retention          | Minimum 3 years from final expenditure report under 2 CFR 200.334; some programs require longer, verify with your award | Varies; 3-5 years is common                                 |
| Formal closeout letter    | Standard for federal awards                                                                                             | Not always issued; request if needed                        |
