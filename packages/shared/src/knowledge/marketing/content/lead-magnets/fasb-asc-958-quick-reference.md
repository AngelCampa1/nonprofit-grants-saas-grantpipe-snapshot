## What ASC 958 Is and Why It Matters

FASB Accounting Standards Codification Topic 958 - Not-for-Profit Entities - is the accounting standard that governs how nonprofit organizations classify and report their financial resources. It establishes the rules for net asset classification, contribution revenue recognition, functional expense reporting, and the disclosures your organization must include in its audited financial statements.

For mid-sized nonprofits managing grants and restricted gifts, ASC 958 is not abstract theory. It is the framework your auditor uses when reviewing your statements, the standard your board should reference when asking about restricted fund balances, and the basis for the disclosures your major funders expect to see in your IRS Form 990. Misapplying it is one of the most common sources of audit findings at organizations in the $500K-$10M budget range.

This guide covers the core concepts in the order you are most likely to encounter them in practice - starting with net asset classification, moving through contribution recognition, and ending with the disclosures and checklist your team can use at annual close.

---

## Net Asset Classification: The 2016 Simplification

Before ASU 2016-14 (effective for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2017), nonprofits classified net assets into three categories: unrestricted, temporarily restricted, and permanently restricted.

ASU 2016-14 eliminated the "temporarily restricted" and "permanently restricted" labels and replaced them with two categories:

**Net assets without donor restriction** - Resources that are available for general use in carrying out the organization's mission. This includes what was previously called unrestricted net assets, plus any board-designated amounts (which are internally restricted but not donor-restricted).

**Net assets with donor restriction** - Resources subject to donor-imposed restrictions that have not yet been met. This combines what was previously called temporarily restricted (restrictions that will lapse over time or upon meeting a purpose condition) and permanently restricted (restrictions that must be maintained in perpetuity, such as endowment principal).

The two-category model is simpler on the face of the financial statements but requires more disclosure in the notes - specifically, organizations must now provide additional information about the nature and amounts of the restrictions within the "with donor restriction" category.

**Why this matters operationally:**

Your accounting system must track individual restrictions at the fund level, not just the aggregate "with donor restriction" total. When a funder asks "what is the unspent balance on our grant?" or when an auditor asks you to support the restricted net asset balance on your balance sheet, you need to be able to produce a fund-by-fund listing - not just a summary figure.

## Contribution Recognition: Conditions vs. Restrictions

This is the most common source of errors in nonprofit accounting under ASC 958, and it is worth being precise about.

**Restrictions** limit how or when resources can be used. A restriction does not prevent recognition of revenue - it requires recognition as restricted revenue. Example: a donor gives $25,000 "to support youth programming in the next fiscal year." This is recognized as contribution revenue with donor restriction when received, because the organization has an unconditional right to the funds - they are just restricted to a particular use.

**Conditions** are barriers that must be overcome before the organization has a right to the funds. If a condition is present, revenue is not recognized until the condition is met. Example: a funder agrees to give $50,000 only if the organization raises a matching $50,000 from other sources. The organization does not have a right to the funds until the match is achieved - so no revenue is recognized until the condition is satisfied.

**The practical test:**

Ask: Does the organization have an unconditional right to the funds, regardless of what happens next? If yes, it is a restriction - recognize as restricted revenue. If the right to receive the funds depends on something the organization must do or achieve, it is a condition - do not recognize until the condition is met.

**Where this goes wrong:**

Organizations frequently treat conditional grants as restricted grants and recognize revenue prematurely. This inflates restricted net assets and overstates revenue in the period. Auditors look for this pattern, particularly in government grants where reimbursement-basis funding (you spend first, then request reimbursement) is common. Reimbursement-basis grants typically contain conditions (you must incur eligible expenses before you have a right to payment), which means revenue is recognized as expenses are incurred, not when the grant is awarded.

**The 2018 update (ASU 2018-08):**

ASU 2018-08 clarified the distinction between conditions and restrictions specifically for grants and contributions received from resource providers (governments, foundations). The update made it easier for organizations to identify when a barrier exists and harder to justify premature revenue recognition. If your organization receives significant grant revenue, ensure your recognition policies reflect the ASU 2018-08 guidance.

## Underwater Endowments

An endowment is "underwater" when its fair value falls below its original gift amount (the "historic dollar value"). This situation requires specific disclosure under ASC 958.

**What disclosure is required:**

Organizations must disclose the aggregate fair value of all underwater endowments, the aggregate original gift amount (historic dollar value), and the aggregate amount by which the fair value falls short of the historic dollar value.

**Common mistake:**

Treating underwater endowment amounts as unrestricted because they appear to be deficits in the restricted endowment fund. The original gift value is permanently restricted - even if the fund is underwater, the organization cannot reclassify it. The deficit is shown as a reduction in net assets with donor restriction, not a charge to unrestricted net assets.

**Board designated endowments:**

A board-designated endowment (also called a quasi-endowment) is an amount the board has set aside to function like an endowment, but which was not donor-restricted. It is classified as net assets without donor restriction regardless of the board's intention to preserve it long-term. This is a frequent misclassification: organizations report quasi-endowments as "restricted" because they feel restricted internally, when the accounting standard requires them to be reported as unrestricted.

## Required Disclosures for Restricted Funds

The notes to your financial statements must include disclosures that allow a reader to understand the nature and amounts of donor-imposed restrictions on net assets. Minimum disclosure requirements:

**Nature and timing of restrictions:**

Describe the types of restrictions in place - purpose restrictions, time restrictions, perpetual restrictions. Do not simply list dollar amounts without explanation.

**Donor-restricted endowment funds:**

For organizations with donor-restricted endowment funds, ASC 958-205-45 requires disclosure of:

- The organization's interpretation of its state's enacted law governing endowment management (most states have adopted UPMIFA, the Uniform Prudent Management of Institutional Funds Act)
- Endowment spending policy and how it is determined
- Investment policies and objectives
- The composition of endowment net assets by net asset class, with a rollforward showing beginning balance, investment return, contributions, appropriations, and ending balance
- Underwater endowment disclosure (described above)

**Net assets released from restrictions:**

The statement of activities must show amounts released from restrictions during the period - the mechanism by which restricted resources become available for general use as purpose conditions are met or time periods elapse. This line item is the accounting bridge between restricted and unrestricted net assets, and its absence is a common deficiency finding.

## Functional Expense Reporting

ASC 958 requires nonprofits to present expenses by functional classification - program services, management and general (M&G), and fundraising - either on the face of the statement of activities or in the notes. ASU 2016-14 added a requirement to also present expenses by their nature (salaries, rent, supplies, etc.) in a matrix that shows how natural expenses are allocated across functional categories.

**The allocation problem:**

Many expenses benefit multiple functions. A Development Director's salary benefits both fundraising (direct solicitation work) and management and general (supervising operations, attending board meetings). Rent for office space used by program and administrative staff benefits both program services and M&G.

Allocations must be based on a reasonable and systematic methodology. Common methods:

| Expense Type            | Common Allocation Method                                  |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Salaries and wages      | Time and effort tracking (time studies, timesheets)       |
| Rent / occupancy        | Square footage by department                              |
| Supplies                | Direct identification where possible; headcount otherwise |
| Depreciation            | Square footage or asset use                               |
| Technology and software | Headcount or direct identification                        |

The methodology must be documented. Auditors look for documentation of the allocation basis, consistency of application from year to year, and whether the allocation reflects actual use rather than convenient percentages.

**Common misclassification: treating too much as program expense**

Organizations under pressure to show low overhead ratios sometimes allocate expenses to program services that would more accurately belong in M&G or fundraising. This is not acceptable under ASC 958 and is one of the areas auditors scrutinize when functional allocations look anomalous relative to sector norms. The Form 990 functional expense schedule is public, and funders and watchdog organizations (Charity Navigator, GuideStar, Better Business Bureau Wise Giving Alliance) use it to assess overhead ratios.

## Common Audit Findings Related to ASC 958

The following are the most frequently cited audit findings related to ASC 958 compliance at mid-sized nonprofits. Each is preventable with documentation and proper accounting practices:

| Finding                                                            | Root Cause                                                                 | Prevention                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Restricted net assets do not agree to fund-level detail            | No fund-level sub-ledger; restricted balance is tracked as a single number | Maintain a fund-by-fund listing that ties to the balance sheet total                                    |
| Revenue recognized on conditional grants before conditions are met | Grant treated as restricted rather than conditional                        | Apply the conditions vs. restrictions test at grant receipt; confirm with auditor for government grants |
| Board-designated funds classified as donor-restricted              | Internal restriction treated as external                                   | Document board-designated funds separately; classify as unrestricted                                    |
| Functional expense allocation not documented                       | Verbal allocation method not written down                                  | Maintain a written allocation policy approved by leadership                                             |
| Net assets released from restrictions not presented correctly      | Reclassification entries absent or incorrect                               | Prepare release-from-restriction entries when purpose conditions are met; review at close               |
| Underwater endowments not disclosed                                | Not identified during close process                                        | Include underwater endowment check in annual close checklist                                            |
| Endowment rollforward not included in notes                        | Oversight                                                                  | Use a standard template for endowment note; include in close package                                    |

## Quick Reference Checklist for Annual Close

Use this checklist to verify ASC 958 compliance before issuing financial statements:

**Net asset classification:**

- [ ] All funds in the general ledger are classified as either with or without donor restriction
- [ ] Board-designated funds are classified as without donor restriction
- [ ] Fund-level listing of restricted net assets reconciles to the balance sheet
- [ ] No temporarily restricted or permanently restricted terminology in financial statements or notes (use current labels)

**Contribution recognition:**

- [ ] All grants received during the period have been evaluated for conditions vs. restrictions
- [ ] Conditional grants are recognized as expenditures are incurred (for reimbursement-basis awards)
- [ ] Revenue recognition policy is documented and consistently applied

**Net assets released from restrictions:**

- [ ] Release-from-restriction entries are prepared for all grants where conditions were met during the period
- [ ] Purpose restrictions released when expenditures were incurred for the restricted purpose
- [ ] Time restrictions released when the designated time period elapsed

**Endowment:**

- [ ] All donor-restricted endowment funds identified
- [ ] Underwater endowment check performed; disclosure prepared if applicable
- [ ] Quasi-endowments classified as without donor restriction
- [ ] Endowment rollforward note prepared showing beginning balance, return, contributions, appropriations, ending balance
- [ ] Spending policy, investment policy, and UPMIFA interpretation disclosed

**Functional expenses:**

- [ ] Expenses allocated across program, M&G, and fundraising using documented methodology
- [ ] Allocation methodology consistent with prior year (or change documented and disclosed)
- [ ] Natural expense matrix prepared and tied to functional totals
- [ ] Depreciation and occupancy allocations supported by documentation (square footage schedule, asset register)

**Disclosure completeness:**

- [ ] Nature and timing of significant restrictions described in notes
- [ ] Endowment disclosures complete (if applicable)
- [ ] Underwater endowment disclosed (if applicable)
- [ ] Prior period comparatives reviewed for consistency
- [ ] Any changes in accounting policies or estimates disclosed
- [ ] Disclosures reviewed against prior year auditor management letter for repeat findings

**Notes completeness:**

- [ ] Significant restrictions described in narrative form - not just dollar amounts
- [ ] Donor-restricted endowment notes complete and tied to rollforward
- [ ] Functional expense allocation methodology disclosed (required by ASU 2016-14)
- [ ] Liquidity and availability of resources disclosure prepared (required for all nonprofits under ASU 2016-14)

**Liquidity disclosure (frequently missed):**

ASU 2016-14 added a requirement that nonprofits disclose qualitative information about how they manage liquid resources to meet general expenditure needs for one year beyond the balance sheet date, and quantitative information about the financial assets available to meet general expenditures within one year. This disclosure is separate from the net asset classification disclosures and is frequently omitted at smaller organizations that did not perform a full post-ASU 2016-14 review of their disclosure package. If your financial statements do not include a liquidity note, add it to your close checklist and confirm the format with your auditor before the engagement begins.
