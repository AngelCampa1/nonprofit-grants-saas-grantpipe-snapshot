## Why Auditors Find Gaps After the Fieldwork Starts

Audit preparation usually happens in two phases: the scramble and the regret. The scramble is the two weeks before the auditor arrives, when the team pulls documents from email archives, shared drives, and accounting exports. The regret is the finding letter, which cites the documents that were missing or couldn't be produced on request.

The gap between the two phases is almost always a document that existed somewhere but wasn't connected to the grant record in any usable way: the time-and-effort certification signed but filed in a personal drive, the amendment letter emailed but never uploaded to the grant file, the restricted fund release processed but never documented with a supporting narrative.

This checklist works backward from what auditors request. It is not a comprehensive compliance guide. It is the document inventory you need before you can say you're ready for fieldwork.

## How to Use This Checklist

Go through each section before the auditor's fieldwork start date. For each item, mark whether the document exists, where it is stored, and whether it is already in your grant management system or needs to be uploaded.

If you're using the GrantPipe Auditor Portal, any document you haven't uploaded yet should be uploaded before you build the evidence bundle. A document that exists in a filing cabinet but isn't in the portal cannot be shared without breaking the controlled-access model that makes the portal useful.

## Section 1: General Organizational Documents

These documents apply to most reviews regardless of the specific grant being audited.

- [ ] Most recent audited financial statements (typically the past two fiscal years)
- [ ] Most recent Form 990 (or 990-EZ / 990-N as applicable)
- [ ] Current organizational chart showing the positions funded by grants under review
- [ ] Current board list with member names and term information
- [ ] Indirect cost rate documentation (NICRA letter, de minimis election, or negotiated rate agreement)
- [ ] Written indirect cost allocation methodology (if allocating indirect costs across grants)
- [ ] Current fiscal policies and procedures manual (or the relevant sections on procurement, expense approval, and cash controls)
- [ ] Current conflict of interest policy and signed disclosures for the audit period

## Section 2: Restricted Fund Evidence

Auditors testing restricted fund compliance want to see that restricted dollars stayed restricted: spending matched the approved purpose and budget, and releases were documented.

- [ ] Restricted fund balance summary as of the end of the audit period, by fund
- [ ] List of all active restrictions with the original fund opening date and donor or award intent statement
- [ ] Documented restriction terms for each fund under review (from the award letter or gift agreement)
- [ ] Fund release documentation: for any fund released during the period, the release authorization and the supporting narrative showing the restriction was satisfied
- [ ] Reconciliation of restricted fund balances to the general ledger for the audit period
- [ ] Board or management approval documentation for any restriction releases above your internal threshold
- [ ] Net assets with donor restrictions roll-forward schedule (FASB ASC 958 presentation)

## Section 3: Grant-Specific Records

Run this section for each grant in audit scope. If you are managing multiple grants, repeat the checklist for each.

**Award and Setup**

- [ ] Signed grant award letter or contract (all pages)
- [ ] All executed amendments, including budget modifications
- [ ] Approved budget as modified through the most recent amendment
- [ ] Grant setup documentation showing how the award was entered in the accounting system
- [ ] Any prior-approval correspondence (budget reallocation requests, no-cost extension requests, key personnel changes)

**Financial Reports**

- [ ] All SF-425 Federal Financial Reports filed during the audit period (for federal grants)
- [ ] All funder-required financial reports filed during the audit period (for foundation and state grants)
- [ ] Final financial report (if the grant closed during the audit period)
- [ ] Budget-to-actual comparison report for the full grant period or the period under review
- [ ] General ledger extract showing all charges to the grant, coded by budget category

**Narrative and Programmatic Reports**

- [ ] All progress reports filed during the audit period
- [ ] Final narrative report (if the grant closed during the audit period)
- [ ] Any written responses to funder questions or monitoring inquiries

**Personnel and Time-and-Effort**

- [ ] Time-and-effort certifications or signed timesheets for all staff whose salary was charged to the grant during the audit period
- [ ] Payroll records showing the salary amounts charged to the grant (summary acceptable if individual timesheets are attached)
- [ ] Documentation of the effort allocation methodology (if a staff member split time across multiple grants or programs)

## Section 4: Supporting Documentation

Auditors performing expenditure testing will sample specific transactions and ask for the underlying documentation. These records should be retrievable within an hour for any expenditure charged to the grant.

**Personnel costs**

- [ ] Offer letters or employment agreements for key personnel named in the award
- [ ] Salary history showing the rate used during the award period
- [ ] Fringe benefit calculation and rate documentation

**Supplies and materials**

- [ ] Original invoices or receipts for expenditures above your documentation threshold
- [ ] Purchase orders or procurement documentation for purchases above your competitive bidding threshold
- [ ] Vendor debarment verification (for federal grants: SAM.gov check documentation)

**Consultants and subcontractors**

- [ ] Signed contracts or statements of work for each subcontractor or consultant
- [ ] Invoices with sufficient detail to verify work was performed
- [ ] For federal grants: documentation that competitive procurement procedures were followed
- [ ] For federal grants: signed subrecipient agreement and monitoring records (if funds were passed to a sub-awardee)

**Travel**

- [ ] Pre-approval documentation (if required by your travel policy or the funder)
- [ ] Receipts for all travel expenses
- [ ] Mileage logs (if vehicle travel was reimbursed)
- [ ] Connection to program purpose (brief notation of why travel supported the funded program)

**Equipment**

- [ ] Purchase documentation and competitive bidding records for any equipment over your capitalization threshold
- [ ] Equipment inventory tag and asset record
- [ ] For federal grants: equipment disposition documentation if the grant closed and equipment ownership was transferred or disposed of

## When You Find a Gap

A missing document discovered during pre-fieldwork checklist review is recoverable. Most gaps fall into one of three categories:

**Document exists but isn't uploaded.** Upload it to the grant record in GrantPipe and add it to the evidence bundle before you send the portal link. If the document is in a file cabinet, scan it now.

**Document was never created.** For historical periods, you may be able to reconstruct some records: payroll records can often be re-exported, budgets can be confirmed against the award letter. For contemporaneous records like timesheets, reconstruction is not an acceptable substitute. Acknowledge the gap and document your internal control response.

**Document existed but can't be located.** Treat this the same as never created for audit purposes. Escalate to your executive director. This is the kind of gap that becomes a finding if discovered during fieldwork rather than disclosed proactively.
