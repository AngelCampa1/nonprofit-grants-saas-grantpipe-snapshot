## What the Assessment Measures

Software tier selection is a sizing exercise. Buy too small and you outgrow it within 18 months and spend the savings on a second migration. Buy too large and you spend years paying for capability you don't use while subsidizing a Salesforce admin retainer or a Blackbaud upgrade fee. The middle outcome - tier-matched software - saves the migration tax and the over-tooling tax simultaneously.

This assessment scores ten variables that together determine where your organization sits:

1. **Operating budget** - correlates with infrastructure complexity but doesn't determine it.
2. **Donor count** - drives database performance and segmentation needs.
3. **Active grants** - drives pipeline and compliance complexity.
4. **Restricted fund tracking** - drives the accounting depth required.
5. **Current systems** - informs migration scope.
6. **Integration depth** - drives middleware and admin overhead.
7. **Team size** - drives training and permission complexity.
8. **Compliance footprint** - federal awards add a tier of audit-grade requirements.
9. **Reporting cadence** - formal funder reports drive automation needs.
10. **Growth trajectory** - buy for 24 months out, not for today.

## How the Tiers Differ

**Starter.** Donor records, basic grant tracking, standard reports. Best for organizations under $1M with simple grant scope (under three concurrent grants), light restricted fund needs, and a small team.

**Growth.** Adds restricted fund accounting, multi-grant pipeline, integration depth, custom field definitions, and team-based permissions. Best for organizations $1M-$5M with active grant pipelines and formal funder reporting.

**Audit-Ready.** Adds federal-grade compliance: subrecipient monitoring, audit-grade documentation export, SAM.gov integration, FFATA reporting helpers, single audit workpaper export, NICRA-aware indirect cost handling, and SSO. Best for organizations with federal awards or the scale and complexity that approaches one.

## How to Read Your Result

The recommendation comes with a short summary explaining why you landed there and a list of supporting guides - usually the CRM Evaluation Scorecard for Starter and Growth recommendations, and the Grant Compliance Checklist plus the SEFA Prep Worksheet for Audit-Ready recommendations.

If the result feels off - say you scored Audit-Ready but you don't have federal awards - re-examine the questions on integration depth, team size, and growth trajectory. The most common reason for a high score without federal awards is multi-program complexity (six or more concurrent foundation grants with formal reporting requirements), which often warrants Audit-Ready capabilities even without the federal compliance overhead.

The recommendation is a starting point, not a contract. Run a side-by-side evaluation using the CRM Evaluation Scorecard before making a final selection.

## What the assessment measures

The assessment scores readiness across the control areas and operating practices that most reliably distinguish well-run mid-sized nonprofits from organizations operating in chronic catch-up mode. Each area is weighted by the operational and audit risk it carries, so the score reflects where remediation effort should go first.

## How to use your score

A score above 80 indicates strong fundamentals across the assessed dimensions. A score between 60 and 80 indicates one or two areas that need attention, usually addressable within a quarter of focused work. A score below 60 indicates structural gaps that benefit from a deliberate remediation calendar with named owners and dates. The questionnaire routes you to the specific remediation worksheet for each gap so you can act on the score immediately rather than scheduling a follow-up project.

## What separates strong programs from weak ones

The pattern across nonprofits that score well: they treat the relevant work as monthly discipline rather than annual scramble. They document as they go, they tie financial and program data together at the moment of entry rather than reconstructing it at report time, and they use systems that surface the relevant constraints and metrics on a regular cadence rather than only at deadline pressure. These are cultural and structural traits more than they are technology choices, but the technology either supports the discipline or fights it.

## After the assessment

Once you have your score, the next step is a remediation calendar with named owners and dates. The remediation worksheets the assessment routes you to are designed to be completed inside one or two work sessions, not as multi-month projects. The principle throughout: shift the work from annual to monthly, document as you go, and the underlying numbers improve on the same multi-year horizon as the underlying balance sheet and operating practices.
