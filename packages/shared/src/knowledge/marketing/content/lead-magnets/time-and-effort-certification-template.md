## What 2 CFR 200.430 Requires

Under 2 CFR 200.430, compensation for personal services charged to federal awards must be based on records that:

1. Reflect the actual activity of the employee
2. Account for the total activity for which the employee is compensated by the organization
3. Are supported by a system of internal controls
4. Are incorporated into the official records of the organization
5. Are reasonably reflect the employee's activity
6. Are certified by the employee or supervisory official with first-hand knowledge of the work

The standard is not a timesheet. It is a certification that the proportion of effort charged to the federal award reasonably reflects the proportion of total compensated effort the employee devoted to that award's activities.

**Key distinction:** Effort reporting documents the percentage of time an employee devoted to an award relative to their total compensated effort - not the hours worked. An employee working 50 hours per week whose 20 hours on the grant project represents 40% of total effort should certify 40% effort to the grant, regardless of the total hours logged.

## Part 2: Certification Frequency and Timing

**Minimum certification frequency:**

2 CFR 200.430 does not specify a fixed interval, but federal agencies and auditors generally expect certifications at a frequency that would catch significant effort misallocations before they become a finding. The standard practice is:

- **Minimum:** Quarterly (covers three-month periods; any less frequent is difficult to defend as reflecting actual activity)
- **Common:** Monthly (reduces reconstruction error; most practical for organizations with high staff turnover)
- **Federal agencies may require more frequent:** Some programs require monthly effort reporting explicitly in the award terms. Check your notice of award.

**Timing:**

Certifications must be completed after the certification period ends - not prospectively. A certification signed before the period it covers has no evidentiary value.

_Best practice:_ Complete certifications within 30 days of the end of the certification period. Calendar the deadline at award setup.

## Part 3: Multi-Source Allocation Methodology

When a staff member's time is split across multiple funding sources, the allocation requires a defensible methodology.

**Acceptable approaches:**

**Approach 1: Activity-based tracking (recommended)**

Maintain brief contemporaneous activity logs during the period - not clock-in/clock-out timesheets, but notes that can support the effort allocation at certification time. Even a weekly calendar or project log is sufficient if it reflects actual activity.

_Why it works:_ The certification becomes a summary of documented activity rather than a reconstruction from memory.

**Approach 2: Budget-based allocation (higher risk)**

Allocate effort in proportion to the budget allocation (e.g., if 30% of salary is budgeted to Award A, certify 30% effort).

_Why it's risky:_ Budget allocation and actual activity routinely diverge. If an auditor asks whether the employee actually spent 30% of their time on Award A activities, the answer cannot be "because that's what the budget says." Some federal agencies explicitly prohibit budget-based allocation.

**Approach 3: Supervisor certification**

Where an employee cannot self-certify (e.g., they have left the organization, or the employee's role makes self-certification impractical), a supervisory official with first-hand knowledge may certify on their behalf.

_Limitation:_ Supervisory certification for a prior period is a reconstruction. Auditors scrutinize it. Minimize reliance on supervisor certification by maintaining the documentation process in real time.

## Part 4: Common Audit Findings in Effort Reporting

**Finding 1: Certifications completed prospectively**

Certifications signed before the period they cover - e.g., signing for July effort in June - are not valid.

_Prevention:_ Configure your effort certification process to open after each period ends. If using a spreadsheet, date-lock the certification column.

**Finding 2: 100% allocation to one award when employee serves multiple programs**

An employee who is 100% funded by a single grant but also performs organizational duties (attending staff meetings, assisting with other programs) should not certify 100% effort to the grant.

_Prevention:_ During award setup, identify all activities the employee will perform. If any time goes to unallowable activities, plan for split coding from the start.

**Finding 3: Effort does not match personnel cost charged**

If the GL shows $50,000 in personnel costs charged to an award, the effort certifications for employees charged to that award should be consistent with approximately that payroll expenditure. A large variance is a red flag.

_Prevention:_ Reconcile effort certifications to payroll draws quarterly. Flag and investigate variances above 5%.

**Finding 4: Retroactive adjustments without documentation**

Revising effort certifications more than 90 days after the fact - without contemporaneous supporting documentation - is a high-risk finding.

_Prevention:_ Complete certifications promptly. If an error is discovered, correct it within the same fiscal year, document the reason for the correction, and note it in the grant file.

**Finding 5: Certifications not in the grant file**

Original signed certifications are required in the grant file. Auditors will ask for them.

_Prevention:_ File completed certifications in the award folder at the end of each certification period. Do not batch them at year-end.

## Part 5: Pre-Audit Documentation Checklist

Complete before a single audit or a program-specific audit that covers federal personnel costs.

**Completeness**

- [ ] Signed effort certifications on file for every employee whose salary was charged to a federal award during the audit period
- [ ] Certifications cover every certification period in the audit year - no gaps
- [ ] Each certification covers 100% of the employee's effort (not just the federal share)

**Timeliness**

- [ ] All certifications were signed after the close of the period they cover
- [ ] No certifications are dated more than 90 days after the period end date (or the organization has documentation explaining the delay)

**Reconciliation**

- [ ] Payroll reconciliation completed: effort percentages reconcile to personnel costs charged to the award within a reasonable variance
- [ ] Personnel costs charged to the award tie to the GL

**Allocation consistency**

- [ ] No employee is certified at 100% effort to one award while the GL shows payroll charges to other funding sources
- [ ] Multi-source allocations sum to 100% for each employee for each period

**Supervisory certifications**

- [ ] Where supervisor certification was used, documented reason for why self-certification was not possible
- [ ] Supervisor has confirmed first-hand knowledge of the employee's work activities during the certified period
