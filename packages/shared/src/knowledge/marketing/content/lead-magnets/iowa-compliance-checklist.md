## Why Iowa Compliance Is Worth Calendaring Now

Iowa does not have ordinary charitable solicitation registration. Compliance is the SOS biennial report plus Iowa Department of Revenue sales/use tax exemption documentation where applicable. The key recurring job is keeping the corporate record current and the finance file ready for grants, banking, and audits.

The governing law in Iowa is Iowa Code ch. 504 (Revised Iowa Nonprofit Corporation Act). The administrative portal is https://sos.iowa.gov/business/biennialreport.html. Fees, deadlines, and forms can change between fiscal years - confirm against the source before each cycle.

This checklist is structured the way the work actually flows: confirm corporate status, keep SOS and DOR records current, calendar the recurring Biennial Report, monitor federal audit exposure, and handle closeout. Each step links to the form name and authority so you can hand it to a colleague without re-explaining context. For broader grant compliance scope, see our [Grant Compliance 101 for Nonprofits](/resources/guides/grant-compliance-101-for-nonprofits) guide and the [Iowa nonprofit software overview](/nonprofit-software/iowa).

## Step 1: Confirm Corporate and Tax Status

- [ ] **Verify Iowa SOS entity status.** Record the entity ID, status, registered agent, principal address, and last Biennial Report.
- [ ] **Confirm DOR exemption documentation.** Keep sales/use tax exemption and related tax correspondence with the finance file if those rules apply.
- [ ] **Document who solicits donations.** Keep fundraising policies and donor disclosures in the development file, but do not create a fake SOS charitable-registration workflow.
- [ ] **Check specialized rules before signing paid fundraiser contracts.** Vendor arrangements can create obligations that ordinary staff fundraising does not.

## Step 2: Complete Initial Registration

- [ ] **Visit the Iowa SOS portal at https://sos.iowa.gov/business/biennialreport.html.**
- [ ] **Gather required attachments**:
- [ ] Articles of Incorporation (or equivalent founding document)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS determination letter
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] List of officers, directors, and key employees with addresses
- [ ] Initial registration fee - $20 (Articles of Incorporation)
- [ ] **Submit formation or foreign qualification before operating in Iowa.** Do not describe this as ordinary charitable solicitation registration.
- [ ] **Record the assigned entity number.** Use it on subsequent corporate filings and internal compliance checklists.

## Step 3: Calendar the Biennial Report

The recurring Biennial Report keeps the corporate record active.

- [ ] **Biennial Report due date:** Biennial - between January 1 and April 1 of odd-numbered years. Enter this date in a shared calendar - not just the ED's personal calendar.
- [ ] **Confirm the fee tier:** $10 biennial report (online). Tiered fees scale with gross revenue or contributions; budget for the higher tier the year you cross a threshold.
- [ ] **Follow current SOS instructions.** Keep IRS Form 990, Department of Revenue documents, and audit files in the finance record unless the filing asks for them.
- [ ] **Set a 60-day reminder** before the deadline. Late filings can affect entity status.
- [ ] **File even in quiet years.** Corporate reporting is separate from whether a fundraising campaign happened.

## Step 4: Manage the Audit Threshold

Iowa does not impose a state audit threshold; federal Single Audit at $1M in federal awards applies for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later.

- [ ] **Calculate qualifying revenue or contributions.** Read the statute carefully - definitions of "gross revenue" or "contributions" can exclude government grants, in-kind contributions, or pass-through funds depending on the state.
- [ ] **Engage a CPA early.** A 990 audit takes 60-120 days. Start procurement before fiscal year end if you are within 10% of the threshold.
- [ ] **Keep audit documentation with the grant and finance file.** Iowa does not create a separate state audit filing just because the federal Single Audit threshold applies.
- [ ] **Maintain audit committee structure** if required by statute or your bylaws. Some states require a board-appointed audit committee for organizations subject to audit; review your governing documents.
- [ ] **Plan for the audit cost.** Independent CPA audits for mid-sized nonprofits typically run $8,000-$25,000 depending on complexity and federal award activity.

## Step 5: Donor Disclosures and Fundraising Vendors

Ordinary fundraising is not an SOS charitable-registration workflow. Keep donor-facing disclosures accurate and check specialized rules before using paid fundraising vendors.

- [ ] **Charity name as registered** with the Iowa Secretary of State.
- [ ] **A clear description of how contributions will be used.**
- [ ] **For commercial fundraisers and fundraising counsel:** review state guidance before signing a contract.

## Step 6: Maintain the Public Registry Profile

- [ ] **Review your public profile annually** at https://sos.iowa.gov/business/biennialreport.html. Confirm name, address, registration status, and most recent filings are current.
- [ ] **Promptly update changes.** Address changes, name changes, fiscal year changes, and dissolution must usually be reported within 30 days. Use the amendment form on the Iowa SOS site.
- [ ] **Status checks before grant applications.** Foundations and government grantors check Iowa registry status. A "Delinquent" or "Suspended" status will disqualify applications regardless of program fit.

## Step 7: Closeout When Operations End

If your organization dissolves, merges, or stops soliciting in Iowa:

- [ ] **File a final Biennial Report** marked "Final" or otherwise indicate closeout per the form instructions.
- [ ] **File dissolution paperwork** with the Iowa Secretary of State or other state authority responsible for corporate filings, along with the AG-required notices where applicable.
- [ ] **Distribute remaining charitable assets** consistent with your dissolution clause and Iowa Code ch. 504 (Revised Iowa Nonprofit Corporation Act).
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least four years after dissolution to respond to any post-closeout inquiries from the Iowa SOS or IRS.

## Common Failure Points

- **Inventing an Iowa charitable-registration task.** Ordinary fundraising is not a standalone SOS charitable-registration workflow.
- **Filing federal Form 990 only.** Federal filing does not satisfy Iowa. The Biennial Report is a separate state requirement.
- **Treating the state audit threshold as a federal issue.** Iowa's audit trigger (Iowa does not impose a state audit threshold; federal Single Audit at $1M in federal awards applies for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later) is independent of the federal Single Audit threshold of $1,000,000 in federal awards for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later. The two can both apply.
- **Ignoring foreign qualification.** A nonprofit headquartered elsewhere may need corporate qualification if it is doing business in Iowa, even though that is different from charitable solicitation registration.
- **Allowing status to lapse to "Delinquent."** Foundations and government grantors check public registry status. A delinquent status can cost a grant award worth far more than the renewal fee.

The Iowa Secretary of State is administrative, not adversarial - agencies generally provide multiple notices before administrative dissolution. The consequence is still serious: a bad entity status can derail grant applications, contracts, and banking updates.
