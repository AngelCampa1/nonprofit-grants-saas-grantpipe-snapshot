## Why This Map Exists

San Diego County hosts roughly 14,000 registered nonprofits and a foundation community where the top 20 funders distribute the bulk of regional giving. Because the funder pool is concentrated, an off-target LOI consumes a disproportionate share of prospecting capacity. Targeting precision matters more here than in larger metros.

This map shows what each foundation has actually funded over the last three years, paired with stated priorities, so LOI capacity goes where it converts.

## How to Use This Map

Pick three funders whose 24-month grant history overlaps your program area, budget tier, and geographic focus. Use the median-grant-size column to right-size every ask. Over-asks remain the most common rejection reason in this region.

## Common Failure Modes This Map Surfaces

The patterns most San Diego nonprofits miss until the second or third declined LOI:

- Foundations whose website lists a program area they have not actually funded in three years.
- Foundations whose typical grant size is well below what your program needs.
- Foundations that only fund prior grantees but accept unsolicited LOIs anyway.
- Foundations where 24-month giving has shifted because of trustee transitions, program officer turnover, or strategic-plan changes the website has not yet reflected.

Each entry in the map flags these patterns where they appear so the prospect list you build is one you can act on.

## What to Do With This Map First

Open it next to your current prospect list. Cross out funders where the median grant size, geographic focus, or program area no longer matches. The remaining names get a 24-month-grant-history pull from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer or Candid before any LOI is drafted.

Most San Diego organizations cut their tracked prospect list by 30-50% after one pass and redirect that capacity to deeper stewardship of the funders that survive the cut.

## Why a Concentrated Region Rewards Precision

San Diego County's foundation community is denser than its size suggests, and a marginal LOI to a poorly matched funder costs you more here than it does in deeper markets. The funder pool is smaller, the philanthropic community talks more across boundaries, and reputation compounds faster.

That is why the precision filter on this map matters more here than in larger metros. The reward for cutting marginal prospects is not only better hit rates; it is reclaimed time for stewarding the funders that remain. Those relationships compound faster than in larger metros because the community is denser, and an organization that consistently asks well-matched funders for well-sized grants becomes known as one to take seriously.

Use the map to make that compounding work in your favor.
