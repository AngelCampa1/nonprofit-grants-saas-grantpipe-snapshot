## The Hidden Cost Problem

Most nonprofit Finance and Development teams can tell you the award amount for every active grant. Very few can tell you what each grant actually costs to manage. That gap - between grant award and net grant value - is what this audit is designed to close.

Grant compliance has four cost buckets most organizations never add together:

1. **Staff time** - the hours spent setting up the grant, coding transactions, reconciling, preparing reports, and responding to auditors
2. **Software gaps** - what your current systems don't cover and what you're doing manually to compensate
3. **Error recovery** - the cost of re-issuing reports, re-coding transactions, correcting documentation after the fact
4. **Risk exposure** - the potential cost of audit findings, funder sanctions, or disallowed expenses

Use this worksheet section by section for your grant portfolio. If you manage more than three grants, replicate the tables in a spreadsheet and add one column per grant. At the end, sum your numbers and compare them to the benchmark.

## What to Do With Your Numbers

**If your total is above $8,000 per grant per year:** Purpose-built grant management software can reduce staff hours, eliminate spreadsheet gap compensation, and lower error recovery events. Software pricing varies by vendor, feature set, and organization size. Request current pricing from any vendor you evaluate rather than relying on general ranges.

**If your indirect cost shortfall exceeds $10,000 annually:** This is an argument for funders, not software. Some large foundations publish indirect cost policies that reflect research into overhead rates at financially healthy nonprofits. Review your key funders' published indirect cost policies and use any available documentation to support funder conversations. The MacArthur Foundation publishes an indirect cost policy; confirm the current rate directly at macfound.org before citing it. The audit is the documentation you need to make the case.

**If your risk exposure is categorized as "high":** The most expensive compliance investment is the one you make after a finding. Proactive investment in systems that create audit trails, enforce fund restrictions at entry, and generate documentation automatically is almost always cheaper than remediation.

The goal of this audit is not to create anxiety. It is to create clarity - so that the cost of the status quo is visible alongside the cost of changing it.
