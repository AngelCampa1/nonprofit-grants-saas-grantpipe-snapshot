## What This Template Does

The donor-to-grant reconciliation template provides a structured working document for matching records between a donor CRM and a grant management or accounting system. It identifies four types of discrepancies, guides the resolution of each, and documents the reconciliation for the audit file.

Use this template annually before the year-end audit. Use a simplified version of the matching section for monthly spot checks during active grant periods.

---

## The Reconciliation Template Structure

**Header Information**

- Reconciliation period (fiscal year or specified date range)
- Date of reconciliation
- Reconciler name
- Reviewer name
- CRM system used
- Grant/accounting system used

---

## Section 1: Funder Master List

Export both systems and build a combined funder master list. For each funder, collect:

From the donor CRM:

- Funder name (as entered in CRM)
- Total gifts recorded in the CRM for the period
- Restriction classification (as entered in CRM: unrestricted, restricted-purpose, restricted-time)
- Fund designation (CRM field linking the gift to a specific fund)
- Contact staff member

From the grants management or accounting system:

- Funder name (as recorded in grants system)
- Total receipts recorded for the period
- Restriction classification (as coded in the accounting system)
- Fund code
- Grant status (active, closed, pending)

**Matching key:** Match records using funder name + approximate amount + period. For funders with multiple grants, match each grant individually.

---

## Section 2: Discrepancy Identification

After matching, classify each unmatched or mismatched record:

**Type 1: CRM-only - record in CRM with no matching grant record**

Common causes:

- Development entered a gift that has not yet been received by finance
- Finance received the payment but did not code it as restricted
- Development and finance use different naming for the same grant

Resolution column: "Missing from accounting system - receipt date [X], amount [Y], reason [Z]"

**Type 2: Grants-only - record in accounting with no matching CRM record**

Common causes:

- Government or agency grant that bypassed development
- CRM entry not made when grant was posted in accounting
- The funder is not managed as a donor relationship (some reporting-only grants fall here)

Resolution column: "Missing from CRM - add funder record [or note: administrative-only, not a cultivation relationship]"

**Type 3: Amount discrepancy - both systems have the record but amounts differ**

Common causes:

- CRM shows full award amount; accounting shows first installment received
- CRM shows pledged amount; accounting shows net of fees
- CRM was updated when the award letter arrived; accounting was updated when check cleared

Resolution column: "Amount in CRM: [X], amount in accounting: [Y], source of discrepancy: [pledge vs. received / full award vs. installment], corrective action: [update CRM to show installment received / update accounting to show full award amount]"

**Type 4: Classification discrepancy - same amount but different restriction coding**

Common causes:

- Development coded a grant as unrestricted general support based on relationship history; finance coded it as restricted after reading the grant agreement
- A grant that was historically unrestricted had restrictions added in the current year's agreement
- Miscommunication between development and finance about what the grant letter said

Resolution column: "CRM codes as [unrestricted/restricted]. Accounting codes as [unrestricted/restricted]. Source document reviewed: [grant agreement, section X]. Correct classification: [restricted to workforce development per grant agreement section 3.1]. Both systems updated to reflect correct classification."

---

## Section 3: Resolution Documentation

For each discrepancy, complete the resolution fields:

| Funder | Discrepancy Type | CRM Amount | Accounting Amount | Correct Amount | Source Document | Corrective Action | Date Resolved | Resolved By |

The "Source Document" field is critical. The authoritative source for resolution is always the grant agreement, award letter, or contribution documentation - not a staff member's recollection. Every resolution should cite a specific document and section.

---

## Section 4: Reconciliation Summary

After resolving all discrepancies:

**Total funders reviewed:** [count]
**Total gifts/grants matched without discrepancy:** [count] ([%])
**Discrepancies identified:** [count]

- Type 1 (CRM-only): [count]
- Type 2 (Grants-only): [count]
- Type 3 (Amount): [count]
- Type 4 (Classification): [count]
  **Discrepancies resolved:** [count]
  **Open items requiring further investigation:** [count, with explanation]

**CRM total restricted gifts for period:** $[amount]
**Accounting total restricted receipts for period:** $[amount]
**Variance after adjustments:** $[amount] - must explain any non-zero variance

## Instructions for Annual Reconciliation

**Timing:** Perform the annual reconciliation after the fiscal year closes and before the audit fieldwork begins. This gives you time to correct errors before the auditor tests the records.

**Step 1: Generate exports**

Export the CRM funder/gift list for the fiscal year. Export the accounting system's restricted revenue and grant receipt list for the same period. Include all funders with gifts or grant awards in the period.

**Step 2: Build the matching table**

Use the funder master list format above. Start with automatic matching (name + amount + period). The remaining unmatched records are your starting discrepancy list.

**Step 3: Investigate each discrepancy**

Pull source documents for each discrepancy. The grant agreement or award letter is always the tie-breaker. Involve the development director for Type 1 and Type 4 discrepancies - they have the relationship context.

**Step 4: Update both systems**

After resolving each discrepancy, update the relevant record in the affected system. Do not update only one system and leave the other uncorrected.

**Step 5: Document and file**

Complete the reconciliation summary. File the completed template alongside the grant files for the year. The auditor will ask for evidence of the reconciliation process - this document is the answer.

---

## Monthly Spot Check Process

A simplified monthly spot check catches discrepancies before they accumulate. During the monthly close process:

1. Review new gifts entered in the CRM for the month (filtered to restricted designation)
2. Confirm each new restricted gift has a corresponding entry in the accounting system within the same period
3. Flag any entries that exist in one system but not the other
4. Investigate and resolve before closing the period

The monthly check should take fifteen to thirty minutes when current. Document it with a date stamp and the reviewer's initials.

---

## When to Use This Template vs. Software

This template is the right tool for organizations managing up to fifteen to twenty active restricted funders, performing annual reconciliation with monthly spot checks, and working with systems that can produce exportable data.

For organizations where the annual reconciliation consistently takes more than a full day, where discrepancies are structural (the same types of errors recurring month after month), or where the reconciliation itself has become a major project rather than a documentation exercise, the underlying problem is likely that the CRM and accounting systems are too separate. A unified system - where funder records and grant records are the same record - eliminates the reconciliation exercise entirely.

The template is a solution to a process gap. A unified platform is a solution to an architectural gap.
