## What a Monitoring Visit Requires

Funder monitoring visits vary in scope and depth. A foundation program officer checking in between formal reporting cycles may want to see current fund balances and a few supporting documents. A government agency reviewer conducting a formal compliance monitoring may request documentation across every compliance area for the full grant period.

Both require you to produce evidence in organized form, scoped to the specific grant under review. The template below structures that evidence so you know what you have before anyone asks for it.

Use this template before the monitoring visit, not during it. The goal is to find gaps while there is still time to close them.

---

## Section 1: Award Documentation

These documents establish the terms of the grant: what was approved, what was later modified, and what the funder expects in return.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Original signed grant award letter or contract (all pages including exhibits)
  _Reviewer purpose: Confirms the original scope, approved budget, and grant period._

- [ ] All executed amendments or modifications, in date order
  _Reviewer purpose: Verifies that spending and reporting reflects the current approved terms, not the original ones._

- [ ] Approved budget as of the most recent amendment
  _Reviewer purpose: The comparison baseline for budget-to-actual review._

- [ ] Any prior approval correspondence (budget modifications, no-cost extensions, key personnel changes, scope changes)
  _Reviewer purpose: Confirms that changes requiring prior approval were approved before they occurred._

- [ ] Current grant period start and end dates (confirmed from the most recent amendment if modified)
  _Reviewer purpose: Verifies that expenditures fall within the period of performance._

---

## Section 2: Financial Evidence

Financial evidence demonstrates that grant funds were spent as approved. For most monitoring visits, this section receives the most scrutiny.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Budget-to-actual comparison for the current grant period (or the period under review)
  _Reviewer purpose: Shows spending rate against approved categories. Significant variances trigger follow-up questions._

- [ ] General ledger extract showing all expenditures charged to the grant, by budget category
  _Reviewer purpose: Provides the transaction-level detail behind the budget-to-actual summary._

- [ ] Restricted fund balance as of the most recent accounting close
  _Reviewer purpose: Confirms the unexpended grant balance and the projected ability to spend within the grant period._

- [ ] All financial reports filed during the grant period (SF-425 for federal grants, foundation financial report templates for private funders)
  _Reviewer purpose: Cross-checks filed reports against the underlying financial records for consistency._

- [ ] Cost allocation methodology documentation, if costs are shared across programs or grants
  _Reviewer purpose: Verifies that indirect costs and shared direct costs are allocated consistently and in accordance with the approved methodology._

- [ ] Indirect cost documentation (NICRA letter, de minimis rate election, or foundation-approved rate)
  _Reviewer purpose: Confirms that indirect charges match the approved rate and calculation base._

---

## Section 3: Personnel and Time Documentation

Personnel costs are the largest line item in most nonprofit grant budgets and the most closely reviewed in monitoring.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Time-and-effort certifications or signed timesheets for all staff whose salary was charged to the grant during the review period
  _Reviewer purpose: Verifies that payroll charges match actual effort. Missing T&E records are the most common monitoring finding._

- [ ] Summary of salary charges to the grant by employee, cross-referenced to payroll records
  _Reviewer purpose: Allows the reviewer to sample specific employee payroll transactions and verify they match T&E documentation._

- [ ] Documentation of effort allocation methodology for split-funded employees
  _Reviewer purpose: Demonstrates that employees whose time is split across multiple grants or programs have documented, consistent allocation._

- [ ] Documentation of any key personnel changes (hiring, termination, leave of absence) that affected grant-funded positions
  _Reviewer purpose: Verifies that any key personnel changes requiring prior funder approval were handled correctly._

---

## Section 4: Programmatic Evidence

Programmatic evidence demonstrates that the grant-funded program is operating as described in the application and delivering the outcomes the funder approved.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] All progress reports filed during the grant period
  _Reviewer purpose: Provides the narrative record of program activity and progress toward outcomes._

- [ ] Data or records supporting the performance measures reported (participant counts, service delivery records, outcome data)
  _Reviewer purpose: Verifies that reported numbers match underlying program records. This is where data integrity is tested._

- [ ] Any written responses to funder questions or inquiries received during the grant period
  _Reviewer purpose: Shows the full communication record with the funder, including any issues raised and how they were addressed._

- [ ] Documentation of any subcontractors or partners delivering program activities (signed agreements, invoices, monitoring records)
  _Reviewer purpose: Confirms that subaward relationships are properly documented and monitored._

---

## Section 5: Supporting Documentation for Expenditure Testing

If the reviewer plans to sample specific transactions, you need supporting documentation retrievable within an hour for any expenditure. This section applies primarily to government funder monitoring; foundation monitoring visits are less likely to go to this level of detail.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Original invoices or receipts for material expenditures
- [ ] Purchase orders or competitive bidding documentation for purchases above your procurement threshold
- [ ] Signed contracts or statements of work for consultants and subcontractors
- [ ] Travel expense reports with receipts and business purpose documentation
- [ ] Equipment purchase documentation (if any equipment was purchased with grant funds)
- [ ] Documentation of any sole-source procurements (written justification on file)

---

## Before the Monitoring Visit

After completing this checklist:

1. **Identify gaps.** Any item marked not available or not found is a gap to close before the monitoring visit.

2. **Prepare the evidence package.** Organize completed items by section. If you are using GrantPipe's Auditor & Funder Portal, verify that all documents are uploaded to the grant record and that the portal session has the correct access scope and expiry date.

3. **Brief key staff.** The person who will accompany the reviewer should know the grant record, understand the budget-to-actual variance if any exists, and be able to locate any document in the package within a few minutes.

4. **Set the access scope.** If you are giving the reviewer portal access, confirm that the session is scoped to this grant only, with no visibility into other grants, other funders, or organizational records outside the grant's scope.

For grants reviewed through GrantPipe's Auditor & Funder Portal, this template maps directly to the document categories you configure in the portal session. See the [portal documentation](/features/auditor-funder-portal) for the setup steps.
