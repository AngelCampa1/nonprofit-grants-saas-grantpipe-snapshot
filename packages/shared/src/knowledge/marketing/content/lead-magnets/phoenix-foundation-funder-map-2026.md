## Why This Map Exists

Maricopa County has a dense nonprofit market and a foundation community where fit matters. Because the funder pool is concentrated, an off-target LOI consumes a disproportionate share of your prospecting capacity. Precision matters more here than in larger metros.

This map structure shows how to pair recent 990-PF giving with stated priorities, so you spend LOI capacity where it has a real chance of converting.

## What's Inside

- A ranked funder table your team fills from current 990-PF and funder-page research
- Grant size, range, and grant count fields to calculate from source filings
- Funded program area fields for the last three years of reviewed grants
- LOI windows, proposal calendars, and time-to-decision fields to verify before outreach
- Which funders accept unsolicited LOIs and which only fund prior grantees

## How to Use This Map

Pick three funders whose 24-month grant history overlaps your program area, budget tier, and geographic focus. Use the median-grant-size column to right-size every ask before you submit. Over-asks are the most common rejection reason in this region.

## Common Failure Modes This Map Surfaces

The patterns most Phoenix nonprofits miss until the second or third declined LOI:

- Foundations whose website lists a program area they have not actually funded in three years.
- Foundations whose typical grant size is well below what your program needs. The mismatch wastes their cycle and yours.
- Foundations that only fund prior grantees but accept unsolicited LOIs anyway.
- Foundations where 24-month giving has shifted because of new trustees, program officer turnover, or strategic-plan changes that the website has not yet caught up to.

Each entry in the map flags these patterns where they appear so the prospect list you build is one you can act on.

## What to Do With This Map First

Open it next to your current prospect list. Cross out funders where the median grant size, geographic focus, or program area no longer matches. The remaining names get a 24-month-grant-history pull from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer or Candid before any LOI is drafted. Either the match holds, or the gap surfaces - and either answer saves time.

Most Phoenix-area organizations cut their tracked prospect list by 30-50% after one pass and redirect that capacity to deeper stewardship of the funders that survive the cut.

## Why a Concentrated Region Rewards Precision

In LA or NYC, a marginal LOI to a poorly matched funder costs you a few staff hours. In Phoenix, the same LOI burns a week of capacity that you cannot easily refill - because the funder pool is smaller and your next pivot has to be smarter, not just faster.

That is why the precision filter on this map matters more here than in deeper markets. The reward for cutting marginal prospects from your list is not just better hit rates; it is reclaimed time for relationship-building with the funders that remain. In Phoenix, those relationships compound faster than in larger metros, because the philanthropic community is denser and word travels.

Use the map to make that compounding work for you instead of against you.
