## Why Illinois Has Two Statutes

Illinois historically separated charitable trusts (gifts and bequests held in trust for charitable purposes) from charitable solicitations (active fundraising appeals). The Charitable Trust Act regulates the former; the Solicitation for Charity Act regulates the latter. Most modern public charities engage in both - accepting gifts that fund their charitable purpose AND soliciting contributions. So most public charities must register under both statutes.

The Attorney General's Charitable Trust Bureau administers both through a single registration process. One CO-1 filing covers both statutes, with separate fees and reporting components. This is administratively efficient but legally significant: enforcement actions can be brought under either or both statutes, depending on the conduct.

## Step 1: Confirm Registration Triggers

- [ ] **Charitable Trust Act applies** if the organization holds property or assets in Illinois for charitable purposes - including a 501(c)(3) bank account, real estate, or pledged contributions.
- [ ] **Solicitation for Charity Act applies** if the organization solicits contributions in Illinois - including direct mail, online appeals targeting Illinois residents, telephone, or in-person solicitation.
- [ ] **Out-of-state organizations** soliciting Illinois residents must register under the Solicitation for Charity Act.
- [ ] **Statutory exemptions** exist for religious organizations, accredited educational institutions, governmental entities, and certain veteran groups. Most 501(c)(3) public charities are not exempt.

## Step 2: Initial Registration on Form CO-1

- [ ] **Download the registration package** (Forms CO-1, CO-2, and CO-3) from the Illinois AG website.
- [ ] **Complete Form CO-1** - Charitable Organization Registration Statement.
- [ ] **Complete Form CO-2** - Financial Information schedule for the most recent fiscal year. Pro forma figures are acceptable for newly formed organizations.
- [ ] **Compile attachments listed on Form CO-3:**
- [ ] Articles of incorporation (or trust instrument, or constitution)
- [ ] Bylaws
- [ ] IRS Form 1023 application AND IRS determination letter
- [ ] Most recent IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF (if available)
- [ ] Audited financial statements (if required - see Step 4)
- [ ] List of officers, directors, and key employees
- [ ] **Pay $15 initial registration fee** - check payable to the Illinois Charity Bureau Fund.
- [ ] **Mail the package** to: Office of the Attorney General, Charitable Trust Bureau, 100 W. Randolph Street, 11th Floor, Chicago, IL 60601.
- [ ] **Record the assigned CO# (charitable organization number)** for use on all subsequent filings.

## Step 3: Annual Filing on Form AG990-IL

- [ ] **AG990-IL due date:** Six months after fiscal year end. Calendar-year filers: June 30.
- [ ] **Filing fee:** $15.
- [ ] **Required attachments:**
- [ ] IRS Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-PF as filed with the IRS
- [ ] Audited financial statements (if revenue triggers apply - see Step 4)
- [ ] Schedule of contributions if a paid solicitor was used
- [ ] **Extension:** Illinois automatically grants an extension of time to file AG990-IL when the IRS extension for Form 990 is filed. Submit a copy of the IRS extension to the Charitable Trust Bureau.
- [ ] **Late fee:** $100 per missed AG990-IL. The Bureau may also revoke registration for repeated late filings.

## Step 4: Audit Requirement

Audited financial statements are required when either condition applies:

- [ ] **Annual gross contributions exceed $300,000.**
- [ ] **The organization used a professional fundraiser** during the fiscal year - even if total revenue is below $300,000.

Audit details:

- [ ] **Engage independent CPA firm** at least 90 days before AG990-IL due date.
- [ ] **Audit must comply with GAAP** and be conducted in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards.
- [ ] **Submit the auditor's report and financial statements** with the AG990-IL.
- [ ] **Organizations with contributions $25,000-$300,000** may submit reviewed (rather than audited) financials in some cases - confirm current Bureau guidance before relying on this.

## Step 5: Professional Fundraiser Registration

If the organization contracts with a paid professional fund raiser or professional solicitor:

- [ ] **Verify the fundraiser is registered** with the Illinois Charitable Trust Bureau.
- [ ] **File a copy of the contract** with the Bureau within 10 days of execution.
- [ ] **The fundraiser must post a $10,000 bond** with the Bureau.
- [ ] **All solicitation materials** must be approved or filed as required.
- [ ] **Maintain records** of contributions received through the fundraiser, percentage retained, and any complaints.

## Step 6: Solicitation Disclosures

Solicitations in Illinois must include certain disclosures under the Solicitation for Charity Act:

- [ ] **Charity name as registered.**
- [ ] **A clear statement of the charitable purpose** to which contributions will be applied.
- [ ] **For solicitations made by paid solicitors:** disclosure that the caller is a paid solicitor and the percentage of contributions retained by the soliciting organization.
- [ ] **A statement that financial information** is available from the Attorney General's Charitable Trust Bureau.

## Step 7: Records and Public Inspection

- [ ] **Maintain books of account** for each charitable program.
- [ ] **Retain Charitable Trust Bureau filings** indefinitely while the organization exists.
- [ ] **IRS Form 1023 and three most recent Form 990s** must be available for public inspection at the principal office.
- [ ] **Track the public Charitable Database** at illinoisattorneygeneral.gov - donors and grantmakers check this before giving.

## Step 8: Closeout

When an Illinois charity dissolves or stops soliciting:

- [ ] **Charitable Trust Act dissolutions** require AG approval of the asset distribution plan. Submit a Petition for Approval of Dissolution before distributing assets.
- [ ] **File final AG990-IL** marked "Final."
- [ ] **Distribute remaining charitable assets** consistent with the dissolution clause and as approved by the Attorney General.
- [ ] **File Articles of Dissolution** with the Illinois Secretary of State only after AG approval.
- [ ] **Retain records** for at least seven years after dissolution.

## Common Failure Points

- **Missing the AG990-IL deadline.** Six months after fiscal year end is unusual - many filers default to the Form 990 deadline (4.5 months) and miss it.
- **Not registering when using a professional fundraiser at low revenue.** The audit requirement triggers on professional fundraiser use even if revenue is below $300,000.
- **Treating CO# as optional.** Foundation grant applications and grant.gov searches reference Illinois CO# numbers; missing or lapsed registration disqualifies funding.
- **Skipping AG approval at dissolution.** Distributing charitable assets without AG approval can subject directors to personal liability and reversal of distributions.
- **Failing to register out-of-state.** A nonprofit headquartered in another state that sends direct mail to Illinois donors is soliciting in Illinois.

The Charitable Trust Bureau pursues a steady stream of enforcement actions each year, with results published on the AG website. Compliance costs are modest - $15 in registration and annual fees plus the cost of the financial schedule and audit when triggered - and far less than the cost of an enforcement action.
