## How to Use This Checklist

Work through each week's items in order. Most items are verification tasks: confirming that records are complete, reconciled, and in the location where the auditor will look for them. A few items are assembly tasks, pulling records from multiple sources into a single organized file.

The goal of each week is a specific outcome, not just a list of tasks completed. The outcome is stated at the top of each week.

**Who should own this checklist:** The Finance Director or Controller, with input from the grants manager for grant-specific records. Assign ownership before beginning Week 1.

---

## Week 1: Accounting Records and SEFA

**Week 1 goal:** The accounting records are reconciled, the SEFA draft is ready, and the grant expenditure totals in the accounting system match what was reported to funders.

### General Ledger and Fund Accounting

- [ ] Run a full-year grant expenditure report by grant, by budget category
      _Purpose: This is the starting point for all reconciliation work. Everything else checks against this._

- [ ] Reconcile the grant expenditure report to the general ledger. Differences require explanation.
      _Purpose: If the GL and the grant expenditure report do not agree, auditors will find the discrepancy._

- [ ] Verify that expenditures are coded to the correct grant and budget category in the GL. Identify any miscoded transactions and correct them now.
      _Purpose: After-the-fact recoding late in the audit is a red flag. Corrections made before fieldwork are routine._

- [ ] Confirm that indirect cost charges are applied at the correct approved rate (NICRA or applicable de minimis rate per 2 CFR 200.414, verify the current rate with your NICRA or the eCFR before applying) using the correct base.
      _Purpose: Incorrect indirect cost application is one of the most common questioned-cost sources._

- [ ] Identify any budget categories that are overspent or close to the limit. Determine whether prior approval for rebudgeting was obtained; if not, flag for discussion with the auditor.
      _Purpose: Overspent categories without prior approval are a disallowance risk._

### SEFA Preparation

- [ ] Draft the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards for the fiscal year.
      _Purpose: The SEFA is the foundation of the single audit. Start it early._

- [ ] Confirm that every federal award active during the year appears on the SEFA, including sub-awards from state pass-through entities.
      _Purpose: Missing awards on the SEFA affect major program selection and may create scope deficiencies._

- [ ] Verify each SEFA row has the correct Assistance Listing (CFDA) number.
      _Purpose: Incorrect CFDA numbers cause misclassification of programs._

- [ ] Reconcile each SEFA row total to the corresponding grant expenditure total in the GL.
      _Purpose: SEFA accuracy is tested by reconciling to accounting records. Discrepancies must be resolved._

## Week 2: Grant Files and Document Completeness

**Week 2 goal:** The grant file for each major program is complete. Every required document is in the correct location and retrievable within five minutes.

### For Each Major Program in Audit Scope

Run this section for each grant that is expected to be selected as a major program. If you do not know the major program selection, complete this section for all active federal grants.

- [ ] Award document (signed grant agreement or Notice of Award, all pages)
- [ ] All amendments and modifications, in date order
- [ ] Approved budget as of the most recent amendment
- [ ] All SF-425 Federal Financial Reports filed during the fiscal year
- [ ] All progress reports filed during the fiscal year
- [ ] Final financial and narrative reports (if the grant closed during the year)
- [ ] Any prior approval correspondence (rebudgeting requests, no-cost extensions, key personnel changes)
- [ ] Procurement records for major purchases: competitive solicitation documentation, selection rationale, signed contracts
- [ ] Subrecipient agreement (if funds were passed to a sub-awardee), monitoring records, and sub-awardee single audit status from fac.gov

### Restricted Fund Documentation

- [ ] Restricted fund balance summary as of the end of the fiscal year, by fund
- [ ] Restriction terms for each fund (from the award letter or gift agreement)
- [ ] Release documentation for any restriction released during the year
- [ ] Reconciliation of restricted fund balances to the general ledger

### Setting Up Auditor Access

- [ ] If you are using GrantPipe's Auditor & Funder Portal: create the portal session for the auditor, scope it to the major programs under review, set the expiry date to cover fieldwork plus a buffer, and confirm the document categories are configured correctly.
      _Purpose: Having the portal ready before fieldwork begins means the auditor can start reviewing documents immediately without a setup delay._

## Week 3: Personnel and Time Documentation

**Week 3 goal:** T&E documentation for all employees with salary charged to a federal grant is complete, organized, and cross-referenced to payroll records.

### Time-and-Effort Documentation

Time-and-effort documentation failures produce the most common single audit finding. Every employee whose salary is charged to a federal grant needs documentation for every period of that charge.

- [ ] Identify every employee whose salary was charged to a federal grant during the fiscal year.
      _Purpose: The list is the scope for everything else in this section._

- [ ] For each employee: confirm that T&E records (timesheets, activity reports, or semi-annual certifications) exist for the full period of the salary charge.
      _Purpose: Missing T&E records for any period mean those salary costs can be questioned._

- [ ] Verify that T&E records are contemporaneous: prepared at or near the time of the work, not retroactively.
      _Purpose: Auditors examine record dates. Year-end batch certifications covering months of activity are flagged._

- [ ] For employees with split funding across multiple grants or programs: verify that monthly activity reports account for 100% of compensated time, not just grant-funded time.
      _Purpose: Partial records fail the standard. All compensated time must be accounted for._

- [ ] For employees working solely on one federal program: verify that semi-annual certifications exist, signed by the employee or supervisor.
      _Purpose: The semi-annual certification is the minimum documentation standard under 2 CFR 200.430(i)(1)(i)._

- [ ] Reconcile total payroll charges to the grant against what the T&E records support. Differences require explanation.
      _Purpose: Discrepancies between T&E records and actual payroll charges indicate the records are not controlling the allocation._

### Payroll Supporting Records

- [ ] Obtain payroll records showing the salary amounts charged to each grant during the fiscal year.
- [ ] Confirm fringe benefit rates used in grant charges are documented and consistent.
- [ ] Compile key personnel offer letters or employment agreements (for any key personnel named in the award).

## Week 4: Final Verification and Pre-Fieldwork Readiness

**Week 4 goal:** The complete audit file is ready. Every document is in its expected location, key staff have been briefed, and the auditor has (or can receive) the access they need to start efficiently.

### Final File Verification

- [ ] Run through the [Auditor Evidence Checklist](/free/auditor-evidence-checklist) for each major program. Confirm each item is either in the file or has a documented explanation for why it is not.

- [ ] Verify that the SEFA draft has been reviewed by the Finance Director or CFO and is ready to share with the auditor for their planning procedures.

- [ ] Confirm the prior year's audit findings (if any) have documented corrective action. The current year's auditors will review the prior year's Summary Schedule of Prior Audit Findings.

- [ ] Confirm that any federal awards expended during the year have been cross-checked against fac.gov for subrecipient single audit status (if you passed federal funds to sub-awardees).

### Briefing Key Staff

- [ ] Brief the Finance Director/Controller: the SEFA is ready, GL is reconciled, and any known issues are documented.
- [ ] Brief the Grants Manager: grant files are complete, T&E records are organized, and the auditor access portal is configured.
- [ ] Brief the Executive Director: the audit preparation is complete and any material issues (overspent categories, missing T&E records, prior findings still in progress) have been escalated.

### Auditor Access Confirmation

- [ ] If using the GrantPipe Auditor & Funder Portal: confirm the session is active, the access scope is correct, and the auditor has received the portal link.
- [ ] If sharing documents by other means: confirm the complete document package is organized, labeled, and ready to deliver to the auditor on the first day of fieldwork.
- [ ] Identify the staff member who will be the primary point of contact during fieldwork and confirm their availability for the fieldwork period.

---

## If You Find a Gap in Week 3 or 4

A gap discovered in the final two weeks is more recoverable than a gap discovered during fieldwork, but time is short.

**Document exists but is not in the file:** Locate it and upload it immediately. If it is in a file cabinet, scan it.

**Document was never created (T&E records, for example):** For historical periods, you cannot create contemporaneous records retroactively. Escalate to the Executive Director. Prepare a disclosure for the auditor rather than waiting for the auditor to find the gap.

**Document cannot be located:** Treat this the same as never created. The audit finding from a missing document disclosed proactively is typically less severe than a finding the auditor surfaces during fieldwork.
