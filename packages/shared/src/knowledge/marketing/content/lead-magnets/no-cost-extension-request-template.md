### Why the work is not complete

Describe the specific circumstances that contributed to the need for additional time. Strong justifications cite external factors or genuinely exceptional circumstances:

**Examples of strong justifications:**

- Key personnel vacancy: "[Position title] was vacant from [Month] to [Month] due to [brief description of hiring challenge]. The position was filled on [date]. The delayed start for this role has compressed the delivery timeline for [specific activities]."
- External disruption: "Program delivery at our primary site was suspended from [Month] to [Month] due to [facility renovation / public health restriction / natural disaster / other]. Activities have resumed and are progressing, but the gap cannot be fully recovered within the current period."
- Required regulatory approval: "Implementation of [specific program element] required [regulatory approval / certification / license] that was not received until [date], [X] months later than anticipated. This delayed the start of [specific activities] by [X] months."

**Weak justifications to avoid:**

- "We need more time to complete the project activities" (vague, no external factor)
- "Our team was busy with other organizational priorities" (internal, not exceptional)
- "We underestimated the complexity of the work" (planning failure, not credible)

## Submission Checklist by Agency Type

### NIH (National Institutes of Health)

- [ ] Log into eRA Commons
- [ ] Navigate to the Status module for the specific grant
- [ ] Select "No-Cost Extension" request option
- [ ] Complete all required fields and attach the justification narrative
- [ ] Submit through eRA Commons (no separate email needed for most NIH awards with expanded authorities)
- [ ] Save the eRA Commons submission confirmation number

### NSF (National Science Foundation)

- [ ] Log into Research.gov
- [ ] Navigate to Award and Reporting Functions
- [ ] Initiate No-Cost Extension request
- [ ] Enter new end date and justification
- [ ] Submit; save confirmation
- [ ] Note: NSF typically allows one NCE up to 12 months without prior Grants Officer approval under expanded authorities

### HHS (HRSA, ACF, CDC, SAMHSA, and other HHS agencies)

- [ ] Contact program officer to confirm the submission process for this specific award
- [ ] Most HHS agencies accept NCE requests through Grants.gov or agency-specific portals; some accept by email
- [ ] Submit written request including this template's Sections 1-5
- [ ] CC the grants management specialist in addition to the program officer

### DOL (Department of Labor)

- [ ] Contact assigned grants officer to confirm submission process
- [ ] Prepare written request letter using this template's content
- [ ] Submit through ETA (Employment and Training Administration) grants management system or as directed

### DOEd (Department of Education)

- [ ] Contact program officer for submission instructions
- [ ] Most ED awards processed through G5 system
- [ ] Submit written request with all sections completed

### HUD (Department of Housing and Urban Development)

- [ ] Contact program officer and grants officer
- [ ] HUD NCE requests typically submitted through IDIS or by direct written request to the program office
- [ ] Allow additional processing time - HUD reviews can take 30+ days

### Private Foundations

- [ ] Contact program officer by email or phone before submitting a formal request
- [ ] Most foundations prefer a brief email or letter - formal templates are less common
- [ ] Include: current project status, reason for extension request, proposed new end date, brief description of remaining work
- [ ] Ask whether the funder needs a formal amendment to the grant agreement or whether email approval is sufficient

## What Gets Approved vs. Denied

### Typically approved:

- Staffing delays due to competitive hiring conditions with evidence the position is now filled
- External disruptions (facility unavailability, policy changes, community events) with documentation
- Regulatory approval delays beyond the organization's control
- Remaining funds clearly tied to remaining deliverables
- Request submitted 30+ days before period end

### Typically denied:

- Vague justification without specific external factors
- Insufficient funds remaining to support the extension meaningfully
- Proposed extension period shorter than the remaining work requires
- Request submitted fewer than 10 days before period end
- Pattern of late implementation across multiple grants from the same organization
- Scope change disguised as a timeline request
- Second NCE request on an award that already received one, without exceptional circumstances
