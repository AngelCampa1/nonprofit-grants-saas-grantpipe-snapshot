## The Sequence That Most New Organizations Get Wrong

The 501(c)(3) application process has a mandatory sequence. You cannot file with the IRS before you incorporate with your state. You cannot operate as a tax-exempt organization before the IRS grants your determination letter. Getting the sequence wrong creates problems that are time-consuming to fix - and some errors (like missing required language in your articles of incorporation) require amending state documents before the IRS will accept your application.

This checklist walks through the sequence in the correct order. Do not skip ahead.

## Phase 1: State Incorporation (Must Complete Before IRS Filing)

The 501(c)(3) determination is a federal tax exemption. Before you can apply for it, your organization must exist as a legal entity under state law. For most nonprofits, that means incorporating as a nonprofit corporation in the state where you'll primarily operate.

### Pre-Incorporation Decisions

- [ ] **State of incorporation selected.** Incorporate in the state where you'll primarily operate. There are rarely tax advantages to incorporating in Delaware or other states for nonprofits - and operating in a state where you're not incorporated creates additional registration requirements.
- [ ] **Organization name reserved.** Check your state's business name database. Most states allow you to reserve a name for a fee before filing.
- [ ] **Registered agent identified.** Every incorporated entity must have a registered agent in the state of incorporation - a person or service that accepts legal documents on behalf of the organization. This must be a physical address (not a PO box).

### Articles of Incorporation

The articles of incorporation are the founding document filed with the state. The IRS reviews them carefully. These errors disqualify applications:

**Required IRS language - the two provisions you must include:**

**1. Exempt Purpose Clause:** The articles must limit your organization's purposes to one or more of the exempt purposes in IRC 501(c)(3): charitable, religious, educational, scientific, literary, testing for public safety, fostering national or international amateur sports competition, or preventing cruelty to children or animals.

Required language (exact wording or equivalent):
_"This corporation is organized exclusively for charitable [and/or educational, religious, scientific] purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code."_

**2. Dissolution Clause:** The articles must specify that upon dissolution, the organization's assets will be distributed to another 501(c)(3) organization or to a governmental unit. Assets cannot go to members, founders, or directors.

Required language (exact wording or equivalent):
_"Upon the dissolution of this corporation, its assets remaining after payment of, or provision for payment of, all debts and liabilities of the corporation shall be distributed to a nonprofit fund, foundation, or corporation which is organized and operated exclusively for charitable [and/or educational, religious, scientific] purposes and which has established its tax-exempt status under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code."_

Many state nonprofit corporation acts have model articles that include this language automatically. Check whether your state's model includes it before assuming it does.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Exempt purpose clause included in articles
- [ ] Dissolution clause included in articles
- [ ] No provision allowing private inurement (profits flowing to insiders)
- [ ] Articles filed with state and filing fee paid
- [ ] State-issued certificate of incorporation received

### Initial Organizational Documents

- [ ] **Bylaws drafted and adopted.** Bylaws govern how the organization operates. They must address: purpose, membership (or lack thereof), board composition and powers, officer positions, meeting requirements, quorum, voting rules, and amendment procedures. The IRS does not require bylaws to be filed with the application, but will request them if the application is questioned.
- [ ] **Conflict of Interest Policy adopted.** The IRS expects 501(c)(3) organizations to have a conflict of interest policy. Form 1023 asks specifically whether you have one. While not legally required, not having one is a red flag. The IRS provides a sample policy in the Form 1023 instructions.
- [ ] **Initial board meeting held.** The first board meeting (often called the organizational meeting) formally adopts the bylaws, elects officers, adopts the conflict of interest policy, and establishes bank accounts. Document it with written minutes.
- [ ] **Employer Identification Number (EIN) obtained.** Apply online at IRS.gov. Takes minutes. Required before filing Form 1023 or opening a bank account.

## Phase 2: The Form 1023 vs. 1023-EZ Decision

The IRS offers two application forms. Using the wrong one can result in an automatic rejection or approval that doesn't match your actual activities.

### Form 1023-EZ Eligibility Criteria

Form 1023-EZ is the streamlined application. It's shorter, has a lower filing fee ($275), and typically processes faster. But many organizations are not eligible to use it.

**You may use Form 1023-EZ if ALL of the following are true:**

- [ ] Projected annual gross receipts are $50,000 or less in each of the next 3 years
- [ ] Current total assets are $250,000 or less
- [ ] You are not a church or church-controlled organization
- [ ] You are not a school, hospital, or medical research organization
- [ ] You are not a supporting organization under IRC 509(a)(3)
- [ ] You have not applied for and been denied 501(c)(3) status by the IRS

**Note:** If you're planning significant fundraising or grant activity in your first few years, you may quickly grow past the $50,000 gross receipts threshold. If that's likely, consider whether Form 1023-EZ accurately represents your organization's anticipated size.

### Form 1023 Full Application

If you don't meet 1023-EZ criteria, or if your activities are complex, use Form 1023.

**Form 1023 requires:**

- [ ] Part 1: Identification of Applicant
- [ ] Part 2: Organizational Structure - articles and bylaws
- [ ] Part 3: Required Provisions in Organizing Document - confirm exempt purpose and dissolution clauses
- [ ] Part 4: Narrative Description of Activities - explain what your organization does, how it does it, who benefits, and how activities further your exempt purpose
- [ ] Part 5: Compensation and Other Financial Arrangements - disclose compensation for officers, directors, trustees, key employees
- [ ] Part 6: Members and Other Individuals and Organizations - relationships with other organizations
- [ ] Part 7: History of Organization - if operating before filing
- [ ] Part 8: Specific Activities - answer questions about activities that require additional scrutiny (fundraising, gaming, foreign activities, housing, lobbying, etc.)
- [ ] Part 9: Financial Data - 3 years of financial information (actual or projected)
- [ ] Part 10: Public Charity Status - declare your basis for public charity status (most organizations: IRC 509(a)(1) or (a)(2))

**Filing fee:** $600 for Form 1023 full application (as of 2026; verify current fee at IRS.gov before filing)

## Phase 3: IRS Review and Timeline

**Timeline expectations:**

- Form 1023-EZ: Typically 2-4 months from submission
- Form 1023 full application: Typically 6-12 months, sometimes longer for complex organizations

The IRS processes applications in the order received, with some exceptions for organizations requesting expedited processing (available only in limited circumstances).

**Expedited processing criteria:** The IRS will expedite an application if delay would cause an immediate adverse impact on the organization - for example, a pending grant that requires proof of 501(c)(3) status. Expedited processing is not guaranteed; you must submit a written request with documentation.

**If the IRS sends a letter requesting additional information:**

- Respond completely and within the deadline stated in the letter
- Incomplete responses restart the review clock
- Keep copies of all correspondence

**Retroactivity:** If your application is approved, the determination letter will state an effective date. For new organizations, this is usually the date of incorporation or the date the organization began operating - not the date of the IRS approval. This means donations received during the review period are retroactively deductible once approval is granted.

## Phase 4: State Charity Registration

Receiving an IRS determination letter does not complete your compliance obligations. Most states require charitable organizations to register before soliciting donations from residents - even online donations.

State requirements vary significantly:

- Some states (e.g., California, New York, Massachusetts) have detailed registration requirements with annual reporting
- A few states have no registration requirement
- Most states are in between

**Multi-state registration:** If you solicit donations nationally (including via your website), you may need to register in multiple states. The Unified Registration Statement (URS) is accepted by most states and simplifies multi-state registration.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Research your home state's charity registration requirements
- [ ] Complete initial charity registration in home state before soliciting donations
- [ ] Calendar annual renewal deadlines (usually tied to fiscal year end)
- [ ] Identify other states where you solicit and determine registration requirements

## Phase 5: Post-Approval Requirements (What Starts the Moment You Receive Your Determination Letter)

### Form 990 Filing

All 501(c)(3) organizations must file an annual information return with the IRS. The form depends on your gross receipts:

| Gross Receipts                             | Form                    | Due Date                                    |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Under $50,000                              | Form 990-N (e-Postcard) | 15th day of 5th month after fiscal year end |
| $50,000-$200,000 AND assets under $500,000 | Form 990-EZ or 990      | Same                                        |
| $200,000+ OR assets $500,000+              | Form 990                | Same                                        |
| Exempt private foundations                 | Form 990-PF             | Same                                        |

**Critical:** Missing the Form 990 deadline three consecutive years results in automatic revocation of your tax-exempt status. Set calendar reminders immediately.

**Extension:** You may request a 6-month extension using Form 8868. The extension must be filed before the original due date.

### Public Disclosure Requirements

501(c)(3) organizations must make the following available for public inspection:

- [ ] Form 990 (and any 990-T) for the past 3 years
- [ ] Form 1023 application for exemption (and all attachments)
- [ ] IRS determination letter

These must be available for public inspection at your principal office during business hours, and copies must be provided upon request (in person within the same business day; by mail within 30 days). Many organizations satisfy this by posting documents on their website.

### Gift Acknowledgment Requirements

From the date your determination letter is effective, all gifts of $250 or more require a written contemporaneous acknowledgment from your organization. See the Donor Thank-You Letter Template Pack for specific requirements.

### Lobbying Restrictions

501(c)(3) organizations may engage in limited lobbying but may not make political campaign expenditures. "Substantial" lobbying activity can jeopardize exempt status. If lobbying is part of your advocacy strategy, consult with an attorney about the applicable limits.

### Checklist Summary

- [ ] IRS determination letter received and stored securely
- [ ] Form 990 due dates calendared
- [ ] Public disclosure procedures implemented
- [ ] State charity registration completed (if required)
- [ ] Donor acknowledgment procedures in place
- [ ] Board educated on lobbying restrictions
- [ ] Board conflict of interest policy in place and signed by all board members
- [ ] Whistleblower policy adopted (required disclosure on Form 990)
- [ ] Document retention policy adopted (required disclosure on Form 990)
