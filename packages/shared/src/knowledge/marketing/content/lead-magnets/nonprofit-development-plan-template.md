## Why Development Plans Fail (and What to Do Instead)

Most nonprofit development plans fail for one of two reasons: they're too ambitious to execute with available staff capacity, or they're disconnected from the data that should drive revenue forecasting.

An ambitious plan that assumes a 40% increase in individual giving when last year's retention rate was 45% isn't a plan - it's a hope document. And a plan that sets goals without knowing the current baseline (what is the average gift by segment? what is the renewal rate for lapsed donors? how many grants are currently in the pipeline?) will produce targets that bear no relationship to what is actually achievable.

A functioning development plan starts with data. Before setting a single revenue goal, you need to know:

- What was your total revenue last year, by source?
- What is your current donor retention rate, overall and by giving tier?
- How many donors upgraded their giving last year versus how many downgraded or lapsed?
- What is the status of your current grant pipeline?
- How much staff time is available for development work?

The template in this guide is structured around those inputs. Fill in the baseline sections first, then set goals. The goals should be aggressive but defensible - meaning you can explain, line by line, how you expect to achieve them.

**Notes instructions:** For each source where you're projecting growth, document the assumption behind the growth. "Foundation grants - new funders: +$50,000" requires an explanation: "Based on two active applications to [Funder A] ($25K) and [Funder B] ($30K) with 80% likelihood each." If you can't document the assumption, the number is aspirational, not a plan.

| Major donors ($1,000+) | | __ | ___% | __ | __ |
| Lapsed (1-year) | Gave 2 years ago, not last year | __ | ___% reactivated | __ | __ |

**Template: Average Gift by Segment**

| Segment | Last Year Average Gift | This Year Goal Average Gift | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-time donors | $______ | $______ | ____% |
| Renewed donors | $______ | $______ | ____% |
| Major donors | $______ | $______ | ____% |
| Recurring donors (monthly average) | $______ | $______ | ____% |

**Key calculation:** Projected donor revenue = (Retained donors - average gift) + (New donors - average first gift) + (Reactivated lapsed - average reactivated gift)

**Notes instructions:** Your donor retention rate is the most important input to the development plan. If your overall retention rate is below 50%, your plan needs to prioritize retention-focused activities over acquisition. It costs more to acquire a new donor than to retain an existing one.

## Section 3: Grant Pipeline

**Template: Active and Pending Grant Pipeline**

| Grant Name | Funder | Amount Requested | Stage | Application Date | Decision Date | Likelihood | Renewal? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Grant A] | [Funder] | $______ | [Research / Draft / Submitted / Under Review] | [Date] | [Date] | ____% | Y/N | |
| [Grant B] | [Funder] | $______ | | [Date] | [Date] | ____% | Y/N | |
| [Grant C] | [Funder] | $______ | | [Date] | [Date] | ____% | Y/N | |

**Template: Grant Renewals Due This Year**

| Grant Name | Funder | Current Award | Renewal Amount | Renewal Application Due | Last Year Decision Date | Renewal Likelihood |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Grant A] | [Funder] | $______ | $______ | [Date] | [Date] | ____% |

**Weighted pipeline total:** Multiply each grant amount by its likelihood percentage and sum. This is your probability-weighted grant projection - a more accurate forecasting input than the full pipeline total.

**Notes instructions:** New funder cultivation should be tracked separately from renewal applications. Renewals are typically higher probability than new applications. If more than 50% of your projected grant revenue comes from new funders you haven't yet applied to, your plan has a revenue gap.

## Section 5: Staff Responsibility Matrix

**Template: Staff Responsibility Matrix**

| Activity | Owner | Support | Review / Approval | Deadline / Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual donor acknowledgments | [Staff] | [Staff] | [Director] | Within 48 hours of gift |
| Major donor calls | [ED / Dev. Director] | [Staff] | - | Monthly |
| Grant applications | [Grants Manager / Writer] | [Program Staff] | [ED] | Per pipeline calendar |
| Lapsed donor outreach | [Staff] | - | [Director] | Quarterly |
| Monthly appeal emails | [Staff] | - | [Director] | Per campaign calendar |
| Recurring gift processing issues | [Staff] | - | - | Weekly |
| Board solicitation coordination | [ED] | [Dev. Director] | - | Annual |
| Pipeline reporting to board | [Dev. Director] | [Staff] | - | Monthly |

**Notes instructions:** Development work requires significant staff time for tasks that aren't visible on activity calendars: data entry and cleanup, gift processing, thank-you letter personalization, funder relationship maintenance, event logistics. Build realistic time estimates for these tasks before committing to campaign timelines.

## Section 7: Year-End Review Framework

At the end of the plan year, complete this review before starting the next year's plan.

**Revenue performance:**
- Total revenue vs. goal: $______ actual vs. $______ goal (___%)
- Which sources exceeded expectations? Why?
- Which sources fell short? Why?

**Donor metrics:**
- Overall retention rate: ___% (vs. goal: ___%)
- Donor count change: +/- ___
- Average gift change: +/- $______

**Grant performance:**
- Applications submitted: __ / __ planned
- Grants awarded: __ (___% of applications)
- Award amount vs. projected: $______ actual vs. $______ projected

**What the plan got wrong:**
Be specific. Which assumptions were wrong? Did a major donor lapse unexpectedly? Did a grant renewal fail? Did an event underperform? The honest post-mortem is the most important input to next year's plan.

**What to change next year:**
Based on the review, identify 3-5 specific changes to strategy, staffing, or execution. Each change should be tied to a performance gap identified in the review.
