## Why Federal Applications Fail on Logistics

Federal grant applications fail more often on logistics than substance. Expired SAM.gov registration. The wrong person designated as Authorized Organization Representative (AOR). A required attachment missing. The budget narrative not matching the SF-424A. Submission attempted at 11:55 PM on the deadline only to discover Grants.gov is rejecting the file.

These are preventable failures. They happen because federal grant applications have many small components, each with its own requirements and timing. This checklist walks through every step, with recommended timing relative to the deadline.

For broader proposal guidance, see the [grant proposal writing guide](/resources/guides/grant-proposal-writing-guide). For post-award compliance, see the [Uniform Guidance practical guide](/resources/guides/uniform-guidance-2-cfr-200-practical-guide).

## Stage 1: Federal Registrations (Complete 60+ Days Before First Application)

You cannot apply without active federal registrations. Complete these once; renew annually.

### SAM.gov Entity Registration

System for Award Management. Required for any organization receiving federal funds.

- Navigate to sam.gov, create user account, then begin entity registration.
- Required information: legal entity name, physical address, EIN, banking information, and authorizing official.
- Initial registration takes 10 to 14 business days.
- Renew annually. Expired registrations make pending applications ineligible.
- Confirm Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) is assigned. UEI replaced the old DUNS number in 2022.

### Grants.gov Account and AOR Designation

- Each individual who will work on applications creates a Grants.gov account.
- The organization designates one or more Authorized Organization Representatives (AORs). The AOR is the only person who can submit applications.
- AOR designation requires the SAM.gov registration to be active.
- AOR approval typically takes 1 to 2 business days.

### Login.gov

Many federal agencies (and Grants.gov) use Login.gov for authentication. Set up Login.gov accounts with multi-factor authentication for everyone touching applications.

### Other registrations (if applicable)

- eRA Commons (HHS, including HRSA, NIH, SAMHSA): required separately from Grants.gov.
- Agency-specific portals (ED.gov, USDA Rural Development): some agencies require additional accounts.

## Stage 2: NOFO Analysis (Complete 45+ Days Before Deadline)

The Notice of Funding Opportunity is the controlling document. Read it three times.

### First read: eligibility and fit

- Are you eligible? Check organization type, geographic restrictions, prior funding requirements.
- Is your program eligible? Check allowable activities and target populations.
- Is the funding meaningful? Check minimum and maximum award size and match requirements.

If eligibility or fit is unclear, contact the program officer listed in the NOFO. Ask before investing time.

### Second read: scoring criteria

Federal NOFOs include explicit scoring criteria. Reviewers score each criterion on a defined point scale. Your narrative must address each criterion in the order presented, with weight proportional to the points available.

Build a scoring matrix:

- Criterion name
- Points available
- Required content
- Where in your narrative you will address it

This matrix becomes the outline for your narrative.

### Third read: requirements and attachments

List every required element:

- Narrative sections and page limits
- Forms (SF-424, SF-424A, SF-424B, SF-LLL, others)
- Budget format
- Attachments (logic model, evaluation plan, organizational documents, letters of support)
- Format specifications (font, margins, file format)
- Submission method

Build an attachment tracker with owner and due date for each.

## Stage 3: Drafting (30-45 Days Before Deadline)

### Narrative

Federal narratives typically run 15 to 30 pages depending on the NOFO. Draft in the order the NOFO presents the scoring criteria, not in the order that feels natural.

Common required sections:

- Project Abstract (1 page)
- Statement of Need
- Project Approach and Methodology
- Logic Model and Theory of Change
- Evaluation Plan
- Organizational Capacity
- Sustainability Plan
- Budget Narrative

For each section, draft against the scoring criteria. Include section headings that mirror the NOFO language so reviewers can find what they are scoring.

### Budget

Federal budgets follow specific structures, usually SF-424A for non-construction grants. Categories:

- Personnel (salary and benefits)
- Travel
- Equipment ($10,000+ per unit, or a lower organizational capitalization threshold)
- Supplies
- Contractual
- Construction (if applicable)
- Other
- Total Direct Costs
- Indirect Costs
- Total

Each line in the budget needs a corresponding paragraph in the budget narrative explaining the calculation. Reviewers will compare narrative and budget for consistency.

### Indirect costs

Three options:

1. **Negotiated indirect cost rate.** If you have a federally negotiated rate, use it.
2. **De minimis rate.** Under 2 CFR 200.414(f), organizations without a negotiated rate can charge 10 percent of modified total direct costs (MTDC).
3. **Cost allocation plan.** Some funders allow direct allocation of shared costs.

Document your choice in the budget narrative.

### Match requirements

If the NOFO requires match (cash or in-kind), document the source for every dollar. Match documentation is reviewed during application review and audited post-award. Inflated or unsubstantiated match is a common audit finding.

## Stage 4: Attachments (Compile 21 Days Before Deadline)

Build the attachment package early. Common federal attachments:

- IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter
- Most recent audited financial statements
- Most recent IRS Form 990
- Board roster with affiliations
- Organizational chart
- Logic model
- Evaluation plan (sometimes part of narrative)
- Letters of support (3 to 10 typically)
- Resumes/CVs of key personnel
- Job descriptions for grant-funded positions
- Memoranda of understanding with partners
- Indirect cost rate agreement (if applicable)
- Single Audit Act certification (if subject)

### Letters of support

Request letters at least 30 days before the deadline. Provide a template with the specific points you need addressed. Letters that all sound the same indicate the applicant wrote them, which reviewers notice.

### Format requirements

- Most NOFOs require PDF format with specific font and margin specifications (typically 12-point Times New Roman, 1-inch margins).
- Some agencies require attachments be combined into a single PDF; others require separate files with specific naming conventions.
- File size limits apply. Compress large PDFs.

## Stage 5: Internal Review (10-14 Days Before Deadline)

Build in time for at least two rounds of internal review.

### First review: substance

- Does the narrative address each scoring criterion?
- Is the logic model coherent (do activities produce the claimed outcomes)?
- Does the budget match the narrative?
- Are claims backed by data?

### Second review: editorial and formatting

- Page limits respected?
- Font, margins, spacing consistent?
- Section headings match the NOFO?
- No typos or grammatical errors?
- All required attachments present?

### External review (optional but recommended)

A grant writer or peer outside your organization can provide an objective read. Schedule the external review 7 days before the deadline so feedback can be incorporated.

## Stage 6: Submission (3 Days Before Deadline)

Plan to submit at least 24 hours before the deadline. Federal submission systems experience load on deadline day, and late submissions are not accepted.

### Pre-submission checks

- SAM.gov registration is active.
- AOR is logged in to Grants.gov.
- All forms are completed.
- All attachments are uploaded.
- File names match the NOFO requirements.
- Budget totals match the form.

### Submit

The AOR submits the package. Grants.gov returns:

1. A tracking number (immediate)
2. A validation result (within 24-48 hours)
3. An agency receipt confirmation (varies)

Validation failures must be corrected and resubmitted before the deadline. Submitting at 11:50 PM on the deadline does not give you time to correct failures.

### Save copies

Save the submitted PDF, all forms, and the Grants.gov confirmation. Retain for the duration of the application review and the grant period if awarded.

## Stage 7: Post-Submission

### Track status

Most agencies provide status updates through their portals. Some send email notifications. Track:

- Receipt confirmed
- Eligibility review passed
- Technical review scheduled or complete
- Award decision

### Notification

Notification timing varies by agency. Typical ranges:

- Quick-turn programs: 60 to 90 days
- Standard programs: 90 to 180 days
- Multi-stage programs (LOI plus full application): 12+ months

### If awarded

You will receive a Notice of Award (NOA). The NOA defines the period of performance, award amount, and any special conditions. Begin post-award compliance setup immediately. See [federal grant reporting requirements](/resources/guides/federal-grant-reporting-requirements).

### If declined

Request reviewer comments. Most federal agencies provide them. Use the comments to strengthen future applications. Most successful federal grant programs have a track record of declined applications that were resubmitted improved.

## Common Federal Application Failures

- **Expired SAM.gov registration discovered the day of submission.** Renew 60+ days before any deadline.
- **Wrong person designated as AOR.** Confirm 30 days before deadline.
- **Narrative does not match scoring criteria order.** Reviewers cannot find what they are scoring; lose points before substance is evaluated.
- **Budget narrative inconsistent with budget form.** Looks careless; reviewers question rigor.
- **Missing letter of support listed in NOFO.** Automatic point deduction or disqualification.
- **Submission attempted in the last hour.** System errors uncorrectable before deadline.
- **PDF over file size limit.** Submission rejected.

The pattern across all of these is the same: federal applications have many small parts, each with rules. Treat the NOFO as a contract you are responding to and the checklist as your project plan. Submit early, keep records, and improve every cycle.
