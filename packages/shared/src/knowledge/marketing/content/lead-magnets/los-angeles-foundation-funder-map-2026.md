## Why This Map Exists

LA County has a crowded nonprofit market and a concentrated foundation landscape. The priority areas overlap, and many rejection letters trace back to a mismatch between an organization's framing and what a foundation has actually funded - not to the strength of the program.

This map pulls the 990-PF data and pairs it with what each foundation says it cares about, so you can spot the gap before you commit to an LOI that was never going to convert.

## What's Inside

- A ranked LA-area funder table your team fills from current 990-PF and funder-page research
- Median grant size, range, and typical grant count per cycle
- The actual program areas funded over the last three years (not the website list)
- LOI windows, full proposal calendars, and realistic time-to-decision
- Which funders accept unsolicited LOIs and which only fund prior grantees
- Program-officer transitions and what they signal about funding stability

## How to Use This Map

Filter the list to funders whose 24-month grant history overlaps your program area, budget tier, and geographic focus. Those are your realistic prospects. Everyone else is noise that pulls staff time away from the funders that might actually say yes.

If your typical ask sits in the $50K-$150K range, the right-sized-funder filter usually halves your prospect list and meaningfully raises hit rate on the remainder.

## Common Failure Modes This Map Surfaces

The patterns most LA nonprofits miss until the second or third declined LOI:

- Funders whose website lists a program area they have not actually funded in three years. The priorities page is aspirational; the 990-PF is descriptive.
- Funders whose typical grant size is a fraction of what your program needs. The mismatch wastes their cycle and yours.
- Funders who only fund prior grantees but accept unsolicited LOIs anyway. The LOI gets read; it does not lead anywhere.
- Funders whose 24-month giving has shifted. New trustees, new program officers, and new strategic plans show up first in the 990-PF.

Each entry in the map flags these patterns where they appear, so the prospect list you build from this document is one you can act on.

## What to Do With This Map First

Open it next to your current prospect list. Cross out the funders where the median grant size, geographic focus, or program area no longer matches your work. The remaining names get a 24-month-grant-history pull from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer or Candid before any LOI is drafted. The pull either confirms the match or surfaces a gap that would have killed the LOI anyway.

Most LA organizations report cutting their tracked prospect list by 30-50% after one pass with this map, and reallocating that capacity to deeper relationship work with the funders that remain.
