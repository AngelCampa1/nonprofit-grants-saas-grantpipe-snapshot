## Executive Summary

Seven findings frame the 2026 nonprofit CRM market:

- **48% of nonprofits are considering switching CRMs within 12 months** (Omatic 2025, 600+ respondents), up sharply from prior years. This is not a gradual dissatisfaction trend - it reflects a step change in how organizations feel about their current platforms.
- **Only 33% of nonprofits rate their CRM as effective** (Fifty & Fifty 2025 Nonprofit Peer Report). Two-thirds of organizations are managing their fundraising operations on tools they do not believe are working.
- **The market reached $846 million in 2025**, driven primarily by price increases and platform consolidation rather than new adoption. Most growth is occurring at existing customers.
- **Salesforce's three-year total cost of ownership for a mid-sized nonprofit runs $75,000-$275,000**, depending on implementation complexity, admin support model, and add-ons. The free Power of Us licenses shift costs but do not reduce them.
- **Mid-market purpose-built alternatives have improved meaningfully** in the 2023-2025 period, with Bloomerang, Neon CRM, and DonorPerfect all adding grant management and compliance features that were previously gaps.
- **Market consolidation is accelerating.** Bonterra absorbed multiple platforms (Network for Good, EveryAction, Salsa). Bloomerang acquired Kindful. Blackbaud continues to consolidate its product line. Organizations evaluating platforms that have been recently acquired face meaningful product direction uncertainty.
- **AI feature announcements are widespread but adoption is shallow.** 48% of nonprofits report experimenting with AI tools (NTEN 2025), but most AI features in CRM platforms are limited to suggested email copy and data hygiene suggestions - useful, but not transformative in the 2025-2026 window.

## Market Overview

The term "nonprofit CRM" covers a wide range of software that is not actually comparable. Organizations using it to describe Salesforce NPSP and organizations using it to describe a $99/month donor database are not shopping in the same market, regardless of how both vendors position themselves.

**The four real market segments:**

**Salesforce ecosystem:** Salesforce NPSP (Nonprofit Success Pack), now being transitioned to Nonprofit Cloud, plus the consulting ecosystem that implements and maintains it. This segment is characterized by high flexibility, high configuration cost, and strong penetration at large ($10M+) nonprofits and national organizations. For mid-sized nonprofits, the value proposition depends almost entirely on whether the organization has the technical capacity and budget to configure and maintain the platform.

**Blackbaud ecosystem:** Raiser's Edge NXT, Financial Edge NXT, Blackbaud CRM, and the broader Blackbaud suite. Historically dominant at mid-to-large nonprofits, universities, and healthcare foundations. Known for deep functionality in major gifts, planned giving, and event management. Also known for high prices, complex implementations, and a reputation for pricing that increases faster than the value delivered.

**Mid-market purpose-built alternatives:** Bloomerang, Neon CRM, DonorPerfect, Little Green Light, Givebutter, GrantPipe. Purpose-built for nonprofits with simpler needs than the enterprise tier, typically priced between $3,000 and $20,000 per year, and designed to be implemented without a consulting partner. The meaningful improvements in this segment over 2023-2025 are making it a credible choice for organizations that would have defaulted to Salesforce in prior years.

**Budget tools and basic donor databases:** DonorSnap, Kindful (now Bloomerang), Apricot, and various church/small nonprofit tools. Appropriate for organizations under $500K in budget with simple donor tracking needs. Not a fit for organizations managing multiple grants, restricted funds, or compliance requirements.

## Vendor Profiles

### Salesforce NPSP / Nonprofit Cloud

**Who it's for:** Organizations with $5M+ budgets, dedicated technical staff or a consulting retainer, and complex relationship management needs (major gifts programs, planned giving, multi-entity structures).

**Pricing:** The Power of Us program provides 10 free Sales Cloud licenses, which sounds like a significant benefit until you account for the Nonprofit Success Pack configuration, the consulting partner required to implement it, and the AppExchange apps needed to fill functionality gaps. Total three-year cost for a mid-sized nonprofit ranges from $75,000 to $275,000 depending on implementation scope and ongoing admin model.

**Honest assessment of limitations:** Salesforce NPSP is a general-purpose CRM adapted for nonprofits, not a purpose-built nonprofit platform. This means it can theoretically do anything - but anything requires configuration, and configuration requires a Salesforce admin or consulting partner. The most common complaint from mid-sized nonprofits is that routine changes (a new field, a modified report, a workflow adjustment) require paid consulting time. Blackbaud built its entire customer base on this problem; Salesforce created the same problem for a different customer set.

**The NPSP to Nonprofit Cloud migration situation:**

This is the most important strategic fact about Salesforce for nonprofits evaluating the platform in 2026. NPSP (Nonprofit Success Pack) has been in maintenance mode since Salesforce released Nonprofit Cloud in 2022. Salesforce is not retiring NPSP in the immediate term, but they have been explicit that Nonprofit Cloud is the strategic direction and that NPSP will not receive new feature investment.

Nonprofit Cloud runs on a different underlying data model (the Salesforce Industries architecture, formerly Vlocity) than NPSP. This means that an organization implementing NPSP today is building on a data model that Salesforce has publicly deprioritized, and that migrating to Nonprofit Cloud in the future will require a non-trivial data migration - not a configuration update. Organizations that have invested three to five years of customization into NPSP face this migration as a real future cost.

The practical implication: organizations starting a Salesforce evaluation in 2026 should evaluate Nonprofit Cloud as the current platform rather than NPSP. Nonprofit Cloud is more expensive (licensing starts higher and the consulting ecosystem for it is newer and smaller) but represents the architecture Salesforce will actually invest in going forward. Any NPSP recommendation from a consulting partner without a discussion of this context deserves scrutiny.

### Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT

**Who it's for:** Mid-to-large nonprofits ($5M+ budgets) with established major gifts programs, planned giving portfolios, and finance teams comfortable with Blackbaud Financial Edge NXT. Best fit when the organization is already in the Blackbaud ecosystem and switching costs are prohibitive.

**Pricing:** Raiser's Edge NXT is priced by modules and record volume. Expect $12,000-$30,000 per year for a mid-sized nonprofit, not including Financial Edge NXT (which adds $8,000-$20,000 annually), implementation costs ($15,000-$60,000), and any customization work. Three-year TCO typically runs $80,000-$200,000 for an organization in the $1M-$5M budget range.

**Honest assessment of limitations:** Blackbaud's pricing has a reputation for increasing faster than its feature improvements. The 2022-2025 period saw meaningful annual price increases while major product gaps (modern UX, API-first integrations, grant compliance workflows) remained. Customer satisfaction data from G2 and Capterra consistently shows Blackbaud below the mid-market alternative segment on value-for-money ratings. The platform's depth in major gifts and planned giving is genuine; its weaknesses in grant compliance and modern integration architecture are also genuine.

**The RE7 to NXT migration path:**

Many organizations still running Classic Raiser's Edge (RE7) face a meaningful transition decision. Raiser's Edge NXT is not a version upgrade of RE7 - it is a different product with a different data model and a cloud-based architecture. The migration from RE7 to NXT typically costs $15,000-$60,000 in consulting and migration fees, depending on the extent of RE7 customizations (Crystal Reports, custom databases, API integrations built against the RE7 API).

For organizations with heavy RE7 customizations - custom reports, custom entry screens, or integrations built using the RE7 SDK - the migration is particularly disruptive. Most of those customizations do not migrate and must be rebuilt from scratch in the NXT architecture. Organizations with moderate RE7 usage and clean data fare better, but the migration still requires a dedicated project with a Blackbaud-certified implementation partner.

The strategic question is whether the NXT platform is meaningfully better than RE7 in ways that justify the migration cost. For organizations whose core workflows are major gifts and event management, the answer may be yes. For organizations with significant grant compliance and restricted fund needs, neither RE7 nor NXT addresses the gap well, and the migration cost might be better allocated toward a platform purpose-built for compliance.

### Bloomerang

**Who it's for:** Nonprofits in the $500K-$5M budget range that prioritize donor retention analytics, have a clear major gifts program, and want a purpose-built system they can implement without a consulting partner.

**Pricing:** $199-$799/month depending on record count and features. Roughly $1,200-$6,000 annually. Implementation is typically self-service with optional paid onboarding support ($500-$2,000). Three-year TCO: $5,000-$25,000 for most mid-sized organizations.

**Honest assessment of limitations:** Bloomerang's strength is donor retention data - its dashboard showing retention rates by segment is genuinely useful and not replicated well by most competitors. Its weaknesses are grant management (limited compared to purpose-built grant tracking systems), restricted fund compliance (requires workarounds), and integration depth. The Kindful acquisition has expanded the product somewhat, but grant-heavy nonprofits typically find Bloomerang insufficient as their sole compliance system.

**The Bloomerang + Qgiv acquisition context:**

In 2023, Bloomerang acquired Qgiv, a peer-to-peer fundraising and event management platform. This acquisition is relevant for organizations that use or are evaluating both platforms. The integration between Bloomerang's donor CRM and Qgiv's fundraising tools has been ongoing since the acquisition, with the stated goal of a more unified data flow.

For organizations currently using both Bloomerang and Qgiv independently, the near-term implication is that the integration should improve over time. For organizations evaluating Bloomerang who also need peer-to-peer or event fundraising capabilities, Qgiv is now the bundled option - evaluate whether it meets your event and peer-to-peer needs before assuming the combined platform covers your use case.

The caveat applies to any acquisition: product roadmap priorities can shift, and the combined platform's direction is determined by Bloomerang's strategy, not by what either product's pre-acquisition customer base preferred. Monitor product communications and release notes, particularly for Qgiv features that matter to your organization's event or peer-to-peer programs.

### DonorPerfect

**Who it's for:** Nonprofits that want a long-established, full-featured donor management system with deep reporting and event management capabilities. Particularly common at organizations with 5,000+ donor records and active major gifts and planned giving programs.

**Pricing:** Starts around $1,500/year for basic plans, scales to $10,000+/year for larger organizations with advanced reporting. Implementation support available at additional cost. Three-year TCO: $8,000-$40,000 depending on plan and services.

**Honest assessment of limitations:** DonorPerfect is a mature product with a large installed base, which means it has deep functionality but also some technical debt in its architecture. The UX reflects years of feature additions without a comprehensive redesign - some users find navigation unintuitive. Grant compliance features exist but are not a primary strength. The vendor's Softrek acquisition and ongoing platform investment suggest the product is being actively maintained, but the pace of UX modernization has been slower than mid-market competitors.

**DonorPerfect's pricing model and TCO escalation:**

DonorPerfect's pricing model is module-based, which creates a deceptive base price. The entry plan covers core donor management, but most organizations need additional modules that add meaningful cost: grant management ($50-$100/month), SmartActions for workflow automation ($50-$75/month), DPO online donations ($varies by transaction volume), event management ($50-$75/month), and integrated email communications ($50-$150/month depending on volume).

An organization that starts at a $1,500/year base plan and adds three modules can quickly reach $3,500-$5,000 annually before including the communications module. Adding the SmartActions automation and a communications integration pushes the annual cost toward $6,000-$8,000 - materially higher than the base plan suggests. When evaluating DonorPerfect, build the full module list you need before comparing the price to competing systems. Comparing base prices is not a fair comparison for a module-based product.

### Neon CRM

**Who it's for:** Nonprofits that want a single platform covering donor management, event management, membership management, and basic grant tracking. Good fit for organizations with diverse revenue streams that need one system rather than multiple point solutions.

**Pricing:** $99-$299/month depending on features. Roughly $1,200-$3,600 annually. Three-year TCO including optional onboarding: $5,000-$20,000.

**Honest assessment of limitations:** Neon's breadth is both its strength and its limitation. It covers many use cases adequately; it covers few use cases deeply. Nonprofits with complex grant compliance requirements, large major gifts programs, or sophisticated segmentation needs typically find Neon's depth insufficient and add point solutions alongside it - which recreates the multi-system problem Neon was supposed to solve.

**The Neon One ecosystem and bundled pricing reality:**

Neon CRM is one product within the Neon One ecosystem, which also includes Neon Pay (payment processing), Neon Fundraise (peer-to-peer and event fundraising), Neon Websites (website builder), and Neon Giving Days (fundraising campaigns). Neon positions the ecosystem as a unified platform for nonprofits that want to manage donor relationships, fundraising, and digital presence in one place.

The bundled pricing argument - that using multiple Neon One products is more cost-effective than a best-of-breed stack - deserves scrutiny. For organizations that genuinely need peer-to-peer fundraising, a donor CRM, and a nonprofit-specific website, the Neon One bundle can represent real cost savings relative to buying separate best-in-class products. For organizations that primarily need a donor CRM with grant management depth, bundling with products they do not need dilutes the value of the per-seat cost.

The practical test: list every Neon One product you would actually use and at what volume. Then price the bundle versus the individual alternatives. If three or more products are genuinely needed, the bundle is often competitive. If one product covers 80% of your needs, evaluate the CRM standalone against purpose-built alternatives and add integrations where needed.

## What's Changed in 2025-2026

**Pricing increases across the market.** Blackbaud, Salesforce, and most mid-market vendors raised prices 5-15% in 2024 and 2025. SaaS cost pressure from rising infrastructure costs and investor return expectations is visible across the sector. Organizations locking in multi-year contracts in 2023-2024 are insulated; organizations on month-to-month or annual plans are absorbing increases.

**Market consolidation is accelerating.** Bonterra absorbed Network for Good, EveryAction, Salsa Labs, and Apricot over 2021-2024 - creating a large mid-market entity with unclear long-term product direction. Bloomerang acquired Kindful. These consolidations create near-term risk for customers of acquired platforms: product investment may slow, roadmaps may shift, and the platform you bought may look different in three years than it did at purchase.

**AI features are everywhere, useful rarely.** Every major vendor announced AI features in 2024-2025. The implementations as of early 2026 are primarily: suggested email copy (useful but available from any AI writing tool), data deduplication assistance (useful), and predictive giving scores (useful when the data volume supports them, which most mid-sized nonprofits don't have). The AI features that would meaningfully reduce compliance work - automated expense coding, grant restriction enforcement, intelligent report generation - are not yet in any major product as production-ready features.

**Grant compliance has become a differentiating category.** Several mid-market vendors have added dedicated grant management modules in 2024-2025, responding to customer demand. The quality varies significantly. Some implementations are genuine restricted fund tracking; others are grant "records" that don't integrate with expense coding. When evaluating grant compliance claims, ask to see the budget-vs.-actual report generation and the expense-to-grant traceability in a demo - not just the grant record screen.

## Buying Decision Framework

Five criteria to weight before choosing a nonprofit CRM:

**1. Does your compliance complexity require dedicated grant tracking, or is donor management the primary need?**

Organizations managing more than five active grants, any federal awards, or significant restricted fund portfolios need a system where grant restrictions are enforced at expense entry - not tracked in a separate spreadsheet. If grant compliance is a primary need, it should be a primary evaluation criterion, not an afterthought.

**2. What is your realistic implementation capacity?**

Enterprise platforms require consulting partners. If your organization cannot budget $25,000-$80,000 for implementation, do not evaluate Salesforce NPSP or Blackbaud as serious options - you will not be able to implement them successfully. Mid-market purpose-built tools are designed for self-implementation or low-cost onboarding. Match the tool to the capacity you actually have, not the capacity you wish you had.

**3. What is your three-year total cost of ownership, including admin?**

Run the cost calculator. Include the real admin model - will a staff member manage the system, will you hire a fractional admin, or will you return to your implementation partner for every change? The admin cost is often the largest ongoing line item and the one most frequently omitted from initial cost models.

**4. What happens to your data if you need to switch?**

Data portability is a risk management question. Can you export your full donor history, giving records, notes, and attachments in a standard format? Vendors with low portability create switching costs that effectively lock you in regardless of how the platform performs. Ask for a data export demonstration before signing.

**5. What does the vendor's track record look like on support quality and price stability?**

Check Capterra and G2 reviews specifically for comments on support responsiveness and pricing changes. Filter to reviews from the last 18 months. Vendors that have raised prices significantly or degraded support quality tend to leave evidence in user reviews.

## Red Flags in Sales Processes

Five patterns in vendor sales engagements that should prompt closer scrutiny:

**The "free licenses" anchor.** When the opening conversation centers on what's free (Salesforce Power of Us program, foundation pricing), ask immediately what the three-year total cost of ownership looks like including implementation, admin, and add-ons. The free licenses are the lead; the real cost is what follows.

**Demo environments that don't match your data complexity.** A demo using clean, simple sample data doesn't tell you how the system handles your data - which has duplicates, multiple giving histories, and complex fund restrictions. Ask to see a demo with realistic complexity.

**References from organizations significantly larger than you.** A reference from a $20M national organization doesn't tell you what the system will be like at $2M. Ask specifically for references from nonprofits at your budget range and staff size.

**Implementation timelines that seem short.** A 4-week implementation timeline for a complex CRM migration is almost never realistic. Overly optimistic timelines often lead to go-live delays that cost additional consulting fees and extend the period during which your team is managing two systems.

**No clear answer on what happens after you cancel.** Before signing, understand what data you can export, in what format, and what the offboarding process looks like. Vendors with clean, documented data export processes have less to hide about their data model than vendors who make this conversation difficult.

## A Note on Market Data

The statistics in this report draw from publicly available research: Omatic's 2025 nonprofit technology survey (n=600+), the Fifty & Fifty 2025 Nonprofit Peer Report, NTEN's 2025 Nonprofit Technology Trends, and pricing data from vendor websites, G2, Capterra, and implementation partner public rate cards. TCO ranges are estimates based on aggregated data; actual costs vary by organization size, data complexity, and implementation model. Where ranges are wide, the low end reflects self-implementation with minimal consulting and the high end reflects full consulting engagement with ongoing admin support. Use these figures as order-of-magnitude guidance, not as quotes.
