Nebraska has approximately 18,000 registered nonprofits, with the largest concentrations in Omaha and Lincoln. The state's nonprofit sector spans a range of organizational sizes, from large Omaha-based health and human services organizations supported by the Peter Kiewit Foundation and Sherwood Foundation to small rural nonprofits in the Sandhills and Panhandle that depend heavily on USDA and state DHHS funding.

## Nebraska's Rural Grant Compliance Gap

Nebraska nonprofits serving rural communities increasingly manage USDA Community Facilities grants alongside state DHHS contracts. These funding sources operate on different fiscal calendars, carry different documentation requirements, and have different cost allowability standards. USDA Community Facilities grants follow federal OMB Uniform Guidance with multi-year compliance timelines and expenditure documentation requirements that extend through facility useful life in some cases. State DHHS contracts align with Nebraska's July 1 through June 30 fiscal year and carry program-specific outcome reporting obligations.

Rural nonprofits managing both sources typically do so with lean staff who also handle program delivery. The compliance overhead per staff member is high, and the knowledge required to satisfy both USDA and DHHS requirements simultaneously is specialized. Organizations that rely on a single staff member for grant compliance carry significant institutional knowledge risk.

## State Registration Requirements

Nebraska requires nonprofits soliciting more than $25,000 annually to register with the Secretary of State using Form CN-1 before soliciting donations from Nebraska residents. Annual renewal is required, and organizations above the $25,000 threshold must file financial statements with their renewal. Organizations above $500,000 in gross revenues must submit audited financial statements.

Nonprofits receiving DHHS state contracts or NDED community development grants face additional program compliance requirements from those agencies, including expenditure tracking and periodic performance reporting.

## Major Grant Programs in Nebraska

Nebraska-specific grant programs that mid-sized nonprofits commonly receive include DHHS grants for health and human services programs, NDED community development grants, and USDA Rural Development Community Facilities grants. Private foundation funding from the Peter Kiewit Foundation and the Sherwood Foundation anchors the Omaha philanthropic market, with the Lincoln Community Foundation serving south-central Nebraska organizations. Both foundations run their own grant cycles with independent reporting requirements.

Federal grants from USDA, HHS, and HUD follow the October 1 through September 30 federal fiscal calendar, while state DHHS and NDED grants align with Nebraska's July 1 through June 30 state fiscal year.

## Why Software Matters for Nebraska Nonprofits

Nebraska nonprofits managing USDA grants with multi-year compliance timelines alongside annual state DHHS contracts face a tracking challenge that grows more complex with each new award. USDA compliance obligations do not end when a grant closes, some facility grants require expenditure documentation for years after the award period. Organizations tracking these obligations manually risk losing the documentation trail when staff turns over.

Grant management software that maintains award records through close-out and beyond, automates restricted fund accounting, and generates compliance-ready reports reduces the long-tail documentation burden that USDA and other federal grants create. Organizations that build this infrastructure before their grant portfolio grows avoid the reconciliation problems that emerge when tracking lags behind complexity.
