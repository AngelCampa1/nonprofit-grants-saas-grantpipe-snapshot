North Dakota has approximately 8,000 registered nonprofits, with the largest concentrations in Fargo, Bismarck, Grand Forks, and Minot. The state's predominantly rural character means most nonprofits outside the eastern corridor depend heavily on state DHS contracts and federal USDA Rural Development funding. A subset of western North Dakota organizations experienced an unusual compliance situation during the Bakken oil boom, and many are still managing the consequences.

## North Dakota's Bakken Legacy Compliance Problem

Western North Dakota nonprofits that received significant philanthropic grants from oil and gas companies during the Bakken boom created multi-year restricted fund obligations that extended beyond the boom period. These awards, often structured as multi-year program grants or endowment contributions with expenditure restrictions, require organizations to track allowable expenditures against original grant terms years after the initial award.

Organizations that received Bakken-era restricted gifts and have since experienced staff turnover may lack current knowledge of the specific expenditure restrictions attached to those funds. The grant terms exist in original award letters and board resolutions, but without a systematic tracking system, ensuring current expenditures remain compliant with historical restricted fund terms is operationally challenging. These same organizations also manage current-year DHS state contracts and USDA Rural Development grants, each with their own compliance calendars and documentation requirements.

## State Registration Requirements

North Dakota requires nonprofits soliciting more than $25,000 annually to register with the Secretary of State before soliciting donations from North Dakota residents. Annual renewal is required, and organizations above the threshold must submit financial statements. Organizations above $500,000 in gross revenues must submit audited financial statements.

Nonprofits receiving DHS state grants or North Dakota Department of Commerce grants face additional program compliance requirements from those agencies, aligned with North Dakota's July 1 through June 30 state fiscal year.

## Major Grant Programs in North Dakota

North Dakota-specific grant programs that mid-sized nonprofits commonly receive include DHS grants for human services and social programs, ND Department of Commerce community development grants, and USDA Rural Development community facilities grants. The Otto Bremer Trust, which serves North Dakota alongside neighboring states, provides grant funding for community development, health, and human services organizations. Fargo-Moorhead area community foundations support nonprofits in the eastern corridor through competitive grant cycles.

Federal grants follow the October 1 through September 30 federal calendar, while state DHS grants align with North Dakota's July 1 through June 30 state fiscal year.

## Why Software Matters for North Dakota Nonprofits

North Dakota nonprofits carry a compliance challenge that combines current-year grant management with multi-year legacy restricted fund tracking. Managing expenditure restrictions from Bakken-era grants alongside current DHS contracts and USDA awards requires a system that maintains award records and expenditure obligations through award close-out and beyond.

Grant management software that preserves the full compliance record for each award, tracks restricted fund balances against original grant terms, and generates expenditure reports for both current and legacy awards reduces the audit risk that comes with managing obligations across multiple time periods. Organizations that formalize their restricted fund tracking before the next major philanthropic cycle protect the compliance infrastructure that sustains their ability to receive future awards.
