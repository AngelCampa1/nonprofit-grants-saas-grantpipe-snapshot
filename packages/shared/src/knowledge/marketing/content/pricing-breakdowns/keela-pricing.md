## How Keela Pricing Works

Keela uses tiered pricing based on features, not record count. Unlike Bloomerang, your cost does not increase as your donor database grows.

The tiers are clearly differentiated: Essentials covers core donor management, Impact adds advanced reporting and fundraising tools, and Growth unlocks custom fields and priority support.

## What Each Tier Includes

### Essentials ($99/month)

Covers donor management, basic reporting, online giving forms, and email marketing. For small nonprofits focused on individual giving, this tier covers the core needs.

### Impact ($199/month)

Adds advanced reporting, peer-to-peer fundraising capabilities, and more sophisticated donor segmentation. This tier targets growing organizations that need more from their analytics and fundraising tools.

### Growth ($299/month)

The top tier adds custom fields, advanced integrations, and priority support. For organizations with complex data structures or integration requirements, these features justify the premium.

## The Grant Management Gap

Keela has basic grant tracking at all tiers, you can record grant awards and set deadline reminders. What it does not have is the depth required for post-award compliance:

- No restricted fund allocation to specific programs
- No expenditure tracking against grant-specific budgets
- No compliance report generation tied to funder requirements
- No audit trail connecting grants to programmatic outcomes

For nonprofits that depend on grant revenue, this gap means running a separate system for compliance alongside Keela for donor management.

## What the published price does not tell you

A pricing page is useful, but it rarely reflects the full first-year cost of adopting the system. Nonprofits should separate recurring subscription spend from setup labor, migration work, training time, and any secondary tools required to close feature gaps. That is especially important when the platform handles donor CRM well but leaves grant reporting, compliance tracking, or restricted-fund visibility to another product.

The practical budget question is not just whether the monthly fee fits today. It is whether the tool keeps your process simple enough to avoid added software or consulting spend later. If a platform requires an add-on, custom reporting layer, or outside administrator before it becomes usable for the whole organization, the headline price understates the real commitment.

## How to budget the first year realistically

A safer budgeting approach is to model year-one cost in three buckets: subscription, implementation effort, and process overhead. Subscription is the visible number. Implementation effort includes migration, cleanup, onboarding, and any partner help. Process overhead is the hidden cost of exports, spreadsheet reconciliation, and report reformatting that continues after launch. Comparing vendors on those three buckets produces a much more accurate view of affordability than monthly price alone.

This is where nonprofit teams should slow down and ask whether the platform removes operational work or simply prices it differently. A lower monthly fee is not a bargain if leadership still needs a second system, outside reporting help, or repeated spreadsheet cleanup to stay audit ready.
