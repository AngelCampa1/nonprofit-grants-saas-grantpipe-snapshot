## How Bloomerang Pricing Works

Bloomerang CRM starts at $125/month, billed annually, according to Bloomerang's public pricing page reviewed on May 20, 2026. The current page lists unlimited users and grant tracking, while broader Giving Platform bundles use contact-sales pricing.

Bloomerang prices around constituent scale and product bundle. Records include active donors, lapsed donors, contacts, and organizations. Every contact can affect budget planning, whether they donated last week or five years ago.

Your costs grow as your database grows, regardless of whether those records generate revenue. If you need donor CRM plus grants, compare the record-count model with [GrantPipe pricing](/pricing/) before you add a second grant compliance tool.

## Where Record Count Changes the Budget

Constituent-based pricing can make budget planning harder for nonprofits with large historical databases. A donor CRM should help you keep a clean record of active donors, lapsed donors, volunteers, households, and institutional contacts. The pricing question is whether every group belongs in the same CRM database and how that database growth affects renewal cost.

The public starting price is useful, but it is only the first budget input. Before signing, model how many constituents you expect to store after migration, how many historical records you need to retain, and whether CRM, fundraising, and volunteer bundles change the quote.

Organizations that have been in operation for years accumulate large databases of lapsed donors. Confirm how Bloomerang treats historical constituents, duplicate records, volunteers, organizations, and inactive contacts before migration. Deleting old records to reduce a quote can make the CRM cheaper, but losing historical data defeats the purpose of a durable donor record.

## What Grant Tracking Does Not Cover

Bloomerang lists grant tracking in its CRM feature set. That can help a development team keep award records visible inside the donor system. It is not the same thing as a full restricted-fund compliance workflow.

For grant-funded nonprofits, the unresolved questions are:

- Can finance see restricted fund tracking by award without a spreadsheet?
- Can staff attach expenditure evidence to the grant budget line it supports?
- Can the team export budget-vs-actual reports in funder-ready formats?
- Can auditors review the evidence trail without broad donor database access?

If the answer is no, Bloomerang's pricing covers the donor CRM side but not the full grant compliance workload. Compare that gap with [grant management software for nonprofits](/resources/guides/grant-management-software-for-nonprofits/) and the broader [nonprofit CRM pricing guide](/resources/guides/nonprofit-crm-pricing-guide/) before signing.

## What the published price does not tell you

A pricing page is useful, but it rarely reflects the full first-year cost of adopting the system. Nonprofits should separate recurring subscription spend from setup labor, migration work, training time, and any secondary tools required to close feature gaps. That is especially important when the platform handles donor CRM well but leaves grant reporting, compliance tracking, or restricted-fund visibility to another product.

The practical budget question is not just whether the monthly fee fits today. It is whether the tool keeps your process simple enough to avoid added software or consulting spend later. If a platform requires an add-on, custom reporting layer, or outside administrator before it becomes usable for the whole organization, the headline price understates the real commitment.

## How to budget the first year realistically

A safer budgeting approach is to model year-one cost in three buckets: subscription, implementation effort, and process overhead. Subscription is the visible number. Implementation effort includes migration, cleanup, onboarding, and any partner help. Process overhead is the hidden cost of exports, spreadsheet reconciliation, and report reformatting that continues after launch. Comparing vendors on those three buckets produces a much more accurate view of affordability than monthly price alone.
