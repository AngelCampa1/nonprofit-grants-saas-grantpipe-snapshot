## How Bloomerang Structures Pricing

Bloomerang publishes a clear starting point for its CRM: $125/month billed annually. It also lists Bloomerang Fundraising at $40/month, while Standard and Pro require contacting sales for pricing.

That means the public page is useful for early budgeting, but a mid-size nonprofit still needs to confirm the right plan for contact volume, feature needs, user needs, and service expectations.

## The Mid-Size Organization Range

For nonprofits with several thousand donor records, the practical question is whether the $125/month CRM entry point is enough or whether the organization will need a Standard or Pro quote. The answer usually depends on database size, reporting expectations, integrations, and how much service the team needs during migration.

Bloomerang is still a donor-centered CRM. It is stronger when donor retention, engagement signals, giving history, email, and online giving are the main priorities.

Mid-size teams should also check who will maintain the database after launch. A CRM can have a reasonable subscription and still become expensive when every report cleanup, import, segmentation change, or process adjustment falls to a consultant or one overloaded staff member. Before buying, ask for the plan limits in writing, confirm whether the quoted price assumes annual billing, and model the cost of the workflows that are outside the CRM.

## Grant Tracking Versus Grant Compliance

Bloomerang's public feature coverage includes grant tracking. That matters, because older comparisons that describe Bloomerang as having no grant capability are now too broad.

The more precise question is whether grant tracking is enough. Grant-funded nonprofits often need restricted fund balances, grant budget burn, reimbursement documentation, award documents, compliance tasks, and audit-ready exports. Those workflows are deeper than tracking a grant record or deadline.

## The GrantPipe Comparison

Bloomerang can be a good fit when donor retention analytics and fundraising workflows are the main job. GrantPipe is built for teams where donor management and grant compliance need to live together.

The cost comparison should include:

- Bloomerang CRM subscription, starting at $125/month billed annually.
- Any Standard or Pro quote if the entry CRM plan is not enough.
- Migration, cleanup, and reporting setup.
- The internal or external cost of restricted fund compliance if grant reporting remains outside the CRM.

GrantPipe publishes self-serve pricing and includes donor CRM, grant pipeline tracking, compliance calendar workflows, restricted fund visibility, and report outputs by plan. The tradeoff is focus: GrantPipe is purpose-built for grant-funded nonprofits rather than a broad donor-retention CRM.

## How To Budget The First Year

A pricing page is useful, but it rarely reflects the full first-year cost of adopting the system. Nonprofits should separate recurring subscription spend from setup labor, migration work, training time, and any secondary tools required to close feature gaps.

For Bloomerang, start with the $125/month CRM price, then ask whether the organization needs Standard or Pro, what migration help will cost, and whether restricted grant reporting will need another workflow. Comparing those buckets produces a more accurate view than monthly CRM price alone.

This is especially important for organizations where fundraising and finance share the same data. Development may judge Bloomerang by donor retention reporting, while finance needs clean support for restricted revenue, grant budgets, reimbursement timing, and audit documentation. If those requirements are handled outside Bloomerang, they belong in the first-year cost model even when they do not appear on the CRM invoice.
