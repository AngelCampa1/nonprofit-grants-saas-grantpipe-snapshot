## The problem

External reviews often turn into file requests, email attachments, and temporary shared-drive folders. That process gives auditors too much context in some places and too little in others, while staff lose time proving what should already be visible.

## How GrantPipe solves it

GrantPipe gives external reviewers a scoped portal tied to evidence bundles, document access, session controls, and activity history. Staff decide what reviewers can see and keep the review trail attached to the grant.

The Auditor & Funder Portal gives external reviewers exactly what they need and nothing else. You invite them by name, choose which grants, funds, and documents are visible, set an expiry date, and send a link. They never see your donor records, your team settings, or anything outside the scope you defined.

That scope is the point of the feature. Reviewers do not need the full operating
account to answer a specific audit or funder question. They need the award,
restriction, report, document, and activity context tied to the request. GrantPipe
keeps that material in a bounded session so staff do not have to choose between
oversharing and rebuilding the file by hand.

## What this feature does

When an audit or funder review is scheduled, the standard response is to pull files and email them as attachments or share a folder that contains far more than the reviewer needs. Neither approach creates a record of what was accessed.

The portal replaces that process. You build a named session, attach the evidence bundle or individual documents, assign the reviewer, and set when access ends. GrantPipe generates a signed link and sends the invitation. The reviewer sees a focused interface with the records you selected. When they open a document or download a file, that action is logged.

Each session has a name, owner, reviewer, expiry date, and selected scope. That
makes it clear later why access existed and what it was intended to cover. If a
review expands, staff can add the missing evidence intentionally instead of
dropping a larger folder into the same conversation.

## How invite and scope work

1. Go to the Portals section in GrantPipe and create a new session
2. Name the session (e.g., "Annual Audit FY 2025 - CPA Review")
3. Select which grants, restricted funds, and documents to include
4. Add the reviewer's name and email address
5. Set an expiry date - the session ends automatically on that date
6. Send the invitation; GrantPipe generates the signed link and emails it

The reviewer follows the link and lands on the portal view. They do not need to create an account or log in through the main application.

## What reviewers see

Reviewers see the grants, fund summaries, and documents included in the session. For each grant, they can see award terms, restriction details, spending summaries, report submissions, and attached documents. They cannot modify anything, cannot see donor records or campaign data, cannot access team settings, and cannot navigate to records outside the session scope.

## How access expires and revokes

Access expires automatically on the date set at invitation time. If you need to end access early - because the review is complete, the scope was wrong, or the relationship changed - go to Settings > Portal access and revoke the session. Revocation is immediate: the link stops working within seconds. Both automatic expiry and manual revocation are logged in the audit trail.

## What the activity trail proves

The portal does not only share files. It records the review. GrantPipe logs the
reviewer, timestamp, viewed record, downloaded document, session expiry, and
revocation event. That gives the organization a defensible answer when someone
asks who received a file, when they opened it, and whether access was still
active after the review ended.

This is especially useful when the same evidence supports several audiences.
The audit firm may need payroll allocation support. A funder may need the
quarterly report packet. A board committee may need restricted fund context.
GrantPipe lets staff package each session separately while preserving a single
history of external access.

## What this replaces

- Email attachments with no reliable view history.
- Shared drive folders that expose unrelated grant or donor files.
- One-off reviewer accounts that remain active after the review.
- Manual logs of who received which audit packet.
- Rebuilt evidence folders for each new external request.
