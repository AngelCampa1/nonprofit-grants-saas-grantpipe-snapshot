## The problem

Audit prep often starts with one hard question: are we ready?

The answer often sits across reports, folders, and email. The auditor portal
may not be set up. The binder may not exist yet. The team may not have made the
audit export. The IRS 990 prep output may still be missing.

That does not mean the team failed. It means the review work has no visible
starting point.

When readiness is invisible, staff wait until the request list arrives. Then
they build the binder under pressure. They search for files. They remake
reports. They ask who has access. Then they try to prove old work was correct.

GrantPipe cannot make audit judgment for your team. It can show whether the
basic review surface is ready before fieldwork starts.

## How GrantPipe solves it

GrantPipe adds an audit readiness panel to Evidence Bundles. The panel checks
four shipped signals:

- Auditor portal access.
- Audit binder starter.
- Audit export readiness.
- IRS 990 prep export readiness.

Each ready signal adds to the score. Missing signals show the next staff step.

The same panel has a button for the audit binder starter. That starter is an
evidence bundle with audit purpose. It gives the team one place for files. It
also holds reports and support for the review.

This release starts the binder. It does not auto-fill every item an auditor may
request. Staff still review the request list. They add the right documents.
They decide what to share.

## What the score measures

The score is simple on purpose. It measures readiness for GrantPipe review
tools, not the final audit result.

The first check is auditor portal access. Audit-Ready and Enterprise plans
include the portal. Staff can invite an auditor or funder. They set an expiry
date. They limit what the reviewer can see.

The second check is the audit binder starter. GrantPipe looks for an evidence
bundle marked for audit use. If none exists, staff can create one from the
panel.

The third check is audit export readiness. GrantPipe checks whether audit
report output exists in the report library. Staff can open, preview, and
download that output.

The fourth check is IRS 990 prep export readiness. This does not file the Form 990. It creates prep output from saved records. Staff can review it before
sharing anything outside GrantPipe.

## How staff use it

Open Evidence Bundles. Review the audit readiness score. If the binder starter
is missing, choose Create audit binder starter.

GrantPipe creates an audit-purpose evidence bundle. Staff then add documents
and reports from the auditor request list. If a report output is missing, staff
make it from Reports. Then they come back to the readiness panel.

The score updates as those signals become ready. This gives the team a small
weekly check during audit prep. It avoids one large rebuild at fieldwork.

## What the binder starter does

The binder starter creates the container. It gives the audit work a name. It
gives the work a purpose. It gives the work a place in the evidence bundle
flow.

That matters because audit prep is not only a file export. It is a trail. Staff
need to know which bundle exists. They need to know which reports support it.
They need to know which reviewer can access it. They also need to know which
actions GrantPipe logged.

GrantPipe keeps that work near the Auditor & Funder Portal and the activity
trail. For scoped reviewer access, see [Auditor & Funder
Portal](/features/auditor-funder-portal). See [Audit Trail Activity
Log](/features/audit-trail-activity-log) for record changes. It also tracks
views.

## What it does not do

Audit Readiness Score does not certify that your nonprofit will pass an audit.
It does not replace your CPA. It does not decide which documents satisfy an
auditor request.

The binder starter also does not auto-assemble every support file. Your
auditor's request list still controls what belongs in the binder. Your team
still reviews documents before sharing them.

This boundary matters. A score can help staff see missing prep work. It should
not pretend that software can replace CPA review.

## Why GrantPipe built it this way

Audit readiness is easier to manage when staff can see it during normal work.

The score uses signals GrantPipe can know. It knows whether portal access is
available. It knows whether an audit-purpose bundle exists. It knows whether
key report outputs exist. It does not claim to know what the auditor will ask
next.

That keeps the feature useful without claiming too much. The team gets a clear
next step. The auditor still gets a reviewed binder.

## What it replaces

- A shared drive folder with no clear purpose.
- Manual reminders to make audit and IRS 990 prep outputs.
- Unclear handoff between evidence bundles and reviewer access.
- A status meeting just to learn which prep steps are still missing.
