faqs:
  - q: "Does Arizona require charitable solicitation registration for Phoenix nonprofits?"
    a: "No. Arizona is one of approximately twelve states that do not require general charitable solicitation registration. Phoenix nonprofits soliciting donations from Arizona residents do not need to register with the Arizona Attorney General or any other state office for solicitation purposes. The exception is veterans' organizations and other narrow categories with specific registration requirements. If your Phoenix nonprofit solicits in other states - including online solicitation reaching donors in registration states - you may still need to register in those states."
  - q: "What is the Arizona Corporation Commission annual report?"
    a: "Arizona nonprofit corporations file an annual report with the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC) Corporations Division each year by the anniversary of incorporation. The fee for nonprofit corporations is $10. The report confirms registered agent, principal office address, and director information. Failure to file results in administrative dissolution after a grace period. Filings are submitted online through the ACC eCorp system."
  - q: "How does Maricopa County property tax exemption work for nonprofits?"
    a: "Property tax exemption in Maricopa County is processed through the Arizona Department of Revenue and the Maricopa County Assessor. Qualifying 501(c)(3) organizations file the Affidavit of Property Tax Exemption with the County Assessor by the statutory deadline (typically the first Monday in June for the current tax year). The property must be owned by the nonprofit and used exclusively for exempt purposes. The exemption does not transfer with property - each owner must apply. Properties leased to non-exempt entities or used commercially may lose exemption."
  - q: "What is Arizona Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT) and how does the nonprofit exemption work?"
    a: "Arizona's Transaction Privilege Tax is technically a tax on the privilege of doing business, not a sales tax - vendors are taxed on gross receipts and typically pass the cost to buyers. Qualifying 501(c)(3) organizations may obtain limited TPT exemptions for purchases used in exempt activities. The exemption is more narrow than typical state sales tax exemptions, and TPT exemption certificates apply to specific transaction types. Phoenix imposes its own city TPT in addition to the state rate, and city TPT licensing is administered through the AZ DOR centralized system."
  - q: "Does Phoenix require a city business license for nonprofits?"
    a: "Phoenix requires a city Transaction Privilege Tax license for organizations conducting business activities within the city, even if the organization is exempt from federal income tax. Pure 501(c)(3) charitable activity is generally not subject to TPT licensing, but operating a thrift store, conference center, or other commercial activity may require licensing. The Phoenix Tax and License office administers city-level TPT collection through the AZ DOR's centralized portal. Confirm specific licensing obligations based on the activity."
  - q: "How does Maricopa County vendor registration work for nonprofit contractors?"
    a: "Maricopa County maintains a vendor registration system through the Office of Procurement Services. Nonprofits seeking county contracts must register before contract execution. Registration requires W-9, IRS determination letter, certificate of good standing from the AZ Corporation Commission, and insurance certificates. Many county contracts include federal pass-through funds and carry Uniform Guidance compliance requirements. Active registration must be maintained for the duration of any county contract."
  - q: "How does the City of Phoenix manage nonprofit vendor registration?"
    a: "The City of Phoenix Finance Department Procurement Division maintains its own vendor registration system separate from Maricopa County. Nonprofits seeking contracts with City of Phoenix departments - including the Human Services Department, Neighborhood Services Department, and Parks and Recreation - must register with the city before contract execution. Documentation requirements include W-9, IRS determination letter, AZ Corporation Commission good standing, and insurance certificates. City and County vendor systems are independent."
  - q: "What insurance does Phoenix typically require on nonprofit contracts?"
    a: "City of Phoenix contracts commonly require general liability insurance ($1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate is standard), workers' compensation as required by Arizona law, auto liability when transportation is involved, and professional liability for service contracts. Insurance certificates must name the City of Phoenix as additional insured. Specific limits are set in the solicitation documents. Lapses in coverage constitute contract default. The Risk Management Division reviews compliance during contract execution."
  - q: "What does the federal single audit require for Phoenix nonprofits?"
    a: "If your Phoenix nonprofit expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in a fiscal year, a single audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F is required. Federal pass-through funds from Arizona state agencies, the City of Phoenix (HUD CDBG, ESG are common), and Maricopa County count toward the threshold. The audit must be completed within nine months of fiscal year-end and submitted to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse. Phoenix's substantial HUD CDBG allocation pushes many nonprofits across the threshold."
  - q: "Are Phoenix nonprofits subject to Arizona state income tax?"
    a: "Arizona generally exempts 501(c)(3) organizations from state income tax on activities related to their exempt purpose. Unrelated business income subject to federal UBIT is also subject to Arizona income tax. The exemption is recognized by the Arizona Department of Revenue based on federal 501(c)(3) status - there is typically no separate state-level exemption application for income tax purposes, though documentation of federal status may be requested. Annual filing requirements depend on activity and unrelated business income."
  - q: "How does Maricopa County handle property tax for properties leased by nonprofits?"
    a: "Property leased to a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and used exclusively for exempt purposes may qualify for exemption depending on lease structure. The property owner remains liable for property tax unless the property qualifies for exemption based on use. If the nonprofit is the owner, exemption is typical when used for exempt purposes. If the nonprofit is the lessee, the analysis depends on whether the lease and use satisfy Arizona statutory requirements. The Maricopa County Assessor reviews each application."
  - q: "What recordkeeping does Arizona require even without solicitation registration?"
    a: "Arizona's lack of solicitation registration does not exempt nonprofits from federal recordkeeping obligations: Form 990 with full Schedule B (donor information), donor acknowledgment letters for gifts of $250 or more, contemporaneous records for in-kind gifts and quid pro quo contributions, and donor restriction documentation. The Arizona Corporation Commission requires accurate corporate records, and Arizona Charity Care or other narrow programs may impose specific recordkeeping. Federal compliance applies in full regardless of state simplicity."

A Phoenix workforce development nonprofit launched a national online giving campaign during a year-end fundraising push. The campaign generated $42,000 in contributions from donors in 18 states. The board treasurer assumed the organization's Arizona-based status meant no state registration was required anywhere - Arizona itself does not require charitable solicitation registration, and the organization had operated in Phoenix for eleven years without ever filing a state solicitation form. Three months later, the organization received a registration inquiry letter from the California Attorney General's Registry of Charitable Trusts asking why it was soliciting California residents without registration. Compliance counsel advised registering retroactively in California, New York, and Florida - three states where the campaign had attracted significant donor traffic and where registration is actively enforced.

Phoenix's compliance environment is among the lightest at the state level but no lighter at the federal level than any other major city. The [Arizona Corporation Commission](https://azcc.gov/) handles nonprofit corporate annual reports, and the [Arizona Department of Revenue](https://azdor.gov/) administers Transaction Privilege Tax licensing and any limited TPT exemptions. There is no Arizona-level charitable solicitation registration requirement, which makes Arizona one of about a dozen states with no general registration regime. At the local level, the [City of Phoenix](https://www.phoenix.gov/) and [Maricopa County](https://www.maricopa.gov/) operate independent vendor registration systems for government contracts. The [Maricopa County Assessor](https://mcassessor.maricopa.gov/) processes property tax exemption applications.

The federal compliance layer applies fully in Phoenix regardless of Arizona's simpler state regime - Form 990 with Schedule B, single audit at $1,000,000 in federal expenditures, and Uniform Guidance on all federal pass-through funds including HUD CDBG, ESG, and HHS grants flowing through Arizona agencies and the City of Phoenix. Phoenix nonprofits soliciting nationally must monitor the registration thresholds in each target state under the Charleston Principles framework. ## Implementation realities and migration notes

Mid-sized nonprofits in this category typically inherit a tangle of restricted-fund histories: federal pass-throughs, state agency contracts, family-foundation grants, and partner funding stretching back many years. Migrating that history cleanly is not optional - auditors and program officers will ask questions that require a year-by-year reconstruction. Implementation timelines run six to ten weeks for organizations that scope the data inventory before signing. Cutting corners on migration to chase a fast launch usually surfaces gaps during the next single-audit cycle, and the cost of fixing those gaps after the fact is meaningfully higher than doing migration right at the start.

Plan accordingly, and require any vendor on the shortlist to demonstrate restricted-fund handling, grant tracking, and donor record migration on a representative sample of your actual historical data before you sign. Vendors that decline to demo on real data are filtering you out for a reason. The demo on your data is where the gaps surface - both the gaps in the vendor's product and the gaps in your existing records that you will need to clean up regardless of which system you choose. Use that demo to set realistic expectations with the board and the audit committee about timeline and scope before contracts get signed.
