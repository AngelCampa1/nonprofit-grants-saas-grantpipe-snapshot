USDA Community Facilities grants often leave a bigger file than a simple program grant. The project may include equipment, building work, matching funds, inspections, or other asset records.

Closeout should prove more than final spending. It should show what was bought or built, how it was funded, which reports were filed, and which records must stay on file after the award ends.

Use this workflow with the general [close federal award file](/workflows/close-federal-award-file) process when the award has federal closeout duties.

## Step 1: confirm the closeout trigger

Start with the USDA award and any amendments. Confirm the project period, final budget, reporting due dates, and closeout instructions.

Check whether the project is complete, whether all costs have posted, and whether any final inspection or approval is still open.

Create a closeout task list with owners. Include finance, grants, program leadership, and anyone who manages equipment or facility records.

## Step 2: reconcile final costs

Pull final ledger detail for the award. Compare it to the approved budget and the final project records.

Review all late costs. A cost posted after the project end may still be valid in some cases, but the file needs support. Check the period, invoice date, service date, and approval.

If costs were moved between lines, keep the journal entry and reason. The closeout file should show why the final cost total is right.

## Step 3: tie out funding sources

Community Facilities projects may combine USDA funds with local funds, debt, donations, or other grants. Build a funding schedule.

For each source, show the amount budgeted, amount received, amount spent, and support record. Match USDA drawdowns to bank records and project costs.

If match or other non-federal share was required, keep proof. Do not rely on the original budget as proof that match happened.

## Step 4: review equipment and facility records

If the award paid for equipment, create or update the equipment record. Include description, serial number if any, acquisition date, cost, funding source, location, condition, and use.

If the award supported a facility, keep property, contract, inspection, and final approval records as required by the award.

Federal equipment rules can continue after closeout. The file should tell future staff what was purchased and what rules still apply.

## Step 5: check final reports and requests

Confirm all final financial, performance, and project reports. Save the submitted versions and the receipts or acceptance notices.

Compare final reports to the final ledger. If a report total differs from the ledger, document why or correct it before closeout.

Check whether any final drawdown is needed. If cash remains, work with the agency on refund or offset steps.

## Step 6: resolve open items

List every open item: unpaid invoice, missing receipt, missing asset tag, pending report, or agency question.

Do not mark the award closed until the list is cleared or assigned with a written plan. Closeout is not just a deadline. It is the point where the final record becomes the source of truth.

## Step 7: save retention notes

Save the final closeout packet in one place. Include the award, amendments, final ledger, drawdowns, bank records, invoices, match support, equipment records, reports, approvals, and agency emails.

Add a retention note. Name the date the retention clock starts, the minimum retention period, and any longer requirement from the award or organization policy.

## Final file check

Ask someone who did not build the file to open it. Can they answer what USDA paid for, what the organization paid for, when reports were filed, and where the equipment or facility records live?

If not, add a short index. A one-page index can save hours later.

## Check what remains after closeout

Closeout does not always end every duty. Equipment records, property records, insurance support, and retention rules may continue after the final report.

Make a short post-closeout list. Include asset checks, record retention dates, reporting notes, and who owns each item. If the project created a facility or long-lived equipment, name the person who will keep the record current.

Also note where the final file lives. Staff turnover is a real risk for facility grants because the asset may last much longer than the grant team that managed the award.

Review the list once a year until no duties remain.

Add the review date to the asset file.

Keep the reviewer name beside that date too.
