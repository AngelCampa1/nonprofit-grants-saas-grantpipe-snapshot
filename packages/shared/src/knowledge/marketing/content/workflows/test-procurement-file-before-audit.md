Procurement findings often come from plain gaps. The team bought the right item, but the file does not prove competition. Or the contract exists, but the conflict check is missing. Or the invoice is paid, but nobody saved the approval.

Pre-audit testing gives the team a chance to find those gaps before fieldwork.

This workflow uses the federal procurement standards in 2 CFR 200.318 through 200.320 as a starting point. Always read the award terms and your own policy too.

## Step 1: identify the purchase

Start with one purchase or contract. Record the grant, vendor, amount, date, budget line, and what was bought.

Then identify the procurement method. Was it a micro-purchase, simplified acquisition, sealed bid, proposal, or noncompetitive procurement? The method drives the support needed.

Do not test every file the same way. A small supply order and a large service contract need different evidence.

## Step 2: check policy thresholds

Compare the purchase amount to your procurement policy and federal thresholds. Use the stricter rule when the award or internal policy requires it.

Save the policy version that applied at the time of purchase. Auditors may ask which rules staff followed.

If the purchase crossed a threshold but the file used a lower method, document the issue. Do not hide it.

## Step 3: review competition support

Look for quotes, bids, proposals, price checks, or written justification. The file should show how the vendor was selected.

For noncompetitive procurement, save the reason. The reason should match an allowed basis. Convenience is not enough.

If competition was limited, explain why. For example, the item may have been available from only one source, or there may have been an emergency that did not allow delay.

## Step 4: check cost reasonableness

The file should show the price was reasonable. Competition can help prove that. If there was no competition, use a price analysis, past purchase history, catalog price, market quote, or other support.

For larger purchases, check whether a cost or price analysis was required.

Tie the purchase back to the award. A reasonable price still must be allowable for the grant.

## Step 5: check conflicts and approvals

Federal rules require written standards of conduct for procurement. The file should show the conflict process was followed.

Check whether any staff, board member, or related party had a financial interest in the vendor. If there was a conflict, the file should show how it was handled.

Also check approvals. The purchase request, contract, and payment should have the right approval for the amount.

## Step 6: match contract, receipt, and payment

The file should show what was ordered, what was received, and what was paid.

Compare the contract or purchase order to the invoice. Then compare the invoice to proof of receipt and payment.

If the work was a service, keep evidence that the work happened. This may be a report, deliverable, timesheet, meeting record, or acceptance email.

## Step 7: document gaps and fixes

If support is missing, look for the real record. Do not create backdated records.

If the record does not exist, write a short gap note. Explain what is missing, why it matters, and what control will prevent the issue next time.

Use the result to update training and checklists. A gap found before audit is still useful.

## Select files to test

Do not wait for the auditor's sample. Pick your own sample during the year.

Include large purchases, purchases near a threshold, sole-source purchases, related-party concerns, equipment, consultant contracts, and purchases charged near the end of the award. These are the files most likely to raise questions.

Also test a few ordinary purchases. If small files are weak, the process may need better training.

## Report the results

Summarize the test results for finance and grants leadership. Show how many files passed, how many had missing support, and which issues repeated.

If the same issue appears in several files, treat it as a process gap. For example, if quotes are missing, add a quote upload step before approval. If conflict checks are missing, add the check to the purchase request.

The best pre-audit test does not only clean one file. It improves the next purchase.

If a grant has many small purchases, test the pattern too. Repeated small errors can become a control issue even when each purchase is below a major threshold.
