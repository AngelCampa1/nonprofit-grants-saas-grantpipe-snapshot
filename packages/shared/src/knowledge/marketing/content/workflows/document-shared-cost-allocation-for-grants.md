Shared costs need a clear file. A reviewer should be able to see the cost, who benefited, why the split was chosen, whether each grant allowed the cost, and who approved the final entry.

This workflow is for shared program costs such as payroll, software, rent, training, supplies, and services. It is not legal or accounting advice. Use funder terms and your policy for final treatment.

## Step 1: confirm the source cost

Start with the actual source. Save the invoice, payroll report, receipt, contract, or journal entry. Confirm the amount, vendor or employee, service dates, and description.

Do not allocate from a budget estimate unless your policy and funder terms allow it. A real cost should support the entry.

## Step 2: list who benefited

Program and finance should list the grants, funds, or programs that benefited from the cost. Do not include a grant because it has money left. Include it because the cost helped that grant's approved work.

If a grant ended before the service date, exclude it. If the grant terms do not allow the cost, exclude it. If the benefit is unclear, ask program staff before posting.

## Step 3: choose the allocation basis

Pick a basis that fits the cost. Use hours for labor, square footage for space, users for software, units for supplies, or participants for shared program service when that fits the work.

The basis should be easy to explain. It should also be supported by a record. A user list, time report, floor plan, attendance record, or service count can support the basis.

For federal grants, 2 CFR 200.405 points to assigning costs in proportion to the benefit received. That is the anchor for the review.

## Step 4: prepare the worksheet

The worksheet should show the total cost, basis used, units by grant, percentage by grant, dollar amount by grant, fund code, grant code, and budget line.

Keep the math visible. Avoid hidden formulas that only one person understands. If the allocation uses a cost allocation plan, cite the policy and attach the relevant section.

## Step 5: check budget and period

Before posting, compare each allocated amount to the approved grant budget and period of performance. If a budget line is over limit, pause. If the cost falls outside the period, pause.

Also check prior approval rules. Some costs require funder approval even when the allocation math is clean.

## Step 6: approve and post

A reviewer should check the source cost, allocation basis, calculation, budget fit, and support. Save approval before or at posting.

The approval can be a system approval, signed worksheet, or dated email. It should show who approved the split, when, and for what amount.

## Step 7: review at month end

Shared costs can change. Staff time changes. Program volume changes. A grant ends. A new grant begins. Review recurring allocations monthly.

If the split changes, write why. "Grant B ended on June 30" or "user count changed from 10 to 14" is enough when the support is attached.

## Tie the split to reports

After posting, check the next funder report or reimbursement request. The allocated cost should appear in the right budget line and period. If the report uses a different category than the ledger, save the mapping.

This step matters when a shared cost affects deadlines. A clean allocation file can still create reporting friction if the funder report groups costs in another way.

## Outputs

The finished workflow produces a source cost file, allocation worksheet, budget and period check, approval record, and ledger posting support.

## Common failures

Common failures include allocating by budget room, using stale percentages, including ended grants, missing the invoice, and failing to get reviewer approval.

Another failure is charging a cost directly when it belongs in an indirect cost pool. Review your cost policy before posting shared administrative costs.

## Review questions

Before approval, ask whether each grant received a clear benefit, whether the basis reflects that benefit, and whether the support would make sense six months later. Also ask whether the same method was used last month. If it changed, save the reason.

For recurring costs, compare the current split to the prior month. A large change may be correct, but it should have a reason tied to staffing, usage, space, participants, or another supportable driver.

## Automation ideas

Automation can reuse approved allocation methods, flag grants outside the service period, compare splits to prior months, and alert reviewers when a recurring allocation changes.

Automation should make the support easier to collect. It should not create a split without proof that each grant benefited.
