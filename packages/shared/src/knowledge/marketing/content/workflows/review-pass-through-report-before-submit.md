Pass-through reports can combine finance data, program updates, match, and subrecipient activity. That mix needs review before submission.

Use this workflow when your nonprofit reports to a state, city, county, foundation, or other pass-through entity.

## Step 1: confirm the report setup

Read the award terms and reporting instructions. Confirm the period, due date, report form, portal, and attachments.

Pull the prior report. The new report should not conflict with earlier cumulative totals unless there is a documented correction.

## Step 2: tie spending to the ledger

Pull ledger detail for the report period and award. Tie report totals to the ledger.

If the report uses budget categories that differ from the chart of accounts, keep the mapping in the packet.

## Step 3: review match and subrecipient activity

If match is required, confirm that match is supported and inside the allowed period.

If subrecipients are included, confirm invoices, reports, and monitoring notes are current. Do not report unsupported subrecipient costs as if they were cleared.

## Step 4: check narrative and numbers together

The narrative should agree with the financial report. If spending is low because hiring was delayed, the program update should not imply full staffing.

Clear reports make later questions easier to answer.

## Step 5: save the final packet

Save the report, support worksheet, reviewer notes, attachments, and submission receipt.

Add the next report due date to the [monitoring calendar](/resources/guides/pass-through-entity-monitoring-calendar-guide).

## Review checklist

Use one checklist for every pass-through report. It keeps the review from turning into a memory test.

| Check              | Evidence                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Award and period   | Award number, report period, and due date match the agreement.               |
| Budget             | Report categories match the approved budget or mapping worksheet.            |
| Spending           | Reported costs tie to the ledger.                                            |
| Match              | Match is supported and not counted twice.                                    |
| Subrecipient costs | Subrecipient invoices and reports are current.                               |
| Prior reports      | Cumulative figures agree with earlier submissions or have a correction note. |
| Narrative          | Program update agrees with financial activity.                               |
| Approval           | Reviewer note is saved before submission.                                    |

If any item fails, fix it before submission or write down why the report can still be submitted. Silence is the weak spot. A short note is usually enough.

## Common report problems

The most common problem is a report that mixes cash and accrual basis without saying so. The ledger may show incurred costs. The funder form may ask for reimbursed costs. If the basis differs, write a reconciliation note.

Another problem is a narrative that sounds complete when spending shows the work is delayed. Do not hide the delay. Explain the cause, the plan, and whether the budget or timeline needs approval.

Subrecipient activity also needs care. A pass-through report may ask for totals that include subrecipient costs. If a subrecipient invoice is still under review, do not roll it into the report as approved spending.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.332 requires pass-through entities to monitor subrecipient activity and follow up on deficiencies. If your nonprofit is reporting to a pass-through entity, that entity may ask for more detail than the federal baseline.

2 CFR 200.329 covers monitoring and reporting program performance. A financial report may still need program context if spending and outcomes do not line up.

2 CFR 200.303 covers internal controls. The review step is part of the control record. Save who reviewed the report, when they reviewed it, and what support they checked.

## When to escalate

Escalate the report before submission when:

- costs do not tie to the ledger
- match is missing or weak
- a required subrecipient report is late
- the report conflicts with prior totals
- the award terms are unclear
- the report would certify something staff cannot prove

Escalation does not have to mean a long meeting. It can be a written question to the executive director, finance lead, or funder. The important part is not to bury the issue inside the report.
