bluf: "A board financial review that takes more than 20 minutes has too much information - the review that produces useful board decisions is structured around three questions: are we solvent, are restricted obligations on track, and is there a decision the board needs to make this month. steps:
  - title: "Generate Statement of Activities (year-to-date actual vs. budget)"
    content: "After month-end close, run the Statement of Activities from your accounting system showing year-to-date actual revenue and expenses against the approved annual budget. This report must show both unrestricted and restricted activity, with restricted and unrestricted columns clearly separated. Look for three things before sending to the board: revenue categories that are more than 10% under budget without a known explanation, expense categories that are more than 10% over budget, and any restricted revenue that appears in the unrestricted column due to a fund coding error. Correct fund coding errors before distribution - they will confuse board members and undermine trust in the numbers."
  - title: "Generate Statement of Financial Position (current period vs. same period prior year)"
    content: "Run the Statement of Financial Position (balance sheet) showing the current month-end balances compared to the same calendar month in the prior year. The year-over-year comparison is more informative than a budget comparison for the balance sheet because assets, liabilities, and net assets are measured at a point in time, not against a spending plan. Check the cash balance against the 90-day cash projection you will generate in Step 3 - if cash is lower than projected, that discrepancy needs an explanation in the narrative. Also verify that net assets are correctly split among unrestricted, temporarily restricted, and permanently restricted."
  - title: "Generate cash flow projection for the next 90 days"
    content: "Build or update a rolling 90-day cash flow projection. The projection lists confirmed cash inflows (grant payments scheduled, pledges with payment dates, contract payments due) and confirmed cash outflows (payroll dates, rent, vendor invoices due) for the next 90 days. Calculate the projected end-of-period cash balance. If the projection shows cash dropping below two weeks of operating expenses at any point in the 90-day window, that is a board-decision item requiring either an accelerated receivable collection, a line of credit draw, or an operating expense deferral. Include the cash flow projection in the board package, not just the balance sheet cash balance."
  - title: "Generate fund-level restricted balance report (each active grant's remaining balance and timeline)"
    content: "Run a fund-level report showing, for each active grant: the total award amount, the cumulative expenditures to date, the remaining balance, the grant end date, and the implied monthly spend rate required to fully expend the award on time. A grant with $40,000 remaining and six months left requires roughly $6,700 per month in spending - if the organization has been spending $3,000 per month, there is an underspending problem that may require a no-cost extension or a budget modification conversation with the funder. Flag any grant where the required monthly spend rate is more than 30% above the current run rate."
  - title: "Calculate operating reserve ratio: current unrestricted net assets divided by average monthly operating expenses"
    content: "The operating reserve ratio answers the question 'how long could we operate if revenue stopped?' Calculate it as: unrestricted net assets from the Statement of Financial Position (excluding board-designated funds and fixed assets) divided by average monthly operating expenses (total operating expenses from the prior 12 months divided by 12). The result is the reserve in months. Compare to the board's reserve policy. Document the calculation explicitly in the board materials - not just the final number, but the inputs, so the board treasurer can verify it independently. A reserve ratio below three months at a grant-dependent organization should trigger a finance committee conversation, not just a line in the report."
  - title: "Write a three-sentence executive summary: financial health, key variances, items requiring board decision"
    content: "Write exactly three sentences in the executive summary. Sentence one states overall financial health for the period: 'As of [date], the organization is operating [within/outside] the board's approved parameters, with year-to-date net income of [amount] against a budgeted [amount].' Sentence two identifies the most significant variance from plan: 'The [category] is [over/under] budget by [amount] due to [specific cause].' Sentence three states whether board action is required: 'No board action is required this period' or 'The board is asked to approve [specific action] - detail on page [X].' Three sentences. No more."
  - title: "Package as a single one-page summary with full statements as appendix"
    content: "Assemble the board financial package in this order: the one-page summary (from Step 6) on page one, followed by the Statement of Activities, Statement of Financial Position, cash flow projection, and fund-level restricted balance report as appendices. Number the pages. Create a header for each appendix that identifies what it is and the period it covers - board members reviewing materials the night before a meeting should not have to identify which report is which by reading the content. The package should be a single PDF, not multiple email attachments."
  - title: "Send to board treasurer three business days before meeting for pre-review"
    content: "Send the complete package to the board treasurer three business days before the full board meeting. The treasurer's pre-review has two purposes: technical verification (does anything in the numbers look wrong) and strategic preparation (what questions is the board likely to ask and are the answers in the package). The treasurer communicates any corrections needed back to finance staff with at least two business days remaining before the meeting. Last-minute corrections discovered at the meeting itself undermine credibility - the pre-review window is a quality control step, not a courtesy."
  - title: "At meeting: present summary, take questions, note any board decisions required"
    content: "The treasurer presents from the one-page summary. The presentation covers the three-sentence executive summary, then opens to questions. Finance staff attend the meeting as technical resources and answer specific questions about the full statements, but do not present. Note any board decisions that arise during the financial discussion on a decisions log - who is authorized to do what, by when. If a vote is needed (budget modification, reserve draw, new line of credit), take it before moving to the next agenda item. Do not table financial decisions without setting a specific resolution date."
  - title: "File all board financial materials for audit record"
    content: "After the meeting, file the complete board financial package - the distributed materials, the meeting minutes noting any decisions taken, and the decisions log - in the organization's board records file for that meeting date. The board records file is part of the audit trail for financial governance. A Single Audit or financial statement audit will often request evidence that the board received and reviewed financial statements regularly - the filed materials are that evidence. Name the file with the meeting date and fiscal year: BoardFinancials_2026-04_FY2026.pdf."
faqs:
  - q: "Does the board need to see the full financial statements or just the summary?"
    a: "Both. The one-page summary is for the board meeting presentation. The full statements are distributed in advance as appendices for board members who want the detail. The finance committee reviews the full statements in depth. This structure gives every board member access to complete information while making the meeting presentation efficient."
  - q: "What is a reasonable operating reserve target for a grant-dependent nonprofit?"
    a: "Three to six months of operating expenses in liquid, unrestricted form. Grant-dependent organizations - those receiving more than 60% of revenue from grants - should target six months because grant revenue is less predictable than contract or earned revenue. The reserve target is a board governance decision documented in a written reserve policy. Without a written policy, the reserve ratio calculation has no benchmark to compare against."
  - q: "How do we handle a board member who asks very detailed questions about individual line items?"
    a: "Refer detailed line-item questions to the finance committee, which has the mandate for detailed financial review. The full board's role is governance oversight, not bookkeeping review. A board meeting that spends 40 minutes on a single supply line item is a board that has not clearly defined the difference between governance and management. The chair can and should redirect overly granular questions to the finance committee."
  - q: "Can we skip the board financial review in a month with no major issues?"
    a: "No. The board's fiduciary duty requires regular financial oversight regardless of whether there are problems. 'No news is good news' is not a financial reporting standard. An organization that has 12 months of documented financial reviews demonstrates stronger governance than one that presents financials only when problems arise - and the difference matters in grant applications, audits, and leadership transitions."
  - q: "What should the finance committee do that the full board does not?"
    a: "The finance committee reviews the full financial statements in detail, questions significant variances, reviews the audit, approves the annual budget before full board vote, monitors the reserve policy compliance, and oversees any major financial risks. The full board receives a summary, votes on budgets and major financial decisions, and has access to the full statements on request. The committee-to-board division of labor ensures that detailed financial oversight happens without consuming full board meeting time."

The board financial review is one of the clearest tests of whether a nonprofit's governance is functioning. Boards that receive financial information in accessible formats, with clear narratives and explicit action items, make better decisions in less time. Boards that receive a 15-page packet five minutes before the meeting tabled for next month end up making reactive decisions after the financial problem has already compounded.

## When to run this workflow

Run this workflow every month as part of the month-end close cycle. For organizations with quarterly board meetings, complete Steps 1-5 monthly for internal management and complete the full workflow for each quarterly board meeting. Do not skip the internal monthly close just because the board does not meet monthly - the management team needs current financial information regardless of the board meeting schedule.

## Common pitfalls

**Waiting for a perfect close before distributing materials.** Month-end close is rarely perfect on time. Distribute preliminary materials with a clear label and commit to a final version timeline. Board members need time to read the materials before the meeting; materials that arrive the morning of the meeting do not get read.

**Not separating restricted and unrestricted activity.** A combined financial statement that does not separate restricted and unrestricted funds can show apparent financial health while the unrestricted fund is running a structural deficit. The restricted and unrestricted separation is a compliance requirement under nonprofit accounting standards (ASC 958), not an optional best practice.

**Treating the 90-day cash projection as optional.** The cash projection is the earliest warning system for liquidity problems. Balance sheet cash balance is a backward-looking number. The 90-day projection is forward-looking. Organizations that skip the projection discover cash shortfalls when they happen, not 90 days before.

**Filing board materials inconsistently.** Audit readiness requires that board meeting records - including financial materials distributed - are filed consistently and retrievably. An organization that sends financial reports by email but does not file them centrally cannot produce them when auditors ask. File every distribution in a named, organized board records folder.
