Grant writing time is limited. A fit screen protects that time. It helps your team decide whether a foundation grant is worth a proposal before someone starts drafting pages, collecting letters, and asking finance for numbers.

The screen does not need to be fancy. It needs to be clear. A good screen tells the team why the grant fits, what could go wrong, who checked the facts, and what evidence should stay in the file.

Use this workflow before a proposal meeting. It is also useful when a board member, funder contact, or program lead sends a new opportunity and asks, "Can we apply?"

## Step 1: save the funder source

Start with the live source. Save the foundation page, request for proposals, email invitation, or portal instructions. If the page may change, save a PDF copy with the date.

Do not screen from memory. Foundation interests can change. Deadlines can move. Attachment rules can be strict. Your team needs the source in the grant file so the decision can be checked later.

Record these basics:

- Foundation name
- Program name
- Link to guidelines
- Deadline and time zone
- Award range, if shown
- Eligible geography
- Eligible organization type
- Required attachments
- Contact rules

This first step often catches a fast no. If the foundation only funds another state, another cause, or invited groups only, stop here.

## Step 2: check mission and program fit

Read the funder purpose next to the program plan. Do not stretch the program to fit the funder. A weak fit can lead to a proposal that sounds thin and a grant that is hard to report.

Ask simple questions. Does the program already exist or have an approved launch plan? Does the work serve the group named by the funder? Can the program owner explain the need, work plan, and result measures without inventing a new project?

If the answer is no, mark the risk. A grant can fund growth, but it should not create a program your team cannot run.

## Step 3: test the budget fit

A foundation grant can still be a bad fit when the program sounds right. Finance should check what the grant can pay for and what it leaves out.

Review salary, fringe, supplies, travel, contractor costs, indirect costs, and match requirements. If the foundation caps overhead or will not pay for staff time, write down how the gap would be covered.

This is where restricted funds matter. If the award would be restricted to a program, site, or period, finance needs a way to track spending and releases. GrantPipe can help connect donor CRM records, grant compliance notes, restricted funds, deadlines, reporting, and audit evidence in one place. The wording should stay practical: the tool helps organize the work, but finance still approves the accounting.

## Step 4: check deadline load

Look at the grant calendar. A deadline may be possible on paper and still be a bad choice this month.

Check staff time, board approval needs, partner letters, budget review, portal setup, and current reporting deadlines. If the proposal needs audited financial statements, Form 990, board list, insurance, or data attachments, name the owner for each item.

Do not use "we can probably get it" as the plan. Use names and dates.

## Step 5: score reporting risk

A good proposal win can become a hard grant if reporting was ignored. Read the reporting section before the go decision.

Look for outcome data, expense backup, site visits, payment timing, restricted use, and final report rules. Ask whether the team already collects the outcome evidence the funder wants. If not, estimate the work needed to collect it.

This is not legal or accounting advice. It is a planning check. For unusual terms, large awards, or unclear restrictions, ask the right advisor before the organization accepts the award.

## Step 6: make the decision

Use a short decision note. It should say go, no-go, or hold. Add the reason, reviewer names, and date.

A go decision should include the proposal owner, finance reviewer, deadline, budget due date, and first draft date. A no-go decision should be saved too. It helps the team avoid rechecking the same weak fit next month.

## Step 7: create the work record

If the decision is go, open the grant record before writing starts. Add the deadline, funder contact, attachment list, budget owner, reporting notes, and source link.

This keeps the proposal tied to the later award file. If the grant is awarded, the team already has the first audit evidence: the source, the decision, the budget review, and the deadline trail.

The best fit screen is plain. It helps a team say yes with a record, no with confidence, or hold until the missing facts are clear.
