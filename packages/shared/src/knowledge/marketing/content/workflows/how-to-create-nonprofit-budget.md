steps:
  - title: "Pull prior year actuals from accounting system by fund"
    content: "Export the prior year actual revenue and expenses from your accounting system segmented by fund - not just by department or program. Each restricted fund should appear as a separate column or segment. Look for three things: revenue that came in but was never reported (one-time windfalls), expenses charged to the wrong fund (misallocations that will repeat), and funds that ran a deficit (which signals a structural problem, not a one-year anomaly). This analysis takes two to four hours and produces the baseline that everything else is built against."
  - title: "List all confirmed and projected funding sources with restriction status"
    content: "Build a revenue schedule showing every funding source expected in the budget year. For each source, record: the amount, the restriction status (unrestricted, temporarily restricted by purpose, temporarily restricted by time), the confidence level (confirmed with signed agreement, likely with verbal commitment, uncertain with no commitment), and the renewal date. Confirmed multi-year grants count at their documented amount. Projected but unsigned grants count at 50-75% of their likely amount, not face value. An optimistic revenue schedule is the most common cause of a deficit budget."
  - title: "Build the personnel budget (salary and fringe rate applied per position)"
    content: "Personnel is typically 60-80% of a nonprofit budget and must be built at the position level, not as a lump sum. For each position, record the annual salary, the planned full-time equivalent (FTE), and the fringe benefit cost - either actual benefits by employee or the organization's pooled fringe rate applied to salary. Include open positions at the salary range midpoint. The output is a personnel budget showing total compensation cost per position before any grant allocation occurs. This is the total the organization owes regardless of how it is funded."
  - title: "Allocate personnel costs across program and admin functions per time allocation"
    content: "For each position, assign the percentage of time spent on each program, grant, and administrative function. A program manager at 60% on Grant A, 30% on Grant B, and 10% on administration has their salary and fringe allocated accordingly. The allocation percentages must add to 100% for each position. The output is a personnel allocation matrix - the key document connecting the personnel budget to the program and grant budgets. If time allocations are not based on how people actually spend their time, everything downstream is wrong."
  - title: "Build program budgets for each grant and fund, including indirect cost recovery"
    content: "For each active grant and restricted fund, build a line-item budget showing all direct costs charged to that fund: personnel (from the allocation matrix), direct program costs (materials, travel, subcontracts), and indirect cost recovery. Indirect recovery is calculated by applying the organization's indirect rate to the modified total direct costs base for that fund. The indirect recovery is revenue to the organization - it is how the organization covers overhead costs on restricted programs. If the grant does not allow indirect, note the shortfall and plan for unrestricted coverage."
  - title: "Build the operations and admin budget with shared cost methodology"
    content: "The operations and admin budget covers costs not directly charged to a specific program: executive leadership, finance, HR, facilities, technology, and development. Some of these costs are recovered through indirect charges built in Step 5; the remainder must be covered by unrestricted revenue. Document the methodology for shared costs - how rent, technology subscriptions, and support staff time are allocated across programs and administration. The methodology must be consistent year-over-year and documented for audit."
  - title: "Assemble the consolidated budget with restricted and unrestricted clearly separated"
    content: "Bring all components together into a single budget document. The consolidated budget shows: total revenue by source with restriction status; total expenses by program, function, and natural category (personnel, occupancy, supplies); and the net surplus or deficit by fund. Restricted funds must balance at or near zero - a significant surplus in a restricted fund signals either over-budgeted restricted costs or restricted revenue being used to cover unrestricted expenses, both of which are compliance problems."
  - title: "Run three scenarios: base, conservative, and optimistic"
    content: "The base scenario reflects confirmed funding and planned expenses. The conservative scenario removes the two most uncertain revenue sources and shows the deficit that would require a response - layoffs, program cuts, or reserve draw. The optimistic scenario adds one high-probability new source and shows what additional capacity the organization could build. The three scenarios are not predictions; they are decision tools. The finance committee uses them to set the minimum unrestricted operating reserve the organization will maintain before spending on growth."
  - title: "Present draft to ED and finance committee with variance explanation"
    content: "The finance staff presents the draft budget to the Executive Director and finance committee before board presentation. Walk through the revenue assumptions line by line, explain any variance from the prior year budget larger than 10%, and flag any structural concerns - revenue concentration, growing deficit in unrestricted funds, reserve below three months. The finance committee's job is to challenge assumptions, not approve line items. Record the decisions made and the assumptions changed."
  - title: "Submit to board for approval with written narrative"
    content: "Present the final budget to the full board for approval. The budget narrative (one to two pages) explains what the budget funds, what the key revenue risks are, what the reserve position is, and what decision the board is making when it approves the budget. The board does not approve line items - it approves the total budget, the reserve policy, and the authority levels for unbudgeted expenditures. After approval, distribute the approved budget to all program and department leads and set up monthly budget-vs-actual reporting."
faqs:
  - q: "When should we start the budget process?"
    a: "Start 90 days before the fiscal year begins. A September 1 fiscal year requires a budget process that begins in June: grant data collection in June, personnel budget in July, program and consolidated budget in August, board approval in late August or early September. Starting later compresses the process, produces shortcuts, and often results in a budget approved after the fiscal year has already started."
  - q: "What fringe rate should we use if we don't have a pooled rate?"
    a: "Calculate your actual fringe rate: total benefits paid in the prior year divided by total salaries paid. For most nonprofits this falls between 18% and 30%. Apply this rate uniformly to all positions in the budget. If your benefits structure varies significantly by employee (full-time vs. part-time, exempt vs. non-exempt), build actual benefits per employee rather than using a pooled rate. Document whichever method you use."
  - q: "How do we budget for a grant we expect to win but haven't received yet?"
    a: "Include pending grants at no more than 50% of the expected award, and only if you have submitted a proposal or been invited to apply. Do not budget for grants you plan to apply for but have not yet applied to. The conservative scenario should show the budget without the pending grant so the board can see the downside. If the pending grant is essential to a balanced budget, that is a structural risk the board needs to understand before approving."
  - q: "What is the right level of operating reserve?"
    a: "The standard guidance is three to six months of operating expenses in unrestricted liquid reserves. Organizations with highly variable revenue (heavy grant dependence, single major funder) should target six months. The board adopts a formal reserve policy stating the target range and the conditions under which reserve can be drawn. Once the reserve policy exists, every budget approval includes a check against it."
  - q: "Can restricted funds show a surplus?"
    a: "Temporarily restricted funds can carry a surplus from one year to the next if the restriction has not yet been satisfied - this is normal when a multi-year grant spans budget years. What you cannot do is use a restricted fund surplus to cover an unrestricted deficit; those funds must be spent in accordance with the restriction. If a restricted fund shows a large surplus and the grant period is ending, you have an unspent balance problem that needs to be resolved with the funder."

The nonprofit budget process has a sequencing problem that most finance staff encounter by accident: you cannot build a program budget before you know what the personnel costs are, and you cannot know what the personnel costs are before you know the funding mix, because the funding mix determines which grant pays for which position. Build in the wrong order and you will spend a week rebuilding the budget from scratch when a grant renewal comes back at a lower amount.

## When to run this workflow

Run this workflow once per fiscal year, 90 days before the fiscal year begins. Also run a mid-year budget revision if actual revenue or expenses have deviated from budget by more than 10% and the deviation is expected to continue. A budget revision is not a sign of poor planning - it is a sign that the organization is paying attention.

## Common pitfalls

**Budgeting revenue optimistically and expenses accurately.** Finance staff tend to be conservative with expenses and optimistic with revenue, which produces budgets that look balanced and run deficits. Apply the same skepticism to revenue assumptions that you apply to expense estimates.

**Mixing restricted and unrestricted funds in the consolidated view.** A budget that shows total revenue and total expenses without separating restricted and unrestricted funds can appear balanced while the unrestricted fund is running a structural deficit covered by restricted revenue. Restricted and unrestricted funds must be reported separately to show the real financial picture.

**Omitting indirect cost recovery from grant budgets.** Every grant that allows indirect cost recovery should have it built into the budget. An organization that undercharges indirect recovery is subsidizing its restricted programs with unrestricted funds and reducing its capacity to cover overhead.

**Building a single-scenario budget.** A budget with no scenario analysis gives the board no ability to manage risk. The three-scenario model is a minimum; organizations with significant revenue uncertainty should add a fourth scenario showing a major-funder-loss event.
