The final invoice closes the claim for grant costs. It should be slow enough to be right.

Use this workflow before submitting a final reimbursement request or final invoice.

## Step 1: confirm closeout instructions

Read the award closeout terms. Confirm due date, form, portal, attachments, and who can certify the invoice.

Also confirm whether a final financial report is due at the same time.

## Step 2: lock the ledger

Ask finance to confirm final costs are posted. Include payroll, vendor invoices, accruals, corrections, and indirect costs.

If entries are still pending, keep the invoice in draft.

## Step 3: review support

Tie each claimed cost to support. Remove costs outside the period unless the award allows them.

Check that late invoices are for work performed during the allowed period.

## Step 4: check reductions

Look for refunds, credits, unused advance cash, rebates, and known disallowed costs. These can reduce the final invoice.

Document the review even if there are no reductions.

## Step 5: compare final reports

Compare the invoice to the final financial report. If they differ, write a short reconciliation note.

Use the [final invoice guide](/resources/guides/federal-award-closeout-final-invoice-guide) for the full closeout review.

## Step 6: submit and save proof

Submit through the required system. Save the final invoice, support packet, approval, receipt, and funder response.

Then update the closeout checklist and retention date.

## Final invoice worksheet

Use one worksheet for the final invoice. It should show the path from the ledger to the claim.

| Field            | What to record                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Award            | Grant name, award number, and period. |
| Final cost total | Amount from the locked grant ledger.  |
| Exclusions       | Costs removed from the claim and why. |
| Indirect costs   | Rate, base, and calculation.          |
| Credits          | Refunds, rebates, or voided costs.    |
| Cash received    | Prior advances or reimbursements.     |
| Amount requested | Final invoice amount.                 |
| Reviewer         | Person approving the packet.          |

This worksheet should not be a second set of books. It should explain the final claim.

## Review late and unpaid costs

Late invoices are common at closeout. Look at the service date, not only the invoice date. If the service happened during the award period, the cost may be allowable if the award permits it and the support is strong.

Unpaid costs need a clear rule. Some funders allow accrued costs on the final claim. Others require payment before reimbursement. Check the award and portal instructions.

If the final invoice includes an unpaid cost, save later payment proof with the closeout file.

## Review credits and cash

Credits are easy to miss. Check vendor statements, credit card refunds, payroll reversals, returned supplies, and cancelled orders.

If the award used advance cash, compare cash received to final allowable costs. If cash remains, follow agency or pass-through instructions. Save the refund or adjustment record.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.344 covers closeout requirements. It does not mean every funder uses the same portal, form, or deadline. Award terms and pass-through instructions still matter.

2 CFR 200.403 covers allowability. A final invoice should not include a cost just because the organization wants to spend the remaining budget.

2 CFR 200.305 matters when the final invoice interacts with cash advances. If the organization has federal cash left, the closeout file should show the decision.

## Closeout packet checklist

Save these records:

- final invoice
- locked ledger detail
- support for sampled costs
- indirect cost calculation
- match support, if required
- credit and refund review
- cash reconciliation
- reviewer approval
- submission receipt
- funder response
- retention date

## Final review meeting

For larger awards, hold a short final review before submission. Finance should bring the invoice worksheet. Grants staff should bring the closeout requirements and report status.

Use the meeting to answer 4 questions:

- Are all final costs posted?
- Are any credits or refunds unresolved?
- Does the invoice tie to the final report?
- Is the support packet ready for audit or funder review?

If any answer is no, assign an owner and date. Submit after the record is clean, not just because the portal deadline is close.
