AmeriCorps member timesheets support service hour totals. They also support the story behind those totals: who served, when they served, what activity they recorded, and who approved it.

Treat reconciliation as a monthly control. If the team waits until a report is due, missing approvals and coding errors are harder to fix.

This workflow is about member service hours. For employee payroll charged to federal awards, see the [time and effort certification guide](/resources/guides/time-and-effort-certification-federal-grants).

## Step 1: gather the member file

Start with the member service agreement, position description, start date, expected term, and hour target. Confirm the member is active for the period being reviewed.

Then pull all timesheets for the month or pay period. Include electronic approvals, edits, and any rejected timesheets.

Create a checklist by member. Each member should have a service term, approved position, timesheet record, and supervisor approval.

## Step 2: check dates and term limits

Review service dates on each timesheet. Hours should fall inside the member's service term and inside the grant period.

Watch for hours before the start date, after the exit date, or during a suspended period. Also check for duplicate dates or unusually high daily hours.

If a timesheet includes a correction, save the corrected version and the reason.

## Step 3: check approvals

Each timesheet should follow the program's approval process. That may include member certification and supervisor approval.

Check that approvals are dated. Late approval is not always fatal, but it should be visible. Missing approval should be fixed before the hours are used for reporting.

Do not assume approval because the hours appear in a system export. Save enough detail to show who approved the record.

## Step 4: review activity coding

Member hours should match allowed service activity. Review descriptions or codes for items that look like fundraising, lobbying, political activity, religious instruction, or other activities that may be barred or restricted.

If the description is too vague, ask for clarification before accepting the hours. "Admin" or "event" may not be enough.

Keep notes short and factual. The goal is to show how the team reviewed the record, not to rewrite the member's daily log.

## Step 5: tie timesheets to hour totals

Compare approved timesheets to the program hour tracking report. Totals should agree by member and by period.

If the system total is different from the timesheet total, find the cause. Common causes include late timesheets, rejected records, duplicate entries, or manual adjustments.

The final report total should come from approved support. Do not use a dashboard number until it agrees with the timesheets behind it.

## Step 6: document exceptions

Create an exception list for missing approvals, date issues, coding questions, duplicate entries, or totals that do not agree.

Assign each exception to an owner. Record the fix and the date it was resolved. If an item cannot be fixed, remove the hours from the reporting total or document the management decision.

This list helps supervisors see patterns. One missing approval may be a mistake. Repeated late approvals may point to a training need.

## Step 7: save the reconciliation

Save the timesheets, approval records, hour total report, exception list, and final reconciliation worksheet.

Use a folder or file name that includes the program, month, and service year. Keep private member details limited to what the grant file needs.

## Before reporting

Before any report uses member hours, rerun the tieout. Confirm that all exceptions from the period are closed or excluded.

If the report uses cumulative hours, compare the new cumulative total to the prior report plus current approved hours. This catches duplicate or missing periods.

## Review patterns, not just errors

After the tieout, look for patterns. A single late timesheet may be a one-off issue. Many late timesheets from one site may mean supervisors need a clearer deadline.

Also look for repeated vague activity descriptions. If many entries say only "service" or "support," update training so members know what detail is needed.

Patterns matter because they help fix the process before the next report. They also show that the organization reviewed the system, not just one worksheet.

Save a short monthly note with the reconciliation. Include the number of member records reviewed, the number of exceptions, and the fixes made.

For broader award support, connect the hour file to the [grant documentation checklist](/resources/guides/grant-documentation-checklist-audit-ready).

GrantPipe can help keep service-hour support, report dates, and exception notes tied to the grant record. It does not decide whether an activity is allowable, but it can make the review trail easier to keep.
