Grant reimbursement backup should tell a reviewer one simple story: the cost belongs to the grant, the amount is correct, and the proof is saved.

The packet should start from accounting records. It should not start from a folder of invoices. The ledger is the base because it shows what the organization recorded. The backup then proves each line.

Use this workflow for federal, state, local, or foundation grants that repay costs after they are incurred. Always follow the funder agreement and written instructions.

## Step 1: confirm the request period

Start with the reimbursement period. Write the start date, end date, request number, funder, grant name, and budget version.

Then check the grant period. Do not include costs outside the allowed dates unless the agreement clearly permits them. If a cost is close to the edge of the period, add a note.

Also check whether the funder requires costs to be paid before reimbursement. Some requests need proof of payment. Others may allow unpaid invoices. The rule should be clear before the packet is built.

## Step 2: pull the ledger detail

Pull ledger detail for the grant and period. Include date, vendor, employee, account, budget line, memo, transaction ID, amount, and coding fields.

The request total should come from the ledger or a controlled reimbursement schedule. Do not type totals by hand if you can avoid it.

Mark each line as included, held, removed, or corrected. Held costs can wait for the next request. Removed costs should have a reason.

## Step 3: attach invoice and receipt support

For each non-payroll cost, attach the invoice, receipt, contract, purchase order, or other source record. The document should show what was bought, who billed it, the date, and the amount.

If the invoice covers more than one grant or program, add an allocation note. The reviewer should be able to see how the grant share was calculated.

If a receipt is unclear, add a plain note. Do not make the reviewer guess why the cost belongs in the request.

## Step 4: attach payment proof

If the funder requires payment proof, attach checks, ACH confirmations, credit card statements, bank detail, or other allowed records.

Match payment proof to the invoice. If one payment covers several invoices, provide a payment schedule. If one invoice is paid in parts, show each payment.

Redact sensitive data that the funder does not need. Keep enough detail to prove the transaction without exposing private information.

## Step 5: prepare payroll support

Payroll support should tie salary and fringe to the funded work. Depending on the grant and policy, support may include time records, effort reports, payroll registers, allocation schedules, or approved cost allocation methods.

Show the employee, period, gross pay or allocated amount, fringe basis, grant share, and support file.

Do not rely on job title alone. A title does not prove the cost belongs to the grant.

## Step 6: test allowability and budget fit

Review each line against the award budget and funder rules. Ask whether the cost is needed for the grant, inside the period, reasonable, supported, and charged to the right line.

For federal awards, 2 CFR 200.403 gives general factors for allowable costs. The award may add stricter limits.

Remove weak costs before submission. A smaller clean packet is better than a larger packet that gets returned.

## Step 7: reconcile the packet

The reimbursement form, support schedule, ledger detail, and backup should agree. If they do not, write the reason.

Check prior requests to avoid duplicates. Keep a request log with dates, amounts, costs covered, payment status, and open issues.

If a cost was corrected from a prior request, include a short note. Save both the old and new support when needed.

## Step 8: review before submission

Use a second review. Finance checks math and support. Program or grants staff confirm activity fit. The approver signs or approves in writing.

The approval should name the request amount, period, reviewer, date, and any costs removed.

GrantPipe can help keep reimbursement status, support files, reviewer notes, and grant deadlines together. That reduces the risk that a packet lives in one inbox with no shared record.

## Step 9: save the submitted version

Save the final packet exactly as submitted. Include the form, support schedule, ledger detail, backup, review notes, approval, and submission confirmation.

When payment arrives, save the payment record. If the funder pays a different amount, record the reason and any follow-up.

Good reimbursement backup is not fancy. It is complete, clear, and easy to trace.
