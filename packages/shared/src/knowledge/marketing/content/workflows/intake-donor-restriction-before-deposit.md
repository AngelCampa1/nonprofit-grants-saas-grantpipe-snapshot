The best time to catch a donor restriction is before the deposit is closed. Once cash is posted, the team may need a journal entry, a donor CRM correction, a new receipt, and a restricted fund cleanup. That work is avoidable when the intake process is clear.

This workflow helps development and finance review gifts before they become accounting problems. It is not legal or accounting advice. Use your policy and adviser for unusual gifts.

## Step 1: collect the source

Start with the document that came with the gift. It may be a check memo, donor letter, online form, email, grant notice, campaign pledge, or donor advised fund sponsor letter.

Save the source before coding. Do not rely on memory or a verbal note. If the source is unclear, mark the gift as unclear and hold the final coding review until the team gets more information.

GrantPipe can help keep donor CRM notes, restricted funds, deadlines, and audit evidence tied to one record. The workflow still starts with the human review of the source document.

## Step 2: classify the restriction

Use a short set of choices. Do not invent new labels each time.

Common choices:

- Unrestricted
- Purpose restricted
- Time restricted
- Purpose and time restricted
- Conditional or needs accounting review
- Unclear

A purpose restriction names how the money can be used. A time restriction limits when it can be used. A condition may affect when revenue is recognized. If the gift depends on a barrier or right of return, stop and ask for accounting review.

## Step 3: match the gift to a fund

Finance should assign the fund code before deposit close when possible. If the fund does not exist, decide whether to create one or use an existing fund with a clear subcode.

The coding review should include:

- Donor or sponsor name
- Amount
- Date received
- Restriction type
- Fund code
- Revenue account
- Campaign or appeal code if needed
- Receipt rule
- Reviewer name

Development can add relationship context. Finance owns the accounting treatment.

## Step 4: check the receipt rule

The acknowledgment should match the gift. A donor advised fund grant may need different handling than a direct gift. A restricted gift may need a note that thanks the donor for the purpose without adding unsupported tax language.

Save a copy of the receipt or acknowledgment. If the gift is unclear, do not send final wording until the restriction is resolved.

## Step 5: close the deposit batch

After review, close the deposit batch with the support attached. The deposit packet should show the gift source, fund code, receipt rule, and any open issue.

If one gift in the batch is unclear, do not let that delay clean gifts forever. Split the unclear item if your process allows it. Keep a review queue for unresolved gifts.

## Step 6: update restricted fund tracking

If the gift is restricted, add it to the restricted fund reconciliation. Note the release trigger. The trigger may be a date, a program cost, a campaign milestone, or another supported event.

This is where many errors start. The gift is coded as restricted, but no one sets a release plan. Then the balance grows until audit time. Add the release rule while the source is fresh.

## Step 7: review open items weekly

Unclear gifts need an owner. Review them weekly until resolved. The owner may need to contact the donor, sponsor, development officer, or executive director.

Do not leave "unclear" as a permanent status. It should mean the gift is waiting for a specific answer.

## Outputs

The finished workflow produces a reviewed deposit packet, a restriction intake log, fund coding approval, and receipt rule. It also creates audit evidence before the gift enters month-end close.

## Common failures

The most common failure is speed. Staff rush to deposit cash and skip the memo line or gift letter. Another failure is ownership. Development knows the donor intent, but finance posts the money without that context.

Other failures include using old fund codes, missing donor advised fund sponsor rules, sending the wrong receipt, and failing to add the gift to the restricted fund reconciliation.

## Automation ideas

Automation can flag words such as scholarship, building, campaign, program, grant, or restricted. It can route unclear gifts to finance and link deposit batches to fund records. It can also remind staff when a restricted gift has no release rule.

Automation should not decide donor intent by itself. It should bring the right document to the right reviewer before the deposit is closed.
