steps:
  - title: "Step 1: Define the upgrade-ready segment"
    content: "Pull donors who meet four criteria: at least two prior gifts in the last 24 months, no current major-gift cultivation in progress, last gift between $50 and $5,000 (the practical mid-level upgrade band), and not currently part of a monthly giving program. From this base, exclude planned-gift prospects, donors flagged as relationship-sensitive, and donors who increased their gift on their own in the last 12 months. The remaining segment is the upgrade pool. Sub-segment into three tiers: tier A (last gift $500-$5,000), tier B (last gift $250-$500), and tier C (last gift $50-$250). Tier A is small, high-value, and warrants individualized treatment. Tier B is the campaign workhorse - large enough for a structured mailing, small enough for personalized salutations and ask amounts. Tier C runs as a leaner email-driven sequence with calculated ask amounts but less personalization. Confirm the segment is genuinely separate from concurrent campaigns; running an upgrade appeal in parallel with a year-end appeal causes message collision and depresses response rates for both."
  - title: "Step 2: Calculate the personalized ask amount per donor"
    content: "Apply a calculated upgrade ask to each donor in the segment. The standard rule is most-recent gift plus 50 percent, rounded to a clean number. A $100 last gift becomes a $150 ask. A $250 last gift becomes a $400 ask. A $500 last gift becomes a $750 ask. Adjust upward for donors with multiple gifts in the last 12 months - two $250 gifts in the period suggest capacity for a $500 single gift more credibly than one $250 gift does. Adjust downward for newer donors (first gift was within the last 6 months); their pattern isn't established yet. Generate a single ask amount per donor and store it in the segment file. The ask amount appears on the reply device, the email, and the personalized landing page - consistency matters. Donors who receive three different ask amounts across channels (a common error from manual segmentation) trust the campaign less. The calculation rule is also documented in the file so the same logic applies to next year's upgrade appeal."
  - title: "Step 3: Draft the appeal narrative"
    content: "An upgrade appeal is more personal than an acquisition appeal and more ambitious than a renewal. The narrative structure: open with a specific donor-recognition line that references the donor's prior support without naming the exact gift amount; pivot to a concrete program impact story; make the upgrade ask explicitly with the calculated amount; close with a personal sign-off from the executive director or a program staff member. Avoid institutional language. Avoid mention of fiscal-year deadlines or budget gaps as the primary reason for the ask - donors upgrade for program impact, not to close an organizational shortfall. The appeal letter is typically 1.5 to 2 pages; the email version is 200-300 words. Both versions reference the same impact story and the same ask amount. A common error is over-writing the upgrade appeal - the donor already knows the organization, doesn't need a full case for support, and wants to see the impact and the ask quickly. A short, confident, specific appeal outperforms a long generic one consistently."
  - title: "Step 4: Build the multi-channel send sequence"
    content: "A standard upgrade sequence runs: direct mail letter (week 1) †’ email reinforcement (week 1, 2-4 days after mail in-home date) †’ email follow-up to non-responders (week 3) †’ optional phone call to tier-A non-responders (week 4). For tier C, the sequence may be email-only, accepting a lower response rate in exchange for lower per-donor cost. The mail piece is the anchor - the email reinforcement leverages the mail in-home date to drive response. Avoid over-sequencing; donors who receive five touchpoints from a single campaign in one month report appeal fatigue and may downgrade or unsubscribe. The reply device on the mail piece pre-prints the calculated ask amount and offers two alternative amounts (one above, one slightly below) to give donors a choice without abandoning the upgrade frame. The landing page mirrors the printed reply device - same ask amount, same impact story, same sign-off."
  - title: "Step 5: Run the campaign and track response in real time"
    content: "Track response daily during the active campaign window. Three metrics matter: response rate (donors who responded with any gift), upgrade rate (donors who responded at or above the calculated ask amount), and revenue lift (total revenue from the campaign minus what the same donors gave in the prior comparable period). A response rate of 5-8 percent is solid for this segment. An upgrade rate of 60-70 percent of responders is realistic - the remainder respond at or near their prior amount, which is still valuable. Tier-A donors who don't respond by week 3 are flagged for the optional phone call. Mid-campaign adjustments are limited; the largest lever is making sure the email follow-up actually sends on schedule. Avoid the temptation to add a 'last chance' email past week 4 - for an upgrade campaign, the additional email rarely lifts response and increases unsubscribe rate."
  - title: "Step 6: Acknowledge upgrades distinctly within 48 hours"
    content: "Upgraded donors get a distinct acknowledgment, not the standard thank-you. The acknowledgment names the upgrade specifically: 'You increased your gift this year from $250 to $400 - that increase makes a real difference in [program-specific outcome].' The acknowledgment is signed by the executive director, not the development office, and arrives within 48 hours. Standard acknowledgment goes to donors who responded at their prior amount (still valuable; just not an upgrade). Donors who responded at a level above the calculated ask receive a short personal note from the ED in addition to the standard upgrade acknowledgment - they signaled stronger capacity and engagement than the rule expected, and that signal warrants direct attention. The acknowledgment language is pre-drafted before the campaign launches; manual drafting at the moment of acknowledgment is what produces 7-day delays."
  - title: "Step 7: Plan the next 12 months for upgraded donors"
    content: "Upgraded donors need a deliberate stewardship plan for the next 12 months. Without it, upgrade revenue is vulnerable - donors who upgraded once and received generic stewardship are at higher risk of downgrading at the next ask cycle than donors who maintained their previous level. A standard upgraded-donor stewardship plan: month 3 personal program update from the ED, month 6 invitation to a program event or site visit, month 9 mid-year impact report referencing the upgraded gift, month 12 renewal appeal at the new level. The renewal ask defaults to the upgraded amount, not the original lower amount. Donors who upgraded once will accept a renewal ask at the upgraded level if the stewardship has been substantive; donors who upgraded once and didn't hear back substantively will revert. The post-campaign stewardship plan is the difference between a one-time upgrade and a permanent shift in donor value."

## What This Workflow Covers

A donor upgrade appeal is a deliberate ask to current donors to increase their giving. It is not a refresh of the year-end appeal sent to the same list at a higher amount. The segmentation is different, the ask amount is calculated per donor, the narrative is more personal, and the post-appeal stewardship is what makes the upgrade stick.

This workflow covers segmenting the upgrade-ready pool, calculating personalized ask amounts, drafting the appeal narrative, building the multi-channel sequence, running the campaign, distinct acknowledgment for upgraded donors, and the 12-month stewardship plan that protects the upgrade.

**Who runs this workflow:** The development director owns the campaign mechanics. The executive director signs the appeal and the acknowledgments. The database manager produces the segment and applies the ask-calculation rule.

**Estimated time:** 2-4 weeks for setup, 4-6 weeks of active campaign time, then ongoing stewardship.

---

## Step 1: Define the Upgrade-Ready Segment

Sample segment criteria:

- At least two prior gifts in the last 24 months
- Last gift between $50 and $5,000
- Not currently in major-gift cultivation (managed separately)
- Not currently a monthly donor (run a separate monthly upgrade flow)
- Not flagged as a planned-gift prospect

Exclude donors who increased their gift on their own in the last 12 months - they've already self-upgraded.

Sub-segment into:

| Tier | Last gift band | Channel mix                            |
| ---- | -------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| A    | $500-$5,000    | Personal letter + ED phone call backup |
| B    | $250-$500      | Direct mail + email + landing page     |
| C    | $50-$250       | Email-led sequence with calculated ask |

The [donor retention strategies guide](/resources/guides/donor-retention-strategies) covers the broader retention frame within which upgrade work sits.

---

## Step 2: Calculate the Ask Amount Per Donor

Standard rule: most-recent gift + 50 percent, rounded to a clean number.

Sample calculations:

| Last gift | Calculated ask | Notes                              |
| --------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| $100      | $150           | Standard tier-C upgrade            |
| $250      | $400           | Round up slightly for stronger ask |
| $500      | $750           | Standard tier-B upgrade            |
| $1,000    | $1,500         | Tier-A; consider personal touch    |
| $2,500    | $4,000         | Tier-A; ED phone call before mail  |

Adjust upward when the donor gave multiple times in the last 12 months. Adjust downward for new donors (first gift in the last 6 months).

---

## Step 3: Draft the Appeal Narrative

Sample appeal-letter opening (tier B):

> Dear [First Name],
>
> Your support of [organization] over the past few years has helped us [specific program outcome]. I'm writing today with a specific request: this year, would you consider increasing your gift to $400?
>
> Here's why the increase matters right now. [One concrete program impact story, 1-2 paragraphs.]
>
> Your gift of $400 this year would [specific impact tied to the increase]. Whatever you're able to give will be received with real gratitude - but if a $400 gift is within reach, it would make a meaningful difference.
>
> Thank you for your steady support.
>
> [ED first name]

The [case for support how to write](/resources/guides/case-for-support-how-to-write) covers the foundational narrative principles; the upgrade variant tightens them with a specific calculated ask.

---

## Step 4: Build the Send Sequence

Tier-B sample sequence:

| Week        | Touch                             | Channel     |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- | ----------- |
| 1           | Appeal letter (in-home)           | Direct mail |
| 1 (+3 days) | Email reinforcement               | Email       |
| 3           | Email follow-up to non-responders | Email       |
| 4           | Closeout email                    | Email       |

Tier-C sequence is email-only with the same logical sequence. Tier-A sequence adds an ED phone call before the mail in-home date for donors with capacity signals.

---

## Step 5: Track Response Daily

Three metrics:

- **Response rate.** Any gift in response to the campaign. Solid: 5-8%. Strong: 10%+.
- **Upgrade rate.** Donors who responded at or above the calculated ask amount, as a share of responders. Realistic: 60-70%.
- **Revenue lift.** Total campaign revenue minus prior-period revenue from the same donors.

A simple daily tracking sheet captures these three numbers. The [annual fund what it is how to build one](/resources/guides/annual-fund-what-it-is-how-to-build-one) covers the broader annual-fund context within which upgrade campaigns operate.

---

## Step 6: Acknowledge Upgrades Distinctly

Sample upgrade acknowledgment (sent within 48 hours, signed by ED):

> Dear [First Name],
>
> Thank you for your gift of $400 - and for the increase from your previous level. That kind of step up is meaningful and I want to acknowledge it directly.
>
> Your additional support this year goes toward [specific program impact]. I'll write again later this year with an update on what your gift made possible.
>
> With real gratitude,
> [ED signature]

Standard acknowledgments go to donors who responded at their prior amount. Donors who responded above the ask amount receive an additional short personal note from the ED.

## Definitions

**Upgrade ask.** A calculated ask amount above the donor's most-recent gift, designed to move the donor up one giving tier.

**Calculated ask amount.** The specific dollar figure generated by applying a rule (commonly last gift + 50 percent, rounded) to each donor in the upgrade segment.

**Mid-level donor.** A donor whose annual giving sits between the broad annual-fund tier and the major-gift tier - typically $100-$5,000 annually for a mid-sized nonprofit, varying by organization.
