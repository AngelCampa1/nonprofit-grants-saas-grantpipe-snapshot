A useful Form 990 board review packet is short, organized, and tied to decisions the board can actually make.

The board does not need every tax workpaper. It does need enough context to see whether the return is accurate, public-ready, and consistent with the organization's records.

## Before you start

Make sure the Form 990 draft is close to final. If revenue, expense, compensation, or governance answers are still changing, wait. Board members should not spend time reviewing a draft that staff already know will be rewritten.

Also confirm who owns the review. In many nonprofits, the finance lead prepares the packet. The executive director checks the public story. The treasurer leads the board discussion.

## Step 1: Put the draft first

The packet starts with the Form 990 draft. Do not split schedules into separate files unless your board portal requires it. Directors need to see the return as one public document.

Add a short cover note with:

- filing year
- preparer name
- filing deadline
- review meeting date
- sections the board should read first
- known open questions

This cover note keeps the review from turning into a scavenger hunt.

## Step 2: Add the financial tie-out

The financial tie-out is the most important support page. It should show how key Form 990 numbers connect to the records the board already knows.

Include:

- total revenue
- contribution and grant revenue
- program service expense
- management and general expense
- fundraising expense
- net assets with donor restrictions
- net assets without donor restrictions
- compensation totals, if material

If the audited statements and Form 990 do not match line by line, explain why. Some differences are normal. Unexplained differences are not.

## Step 3: Add grant and restricted fund support

Grant-funded nonprofits should include a restricted fund and grant revenue summary. This does not need every transaction. It should show the big picture.

Use a table with:

- major grant or fund name
- beginning restricted balance
- current-year additions
- releases or spending
- ending restricted balance
- final report or compliance status

## Step 4: Build the governance answer sheet

Part VI asks about board independence, policies, pay review, records, and Form 990 review.

Create one page with each key answer and its support. For example:

- conflict policy: approved board policy dated May 14
- whistleblower policy: employee handbook section 7
- compensation review: executive session minutes from March 3
- Form 990 review: draft sent to full board on July 10

If you cannot name the support, do not treat the answer as done.

## Step 5: Add related-party review

Related-party items need a clean check because they can create public trust issues. Ask about transactions with board members, officers, key employees, family members, and connected businesses.

The packet can use a simple checklist:

- any leases with insiders
- any consulting contracts with insiders
- any loans to or from insiders
- any grants to insider-connected groups
- any family employment relationships
- any business owned by a board member paid by the organization

If the answer is yes, add the support and ask the preparer how it appears on the return.

## Step 6: Review Schedule O language

Schedule O is where the organization explains its process. It should be plain and true.

Read the explanation of the Form 990 review process. Does it match what actually happened? If the draft says the full board reviewed the return, confirm the full board really received it before filing.

Also read any explanations tied to governance, compensation, or unusual transactions. These notes should be direct. Vague language makes a weak process look weaker.

## Step 7: Keep open questions in one place

Directors will have questions. Staff will have follow-ups. The preparer may need to change language. Put all of it in one open questions list.

Use four columns:

- question
- owner
- due date
- status

Do not file while material questions are still open.

## Step 8: Save the review record

After the board review, save the final packet and the meeting record. Keep:

- packet version sent to directors
- date sent
- attendance
- questions raised
- changes made
- final approval or acceptance record

This record supports the Schedule O explanation next year.

## How to keep the packet small

The board packet should not become a document dump. If a support item does not help the board check the public return, leave it out. Keep it available on request.

Keep support pages short. The Form 990 and required schedules will set the final packet length.
