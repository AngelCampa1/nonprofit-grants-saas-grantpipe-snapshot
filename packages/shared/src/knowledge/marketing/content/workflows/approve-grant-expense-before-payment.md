Approving a grant expense before payment is one of the simplest ways to prevent compliance cleanup. The review should answer whether the cost is allowed, budgeted, in period, supported, coded correctly, and approved by the right person.

This workflow applies to invoices, reimbursements, card charges, purchase requests, and other costs that may be charged to a grant or restricted fund. Use your award terms and policy as the final rule.

## Step 1: collect the source support

Start with the record that proves the cost. That may be an invoice, receipt, contract, purchase order, travel form, reimbursement request, payroll report, or vendor statement.

Check the vendor, amount, date, service period, item description, and requester. If the record does not explain what was bought or when the service happened, ask for more support.

GrantPipe can help keep payment support, grant budgets, restricted funds, approvals, and compliance files connected. The approval still starts with the source record.

## Step 2: confirm the business purpose

The requester should explain why the cost is needed. The purpose should connect to the grant work, not just to the organization in general.

Good purpose notes are short and specific. "Supplies for June youth workshop under Grant A" is stronger than "program supplies."

If the cost supports multiple grants or programs, note that now. Shared costs need allocation review before payment.

## Step 3: check allowability

Review the award terms, cost rules, and organizational policy. For federal grants, 2 CFR 200.403 points to standards such as necessary, reasonable, allocable, consistent, and documented.

Ask whether the cost type is allowed, whether it needs prior approval, and whether the amount is reasonable. If the award bars the cost, stop the payment from that grant. If another funding source can pay, route it there with clear coding.

Do not treat budget room as proof that the cost is allowed.

## Step 4: check the grant period

Review the service date, delivery date, travel date, or payroll period. The invoice date alone is not enough.

Confirm that the cost fits the award period and any closeout rules. If the payment is near the start or end date, check for pre-award approval, closeout limits, or delayed invoice rules.

If the period is unclear, hold the approval until the requester or vendor clarifies it.

## Step 5: check budget fit

Compare the cost to the approved budget line. Review the current balance, cumulative spending, line item limits, and any amendment history.

If the cost would exceed the budget line, do not approve by habit. The next step may be a budget revision, funder approval, another funding source, or a leadership decision to absorb the cost with unrestricted funds.

Save the budget check. A screenshot, system note, or exported report can work if it shows the date and grant.

## Step 6: review coding

Check the natural account, grant code, fund code, program, department, restriction code, and reporting category.

Coding should match both accounting needs and funder reporting needs. If the ledger account does not map cleanly to the funder report, save the mapping.

Wrong coding can create errors in reimbursement requests, restricted fund balances, board reports, and audit samples. Fix it before payment.

## Step 7: handle shared costs

If the cost benefits more than one grant or program, prepare the allocation before approval. The file should show the allocation basis, calculation, grants charged, and support for the basis.

Use benefit as the anchor. Hours, units, users, square footage, or participant counts may fit different costs. Do not allocate based only on which grant has money left.

Attach the allocation worksheet to the payment record.

## Step 8: approve or reject

The approver should see the source support, purpose, allowability review, budget check, period check, coding, and allocation support if needed.

Approval evidence should show the approver, date, amount, grant, and decision. If the approver rejects or reduces the amount, document why.

If emergency payment is allowed, use a written emergency path. The file should still show who approved the exception and when the missing support must be completed.

## Step 9: release payment and file evidence

After approval, release payment through the normal accounts payable process. Save the payment reference with the approval file.

The final file should allow someone to trace the cost from source record to approval to payment to ledger entry.

## Step 10: review exceptions

Each month, review exceptions. Look for late approvals, missing support, wrong coding, out of period costs, and costs that needed prior approval.

Use the findings to update forms, training, thresholds, or system rules. The point is to prevent repeat errors, not to create a separate report that nobody uses.

The completed workflow produces an approved payment file, budget and period check, coding review, allocation support when needed, and dated approval evidence.
