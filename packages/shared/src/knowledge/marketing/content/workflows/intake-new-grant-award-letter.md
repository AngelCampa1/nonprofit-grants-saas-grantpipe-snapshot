A new award letter feels like the finish line. It is really the start of grant management.

The first hour after award matters. This is when the team should capture terms, assign owners, set report dates, and decide how the money will be tracked. If those steps are skipped, the grant can start with confusion.

Use this workflow when an award letter, award notice, grant agreement, or contract arrives. The goal is to turn the document into work the team can manage.

## Step 1: save the award file

Save the award letter in the grant file before forwarding it around. Use a clear file name with the funder, program, award date, and version.

Keep the proposal and final budget nearby. The intake is not only about the new letter. It is also about checking whether the award matches what the organization requested.

If the letter came by email, save the email or a PDF copy. If acceptance happens in a portal, save the portal receipt or screen export.

## Step 2: capture the basic terms

Create a short award summary. Include the funder, award name, award number if one exists, amount, grant period, budget period, start date, end date, and main funder contact.

Then capture the payment method. Some grants pay up front. Some reimburse after costs are paid. Some pay by milestone. This affects cash flow and how finance schedules work.

Do not rely on memory for the dates. Copy them into a calendar and keep the source page or letter section in the file.

## Step 3: compare the award to the proposal

Awards do not always match the proposal. The funder may reduce the amount, change the period, remove a budget line, add reporting terms, or require a new condition.

Compare the award to the proposal budget and work plan. Ask:

- Did the amount change?
- Did the grant period change?
- Did match change?
- Did reporting change?
- Did the funder add limits?
- Did the funder require approval before certain costs?

If anything changed, pause setup until the right people review it.

## Step 4: review restrictions

Write the restriction in plain words. For example, "Funds must support after-school tutoring staff from July 1 through June 30."

Then decide how finance will track it. The method may use a restricted fund, grant code, class, project, or another field. The method should let finance answer how much was received, spent, released, reimbursed, and remaining.

GrantPipe can help connect the award, restricted fund, budget, report dates, and source files. The team still needs to decide the internal coding rule before transactions start.

## Step 5: review payment and cash rules

Payment terms affect operations. Reimbursement awards may require the nonprofit to spend first. Advance awards may require close cash control. Milestone awards may depend on program proof.

Finance should review cash timing before the program starts spending. If the grant creates a cash gap, leadership should know early.

Also check whether the award requires a separate bank account, interest treatment, draw request, invoice package, or proof of payment.

## Step 6: build the report calendar

List every due date. Include program reports, financial reports, reimbursement requests, board updates, renewal dates, and closeout dates.

For each date, assign an owner and reviewer. Then set an internal due date before the funder due date. Reports need time for data cleanup, finance review, and approval.

If the funder requires a report form or portal, save the form or link. Do not wait until the due date to learn the fields.

## Step 7: assign setup tasks

Create tasks for finance, program, grants, and leadership.

Finance may need to set up codes, funds, budgets, billing rules, and report templates. Program staff may need attendance logs, case note rules, outcome measures, or demographic fields. Grants staff may need report dates, contact notes, and document folders.

Leadership may need to approve match, policy exceptions, or contract terms.

## Step 8: record acceptance

Before signing or accepting, check the internal approval policy. Some awards need board or executive approval. Some contracts need legal review.

Save the acceptance record. This can be a signed agreement, board minute, email approval, or portal confirmation.

If the team accepts with known risk, write the risk down. Examples include unrecovered costs, tight cash timing, unclear data, or a short report window.

## Step 9: hold a start meeting

Hold a short start meeting with finance, program, grants, and the owner of reporting. Review the award summary, restrictions, payment terms, report calendar, and evidence rules.

End the meeting with clear next steps. The grant should not depend on one person remembering a detail from the award letter.

A clean award intake makes the first report easier. It also gives the team a shared record when the funder asks a question months later.
