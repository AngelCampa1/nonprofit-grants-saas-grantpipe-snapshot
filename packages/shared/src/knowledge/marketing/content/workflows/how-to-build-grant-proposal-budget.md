steps:
  - title: "Start with the scope of work, not a budget template"
    content: "Before opening a budget spreadsheet, read the full scope of work and produce a list of every activity the proposal describes, the staff involved in each activity, and the approximate duration. This activity inventory is the foundation the budget is built on. A budget built from a prior-year template or a standard overhead model will include costs not connected to the scope and miss costs that are. The activity inventory takes 30-60 minutes and prevents a week of budget revisions after the narrative is written."
  - title: "Calculate personnel costs: salary, fringe rate, and time allocation for each position"
    content: "For each position involved in the project, calculate the project cost as: annual salary - FTE allocation percentage - project period fraction - (1 + fringe rate). A program coordinator at $52,000 annual salary, 50% time on the project, for 12 months, with a 28% fringe rate costs $52,000 - 0.50 - 1.00 - 1.28 = $33,280. Use the current salary for each named position; use the midpoint of the salary range for positions that will be hired for the grant. Document the calculation for each position - the budget narrative will reproduce it in sentence form."
  - title: "Calculate direct costs: materials, travel, equipment, subcontracts"
    content: "For each non-personnel direct cost category, build the calculation from the activity inventory. Travel is calculated by trip: destination, number of travelers, purpose, estimated cost per trip. Materials are itemized by type and quantity. Equipment over the threshold requiring prior approval (typically $10,000 per item for federal grants, or a lower organizational capitalization threshold) is listed separately and flagged. Subcontracts are listed by entity with the scope and estimated value. Do not lump costs into broad categories without itemization - 'program supplies: $8,000' is a weaker budget line than three specific supply categories totaling $8,000."
  - title: "Calculate indirect costs: apply your rate to the MTDC base"
    content: "Indirect costs are calculated by applying your organization's indirect rate to the modified total direct costs (MTDC) base. The MTDC base includes direct salaries, wages, and fringe benefits but excludes equipment, capital costs, patient care, tuition, and subcontracts over $50,000. If your organization has a NICRA (negotiated indirect cost rate agreement) with a federal agency, use that rate. If your organization has never negotiated a rate, the 2024 Uniform Guidance de minimis rate is 15% of MTDC. If the funder prohibits indirect costs or caps them, calculate both the full indirect cost and the allowed amount - the gap is a real cost the organization must cover from unrestricted funds."
  - title: "Cross-check every budget line against the narrative (each dollar must trace to an activity)"
    content: "Build a two-column crosswalk: left column lists every budget line; right column identifies the specific narrative section or activity that justifies the cost. If any budget line has no corresponding narrative reference, either the budget line needs to be removed or the narrative needs to be updated to describe the activity. Then run the reverse check: for every narrative activity that involves spending, confirm there is a corresponding budget line. The crosswalk is a working document, not a deliverable - it is how you verify alignment before submission."
  - title: "Write the budget narrative: explain every line in one to three sentences"
    content: "The budget narrative is a line-by-line explanation of the budget. Each line gets one to three sentences explaining what it is, how the cost was calculated, and why it is necessary for the project. For personnel: 'The Project Coordinator (0.5 FTE, $26,000 salary + 28% fringe = $33,280) will manage client intake, maintain program records, and coordinate with partner organizations as described in Section III of the narrative.' For travel: 'Two staff will travel to the state capital twice annually for funder-required convenings. Cost is estimated at $450 per trip - 4 trips = $1,800.' The narrative is how reviewers verify that the costs are reasonable and allocable."
  - title: "Flag lines that require prior approval (equipment over $10,000, foreign travel, subcontracts over $150,000)"
    content: "Federal grants and many private foundations require prior written approval before incurring certain cost categories, regardless of whether they are in the approved budget. Prior approval requirements typically include: equipment purchases exceeding $10,000 per item, or a lower award or organizational capitalization threshold, any foreign travel, subcontracts exceeding $150,000 (federal awards, per 2 CFR 200.318), and changes in key personnel. Identify every budget line that falls into a prior approval category and note it in the budget narrative with the statement: 'Prior written approval will be sought before incurring this cost.' Knowing the prior-approval requirements before the award is made prevents a compliance issue after the award."
  - title: "Review against the funder's budget guidelines for prohibited cost categories"
    content: "Each funder publishes cost categories that are prohibited - costs they will not reimburse under any circumstances. Common prohibitions include: lobbying activities, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, fundraising costs, and depreciation on assets purchased before the grant period. Federal funders publish allowable cost guidance in 2 CFR 200 Subpart E. Private foundations publish restrictions in their grant guidelines. Review each budget line against the funder's prohibited list before submission. A budget that includes a prohibited cost category is declined, even if the prohibited cost is a small percentage of the total."
  - title: "Have someone who did not write the narrative check the budget for alignment"
    content: "Ask a colleague - the finance director, another grants staff member, or the ED - who did not write the proposal narrative to read the budget and budget narrative against the scope of work. Give them the crosswalk from Step 5 and ask them to flag any line they cannot connect to a described activity. This reviewer is looking for two things: budget lines with no narrative home, and narrative activities with no budget line. A fresh reader finds alignment gaps the writer has become blind to. This review takes 30-45 minutes and consistently catches at least one material gap."
  - title: "Submit with the budget narrative as a separate attachment if the funder requires it"
    content: "Most federal funders require the budget narrative as a separate document from the line-item budget. Check the submission guidelines: some require a separate PDF for the budget narrative; others embed it in the application portal. Submit both the line-item budget (in the required format - SF-424A for most federal programs, or the funder's required Excel template) and the budget narrative as specified. Update your grants register with the submission date, the total ask amount, and the decision date. If the proposal advances, the approved budget becomes the budget ceiling for the award - preserve the exact version submitted."
faqs:
  - q: "What fringe rate should we use if we do not have a pooled rate?"
    a: "Calculate your actual fringe rate from the prior year: total benefits paid divided by total salaries paid. For most nonprofits this falls between 18% and 30%. Apply this rate consistently across all positions in the budget. If your benefits vary significantly by employee type, build actual benefits by position rather than using a pooled rate. Document the calculation in the budget narrative. A pooled rate that is calculated and documented is defensible; a rate pulled from memory is not."
  - q: "Can we use the 15% de minimis indirect rate on private foundation grants?"
    a: "The de minimis rate is a federal Uniform Guidance provision applicable to federal awards. Private foundations set their own indirect cost policies. Some accept the de minimis rate; many cap indirect at 10% or 15% by their own policy; some prohibit indirect entirely. Check the funder's guidelines. If the funder caps indirect below your actual indirect cost rate, note the gap in your budget tracking - it represents a real subsidy from unrestricted funds."
  - q: "What if the grant budget total exceeds the award amount we are requesting?"
    a: "This is a cost-sharing or matching scenario. If the project costs $200,000 and you are requesting $150,000, the remaining $50,000 is your match. Show both columns in the budget: amount requested from funder and total project cost. Identify the source of your match contribution. If the match source is another grant, verify that grant allows cost-sharing. Federal awards require match sources to be documented at the time of award and tracked throughout the period - phantom match commitments are an audit finding."
  - q: "How do we budget for a position that will be hired for the grant?"
    a: "Use the salary range midpoint for the position as currently budgeted in your compensation structure. Note in the budget narrative that the position will be recruited following award and that the salary listed is the midpoint of the approved range. If the actual hire comes in above the budgeted midpoint, a budget modification may be required. Budgeting at the range minimum understates the true cost; budgeting at the maximum creates an unrealistic ask. The midpoint is the standard."
  - q: "What happens if we spend less than the budget in a cost category?"
    a: "Budget flexibility depends on the funder and the award terms. Many federal awards allow reallocation within approved budget categories without prior approval for variances below 10% of the total award. Variances over 10% in any single category, or any reallocation into a prohibited category, typically require prior written approval. Private foundations generally allow more flexibility. Review the award terms for the specific flexibility provisions before reallocating mid-award."

Grant proposal budgets are compliance documents, not just financial estimates. The budget is an offer to a funder: for this amount of money, you will produce these activities, employing these staff, using these materials, according to this timeline. A budget with unexplained lines, inflated indirect, or costs disconnected from the narrative is an offer the funder cannot accept - not because the costs are wrong, but because the reviewer cannot verify that they are reasonable and allocable.

## When to run this workflow

Run this workflow for every grant proposal, regardless of whether the funder requires a budget narrative or just a budget form. The narrative is as much for internal alignment as for reviewer review - the process of explaining every line in writing catches calculation errors, alignment gaps, and unallowable costs before they become problems after the award.

## Common pitfalls

**Building the budget before the narrative is complete.** A budget built before the narrative is finished will have lines that do not match the final scope. Build a preliminary budget from the activity inventory, write the narrative, then finalize the budget against the completed narrative.

**Using round numbers throughout.** A budget where every line ends in ,000 signals that the costs were not calculated - they were estimated. Reviewers who see a budget of $50,000 in personnel, $10,000 in travel, $5,000 in supplies, and $8,000 in indirect assume the numbers are aspirational. Calculated costs that end in odd numbers are more credible precisely because they appear to have been computed.

**Treating the budget narrative as optional.** Even when the funder does not require a budget narrative, the crosswalk in Step 5 should be completed internally. The internal version becomes the budget narrative if the funder does require it and serves as the budget justification file for auditors if the award is received.

**Omitting prior-approval flags.** A grant-funded purchase of equipment over $10,000 made without prior approval - even if it is in the approved budget - can be a questioned cost under federal awards. The prior-approval flag in Step 7 is a compliance safeguard built into the proposal stage.
