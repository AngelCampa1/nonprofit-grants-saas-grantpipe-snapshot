Reviewing a subaward invoice before payment protects the grant, the pass through organization, and the subrecipient. The review should confirm that the invoice is tied to an active agreement, fits the approved budget, covers an allowed period, reflects real progress, includes required support, and has no unresolved monitoring issue that affects payment.

This workflow is for nonprofit teams that pay subrecipients. Follow the signed subaward, funder terms, and monitoring plan first.

## Step 1: log the invoice

Record the date received, invoice number, subrecipient, grant, invoice period, amount requested, and due date. Save the invoice in the grant or subaward file.

If the invoice lacks a period, agreement reference, authorized signer, or cost detail required by the agreement, return it for correction before review.

GrantPipe can help keep subaward invoices, monitoring notes, grant budgets, and follow-up tasks near the grant record. That reduces the chance that finance pays while program still has open concerns.

## Step 2: match the subaward agreement

Open the signed agreement and amendments. Check payment terms, budget categories, deliverables, reporting requirements, backup requirements, and special conditions.

The invoice should follow the agreement. If the agreement requires a certified invoice or specific backup schedule, the invoice should include it.

Do not approve a cost because it seems reasonable if the agreement requires a different format or approval path.

## Step 3: check the billing period

Confirm that the invoice period fits the subaward period and parent grant period. Watch for overlapping invoices, late invoices, and costs outside the allowed dates.

If the invoice includes work near the end date, check closeout rules. Late billing can create closeout pressure and may affect the parent award.

Hold unclear periods until the subrecipient clarifies the service dates.

## Step 4: compare to the budget

Review each invoice line against the approved budget. Check current period amount, cumulative billed amount, remaining balance, and line item limits.

If the invoice shifts money between categories, look for an approved budget modification. A request on the invoice is not the same as approval.

For milestone agreements, compare the amount to the milestone schedule. For cost reimbursement agreements, compare the amount to actual cost support.

## Step 5: review required backup

Backup should match the agreement and risk level. Required support may include general ledger detail, payroll reports, receipts, invoices, participant records, match support, mileage logs, or deliverable evidence.

Do not ask for extra records without a reason. Also do not skip required records because the subrecipient has been reliable in the past.

If backup is sampled, document the sample, records reviewed, findings, and conclusion.

## Step 6: check performance

Program or grants staff should confirm that required reports are current and deliverables are on track. The invoice may be accurate mathematically but still raise a performance concern.

Compare the invoice to recent reports, service counts, milestones, site visit notes, and open corrective action items.

If performance is behind, decide whether the agreement allows payment, partial payment, hold, or escalation.

## Step 7: review monitoring status

Check the subrecipient risk file before approval. Open findings, late reports, audit issues, expired insurance, missing certifications, or unresolved corrective actions may affect payment.

For federal awards, subrecipient monitoring requirements under 2 CFR 200.332 make this review part of the pass through role. The invoice file should show that monitoring status was considered.

If the monitoring plan requires extra review for high risk subrecipients, complete that review before payment.

## Step 8: decide the payment action

There are usually three outcomes: approve, hold, or reject.

Approve when the invoice meets the agreement, budget, period, support, performance, and monitoring checks.

Hold when support is missing or an issue needs correction. Document the held amount, reason, owner, and due date.

Reject when the invoice is not payable under the agreement or includes costs that cannot be fixed.

## Step 9: save approval evidence

Approval should show who reviewed finance, who reviewed program performance, who approved payment, the amount approved, and the date.

If the approved amount differs from the requested amount, save the explanation. Partial approvals should be easy to trace.

Attach payment confirmation after payment is released.

## Step 10: update monitoring records

After the decision, update the subrecipient file. Note missing support, exceptions, late reports, or corrective actions. If the issue repeats, update the risk rating or monitoring plan according to policy.

The finished workflow produces a reviewed invoice file, budget comparison, support review, performance review notes, monitoring status check, and payment decision.

Common failures include paying before program review, ignoring open findings, assuming budget revisions were approved, and failing to track held amounts. A clear workflow prevents those mistakes from becoming audit findings.
