Grant funded staff time certification connects payroll to actual grant work. It should show who worked, what grant benefited, which period was charged, how the cost was allocated, who reviewed it, and what changed if corrections were needed.

This workflow is for nonprofit teams that charge salaries or wages to grants. It is useful for federal awards, foundation grants, restricted funds, and cost reimbursement contracts. Follow your award terms and policy first.

## Step 1: collect payroll records

Start with the payroll register for the review period. Confirm employee name, pay period, gross pay, fringe if charged, department, grant code, fund code, and account.

Then collect the time or activity records that support the payroll charge. This may include timesheets, activity reports, case assignments, work logs, or certifications.

GrantPipe can help connect payroll support, grant budgets, files, and review tasks. The certification still needs a reviewer who can confirm the work.

## Step 2: confirm staff assignment

Compare the employee's charged time to the approved grant budget, job role, workplan, and any staffing changes.

If the grant funds a case manager, the record should support case management work for that grant. If the employee changed roles during the period, the certification should reflect the change.

Do not certify a staff member based only on the original budget if the real work changed.

## Step 3: check the period

The work period should fit the grant period. If a payroll cycle crosses the grant start or end date, split the time correctly.

Also check whether the work happened before or after closeout. Some closeout work may be allowed if the award terms allow it. Other work may need another funding source.

If the period is unclear, pause certification until the time record is corrected.

## Step 4: confirm grant benefit

The time record should show that the grant benefited from the work. The detail needed depends on the role.

For hourly staff, timesheets may be enough if they identify the grant or activity. For salaried staff working across multiple grants, activity records or allocations may need more detail.

If a description says only "program work," ask whether the program maps to the grant. The reviewer should be able to explain the charge later.

## Step 5: review allocation

For split funded staff, review the allocation basis. The split may use actual hours, activity records, caseload, work assignments, or another approved basis.

The allocation should reflect actual work or a supported method allowed by policy and award terms. Do not use a budget percentage when the work record shows a different pattern.

Check fringe treatment too. If fringe follows salary, the worksheet should show that. If fringe uses a separate method, save the support.

## Step 6: check coding

Review the grant code, fund code, program, department, natural account, and restriction code. Check whether the payroll charge maps to the right funder report category.

Payroll coding errors can affect reimbursement requests, budget to actual reports, restricted fund balances, and audit samples.

If coding is wrong, prepare a correction before certification is final.

## Step 7: get supervisor review

A supervisor or responsible official should review the time record if policy requires it. The reviewer should know enough about the employee's work to confirm the charge.

Finance should review math and coding. Grants staff may need to review award terms and budget categories.

The certification should show the period, employee, grant, amount or hours, reviewer, date, and approval decision.

## Step 8: document corrections

If time was charged to the wrong grant, document the correction. Save the original entry, corrected entry, reason, support, approval, and date.

Avoid vague correction notes. "Moved 8 hours to Grant B based on corrected timesheet" is much better than "payroll cleanup."

Corrections near report deadlines or closeout should be reviewed against submitted reports and reimbursement requests.

## Step 9: retain the file

Save the payroll register, time record, allocation worksheet, certification, approval, and correction records. The file should let a reviewer trace the payroll cost from work performed to grant charge.

Retention rules depend on award terms and record type. For federal awards, document retention rules can extend after closeout in some cases.

## Step 10: review trends

Each month, compare current payroll allocations to prior periods and budget expectations. Large changes may be correct, but they should have a reason tied to work.

Common failures include missing supervisor approval, stale allocation percentages, unsupported corrections, and payroll periods that cross grant dates without a split.

The completed workflow produces a certified staff time file, allocation worksheet, payroll coding review, supervisor approval, and correction record when needed. That evidence is much easier to keep current than rebuild later.
