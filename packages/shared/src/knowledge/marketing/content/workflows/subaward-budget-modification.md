steps:
  - title: "Receive and document the modification request"
    content: "Require all subaward modification requests in writing. The subrecipient should provide the current budget, the requested revised budget by category, the dollar amount of the change, the justification for the modification, and certification that the requested change is allowable under the prime award terms. An email is acceptable documentation; a formal change-request form is better."
  - title: "Review the prime award terms for prior approval requirements"
    content: "Check your prime award agreement, Notice of Award, and any applicable program-specific requirements for what budget changes require prior federal approval. Under 2 CFR 200.407, the general categories requiring prior approval include: changing the scope of the project, acquiring equipment not in the original budget, and transferring funds into participant support costs. Also check whether your prime award has waived any of these requirements for your organization."
  - title: "Apply the 10% budget flexibility rule"
    content: "Under 2 CFR 200.308(e)(4), non-federal entities may transfer funds among direct cost categories when the total of transfers does not exceed 10% of the total award budget and the change does not alter the project scope. If the requested subaward modification falls within this threshold and does not change scope, you may approve it without passing the request up to the federal agency. Document the calculation."
  - title: "Determine pass-up approval requirement"
    content: "If the modification exceeds the 10% threshold, changes the scope, involves equipment purchase not originally budgeted, or affects any category specifically restricted in the prime award, you must request prior approval from the federal awarding agency before approving the subrecipient's request. Notify the subrecipient of the expected timeline - federal prior approval can take four to twelve weeks depending on the agency."
  - title: "Request federal prior approval if required"
    content: "Submit a written request to the federal program officer or grants management specialist. The request should include: the nature of the modification, the subrecipient's justification, the impact on project scope and outcomes, and your recommendation as the pass-through entity. Keep the request in writing; verbal approvals are not sufficient under 2 CFR 200."
  - title: "Issue the subaward amendment"
    content: "Once you have authority to approve - either within your own authority or after receiving federal approval - issue a formal subaward amendment. The amendment must reference the original subaward number, specify the effective date of the budget change, replace or append the original budget exhibit, and be signed by authorized officials of both the pass-through entity and the subrecipient."
  - title: "Update the subrecipient monitoring file"
    content: "File the amendment in the subrecipient's monitoring folder alongside the original subaward agreement. Update your subaward tracking system with the revised budget by category.   - title: "Notify the subrecipient and confirm closeout alignment"
    content: "Confirm with the subrecipient that the revised budget will be reflected in their next financial progress report. If the modification occurs near the end of the subaward period, confirm that the revised categories are achievable within the remaining period of performance. A budget modification approved two weeks before closeout that cannot produce actual expenditures is a paper exercise with no value."

Managing subaward budget modifications is one of the more procedurally precise responsibilities a pass-through entity carries. The approval authority question - what you can approve yourself versus what requires federal sign-off first - determines whether subrecipient spending is authorized or questioned. Getting it backward is a finding either way.

## When to run this workflow

Run this workflow whenever a subrecipient submits a request to change their approved budget - regardless of the dollar amount. Even a small reallocation between categories requires checking whether the change is within the pass-through entity's self-approval authority. Common triggers include: subrecipients facing unexpected cost overruns in one category, project scope refinements that shift resources, and personnel changes requiring budget reallocation between salary lines.

The earlier in the subaward period a modification is requested, the more room exists to seek federal prior approval if needed. Modifications requested in the final 60 days of a subaward period create time pressure that makes compliance harder. Build modification timelines into the subaward monitoring calendar from day one.

## Common pitfalls

**Approving first, documenting second.** A verbal agreement to allow a budget change, followed by written amendment paperwork weeks later, leaves a gap in the audit trail. The amendment effective date must precede the expenditures made under the modified budget.

**Misapplying the 10% flexibility rule.** The 10% threshold is calculated against the total federal award budget - not the individual budget category being changed. An organization with a $500,000 award can transfer up to $50,000 across categories without federal prior approval, but only if the change does not alter scope.

**Failing to assess scope impact.** The most common error is approving a reallocation that effectively changes what the subrecipient is doing without recognizing it as a scope change. If the modified budget funds a new activity not described in the approved scope of work, federal prior approval is required even if the dollar amount is small.

**Not updating the subrecipient monitoring file.** Amendment paperwork filed in accounting but not in the monitoring file creates inconsistencies. Auditors reviewing Single Audit workpapers check that monitoring files reflect current award terms, including all executed amendments.

## Audit trail requirements

Subaward modifications require a complete paper trail from request to execution:

- The subrecipient's written request with budget and justification
- Your internal review noting the applicable regulatory authority for approval
- Federal prior approval correspondence (if required)
- The executed amendment signed by both parties
- Confirmation that the subrecipient's monitoring file was updated

For Single Audit purposes, auditors testing pass-through compliance will pull subaward files and verify that all amendments are present, authorized, and executed before the expenditures they authorize were incurred. Retroactive amendments are a red flag.
