Restricted grant cash can look simple in the bank and messy in the books. Cash may arrive before expenses. Expenses may post before reimbursement. Releases from restriction may happen after finance confirms the work was done.

The reconciliation answers one plain question: how much grant cash is still restricted, and why?

This workflow is for monthly close, reporting prep, and audit support. It works with the [restricted fund tracking guide](/resources/guides/restricted-fund-tracking) and the [net asset release workflow](/resources/guides/net-asset-release-workflow).

## Step 1: list cash receipts by restriction

Start with deposits. For each restricted grant receipt, capture the funder, award, amount, date, restriction, and bank reference.

Do not stop at the bank feed. Tie each receipt to the award agreement or donor restriction. If a deposit includes several awards, split the amount in the worksheet.

For federal awards, separate advances from reimbursements. The cash rule in 2 CFR 200.305 matters when federal cash is drawn before disbursement.

## Step 2: compare cash to award records

Compare total cash received to the award budget, payment schedule, and drawdown history.

Ask:

- Did the organization receive more cash than expected?
- Is any expected payment missing?
- Was cash posted to the right fund?
- Does the award have matching or cost-share terms?
- Are there pass-through payment notices to save?

If cash was posted to the wrong fund or grant, prepare a correcting entry with support.

## Step 3: tie expenses to allowable work

Pull expenses by grant and fund. Review whether the costs match the restriction and award terms.

For federal grants, costs should be allowable, allocable, reasonable, and supported. 2 CFR 200.302 also expects financial records that identify the source and use of funds for federal awards.

If costs are coded to the restricted fund but do not fit the grant, move them before release entries are approved.

## Step 4: review release entries

A release from restriction should follow supported spending or another term that satisfies the restriction. It should not be booked just to match a budget.

For each release entry, tie the amount to eligible expenses or the award condition that was met. Save the support.

If the release is partial, show the remaining restricted balance. Leadership should be able to see what is still limited and what can be used.

## Step 5: identify open obligations

Some restricted cash may be tied to work already committed but not paid. List open purchase orders, contracts, payroll commitments, or subrecipient obligations.

Do not treat these as spent unless your accounting policy and award terms support that treatment. But do use them to explain why cash remains restricted.

This helps the grants manager plan reports and helps finance avoid using restricted cash for general needs.

## Step 6: resolve differences

Differences usually fall into a few buckets: timing, coding, missing support, wrong fund, wrong grant, or release entry error.

Write the reason next to each difference. Then assign the fix. If the fix needs approval, note who owns it and when it is due.

The final worksheet should show beginning restricted cash, receipts, eligible spending, releases, remaining obligations, corrections, and ending restricted cash.

## Step 7: review monthly

Make the review part of month-end close. The finance reviewer should sign off. Grants staff should review open reports, budget changes, and closeout dates.

If the organization waits until audit, people will be reconstructing old decisions. Monthly review keeps the record fresh.

## Questions for leadership

Restricted cash affects more than accounting. It affects what cash leadership can safely use.

Give leadership a short view after reconciliation. Show cash that is restricted, cash expected soon, cash tied to open obligations, and cash that may become available after releases. Do not bury this in ledger codes.

If the organization is tight on cash, this view matters. Restricted dollars may be in the bank but unavailable for payroll, rent, or general bills. A clean reconciliation helps the executive director avoid treating restricted cash as flexible cash.

## Watch for common errors

Common errors include posting a restricted receipt to unrestricted revenue, releasing restriction before costs are supported, missing a refund, or coding expenses to the wrong fund.

Another common error is losing the link between the grant and the donor restriction. A grant can have both program terms and accounting restriction terms. Keep both in view.

If the same error appears more than once, fix the process. Add a receipt coding review, a release checklist, or a month-end grant cash review. The goal is fewer surprises at report time.
