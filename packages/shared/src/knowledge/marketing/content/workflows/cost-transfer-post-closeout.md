steps:
  - title: "Confirm the specific cost and why transfer is needed"
    content: "Document exactly which cost needs to transfer: the original posting date, the amount, the account, and the grant it was incorrectly charged to. Then identify the grant it should have been charged to, confirming that grant's period of performance included the date the expense was incurred. A cost incurred after a grant's period of performance cannot be transferred to that grant regardless of administrative convenience."
  - title: "Assess whether the 120-day closeout window is still open"
    content: "Under 2 CFR 200.344, the standard federal closeout window is 120 calendar days after the period of performance end date. If the final FFR and final performance report have already been submitted and accepted, the award is administratively closed. Determine the award's current status - if closeout is in progress, a transfer may still be possible within the window with agency notification; if the award is fully closed, federal prior approval is required for any adjustment."
  - title: "Contact the federal program officer immediately"
    content: "Do not attempt to process the transfer unilaterally. Contact the federal program officer (for programmatic questions) and the grants management specialist (for financial authority) in writing by email. Describe the situation, the amount, the affected awards, the reason the error occurred, and what corrective action you are requesting. An early notification that demonstrates transparency is treated more favorably than a transfer request that arrives after the auditors have found the problem."
  - title: "Prepare the prior approval request package"
    content: "Formal requests should include: a written narrative explaining the error and the proposed correction, copies of the original expenditure documentation (invoice, payroll register), copies of the general ledger entries showing where the cost is currently coded, a statement that the expenditure was incurred during the period of performance of the receiving grant, and confirmation that the cost is allowable, allocable, and reasonable under both awards' terms."
  - title: "Await written federal authorization"
    content: "Do not process the cost transfer entry until you receive written authorization from the federal awarding agency. The authorization must come from an official with grants management authority - a program officer's verbal concurrence or email saying 'sounds fine' is not sufficient. Request a formal letter or written determination on agency letterhead or official email. Average response times range from 30 to 90 days."
  - title: "Book the cost transfer journal entry"
    content: "Once you have written authorization, book the transfer entry. The entry credits the grant that currently holds the cost (returning the expenditure) and debits the correct grant. Attach the federal authorization letter, the original expenditure documentation, and your prior approval request to the journal entry record. Date the entry accurately - do not backdate to the original expenditure date."
  - title: "Prepare and submit an amended final financial report"
    content: "If the cost transfer changes the financial figures previously reported on the final SF-425, you must submit an amended FFR through the federal payment system or grants management portal. Coordinate with the grants management specialist on the timing and submission process - some agencies require the amended FFR before closing out the corrected financial records."
  - title: "Update the grant file and document lessons learned"
    content: "File all post-closeout transfer documentation in both the closed award's file and the receiving award's file. Document what internal control failure allowed the misposting to go undetected until after closeout, and what process change will prevent recurrence. Auditors reviewing a post-closeout transfer will look for evidence that the organization identified and corrected the root cause."

Post-closeout cost transfers are the compliance scenario most organizations hope never to face. They require admitting an error to a federal agency after formally certifying the award's financial records were correct, then waiting weeks or months for written authorization before the error can be fixed. The best prevention is rigorous reconciliation that catches the problem before closeout.

## When to run this workflow

Run this workflow the moment you discover a potential misposting on a federal award that has already entered the closeout process - or one that closed within the past year. Time matters: an award within the 120-day closeout window is far easier to correct than one that is fully closed. Even an award in final report review is preferable to a fully closed award, because the records are still considered open for administrative purposes.

Also run this workflow if an auditor identifies a misposting during Single Audit fieldwork. The auditor finding it first does not change the approval requirement, but it does change the context - you will be correcting under scrutiny rather than proactively.

## Common pitfalls

**Attempting the transfer without written approval.** Booking the journal entry before receiving written federal authorization creates a second compliance problem on top of the original misposting. The correcting entry is unauthorized. Always wait for written approval.

**Requesting approval without adequate documentation.** A request that says "we made an error and need to fix it" without supporting documents will generate follow-up questions that add weeks to the timeline. Include everything in the initial request.

**Missing the 120-day window.** Organizations that discover closeout errors in the course of annual audit preparation - which often happens six to eight months after fiscal year end - may find that the affected award has already passed the 120-day window. Monthly reconciliation catches these errors while the window is still open.

**Assuming verbal approval from a program officer is sufficient.** Program officers are often knowledgeable and helpful, but they typically do not have independent financial authority to authorize post-closeout adjustments. A written determination from the grants management specialist or contracting officer is required.

**Failing to amend the FFR.** If the transfer changes the figures previously reported, the final FFR is misstated and must be amended. Leaving the FFR as submitted while correcting the general ledger creates a permanent inconsistency between the organization's books and the federal reporting record.

## Audit trail requirements

A complete post-closeout transfer file must contain:

- Written federal agency prior approval (signed, dated, on agency letterhead or official email)
- Original expenditure documentation for the transferred cost
- General ledger entries showing the original posting and the corrected posting
- The cost transfer journal entry with preparer and reviewer signatures
- Amended FFR submission confirmation if applicable
- Root cause analysis memo explaining how the original error occurred and what controls changed

File this documentation in both the closed award's file and the receiving award's file. Auditors will look for it in both places.
