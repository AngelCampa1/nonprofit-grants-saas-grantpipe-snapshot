steps:
  - title: "Step 1: Build the renewal calendar 90 days out"
    content: "Open the original grant agreement and locate the end date, the next reporting deadline, and any renewal-cycle language. Work backward from the funder's renewal submission window, not your fiscal calendar. Plot four checkpoints in your project plan: outcomes pull (T-90), narrative draft (T-60), budget refresh (T-45), and final review (T-14). Add the program officer's name, the portal URL, and the most recent docket calendar to the file. If the funder has a published renewal cycle (most family foundations do), confirm it against the funder's most recent letter - schedules shift more often than they used to. Block the calendar checkpoints onto the development director's and grants manager's calendars now. Renewals miss because no one owned the start date, not because the work itself was hard. The 90-day runway also gives you time to surface a problem early - a missed outcome, a budget overrun, a staffing change - and address it in the renewal narrative rather than have the funder discover it during review."
  - title: "Step 2: Pull outcomes against the original proposal's promises"
    content: "Open the original proposal and locate the outcomes section, the logic model, and any quantitative targets you committed to. Build a two-column comparison: what was promised, what was achieved. Pull the underlying data from your program system or outcomes tracker. If you committed to serving 200 clients and served 187, write 187. If you committed to a 70 percent completion rate and the actual rate was 64 percent, write 64 percent. Resist the impulse to round up or to substitute a different metric than the one you originally promised. Where targets were missed, prepare a one-paragraph explanation: what happened, what you learned, what you'll do differently in the renewed period. Where targets were exceeded, prepare a similar paragraph - funders want to know whether the over-performance was a sign of unmet demand, a sign that the original target was set too low, or a sign of stronger-than-expected program effectiveness. The outcomes pull is the foundation of the renewal narrative; do it before you write a word of new copy."
  - title: "Step 3: Reconcile the grant budget against actual expenditures"
    content: "Run a fund-level expenditure report for the grant period. Reconcile it against the original budget categories. Identify variances above 10 percent in either direction by category - personnel, fringe, travel, supplies, indirect, and any program-specific lines. Document the cause for each material variance: a delayed hire, a vendor change, a program redesign, a procurement that came in under bid. If you transferred costs between categories during the period, confirm the transfers are documented in the activity log. If the funder required prior approval for budget revisions and you obtained it, locate the approval letters and add them to the renewal file. A clean financial reconciliation is what separates a renewal that gets funded from one that gets a follow-up call. Funders read the variance commentary closely; vague variance explanations are the second-most-common reason for renewal pushback after missed outcomes."
  - title: "Step 4: Draft the renewal narrative around what changed"
    content: "The renewal narrative is structured differently from the original proposal. Open with a brief recap of the funded period: what you set out to do, what you achieved, what you learned. Move into the renewed period: what's the same, what's different, and why. Funders fund continuity, but they also fund organizations that adapt. A renewal narrative that reads like a verbatim resubmission of the original signals that the program team didn't reflect on the past year. A renewal narrative that introduces an entirely new program signals that the original investment didn't take hold. Aim for the middle: clear continuity with deliberate adjustments. Cite the outcomes from Step 2 directly in the body - not as an appendix. Address any missed targets in the narrative itself. Update the timeline, the staffing chart, and the partnerships section to reflect current reality, not the original proposal's reality. The renewal narrative is typically 30-50 percent shorter than the original proposal because the funder already understands the organization and the core program."
  - title: "Step 5: Refresh the budget for the renewed period"
    content: "Build the renewed-period budget from the bottom up, using the actual run-rate from the funded period as the starting point - not the original budget. Adjust personnel for raises, fringe rate updates, and any staffing changes. Refresh non-personnel categories using the most recent actuals. Update the indirect cost calculation if your federally negotiated rate (NICRA) changed or if the de minimis rate applies (15 percent under the 2024 Uniform Guidance update; previously 10 percent). Build in a cost-of-living adjustment where the funder's renewal cycle has historically allowed it; do not assume an increase the funder did not signal. Cross-check the total against the original ask and against the funder's typical renewal ceiling. If you're requesting an increase, prepare a one-paragraph justification tied to specific program changes - not a generic 'inflation' argument. Submit the budget in the funder's required template, even if you also maintain a more detailed internal version."
  - title: "Step 6: Quality-check, route for approval, and submit"
    content: "Two weeks before the deadline, route the renewal package - narrative, budget, outcomes summary, and any required attachments (current 990, audit, board list, organizational chart) - to the executive director and finance lead for sign-off. Confirm the funder's portal accepts your file formats. Run the narrative through a final consistency check: numbers in the narrative should match the budget, dates should match the calendar, and named staff should match the staffing chart. Submit at least 48 hours before the deadline. Funder portals fail under load in the last 24 hours of any submission window. After submission, capture the confirmation receipt and the timestamp in the grant file. Send a brief follow-up email to the program officer confirming submission. Move the calendar entry for next year's renewal start date forward 12 months - closing the loop is what makes the next renewal start on time instead of late."
  - title: "Step 7: Plan the post-decision response - funded or not"
    content: "Pre-build two short responses before you hear back. If the renewal is funded, the response is a same-day acknowledgment to the program officer, a same-week internal kickoff for the renewed period, and an updated entry in your funder stewardship calendar for the new reporting cycle. If the renewal is not funded, the response is a 24-hour acknowledgment, a 30-day request for a debrief call with the program officer, and an internal review of the renewal package against the funder's stated reasons for declining. Most non-renewals are not personal; the funder's docket changed, the program area shifted, or another applicant fit the cycle better. A respectful debrief and a relationship that survives the no positions you for a future cycle. The post-decision step is what separates a transactional grant relationship from a sustained one."

## What This Workflow Covers

A grant renewal is the moment a funder decides whether the program they funded last cycle deserves another. It is not a low-stakes administrative refresh - it's the most concentrated test of whether your outcomes, your financial discipline, and your ability to articulate what changed match the relationship the funder thought they were entering.

This workflow covers building a 90-day renewal runway, pulling outcomes against the original proposal, reconciling the budget, drafting a renewal-specific narrative, refreshing the budget for the next period, and managing the post-decision response.

**Who runs this workflow:** The development director or grants manager owns the calendar and narrative. The finance lead owns the budget reconciliation and refresh. The program staff lead provides the outcomes and the narrative input on what changed.

**Estimated working time:** 60-120 minutes of concentrated workflow time per renewal, excluding the underlying outcomes gathering and financial reconciliation, which depend on the maturity of your tracking systems.

---

## Step 1: Build the Renewal Calendar 90 Days Out

The single most reliable predictor of whether a renewal lands well is when you started. Renewals that start at the 90-day mark have time to absorb a missed outcome, a financial variance, or a staffing change. Renewals that start at the 30-day mark do not.

Open the original grant agreement and pull three dates: the grant end date, the next required report due date, and any renewal-cycle language the funder included. Add the program officer's current contact information and the funder portal URL.

Set four checkpoints, each on the calendar:

- **T-90 days:** Outcomes pull complete
- **T-60 days:** Renewal narrative first draft complete
- **T-45 days:** Budget refresh complete
- **T-14 days:** Internal review and sign-off complete, submission within 48 hours of deadline

The renewal calendar lives next to the rest of your funder stewardship work. The free [Funder Stewardship Calendar Template](/free/funder-stewardship-calendar-template) is built around this 90-day cadence and includes the outcome-pull, narrative, and budget checkpoints as recurring events.

---

## Step 2: Pull Outcomes Against the Original Proposal

Open the original proposal. Locate the outcomes section, the logic model, and any quantitative targets. Build a two-column comparison: what was promised, what was achieved.

Sample comparison row:

| Promised                       | Achieved   | Variance commentary                                            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Serve 200 unduplicated clients | Served 187 | Q3 staffing gap reduced intake; corrected in Q4 with new hire  |
| 70% program completion rate    | 64%        | Cohort 2 had higher external dropout; revised intake screening |
| 3 community partnerships       | 4          | Added unplanned partnership with regional health system        |

The variance commentary is what funders read. Do not over-explain wins or minimize misses. Both signal that the program team is operating with data.

---

## Step 3: Reconcile the Grant Budget

Run a fund-level expenditure report covering the grant period. Reconcile against the original budget by category.

A typical reconciliation produces three groupings:

- **On-budget categories** (within ±10%): No commentary required.
- **Underspent categories** (>10% under): Explain the cause and whether unspent funds were returned, reallocated with funder approval, or are expected to spend down before period end.
- **Overspent categories** (>10% over): Explain the cause and confirm the offsetting reduction in another category if applicable. Confirm any required prior-approval transfer is documented.

Use the [grant lifecycle guide](/resources/guides/grant-lifecycle-guide) for the underlying reconciliation and audit-binder structure. The reconciliation file feeds both the renewal and any year-end audit.

---

## Step 4: Draft the Renewal Narrative

A renewal narrative is shorter and more focused than the original proposal. A typical structure:

1. **Recap (1-2 paragraphs):** What we set out to do, what we achieved, what we learned.
2. **Continuity (2-3 paragraphs):** What is the same in the renewed period and why.
3. **Adjustments (1-2 paragraphs):** What is changing and the reasoning.
4. **Outcomes plan (1 paragraph):** Targets for the renewed period, with a note on whether they're held flat, increased, or refined based on what you learned.
5. **Sustainability (1 paragraph):** How this funded period fits into the program's longer trajectory.

Funders renew programs that demonstrate reflection. The [grant proposal writing guide](/resources/guides/grant-proposal-writing-guide) covers the narrative principles in more depth; the renewal version applies the same principles with the addition of explicit reflection on the prior period.

---

## Step 5: Refresh the Budget

Build the renewed-period budget bottom-up from current actuals, not from the prior budget.

Sample renewal-budget refresh checklist:

- Personnel adjusted for actual salaries plus annual increases
- Fringe rate updated to current organizational rate
- Indirect cost rate confirmed (NICRA letter date or de minimis confirmation under 2 CFR 200.414)
- Travel updated using current GSA per diem and lodging rates
- Vendor lines updated against current quotes (procurement files attached)
- Cost-of-living adjustment justified or omitted
- Total cross-checked against funder's renewal ceiling

If your organization expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards (the single audit threshold for fiscal years ending September 30, 2025 or later - previously lower), audit costs may be allocable to federal awards on a pro-rata basis under 2 CFR 200.425. The [grant management best practices guide](/resources/guides/grant-management-best-practices) covers indirect-cost mechanics in more detail.

---

## Step 6: Quality-Check, Route, and Submit

Two weeks before the deadline, the renewal package is in final-review state, not first-draft state. Late drafting produces internal-consistency errors that funders catch.

Final QA checklist:

- Narrative numbers match the budget
- Outcomes table matches the program system pull
- Named staff match the current organizational chart
- Funder-required attachments included (audit, 990, board list, fiscal sponsor letter if applicable)
- Funder's narrative length and format requirements met
- Portal upload tested at least 48 hours before deadline

Submit, capture the confirmation receipt, and email the program officer with a one-line acknowledgment that the package is in.

---

## Step 7: Plan the Post-Decision Response

The post-decision response is pre-built before you hear back. Two short templates:

**If funded:**

> Thank you for the renewed support. We've received the award notification and will be in touch within the week with the kickoff plan for the renewed period. Looking forward to continuing the work.

**If not funded:**

> Thank you for the consideration and for the time you and the committee gave to our application. We'd value a brief debrief call when your schedule allows - even ten minutes of feedback would help us strengthen future applications. Either way, we appreciate the relationship and will keep you posted on the program's progress.

The not-funded response that asks for a debrief - without arguing the decision - preserves the relationship for a future cycle. Most foundation program officers report that organizations that handle a no professionally are over-represented among future grantees.

---

## Definitions

**Renewal narrative.** The proposal-equivalent document submitted for a renewal cycle. Differs from an original proposal in that it explicitly addresses the funded period's outcomes and reflects on what changed.

**LYBUNT (in renewal context).** "Last Year But Unfortunately Not This Year" - though more commonly used for individual donors, the same concept applies to lapsed institutional funders whose renewal cycle was missed.

**Variance commentary.** The category-by-category explanation of where actual spending differed from budgeted spending in a grant period.
