Federal cash should not be drawn just because the award has budget left. It should be drawn because the organization has a supported need.

This workflow helps finance test the timing before submitting the request.

## Step 1: list the costs

Start with costs that are supported and tied to the award. Remove estimates that are not ready.

For reimbursement grants, use incurred costs. For advance grants, use costs that will be paid soon.

## Step 2: check the payment date

Write the expected payment date for each cost. The draw should be close to the payment need.

If a cost will not be paid soon, do not include it unless the award gives different written instructions.

## Step 3: review current cash

Check whether prior federal cash is still on hand. If cash remains from a prior draw, explain why more cash is needed.

This step protects the organization from drawing too early.

## Step 4: approve the request

Have a reviewer check the worksheet, award terms, and support before submission.

Save the approval with the draw packet.

## Step 5: reconcile after receipt

After the draw is paid, match the request to the payment system, bank deposit, and ledger entry.

If the draw differs from the request, add a note.

## Cash timing worksheet

Use a short worksheet before each draw.

| Field        | What to enter                                      |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Award        | Grant name and award number.                       |
| Draw method  | Advance, reimbursement, or mixed.                  |
| Cost list    | Payroll, vendor, travel, or other supported costs. |
| Support      | Ledger, invoice, payroll, or approved obligation.  |
| Payment date | Date the cost was paid or will be paid.            |
| Prior cash   | Federal cash still on hand, if any.                |
| Draw amount  | Amount requested.                                  |
| Reviewer     | Person approving the request.                      |

This worksheet should be saved with the draw packet. It gives the next reviewer the reason for the timing.

## Advance draws need a tighter note

For an advance draw, the note should explain why cash is needed now. A payroll draw may be tied to the next payroll date. A vendor draw may be tied to approved invoices due this week.

If the organization draws 2 weeks before it plans to spend the money, write the reason. If there is no reason, wait.

If the organization already has federal cash on hand, explain why the current balance is not enough or why it is reserved for other approved payments.

## Reimbursement draws need a support check

For reimbursement, timing risk is lower, but support risk remains. Confirm costs were incurred, paid if required by the funder, coded to the award, and allowed by the award.

Do not include estimates. Do not include costs from outside the period unless the award allows them. Do not include costs still under dispute.

## Source-backed caveats

2 CFR 200.305 addresses federal payment timing. It expects cash requests to be close to actual disbursement needs. The award and agency payment system may add stricter steps.

2 CFR 200.302 requires financial records that identify the source and use of federal funds. The draw packet should show both.

2 CFR 200.303 covers internal controls. A reviewer approval and reconciliation record help prove the control operated.

## After the draw

The draw is not finished when cash arrives. Reconcile the deposit to the bank. Post it to the ledger. Match the draw to the costs it paid. Save the bank record and ledger entry.

If cash remains after the planned payments, note why. If the balance stays open, review it at month end and before the next draw.

GrantPipe should not replace finance judgment here. It can keep the timing worksheet, support, approval, and reconciliation visible so staff do not rebuild the draw history from scratch.

## Month-end review

At month end, compare all draws to payments. Look for cash that arrived but did not leave the bank soon after. Also look for payments made before the draw that should have been treated as reimbursement.

Save a short month-end note when there is a timing difference. The note should say the amount, reason, planned action, and owner.

This review is useful before financial reports. It keeps the cash record, ledger, and report from drifting apart.
