bluf: "Once you elect additive or deductive treatment, it is binding for the life of the award. Under 2 CFR 200.307, program income - revenue earned by the grantee that is directly generated by activities under the federal award - must be tracked, reported, and disposed of according to a treatment election made at award time. The additive-vs-deductive election is made up front and most federal program officers never ask about it until audit - at which point changing it requires retroactive correction."
steps:
  - title: "Identify all program income sources under each award"
    content: "Program income is revenue generated from activities that are supported by the federal award - patient care fees at a federally-funded health center, admission fees at a grant-funded exhibition, tuition at a federally-funded training program, rental income from a federally-funded facility. It does not include unrelated income (a bake sale to support the organization generally), interest earned on advance drawdowns (handled separately under 2 CFR 200.305), or proceeds from property purchased entirely with non-federal funds."
  - title: "Confirm the income disposition election in the award"
    content: "Federal awards may specify the required treatment method, or may leave the election to the grantee. The three treatments under 2 CFR 200.307 are: additive (program income is added to the federal award amount and used to further the project), deductive (program income is deducted from the federal award amount, reducing the drawdown), and matching (program income is used to meet a cost-share requirement). If the award does not specify, deductive is the default under 2 CFR 200.307(e)(1) unless the program office has approved otherwise."
  - title: "Establish the program income ledger"
    content: "Set up a tracking ledger or account that records: the award number, the income source description, the date earned, the amount, and the cumulative total by period. Program income must be segregated from the organization's general operating revenue - it is federal money earned under the award's authority, not unrestricted income. If it is commingled with operating revenue, it becomes invisible to auditors and unfindable for FFR reporting."
  - title: "Record program income as earned"
    content: "Book program income when earned, not when collected - the accrual basis applies. A patient visit on September 30 generates program income on September 30, even if the insurance payment arrives in November. The earning event is providing the federally-funded service or product; the receipt of payment is a separate cash event. The distinction matters for FFR reporting alignment."
  - title: "Apply the disposition treatment to expenditures"
    content: "Under additive treatment: spend program income on additional allowable project costs. Track these expenditures separately so you can show that the program income funded additional work, not costs that would have been incurred anyway. Under deductive treatment: reduce the next federal drawdown by the program income amount. The program income offsets the federal share. Under matching treatment: document program income as the matching contribution on the FFR."
  - title: "Report program income on the quarterly FFR"
    content: "The Federal Financial Report (SF-425) has a dedicated section for program income: Line 10 (Program Income Earned) and Line 10a (Program Income Expended). Report the cumulative program income earned through the end of the reporting period and the cumulative amount expended under the applicable disposition method. Zero on Line 10 when you have known program income sources is an incomplete FFR. Auditors cross-reference Line 10 against your accounting records during Single Audit testing."
  - title: "Track post-award period program income if applicable"
    content: "Under 2 CFR 200.307(f), income earned after the award period of performance - from activities funded during the award - may still be subject to the income rules, depending on the federal program and award terms. Check the award for post-award period income requirements. For some programs (certain HUD and USDA awards), program income earned after the period ends must still be reported and applied per the original election."
  - title: "Reconcile program income to the general ledger and FFR quarterly"
    content: "At each FFR due date, reconcile the program income ledger to the GL account, confirm the disposition was applied correctly (additional expenditures, draw reduction, or match documentation), and verify that FFR Lines 10 and 10a reflect the correct cumulative amounts. A program income balance remaining at year-end that is not reflected on the FFR is a reporting error that auditors find during Single Audit testing of the SF-425."
faqs:
  - q: "What is the default program income treatment if the award is silent?"
    a: "Deductive treatment, under 2 CFR 200.307(e)(1). The organization must reduce its federal drawdowns by the amount of program income earned. If the organization intends to use additive or matching treatment instead, it must receive prior written approval from the federal program officer at or before the time of the award. An unapproved additive treatment is a compliance finding."
  - q: "Does interest earned on advance drawdowns count as program income?"
    a: "No. Interest earned on advance federal funds is handled separately under 2 CFR 200.305(b)(9), not as program income. Interest on advances must be remitted annually to HHS Program Support Center for amounts above $500 per year. Program income is revenue from activities funded by the grant, not from holding federal cash."
  - q: "What if we earn more program income than anticipated in the budget?"
    a: "Unexpected program income is still subject to the disposition rules. Under additive treatment, the excess may expand the scope of the project (with program officer awareness). Under deductive treatment, it reduces the federal share correspondingly. Notify your program officer of material differences from projected program income - large unplanned income sometimes triggers a formal program amendment."
  - q: "Does program income reduce indirect cost charges?"
    a: "Not automatically. Under additive treatment, indirect costs may be charged on program income expenditures at the approved rate, increasing total indirect cost recovery. Under deductive treatment, the reduced drawdown reduces the indirect cost base, lowering indirect recovery. The indirect cost treatment of program income depends on the award terms and the indirect rate agreement."
  - q: "Do we need to report program income if the amount is small?"
    a: "Yes, if the award and regulations require it. There is no de minimis exemption for program income reporting under 2 CFR 200.307. Even $200 of patient fees or rental income generated under a federally-funded activity is program income that must be tracked and reported on the FFR. Omitting small amounts because they seem immaterial is a compliance error, not a judgment call."

  - term: "Program income"
    definition: "Gross income earned by a non-federal entity that is directly generated by a supported activity or earned as a result of the federal award during the period of performance. Defined and regulated under 2 CFR 200.307."
  - term: "Additive treatment"
    definition: "A program income disposition method where income is added to the total award amount and used to fund additional allowable project activities, increasing total project scope."
  - term: "Deductive treatment"
    definition: "A program income disposition method where income is subtracted from the total federal expenditures, reducing the amount of federal drawdown required. The default under 2 CFR 200.307(e)(1) when the award is silent."
  - term: "Matching treatment"
    definition: "A program income disposition method where income is counted as the non-federal share of project costs, satisfying a cost-share or matching requirement of the award."
  - term: "Post-award period income"
    definition: "Program income earned after the period of performance ends, from activities that were supported during the award period. Subject to reporting requirements under certain federal programs per 2 CFR 200.307(f)."

Under deductive treatment, Line 10a offsets the federal expenditures, reducing the effective federal share. Under additive treatment, Line 10a adds to the total expenditures.     answer: "Changing from one disposition method to another requires written federal agency prior approval. An unapproved mid-award change is not recognized by the federal agency - the original election remains binding, and any activity inconsistent with it is a compliance finding. Plan the election carefully at award time."

Program income reporting is one of the least understood compliance requirements in federal grant management. Many organizations earn it - patient fees, admission charges, training tuition, facility rentals - and either do not recognize it as program income or do not track it consistently enough to report accurately on the FFR. Auditors know this pattern and test program income specifically as a Single Audit compliance element.

## When to run this workflow

Run the setup steps at award start - identify income sources, confirm the disposition election, and establish the tracking account - before any program activity begins. Program income earned before you have a ledger tracking it is program income you cannot reconcile retroactively.

Run the ongoing steps quarterly, aligned to the FFR submission schedule. Most federal awards require quarterly FFRs; the FFR due date is the deadline by which the program income earned in the preceding quarter must be reconciled, documented, and reported.

## Common pitfalls

**Not recognizing an activity as generating program income.** Organizations often have a narrow view of what constitutes program income - patient fees at a federally-funded clinic, yes; rental of federally-funded space to a community organization at below-market rates, no. But any revenue earned through an activity that the federal award supports is potentially program income. If in doubt, ask the program officer before the revenue is earned, not after.

**Commingling program income with operating revenue.** Program income deposited into the general operating account without a separate tracking mechanism becomes invisible within 30 days. It must be segregated in the accounting system from the date of receipt.

**Assuming the election does not matter for small amounts.** The election is binding regardless of amount. An organization that elects additive treatment and earns $500 in program income must apply it additively - to additional project costs - not deductively by reducing the draw. The $500 is small; the compliance principle is not.

**Missing FFR Lines 10 and 10a.** Some finance staff who prepare FFRs are not aware that Lines 10 and 10a require reporting. Others assume that if the amount is small, leaving the lines blank is acceptable. Both assumptions lead to incomplete FFRs that an auditor will flag.

## Audit trail requirements

The program income file for each award should contain:

- The award document identifying income-generating activities
- The written disposition election (or confirmation of the default deductive treatment)
- The program income ledger showing earnings by date and source
- Documentation of how income was applied (additional expenditures under additive; drawdown reduction log under deductive; match documentation under matching)
- Quarterly FFR copies showing Lines 10 and 10a for each reporting period
- Reconciliation of program income ledger to GL and to FFR, by quarter

Auditors testing program income compliance will request the award terms, the ledger, the application documentation, and the FFRs - verifying all four are internally consistent.
