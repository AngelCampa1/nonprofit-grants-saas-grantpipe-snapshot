HRSA drawdown support should answer one question: why is this cash needed now?

Start there. Do not begin with the award balance. Do not draw a round number because funds remain. The file should show real costs, the timing of payment, and the rule that supports the request.

This workflow builds on the general [federal drawdown review](/workflows/review-federal-drawdown-before-request), but it adds the HRSA and HHS Payment Management System details that matter for health and service grants.

## Step 1: confirm the HRSA award setup

Open the notice of award. Confirm the award number, budget period, project period, approved budget, and any special terms.

Look for limits on cost categories, prior approval needs, or reporting dates. If the budget period is near its end, check whether costs are inside the period. A cost after the period may need different handling.

Save the award page or terms in the draw packet. A reviewer should not have to search for the source of the budget rule.

## Step 2: list the costs behind the request

Build the request from cost lines. Use payroll, fringe, invoices, travel support, supplies, contracts, or approved indirect costs.

For each line, list the vendor or payroll period, amount, budget category, grant code, planned payment date, and support file. If the cost was already paid, add the bank date.

This list is the base of the draw. It should total to the amount requested in PMS, or to a lower amount if cash on hand reduces the need.

## Step 3: check allowable cost and budget fit

Review each line before it enters the draw. The cost should be needed for the HRSA award, charged during the right period, and supported by the approved budget.

If the award budget has line limits, compare the current request to year-to-date spending. If the line is close to the limit, check whether a budget revision or prior approval is needed.

For shared costs, keep the allocation support. A shared payroll line or shared rent line needs a clear basis. The reviewer should see how the HRSA share was calculated.

## Step 4: check cash already on hand

Federal payment rules under 2 CFR 200.305 expect advance payments to stay close to actual cash need. Compare the draw request to cash already received for the HRSA award.

If prior cash is still in the bank, reduce the request or explain the timing. If a payment was delayed, keep the note in the file.

The cash check does not need to be long. A simple worksheet can show prior draw, bank receipt, costs paid, cash left, and new need.

## Step 5: prepare the PMS request

Use the worksheet total to prepare the Payment Management System request. Match the request amount to the support.

Before submission, have a second person review the packet. The reviewer should check the award, budget fit, cash need, support, and PMS amount.

The approval can be a signed checklist, a system approval, or a dated email. It should name the amount approved and the date.

## Step 6: save the complete packet

After submission, save the PMS request record. When cash reaches the bank, save the bank receipt too.

The final packet should include:

- notice of award or relevant award terms
- draw worksheet
- ledger detail
- invoices, payroll, or other support
- cash on hand calculation
- reviewer approval
- PMS request record
- bank receipt

Use a file name that includes the award, date, and amount. Consistent names help later when finance prepares the [SF-425 report](/workflows/prepare-sf-425-quarterly-report).

## Step 7: close the loop after cash arrives

Match the cash receipt to the costs in the packet. If the request was for near-term payroll or payables, check that those payments happened.

If cash sat longer than expected, note why. Then reduce the next request if needed. This small check keeps the file clean and avoids a pattern of excess cash.

## Add a monthly draw review

Once a month, compare all HRSA draws to the ledger and bank. The goal is to catch timing problems before the next report.

Use three columns: cash requested, costs supported, and cash received. The totals should make sense together. If the cash received is higher than supported costs, write down the reason and the next step.

Also check whether the team is drawing too late. A reimbursement delay can force the organization to carry grant costs with operating cash. That may be allowed, but leadership should know it is happening.

Keep the monthly review with the award file. It gives the certifying official a cleaner trail before financial reporting.

## Common review problems

Watch for draws that equal the remaining award balance. That is rarely a good support method.

Also watch for unsupported shared costs, late budget changes, costs outside the budget period, and requests made far before payment. These may be fixable, but they should be fixed before PMS submission.

If the team uses GrantPipe, the draw support, award terms, report dates, and reviewer notes can sit with the grant record. The system does not approve the draw for you. It helps keep the proof in one place.
