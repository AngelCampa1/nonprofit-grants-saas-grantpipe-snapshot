Cost share is the non-federal portion of total allowable project cost that a grant recipient contributes. It may be mandatory - required by the award - or voluntary and committed, offered in the application. Both types become binding once written into a federal award agreement. Voluntary committed cost share is where most organizations get caught.

## How it works

Federal cost share requirements exist to ensure that grantees have a genuine stake in a project's outcomes. The contribution is expressed as a percentage of total project cost, a ratio of non-federal to federal dollars, or a fixed amount. All three formats appear regularly in federal Notices of Funding Opportunity.

Acceptable forms of cost share under 2 CFR 200.306 include:

- **Cash contributions** - direct expenditures from non-federal accounts: staff payroll, vendor invoices, operating expenses. The most straightforward form; requires ledger documentation.
- **In-kind contributions** - volunteer labor valued at documented fair market rates, donated goods valued at current market price, donated space valued at a documented fair rental rate. Requires contemporaneous logs and valuation documentation.
- **Third-party contributions** - cash or in-kind from other non-federal sources that are committed to the project. Private foundation grants, state appropriations, and community donations can serve as third-party cost share.

Every form of cost share must meet the same five requirements: verifiable from grantee records, necessary and reasonable for the project, allowable under applicable cost principles, not from another federal award (without statutory exception), and included in the approved budget.

## When it applies

Cost share applies when a federal award agreement - whether from a direct federal program or a pass-through entity - specifies a match requirement. The requirement is triggered at two points:

**Application stage.** Mandatory cost share requirements appear in the NOFO. Organizations decide at this point how they will meet the requirement and document that plan in the budget narrative.

**Award stage.** Once the award is signed, both mandatory and voluntary committed cost share become legally binding. This is when voluntary cost share becomes a compliance obligation - not a proposal convenience.

## Common misconceptions

**Voluntary committed cost share is not optional after award.** This is the most consequential misunderstanding in cost share compliance. A nonprofit that offers additional match in an application to strengthen its score, and then receives the award, must document and achieve that additional match at the same compliance standard as mandatory match. Shortfalls are audit findings with the same consequences.

**Federal pass-through funds cannot serve as match.** An organization receiving a state-administered CDBG grant cannot use those federal dollars to match a HOME grant. Even when the federal origin of the state funds is obscured in the funding flow, auditors trace source documentation.

**In-kind match is not less rigorous.** Organizations sometimes treat in-kind match as a softer requirement because there is no cash transaction. Auditors test in-kind match the same way they test direct cost claims: they look for contemporaneous documentation, fair market valuations, and consistency with the approved budget.

**A shortfall in cost share is not just a documentation problem.** If cost share contributions were genuinely not made at the required level, the federal agency may reduce the award proportionally. This is not remediated by better record-keeping after the fact.

## Related terms

- **[Matching funds](/glossary/matching-funds/)** - the same concept; the terms are used interchangeably in grants management.
- **In-kind contribution** - non-cash cost share from volunteer labor, donated goods, or facility use.
- **Voluntary committed cost share** - match beyond required minimum that becomes binding on award execution.
- **[Unallowable costs](/glossary/unallowable-costs/)** - costs that cannot count toward cost share because they violate 2 CFR 200 cost principles.
- **[Questioned costs](/glossary/questioned-costs/)** - what undocumented or non-compliant cost share becomes in a single audit finding.

## How GrantPipe handles cost share

GrantPipe tracks mandatory and voluntary committed cost share separately within each grant record. At award setup, the required match amount and type are entered alongside federal award terms. As costs are posted and in-kind contributions logged, the system calculates the running match percentage and flags gaps against the required amount. Before each reporting period, Finance Directors see exactly where they stand - so a shortfall is addressed during implementation, not discovered in an audit.
