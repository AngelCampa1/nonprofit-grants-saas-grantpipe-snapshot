# Product Marketing Context

_Last updated: 2026-07-03_

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** The operating system for grant-funded nonprofits.

**What it does:** GrantPipe connects donor records, grants, restricted funds, compliance deadlines, evidence, and reporting in one workspace so program, development, and finance staff operate from a shared source of truth instead of rebuilding the story across spreadsheets, donor CRMs, and accounting tools.

**Product category:** Nonprofit operating system spanning donor CRM, grant management, restricted-fund tracking, compliance, and fund accounting. Sits on the shelf with nonprofit CRMs (Bloomerang, Salesforce Nonprofit, Blackbaud) and grant management tools (Instrumentl, GrantHub) but explicitly unifies both.

**Product type:** B2B SaaS, self-serve with sales-assisted Enterprise tier.

**Business model:** Tiered subscriptions (monthly or annual), unlimited users, no setup fee.

**Pricing (USD):**

- **Starter** - $49/mo billed monthly, or $39/mo billed annually ($468/yr).
- **Growth** - $99/mo billed monthly, or $79/mo billed annually ($948/yr). Includes QuickBooks Online read-only.
- **Audit-Ready** - $199/mo billed monthly, or $159/mo billed annually ($1,908/yr).
- **Enterprise** - custom founder-contact path for complex grant-funded teams needing custom terms.

Annual billing is 20% off monthly. 1-month free trial, no credit card.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Mid-sized US nonprofits with $500K–$10M annual budgets that run grants and restricted gifts. Vertical-agnostic — the ICP is defined by operating model (grant-funded, restricted funds, mid-sized, US) rather than cause area. Geography: US only.

**Wedge vs. long-term position:** Compliance and grant management is the _wedge_ — the sharpest pain mid-sized nonprofits feel and the easiest reason to switch. The _destination_ is the operating system: donor CRM, grants, restricted funds, compliance, fund accounting, and reporting unified. Marketing should lead with the wedge but the product narrative is OS-first.

**Decision-makers:** Executive Director (final buyer), Development Director (champion/user), Finance/Operations lead (technical influencer on restricted funds and accounting integration).

**Primary use case:** Run the full grant-funded nonprofit operating model — from donor cultivation through award, restricted-fund spend-down, compliance reporting, and audit evidence — without consultants, parallel trackers, or a Salesforce admin.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Know what is funded, restricted, due, and reportable at any moment.
- Connect grant work to restricted funds and finance review without parallel spreadsheets.
- Hand auditors and funders a clean evidence trail without rebuilding it under deadline pressure.

**Use cases:**

- Track grant opportunities from drafting → submitted → awarded → reporting in one pipeline.
- Manage restricted-fund balances with FASB ASC 958 fund context, releases, and spend-down.
- Search Grants.gov federal opportunities and convert hits into pipeline records.
- Generate board-ready reports from live data instead of monthly spreadsheet rebuilds.
- Provide auditors and funders limited-scope portal access to evidence.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Grant-funded nonprofits live between donor CRMs, accounting systems, shared drives, and spreadsheets. Donor tools rarely carry the full grant compliance story. Accounting systems aren't built for program teams. Reporting evidence gets rebuilt every time a funder, board member, or auditor asks a question.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Donor CRMs (Bloomerang, DonorPerfect, Little Green Light, Virtuous, Keela) — strong on donor retention, weak on restricted funds and post-award compliance.
- Salesforce Nonprofit / Blackbaud — powerful but require admin capacity, consulting budget, or annual contracts; mid-sized teams pay for capability they can't operate.
- Grant-only tools (Instrumentl, GrantHub) — solve discovery and applications but aren't a donor CRM and don't anchor restricted-fund accounting.
- General CRMs (HubSpot, Zoho) — no nonprofit-native workflows; restricted-fund and grant compliance need bolt-ons or custom builds.
- Spreadsheets + drive — works until it doesn't; breaks at audit, board reporting, or staff turnover.

**What it costs them:** Hours rebuilding board and funder reports from scratch. Missed reporting deadlines. Restricted-fund slippage that surfaces during audit. Staff burnout from running parallel trackers. Implementation fees ($10K–$100K+) before the system runs.

**Emotional tension:** Stress and quiet dread before audits. Fear of looking unprofessional in front of funders or boards. Doubt that the next CRM purchase won't repeat the last one. Resentment toward consultant-dependent enterprise tools.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct (same problem, same shelf):**

- **Bloomerang** — Strong donor CRM; published grant tracking, but not compliance-first post-award. Limited restricted-fund/audit depth.
- **Blackbaud (Raiser's Edge NXT + Financial Edge NXT)** — Deep CRM and accounting, but spread across products with implementation services and enterprise contracts.
- **Salesforce Nonprofit** — $60/user/mo Enterprise plus implementation; powerful when configured, requires admin/consulting capacity.
- **DonorPerfect, Neon CRM, Virtuous, Keela, Little Green Light, Givebutter, CharityEngine, Network for Good** — Donor- or fundraising-led; weaker on restricted-fund and grant compliance depth.
- **HubSpot, Zoho** — General CRMs adapted via configuration; no nonprofit-native depth.

**Secondary (different solution, same problem):**

- **Instrumentl, GrantHub** — Grant lifecycle tools; strong for discovery/applications but not a donor CRM and not finance-grade restricted-fund tracking.
- **QuickBooks Online + spreadsheets** — Accounting plus manual trackers. Works until reporting volume or audit pressure breaks it.

**Indirect:**

- **Hire a consultant or fractional grants manager** — Solves capacity, doesn't build durable infrastructure, and repeats the cost every cycle.
- **DIY in spreadsheets and Drive** — Status quo for many sub-$2M orgs; cheap until staff turnover or audit.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Donor CRM, grant pipeline, restricted funds, compliance calendar, federal grants database, fund accounting, auditor/funder portal, and reports unified in one workspace — eight connected areas, with the pricing page showing exactly which areas each tier includes.
- FASB ASC 958 restricted-fund modeling treated as core, not bolt-on. Grants and funds are separate entities with many-to-many allocations — matches how Sage Intacct and Blackbaud model it.
- Grants.gov federal opportunity search built in, with conversion to pipeline records preserving the original source.
- Read-only auditor/funder portal scoped to grants, funds, documents, compliance, accounting, and reports — no donor or settings access.
- No setup fee, unlimited users, month-to-month or annual billing, self-serve onboarding. No Salesforce admin required.
- Activity log + evidence trail surfaced next to grant and fund records, not buried in audit logs.

**How we do it differently:** Built as a compliance-first grant management system for the actual workflow — donor → award → restricted fund → spend-down → reporting → audit — instead of bolting compliance onto a fundraising CRM or stretching enterprise accounting software toward program teams.

**Why that's better:** The team operates from one source of truth. Reporting and audit evidence are byproducts of normal work instead of a separate rebuild project. Decisions about scope, fit, and budget happen on a public pricing page in minutes, not in a sales cycle.

**Why customers choose us:** They've been burned by Salesforce/Blackbaud implementation costs, or they've outgrown spreadsheets, or their donor CRM can't carry the grant compliance story. They want one system, no consultant, transparent pricing, and a workflow built around grants and restricted funds.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Observed signal so far: teams moving off spreadsheets. The "failed Salesforce/Blackbaud implementation" and "audit findings" pushes are plausible but **unverified** — flagged for customer research. Don't lean on them in copy until validated.

**Pull:** Public pricing. 1-month free trial with their actual data. Eight connected areas with one source of truth. No Salesforce admin. Restricted funds and audit evidence treated as first-class. Plain-English UI built for nonprofit staff, not consultants.

**Habit:** Spreadsheets that sort-of work. Email threads as the audit trail. The donor CRM the org already pays for. Tribal knowledge held by the Development Director.

**Anxiety:** "Can we trust a newer product with our donor data?" "What happens if migration breaks?" "Will the team actually adopt it?" "Is the founder real and accountable?" "Will pricing balloon at renewal?"
