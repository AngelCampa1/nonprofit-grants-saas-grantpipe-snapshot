## Article requirements

1. **Length:** 1,200-2,000 words. Practitioners read it because it contains information they can use, not because it is long.
2. **Structure:**
   - Opening: a practitioner-specific scenario or fact (not a generic problem statement). First sentence must hook.
   - Problem section: name the specific problem with data, regulation, or named consequences.
   - Framework section: give it a name (e.g., "The 3-Step Monthly Reconciliation", "The Grant Budget Amendment Process"). 3-5 clear named points.
   - Step-by-step or examples: concrete, numbered where applicable, with specific amounts/citations.
   - Common mistake: name the single most common failure and its consequence.
   - Takeaway: 1 paragraph summary, what to do this week, and one soft mention of GrantPipe.
3. **No marketing opener.** The first sentence is a fact, a scenario, or a problem, not "GrantPipe is a platform that..."
4. **CTA placement:** Only in the final paragraph. One mention of GrantPipe by name. Do not repeat it elsewhere in the article.
5. **Citations and specifics:** When the source material includes regulation cites (2 CFR 200.430, FASB ASC 958, etc.), dollar thresholds, or percentages, use them. Specifics are what make this content worth reading.
6. Use short paragraphs (3-5 sentences max). No walls of text.
7. Subheadings should be plain language, not questions, not "How to X". Just state what the section covers.
