We decided early that grants and funds would be separate entities in the data model. Not the same table with a flag.

A grant is an agreement with a funder. Start date, end date, award amount, reporting cadence.

A fund is a pool of restricted dollars governed by donor or grant stipulations, with a balance, a schedule of activity, and a net asset classification.

One grant can fund three programs across two funds. One fund can be supplied by five gifts and two grants. The cardinality is many-to-many.

Collapse these into one record and you model the simple case but misrepresent the realistic one. The realistic case is what auditors examine.

So we built the join table on day one. It cost more upfront. It removes a class of reconciliation work for the rest of the product's life. This is what FASB ASC 958 requires. We did not invent it. We refused to pretend it was simpler.
