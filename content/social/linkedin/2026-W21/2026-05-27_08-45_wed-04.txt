Six things federal NOFOs hide in the fine print, and where to look for each one.

1. Pre-award costs. Subpart D of 2 CFR 200 says you need prior approval. The NOFO usually buries the rule in a paragraph about budget periods. If you start spending before the award date, you eat the cost.

2. Cost share or match. Sometimes called "applicant contribution." Check whether it is required or just encouraged. The difference is whether you are reporting on it for the next three years.

3. Indirect cost treatment. NICRA, de minimis 15%, or capped at a program-specific rate. The NOFO often references a program statute that overrides the default.

4. Period of performance vs project period. Two different timelines. Money obligated outside one of them is unallowable.

5. Reporting cadence. FFR quarterly is common, but Performance Progress Report intervals vary by agency. Read the terms and conditions, not just the program description.

6. Single audit threshold. $1M in federal awards in a fiscal year triggers it. The NOFO will not tell you. Your aggregate spend will.

The fine print is where the compliance burden actually lives.
