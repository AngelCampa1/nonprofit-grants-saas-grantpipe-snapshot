Six things that should be in your audit binder before May 31, if you operate on a calendar fiscal year.

1. The grant agreement itself, fully executed, with all amendments. Not the application. Not the award letter. The signed contract and every modification, in date order.

2. The internal budget that maps grant line items to your chart of accounts, with version history. Auditors want to see how the budget evolved and who approved each change.

3. Drawdown records or invoice copies for every reimbursement request. Date submitted, date received, amount, expenses claimed, tied back to the GL.

4. Time and effort certifications for any staff allocated to the grant. Signed by the employee and supervisor. Quarterly at minimum, monthly is safer.

5. Procurement documentation for any purchase over your micro-purchase threshold. Quotes, sole-source justifications, conflict of interest disclosures. 2 CFR 200.318 through 200.327 for federal awards.

6. The reconciliation between what you reported on the most recent FFR and what the GL says, with explanations for any variance over your materiality threshold.

If you can produce all six in under an hour, you are ready. If you need a week, the binder is fiction. Build the workflow now, while May still has a few days left.
