Net assets with donor restrictions: dollars donors and grant makers gave you for a specific purpose or time. You own them. The constraint travels with them.

Net assets without donor restrictions: dollars you can use for general operations. Your board decides priorities.

Board-designated: a subset of "without donor restrictions" the board has internally earmarked. The board can undesignate them at any time. Not restricted in the FASB sense.

Three categories. They appear on every nonprofit Statement of Financial Position. They drive how the audit reads, how grants release, and what the 990 says.

If your software treats all three as the same field with a label, the audit binder gets harder every year. The ledger has to be the model, not a reporting overlay.
