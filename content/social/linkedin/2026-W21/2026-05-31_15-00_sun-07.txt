We decided early that grants and funds would be separate entities in the data model. Not the same table with a flag. Two distinct things, joined by a many-to-many allocation table.

Here is why that matters in practice.

A single grant from a community foundation can fund three programs at once. A single program can be funded by four grants and two donor-restricted gifts. The dollars do not flow one-to-one. They flow many-to-many.

If grants and funds are the same object, you cannot represent that without overloading fields or duplicating records. Once you duplicate, the audit trail starts to drift.

Sage Intacct and Blackbaud Financial Edge both model these as separate entities for the same reason. FASB ASC 958 treats restriction status as a property of net assets, not of the gift transaction. The accounting follows the model.

We could have shipped a simpler version sooner. We did not. The first audit run on data inside GrantPipe will be evidence of whether that decision was right.
