Seven items that show up on almost every single audit finding letter under 2 CFR 200. Worth checking yours against, before fieldwork starts.

1. Procurement file gaps. The award required price quotes from two vendors over the small purchase threshold. The file has one.

2. Time-and-effort certifications signed by the wrong person. Federal rules want first-hand knowledge. A bookkeeper signing for a Program Director does not pass.

3. equipment over $10,000 not on the inventory list, or on the list with no physical tag. The auditor will walk through the building and look.

4. Cost transfers booked more than 90 days after the original posting, with no contemporaneous explanation. Late transfers are presumed unallowable.

5. Sub-recipient monitoring missing. A risk assessment, a monitoring plan, documentation of the work performed.

6. FFATA filings late or missing on first-tier sub-awards over $25,000.

7. Indirect costs charged at a rate that does not match the NICRA, or charged with no NICRA and no 15% de minimis election.

None of these are exotic. They become findings because the documentation lives in someone's head or in an email thread.

The fix is not more rigor at fieldwork. It is the file built on the day the cost was incurred.
