For the Finance Director who inherited the grants binder from a predecessor and is now reading FFR instructions for the first time.

The Federal Financial Report (FFR, formerly SF-425) is the standard cash and expenditure report most federal grants require. Box 10 is where most of the work lives: federal cash on hand, expenditures, unobligated balance, program income.

Two cadences. Cash transactions are usually quarterly, due 30 days after the quarter ends. Final FFR is due 120 days after the period of performance ends. Some agencies pull that to 90. Read the award.

The piece that catches new Finance Directors: Box 12, remarks. If indirect costs were charged, if program income was earned, if there are reconciling items, Box 12 is where the explanation goes. Auditors read it.

The FFR is not hard. It is unforgiving. The two are different.
