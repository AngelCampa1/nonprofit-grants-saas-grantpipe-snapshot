Your CRM is not a compliance-first grant management system.

When the donor CRM gets asked to track grants, the request usually starts honest. A custom field for the funder. A pipeline stage for "submitted". A tag for "restricted".

Six months in you have a Frankenstein. Grant deadlines living in calendar invites. Spend-down schedules in a shared sheet. The actual award letter in someone's inbox. The 990 disclosure pulled from QuickBooks because the CRM never had the dollar figure right.

The fix is not a better field structure. It is admitting that grant compliance is a different shape of work than donor stewardship, and giving it a place to live that knows about period of performance, allowable costs, and FFR cadence.

Donor CRMs do not lose data. They just do not have anywhere to put the data that an auditor needs.
