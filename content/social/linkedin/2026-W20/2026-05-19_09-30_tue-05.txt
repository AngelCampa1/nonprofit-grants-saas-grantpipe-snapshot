Six documents an auditor will ask for in the first thirty minutes of a single audit engagement.

1. The schedule of expenditures of federal awards (SEFA), reconciled to the general ledger.
2. The internal control narrative covering federal awards. Not a paragraph. A document that names the control, the owner, and the frequency.
3. Time-and-effort certifications for every employee whose salary touched a federal grant during the fiscal year. Signed, dated, by pay period or semi-annually depending on your method.
4. The procurement file for any federal purchase over the micro-purchase threshold. Bids, justification, sole-source memos if applicable.
5. Sub-recipient monitoring documentation. Risk assessment, monitoring plan, and the actual evidence that monitoring happened.
6. The drawdown log, tied to actual expenditures, for every federal award drawn during the year.

If any of these takes longer than ten minutes to produce, you have already told the auditor where to focus. Audit findings start with the binder that was not ready.

The first rule of audit prep: the auditor is not your enemy, but they are watching how you organize your evidence as closely as they are watching the evidence itself.
