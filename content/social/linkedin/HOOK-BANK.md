# LinkedIn Hook Bank

Reusable templates. Hooks are the first 1 to 3 lines of a post (the part that survives LinkedIn's "see more" truncation). The best hook is the post's whole job in 180 characters.

## Question hooks

- "What does your audit binder look like the week before review?"
- "How long would it take you to answer this funder question right now: how much of the Youth STEM grant has been spent?"
- "If your Development Director left tomorrow, who would know which grant reports are due in June?"
- "Quick check: do you know which restricted funds need to be released this quarter?"

## Number-led hooks

- "Three reasons restricted-fund tracking breaks in donor CRMs."
- "$48,000+. Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud implementation quotes for a $3M nonprofit frequently land in this range, before annual license fees."
- "12 minutes. That is how long it should take to find any grant document during an audit."
- "5 grants. 5 deadlines. 5 funders. 5 spreadsheets. Pick the one that is wrong."

## Contrarian hooks

- "Your CRM is not your grant compliance system. It only holds part of the work."
- "The cheapest grant management system is the one you do not have to hire someone to operate."
- "Audit-ready is not a feature. It is a workflow."
- "Nonprofit software is often sold to people who will not use it."

## Story hooks

- "Picture this: an auditor walks in. They ask for the spend-down detail on the Federal pass-through grant. The Finance Director opens a spreadsheet. The Development Director opens a different spreadsheet. They do not match."
- "Picture this: three versions of the same grant budget. QuickBooks has the GL detail. The Sheet has the funder categories. The CRM has the award amount. None of them wrong. All of them incomplete."

## Definition hooks

- "Restricted fund: dollars a donor or grant maker has tied to a specific purpose. The org owns the dollars; the constraint travels with them."
- "FFR (Federal Financial Report, formerly SF-425): the quarterly spending report most federal grants require. Cash basis, accrual basis, or both, depending on the award."
- "Indirect cost rate: the percentage of total direct costs you can charge to administrative overhead under a federal grant."
- "Spend-down: the act of using restricted dollars on the purpose they were given for, on the schedule the donor or grant requires."

## List hooks

- "Six things that should be in your audit binder before May 31."
- "Four signs your donor CRM is not the right home for grant compliance."
- "The five reports a board member will ask for, ranked by how often they ask."

## Before / after hooks

- "Before: 11 tabs open across QuickBooks, the donor CRM, two Google Sheets, and a shared drive. After: one workspace where the same number lives in one place."
- "Before: 'I think we have $42K left on the Clinic Capacity grant.' After: '$41,847. Released $8,200 last week. SF-425 due in 14 days.'"

## Callout hooks

- "If you have ever rebuilt a board report from scratch the night before the meeting, this is for you."
- "For every nonprofit Finance Director who has been told 'just configure the CRM for it'."
- "To the Executive Director who has signed two failed implementation contracts in five years: this is a different shape of product."

## Build-in-public hooks

- "We just shipped a change to how restricted funds release on the spend-down schedule. Here is why."
- "Public pricing on a B2B nonprofit product is rarer than it should be. Here is what ours looks like and why."
- "Reading the 2 CFR 200 update changed how we display indirect cost rates inside the app. Notes."

## Anti-pattern hooks (use sparingly)

- "Grant tracking spreadsheets often have one column missing. It is the column that becomes audit evidence."
- "If your reporting workflow involves 'rebuild from scratch monthly', the workflow is the bug."

## Glossary hooks

- "Plain-English glossary entry, today: 'pass-through grant'."
- "Term of the day: deobligation. What it costs you when you do not catch it."

## Hook style mix target

Across 285 posts, distribute roughly:

- Question: 18%
- Number-led: 18%
- Story: 14%
- List: 12%
- Definition / glossary: 12%
- Contrarian: 10%
- Callout: 8%
- Before / after: 5%
- Build-in-public: 3%

No hook style appears in more than 2 of any 24-hour window.
