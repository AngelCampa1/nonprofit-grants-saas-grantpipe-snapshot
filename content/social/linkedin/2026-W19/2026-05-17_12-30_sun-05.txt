Most "audit prep" advice tells you to start in August. That is too late.

The audit binder is not a document you assemble. It is a byproduct of how you ran the year. If your evidence trail only exists when an auditor asks for it, you do not have an evidence trail. You have a scramble.

Here is the inversion. The week-by-week version of audit prep is not 14 frantic weeks before fieldwork. It is 52 quiet weeks that produce a binder as a side effect.

What that looks like in practice:

Each award has a folder with the executed agreement, the approved budget, every amendment, and the board minutes that authorized it. That folder gets touched on day one and never restructured.

Each drawdown gets a memo. One paragraph. Date, amount, period covered, the GL accounts it ties to. Filed in the award folder when the cash hits.

Each personnel allocation gets a certification at the cadence the funder requires. Federal awards under 2 CFR 200.430 require after-the-fact certification that payroll charges reflect actual work. Frequency varies by agency and funding structure. Filed in the award folder, not in HR.

Each sub-recipient sends back a quarterly compliance attestation. Filed in the award folder. Read by someone, not just received.

When fieldwork starts, the binder is already built. The auditor's PBC list is a retrieval task, not a manufacturing task.

This is the difference between organizations that dread audit week and organizations that schedule it like a dental cleaning. The work was done in the months before. May and June are the right months to set it up for the next cycle.
