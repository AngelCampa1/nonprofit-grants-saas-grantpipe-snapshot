Three reasons restricted-fund tracking breaks in donor CRMs:

One. Restrictions are properties of the gift, not the donor. A single donor can give a restricted gift this year and an unrestricted gift next. CRMs index on the donor first.

Two. Releases from restriction are events, not statuses. When the Youth STEM program spends $4,200 of a $50,000 restricted grant, that is a release event with a date, an amount, and a reference to the underlying expense. Most CRMs have no event model for this.

Three. The schedule of activity is a financial statement disclosure. It needs to roll up by net asset class, by restriction type, and by fund. CRMs report by donor, by campaign, by appeal. Different axes.

This is not a CRM critique. It is a fit observation. The CRM does what it was built to do. Restricted fund accounting is not on that list.
