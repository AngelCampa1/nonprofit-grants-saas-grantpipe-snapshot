Six things that should already be in your audit binder before May 31.

Single audit season starts earlier than teams often plan for. The $1M threshold catches orgs that cross it mid-year without realizing, and "we will pull it together when they ask" is how Q3 becomes a finding.

Here is what auditors typically ask for first under 2 CFR 200.

1. The schedule of expenditures of federal awards (SEFA), organized by ALN, pass-through entity, and direct vs sub-award. Tied to the GL.

2. A reconciliation between the SEFA total and the federal revenue line on your Statement of Activities. Auditors want the bridge.

3. Internal control narratives for grants management, cash receipts, disbursements, and payroll allocation. Especially at small orgs.

4. Time and effort certifications for staff charged across multiple federal awards. Must reflect actual after-the-fact activity, not the budget.

5. Sub-recipient monitoring: risk assessments, recent audit reports, and the monitoring activity you performed during the period.

6. Procurement records under 2 CFR 200.318. Method, conflict of interest disclosures, cost or price analysis where required.

If any of these live in someone's head or someone's inbox, that is where the audit finding will land.
