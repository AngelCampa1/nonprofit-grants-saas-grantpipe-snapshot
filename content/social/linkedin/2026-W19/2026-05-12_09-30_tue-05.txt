Six things that belong in your audit binder before fieldwork starts, in the order an auditor will ask for them:

1. Trial balance with prior-year comparison, signed by the CFO or Finance lead.
2. Schedule of net assets with donor restrictions, with a roll-forward of additions, releases, and ending balance per fund.
3. Grant agreements for every active award, including modifications and amendments. Originals, not summaries.
4. Schedule of expenditures of federal awards (SEFA) if you crossed the $1,000,000 single audit threshold. With ALN numbers, pass-through identifiers, and direct vs pass-through breakouts.
5. Board minutes for the audit period, with motions related to budget approval, restricted fund designations, and any related-party disclosures highlighted.
6. Bank reconciliations for every month, with the reconciler and reviewer initialed.

The binder is not the audit. It is the artifact that proves the audit can move quickly.

If item two takes more than a day to assemble, you do not have a reporting problem. You have a system-of-record problem.
