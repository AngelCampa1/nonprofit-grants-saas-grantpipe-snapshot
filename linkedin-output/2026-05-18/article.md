# Massachusetts Form PC: What Nonprofits Get Wrong and What the AG's Office Checks First

A Massachusetts nonprofit at $520,000 in gross support and revenue needs an independent CPA audit. Its counterpart in Texas or Florida at the same revenue level does not — not until it crosses $1,000,000 or more in federal expenditures and hits the Single Audit threshold. That asymmetry catches Massachusetts charities off guard more often than the AG's office probably intends.

Form PC is the Massachusetts Annual Charities Filing, administered by the Attorney General's Non-Profit Organizations/Public Charities Division under M.G.L. Chapter 12 Sections 8E through 8M. It is due every year, it carries fees, and it has attestation requirements that kick in well below the federal thresholds most compliance staff know by heart.

## Who Is Actually Affected

Every public charity registered with the Massachusetts Public Charities Division files Form PC. That includes Massachusetts-incorporated 501(c)(3)s, out-of-state charities soliciting in the state, charitable trusts holding Massachusetts assets, and private foundations with Massachusetts nexus. Most conventional nonprofits operating in the state are covered.

The filing population is substantial. Massachusetts has approximately 37,000 registered public charities, with the Boston area accounting for the largest concentration of nonprofit employment in New England. The oversight regime is active — the Public Charities Division issues deficiency notices and escalates unresolved issues to registration suspension and loss of solicitation authority.

## The Deadlines That Trip Organizations Up

The base due date for Form PC is 4.5 months after fiscal year end. For calendar-year filers, that is May 15. Most Massachusetts charities do not file by May 15 because the audit cycle rarely completes in time. A six-month extension moves the effective deadline to November 15 for calendar-year filers.

The extension is available, but it must be documented. Organizations that assume the extension is automatic and file in November without a properly documented extension request can face a deficiency notice for late filing. Document the extension request, retain the confirmation, and calendar both the base deadline and the extended deadline.

For organizations with non-December fiscal year ends, calculate forward: 4.5 months after your fiscal year close is your base date, 10.5 months is your extended date. The November 15 figure applies only to December 31 fiscal year filers.

## The Fee Schedule and How It Generates Deficiency Notices

Form PC fees are tiered by gross support and revenue:

- Under $100,000: $35
- $100,000 to $250,000: $70
- $250,000 to $500,000: $125
- $500,000 to $1,000,000: $250
- $1,000,000 to $10,000,000: $500
- $10,000,000 to $100,000,000: $1,000
- Above $100,000,000: $2,000

The most common fee error is using last year's gross support and revenue figure when current-year revenue has crossed a tier boundary. An organization that moved from $480,000 to $530,000 in revenue this year owes $250, not $125. The calculation should use current-year figures from the Form 990 that accompanies the filing.

Fee errors generate deficiency notices with a cure window. They do not automatically result in enforcement action, but they require a corrected filing and additional administrative work. Getting the fee right the first time is straightforward — the only variable is using the correct year's figures.

## The Attestation Threshold That Surprises Finance Teams

The audit and review thresholds under Massachusetts law are lower than most compliance staff anticipate:

**$500,000 or more in gross support and revenue:** Independent audit by a licensed CPA is required. The audited financial statements must be attached to Form PC.

**$200,000 to $500,000:** A CPA review is required. The review report must be attached.

**Below $200,000:** No attestation required. Form PC is still due.

The strategic implication: a mid-sized nonprofit at $600,000 in Massachusetts needs an independent audit for state purposes even though it is well below the federal Single Audit threshold of $1,000,000 in federal expenditures (as revised under the 2024 OMB update). Finance teams that plan their audit engagement based on federal thresholds alone will miss the state obligation.

For growing organizations, the $200,000 threshold is the first trigger. Once gross support and revenue crosses $200,000, a CPA review must be in the budget and on the calendar. The audit obligation at $500,000 is typically not a surprise for organizations that have already been navigating the review requirement for a year or two — but organizations that grow quickly can cross both thresholds within a short period.

## The Required Attachments — All of Them

The most common rejection reasons for Form PC center on missing attachments, not substantive errors in the form itself. Required attachments:

1. Complete copy of the federal Form 990 (or 990-EZ, 990-PF). Organizations filing the federal 990-N postcard still submit a full Form PC with Massachusetts-level financial detail — the postcard does not satisfy the state's requirement.
2. Audited financial statements, if gross support and revenue is $500,000 or more.
3. Reviewed financial statements, if gross support and revenue is $200,000 to $500,000.
4. Signed Form PC — unsigned forms are rejected.
5. Correct fee payment.

The Form 990 attachment is the most frequently missing item. Organizations whose federal Form 990 is on extension sometimes file Form PC without the 990, intending to submit the 990 later. The Public Charities Division requires the 990 at the time of filing. If the federal return is not complete by the extended Form PC deadline, the organization faces a timing problem that requires direct coordination with the division.

## The 4-Part Pre-Filing Checklist

Working backward from the November 15 extended deadline for calendar-year filers, the practical sequence:

**January through May:** Audit or review fieldwork. Engage the auditor or reviewer early — Massachusetts CPA firms have concentrated demand in this window, and late engagement means late delivery of audited statements.

**June through September:** Final financial statements and audit deliverables. The audit should be complete and the final statements signed before October begins.

**October:** Form PC preparation. Verify registration is active with the Public Charities Division (lapsed registrations must be cured before the filing is accepted), calculate current-year gross support and revenue, confirm the correct fee tier, and assemble all attachments.

**November 15:** Extended filing due. File online through the Charity Portal. Online filing reduces deficiency notices from missing signatures and attachment errors because the portal enforces completeness before submission.

## The Most Common Mistake

The single most common failure is not a calculation error or a missed deadline. It is the audit engagement starting too late.

The audit cycle for a mid-sized nonprofit typically runs 6–10 weeks from fieldwork start to final signed opinions. For an organization with a December 31 fiscal year end, beginning fieldwork in August or September and receiving final statements in October or November — with the November 15 deadline looming — leaves no margin for review questions, restatements, or rescheduled fieldwork.

Finance teams that build the audit start date into the January calendar, treat it as a fixed production commitment, and track it alongside the filing deadline avoid this compression. Teams that treat the audit as something that gets scheduled whenever the auditor is available routinely find themselves in October with incomplete statements and a November 15 deadline.

The consequence of missing the deadline without an approved extension is a deficiency notice. Deficiency notices from the Public Charities Division typically allow 30–60 days to cure. Unresolved deficiencies escalate to registration suspension, which means the organization loses its solicitation authority in Massachusetts. For an organization that relies on Massachusetts-resident donors, that consequence is material.

## What to Do This Week

If your organization is a Massachusetts public charity with a December 31 fiscal year end, three concrete actions matter now:

First, confirm that Public Charities Division registration is active. Log into the Charity Portal and verify status. A lapsed registration is not cured by paying the Form PC fee — it requires a separate reinstatement process that takes time.

Second, calculate current-year gross support and revenue from your year-to-date financials. Confirm which fee tier you will be in and whether you are above or below the $200,000 and $500,000 attestation thresholds. If you are within $50,000 of either threshold, plan for the attestation obligation as a contingency.

Third, confirm your audit or review engagement is scheduled and the fieldwork start date is on the calendar. If the engagement has not been signed, contact your CPA now. Fall availability fills quickly.
