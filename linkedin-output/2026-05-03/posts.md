**Post 3 — workflow_step**

The SF-SAC has two certifications, and missing either one keeps the submission in draft.

Step seven of the Single Audit Data Collection Form workflow at fac.gov: the auditee certifies the submission is accurate and complete, then the auditor separately certifies the audit findings.

Neither party can submit unilaterally. A form sitting at one signature is not filed, even if every field is complete and the reporting package uploaded cleanly.

Deadline pressure compounds this. The SF-SAC is due 30 days after the auditor's report or 9 months after fiscal year-end, whichever is earlier. Late filings affect low-risk auditee status under 2 CFR 200.520 in subsequent years.

Confirm both certifications cleared before treating an SF-SAC as submitted. Download the FAC confirmation PDF and retain it in the audit binder.

**Post 4 — term_explained**

The de minimis indirect cost rate is 15% of MTDC, raised from 10% by the 2024 Uniform Guidance revision under 2 CFR 200.414(f).

Eligible nonprofits — those that have never received a federally negotiated indirect cost rate — can claim it on federal awards without going through NICRA negotiation.

Two facts that get organizations into trouble.

First, the base is Modified Total Direct Costs, not total direct costs. MTDC excludes equipment, capital expenditures, participant support, and sub-award amounts above $50,000 per subcontract. Applying 15% to the full budget is a recurring audit finding.

Second, once a NICRA is executed, there is no return to de minimis — even after the NICRA period closes.

Urban Institute analysis of Form 990 data found roughly 25% of eligible nonprofits claim no indirect cost recovery at all. That is real money left unrecovered.

**Post 6 — vertical_hook**

A mid-sized homeless shelter manages 8-12 simultaneous funding streams: HUD ESG, HUD Continuum of Care, CDBG sub-allocations, state housing authority grants, county homelessness funds, United Way, and three or four foundation awards.

Each has distinct allowable expense categories. ESG alone has five activity buckets — street outreach, shelter operations, rapid re-housing, prevention, and HMIS.

When a case manager's salary touches multiple grants, the time must be allocated contemporaneously. Month-end reconstructions from memory create the documentation gaps that auditors flag.

HUD's Continuum of Care Program awarded approximately $3.16 billion in FY2023 to more than 7,000 projects. ESG distributed $375 million. The compliance load is real.

Commingling federal ESG with state housing funds is an audit finding that can trigger repayment demands and loss of future HUD funding. The reconciliation needs to be continuous, not deadline-driven.

**Post 7 — free_resource**

Most development directors at mid-sized nonprofits manage 10-30 active grants. Each grant has its own reporting schedule, format, and renewal timeline. That is 60-100 deadline events per year for an 8-funder portfolio.

Missed deadlines do not happen because someone forgot. They happen because the grants manager remembered with three days to go and could not assemble narrative, financial reconciliation, and ED approval in time.

Lead time rules that work for a team of 1-3 people: 3 weeks for an interim narrative report, 2 weeks for an interim financial report, 4 weeks for a final narrative, 6 weeks for a new application, 4 weeks before grant end for a no-cost extension.

Request financial data from the bookkeeper at least 10 business days before any financial report is due. That accounts for workload, coding questions, and one round of corrections.
