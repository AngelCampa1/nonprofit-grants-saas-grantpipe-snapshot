# SEFA Preparation Checklist: Where 80% of Single Audit Findings Originate

The Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards is the foundation of every single audit. Auditors use it to determine which programs are "major programs" requiring opinions on compliance. When the SEFA is wrong, audit scope expands, fieldwork compresses, and findings multiply. The OMB Single Audit Quality Study has cited SEFA cluster-identification errors as one of the top five recurring findings, appearing in roughly 30% of audited entities with multiple federal programs.

Most SEFA errors are not accounting mistakes. They are classification errors: programs that should be in a cluster get listed separately, pass-through subrecipient data is missing, indirect costs are omitted from program expenditures, or pass-through awards are filed under the state agency that disbursed the cash rather than the prime federal agency. Each of these has a specific fix.

## What the SEFA Has to Show

Per 2 CFR 200.510(b), each line of the SEFA must include the federal agency name, pass-through entity name when applicable, the Assistance Listing Number (ALN, formerly the CFDA number), federal program name, program cluster name when applicable, total expenditures, and amounts passed to subrecipients. The SEFA is a supplemental schedule — not part of the basic financial statements, but presented with them — and it is management's representation of every federal program the organization administered during the fiscal year.

The single audit threshold is $1,000,000 in federal expenditures during the fiscal year per 2 CFR 200.501(a). This figure has been in place since the 2014 Uniform Guidance and was retained in the 2024 revision. Organizations crossing the threshold must file a single audit; organizations operating just below it should track aggregate federal expenditures month-by-month so the trigger does not arrive as a surprise.

The Federal Audit Clearinghouse receives roughly 30,000 single audit submissions annually. The volume is large; the rules are specific. Knowing what each column requires is the difference between a clean opinion and a finding.

## The Five-Part Preparation Framework

A reliable SEFA preparation process has five named components. Each addresses a specific failure mode auditors have flagged repeatedly across federal awarding agencies.

### Part 1: The Federal Award Inventory

Pull a complete list of all federal awards active during the fiscal year, including awards that started or ended mid-year. Three categories must appear: direct federal awards (received from a federal agency), pass-through federal awards (received from a state agency, local government, or another nonprofit acting as pass-through entity), and federal cost-reimbursement contracts.

The most common omission is pass-through funding from state agencies that administer federal programs. A state Department of Health receiving HHS dollars and disbursing them to nonprofits is a pass-through entity. The award is federal, not state, on the SEFA. State workforce boards distributing Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act dollars are passing federal funds. The same is true for state arts agencies disbursing NEA grants-to-states.

Capturing pass-through status at award acceptance — when the grant agreement is signed — is far more reliable than reconstructing it during SEFA prep. The grant agreement should disclose the federal source. If it does not, the pass-through entity is required to provide that information under 2 CFR 200.332.

### Part 2: ALN Lookup and Verification

Look up each program's ALN at SAM.gov. The format is XX.XXX (for example, 93.914 for HIV Emergency Relief, 10.561 for SNAP, 84.063 for Federal Pell Grants). Every SEFA row requires an ALN.

For pass-through awards, the pass-through entity is required to provide the ALN in the award agreement. If it is missing, contact the pass-through entity and request it before SEFA finalization. Awards without an ALN cannot be correctly reported on the SEFA.

ALNs change over time as programs are reorganized at the federal level. Using a stale ALN from a prior-year grant agreement is a finding when the auditor cross-references against the current SAM.gov listing. The verification step is brief; the cost of skipping it is not.

### Part 3: Cluster Identification

This is where 80% of cluster-related findings originate. OMB defines program clusters in Appendix VI to 2 CFR Part 200 and updates the cluster list annually in the OMB Compliance Supplement. A cluster groups related programs — Research and Development cluster, Student Financial Aid cluster, SNAP cluster, Aging cluster — and treats them as one program for major-program selection.

When an organization receives funding under multiple ALNs that belong to a defined cluster, those ALNs must be grouped on the SEFA and labeled with the cluster name. An organization receiving funds under ALN 93.914 (HIV Emergency Relief) and ALN 93.917 (Ryan White Part B), both in the same cluster, must list them together as a cluster — not as two standalone programs.

Listing them separately understates cluster expenditures. The auditor uses cluster totals in major-program determination under 2 CFR 200.518. Misclassifying a cluster shifts which programs get tested, which can either expand scope (additional programs become major) or undertest (a program that should have been major is treated as non-major).

The OMB Compliance Supplement is updated each year. The current year's supplement controls. Cluster membership shifts year to year, particularly as new programs are added or older programs are reorganized. Use the current supplement, not a prior year's reference document.

### Part 4: Pass-Through Subrecipient Reporting

For each program where the organization passes federal funds to a subrecipient, the SEFA must identify the total amount passed through and the names of the subrecipient organizations. 2 CFR 200.510(b)(4) requires this disclosure regardless of dollar amount. A $5,000 subaward from a $500,000 federal grant must be disclosed.

This is one of the most common findings in HHS-funded single audits. The omission usually traces to subawards that were administered outside the grants management process — small community-partner grants treated as program expenses rather than as pass-through activity.

Document the subrecipient's UEI number, prior DUNS if available, and the federal program ALN under which the funds passed. Subrecipient monitoring documentation is a separate requirement under 2 CFR 200.332; the SEFA disclosure is only the dollar amount and the subrecipient identification.

### Part 5: Reconciliation to the General Ledger

Every dollar on the SEFA must trace to a general ledger transaction. Auditors reconcile SEFA to GL detail. Unexplained differences become findings.

Common reconciling items that must be explained in the notes to the SEFA: accrued expenditures recognized in one period and paid in another, indirect cost allocations posted at year-end versus grants-paid basis, deferred revenue for federal advances not yet earned, and cost-sharing contributions if separately tracked.

Indirect costs deserve special attention. Indirect costs charged to a federal award are federal expenditures and belong on the SEFA. Organizations that omit indirect cost recovery understate program activity and may misidentify which programs cross the major-program threshold. For organizations using a negotiated indirect cost rate (NICRA), include the indirect costs recovered during the year. For organizations using the de minimis rate (now 15% of modified total direct costs under the 2024 revision), include indirect costs at that rate. The SEFA notes must disclose which rate was used.

## Step-by-Step Preparation Sequence

The five-part framework translates into a numbered preparation sequence:

1. Compile the federal award inventory from grant agreements and subaward notifications. Capture pass-through entity information at award acceptance, not at year-end.
2. Verify ALN for every program against the current SAM.gov Assistance Listings.
3. Reference Appendix VI to 2 CFR Part 200 and the current OMB Compliance Supplement to identify clusters. Group ALNs that belong together.
4. Calculate expenditures per program — direct costs, allocated indirect costs, and subaward amounts — using the accrual basis unless the award requires cash basis.
5. Document each pass-through subrecipient with name, UEI, and amount passed through under each ALN.
6. Address loan and loan guarantee programs separately under 2 CFR 200.502. Outstanding balances plus current-year disbursements are typically reportable.
7. Reconcile SEFA totals to federal revenue per the Statement of Activities. Document every reconciling item.
8. Prepare SEFA notes disclosing basis of accounting, indirect cost rate (NICRA or de minimis), and significant accounting policies.
9. Deliver the SEFA to the auditor at least 30 days before fieldwork begins.

For a well-maintained ledger with grant coding throughout the year, this sequence takes 8 to 16 hours. Organizations reconstructing grant detail from function-level coding can spend 40+ hours, with material risk of error.

## The Single Most Common Failure

The single most common SEFA failure is not a math error. It is reporting pass-through awards under the pass-through entity instead of under the prime federal agency.

A nonprofit receiving CDBG dollars from a state Housing Finance Agency is receiving HUD funds. The SEFA must list the program under HUD with the HFA disclosed as the pass-through entity. Filing it under "State Housing Finance Agency" as a state grant fails the classification test entirely.

This error appears every audit cycle because the cash receipt looks like state revenue in the bank — the wire is from the state agency, the contract is signed with the state, the reporting flows back to the state. The federal source is upstream, not visible in day-to-day operations. The fix is capturing federal-source status at the moment the grant agreement is reviewed, not trying to reverse-engineer it from a state-issued check.

GAO has identified incomplete SEFA as a material weakness classification risk. $1.17 trillion of federal award spending from 2017-2021 was linked to severe and persistent single audit findings according to GAO April 2024 analysis. The classification problem is at the core of those numbers.

## What to Do This Week

If your organization is approaching fiscal year-end with federal awards, run three checks now rather than at SEFA prep time. First, list every active federal award and confirm the pass-through entity for each — identify the prime federal agency, not just the entity that issued the contract. Second, pull the current OMB Compliance Supplement Appendix VI and verify cluster membership for each ALN. Third, run a query of subaward payments and confirm every subrecipient is captured with name and amount, regardless of dollar size.

These three checks take a few hours. They prevent the most common findings on a document that drives audit scope.
