Custom fields buried in a notes column aren't data. They're archaeology.

Every nonprofit tracks something the standard donor schema doesn't capture — cultivation score, board relationship, interest area, legacy intent, ask amount, fiscal sponsor, project code. When that lives in a free-text "notes" field, it can't be filtered, exported, reported on, or used to segment a campaign. It exists, but only for the person who wrote it down.

The fix is typed custom fields: text, number, date, select, or boolean, defined per entity (donor, grant, gift). Once the field has a type, it shows up in filters and reports the same way the standard fields do. The data stays structured from the moment it's entered.

The discipline is naming and pruning. Every custom field added is a column someone has to maintain. Review the list once a year and delete what no one fills in.

GrantPipe lets you define custom fields on donors, grants, and gifts that flow into filters, exports, and reports alongside the built-in fields — no notes-column workarounds.
