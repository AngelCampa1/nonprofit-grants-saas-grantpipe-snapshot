Step 3 of a subaward budget modification: apply the 10% rule before you escalate.

Under 2 CFR 200.308(e)(4), a non-federal entity may transfer funds among direct cost categories when the total of transfers does not exceed 10% of the total award budget and the change does not alter project scope. Inside that envelope, the pass-through can approve a subrecipient request without going up to the federal awarding agency.

Step 4 is where most pass-throughs get the sequence wrong. If the modification crosses 10%, changes scope, adds equipment that wasn't budgeted, or touches a category the prime award restricted, federal prior approval is required before the pass-through can approve the subrecipient.

Tell the subrecipient up front: federal prior approval typically runs four to twelve weeks depending on the agency. That sets the modification request deadline to roughly three months before the change needs to take effect.

Document the 10% calculation in the file. Auditors look for the math, not just the email.
