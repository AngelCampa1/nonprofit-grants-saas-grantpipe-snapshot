Development directors at $2M–$10M nonprofits typically carry 40 to 120 major donor prospects in active moves management alongside 8 to 25 active grants at any given time.

The Fundraising Effectiveness Project has reported sector-wide donor retention between 40 and 45 percent every year for over a decade. The cause is rarely appeal quality. It is the failure to follow up the second gift, track the lapsed-donor list, or notice when a multi-year pledge is 90 days overdue. These are operational failures — ones that fragmented tooling makes routine.

Most development directors run four systems that do not talk to each other: a donor CRM for gift history, a spreadsheet for the grant pipeline, QuickBooks for restricted fund balances, and a slide deck for the board that requires manually joining all three. Reconciling across those systems to build a board packet consumes 4 to 8 hours per cycle. If the CFO's numbers and the development report disagree, the development director explains it at a board meeting.

The job that needs doing is not complicated: moves management, grant pipeline, restricted fund visibility, and board-ready reporting from one source of truth.
