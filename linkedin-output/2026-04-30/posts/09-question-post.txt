Program directors own program outcomes and the budget that funds them — usually with no formal finance training and no purpose-built tool for the budget-to-delivery connection.

The standard setup at mid-sized nonprofits: program budget in Excel built from the award template, actuals in QuickBooks, reconciliation once a month if someone has time. When a program director approves a subrecipient invoice or authorizes a venue deposit, there is no live check against the remaining budget line. The check happens 30 days later, by which point the overage is booked.

Cost disallowance was named one of the five most common Single Audit findings in the GAO's April 2024 analysis (GAO-24-106173), contributing to the 17% of federal spending linked to severe findings. The Center for Effective Philanthropy puts reporting alone at 6 to 8 hours per grant per year. Multiply by 10 to 20 active grants and a program director loses one to two weeks annually to report assembly.

Question for the program directors and grants managers reading this: when you approve a vendor invoice, can you see the remaining balance on that grant's budget line right then, or do you find out at month-end. Does the disconnect between program tracking and the GL ever surface a 70%-consumed warning early enough to actually file a budget modification.
