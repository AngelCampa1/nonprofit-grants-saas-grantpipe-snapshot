A grant writer starts the year with 30 to 50 prospects on a spreadsheet. By Q2 the deadlines have shifted, two program officers have changed jobs, the organization has pivoted one program, and the spreadsheet has not kept up.

The writing itself is maybe 70 percent of the role. The other 30 is pipeline hygiene — tracking deadlines, reusing narrative content across submissions, and handing off won awards to the grants manager with the budget intact.

The handoff is where most awarded grants quietly start losing money. The proposal said one thing, the grants manager inherits another, and by the second drawdown the budget categories no longer line up with what the funder approved.

Question for the grant writers and development directors here: when you win a grant, what does your handoff to compliance actually look like? Is it a meeting, a folder, an email thread, or a system that carries the budget and narrative forward without retyping?

The answer says a lot about how your post-award reporting will go six months from now.
