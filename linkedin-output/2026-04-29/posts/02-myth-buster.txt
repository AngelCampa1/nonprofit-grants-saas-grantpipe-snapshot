Myth: interest earned on a federal advance drawdown counts as program income.

Reality: it does not. Program income is governed by 2 CFR 200.307 and means revenue earned by the recipient that is directly generated by activities supported by the federal award. Interest on cash advances is a separate animal handled under 2 CFR 200.305(b)(9), which requires recipients to remit interest above $500 per year to the HHS Program Support Center.

The two get conflated because both involve money sitting in the recipient's bank account that was not in the original budget. They are not the same thing and they get reported on different schedules.

The other place this matters: the additive-versus-deductive treatment election under 2 CFR 200.307. Once made at award time, that election is binding for the life of the award. Most program officers never ask about it until the auditor does, and by then changing it requires retroactive correction.

If your sub-award says nothing about program income treatment, that is itself a finding waiting to happen.
