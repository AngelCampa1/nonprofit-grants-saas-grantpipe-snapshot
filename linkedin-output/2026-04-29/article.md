# What 5,000+ Nonprofit CRM Reviews Actually Tell Buyers in 2026

Across Capterra, G2, and Software Advice, more than 5,000 nonprofit CRM users have left ratings on the platforms they use every day. The aggregate picture as of early 2026 is striking: Little Green Light sits at 4.75/5, Bonterra/Network for Good at 4.72/5, Bloomerang at 4.66/5, and DonorPerfect at 4.55/5. Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT trails at 4.0 to 4.2/5 and Salesforce Nonprofit at 3.8 to 4.1/5. The gap between the top and bottom of the list — nearly a full point on a 5-point scale — is unusually large for a mature software category.

The 2025 Fifty & Fifty Nonprofit Peer Report adds the category-level frame: only 33.3 percent of nonprofits rate their CRM as effective, 46.5 percent are neutral, and 20.1 percent rate it outright ineffective. ITQlick's separate sentiment analysis confirms the pattern from a different methodology: DonorPerfect 85/100, Bloomerang 82/100, Neon CRM 80/100.

Aggregated reviews are not perfect, but the consistency across three independent review platforms and two independent sentiment data sources is hard to dismiss. The pattern is real. The question is what to do with it.

## The Tier Structure Vendor Marketing Hides

The data sorts cleanly into two tiers.

The purpose-built tier — Little Green Light, Bonterra, Bloomerang, DonorPerfect — sits above 4.5/5. These tools were built for nonprofit fundraising teams that do not have dedicated technical staff. They make a deliberate tradeoff: less customization in exchange for a working product on day one.

The mid-tier and enterprise tier — Neon CRM, Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT, Salesforce Nonprofit — sits between 3.8 and 4.3/5. These platforms offer more depth, more customization, and in Salesforce's case more integration surface than any other product in the market. The cost is implementation complexity that most mid-sized nonprofits cannot absorb without consultant support.

This tier structure is not a moral judgment. It is a fit signal. A platform that requires three months and $40,000 of consultant time to configure will deliver high satisfaction at a $50M-budget healthcare nonprofit and low satisfaction at a $2M community arts org. The review averages reflect the audience that actually buys each tool, weighted by how well that audience can support the tool after purchase.

## Why Ease of Use Wins

The single strongest predictor of overall rating across all 5,000+ reviews is one sub-score: ease of use. Platforms scoring above 4.5/5 on ease of use score above 4.5/5 overall. Platforms below 4.0/5 on ease of use — Blackbaud and Salesforce — pull their overall ratings down regardless of feature depth.

The staffing data explains why. Most mid-sized nonprofits do not employ a CRM administrator. The development director, the office manager, or the executive director themselves are the primary users. A platform that requires a trained admin to maintain will be used poorly by this group, regardless of what is theoretically possible inside it.

The NTEN Benchmarks Study found that 60 percent of fundraisers say better training would improve their technology use. Yet only 1 percent of nonprofit technology budgets goes to training. The result: tools that need training to deliver value are used at a fraction of their capability, and the satisfaction scores reflect that gap.

For software buyers, the practical implication is clear. The platform's feature ceiling matters less than its floor. The right question is not "what can this tool do?" but "what can this tool do without a consultant?"

## Enterprise Platforms Are Not Bad — They Are Mismatched

Salesforce Nonprofit and Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT score lower not because the products are weak. They score lower because most reviewers are not the audience the platforms were designed for.

Salesforce Nonprofit (formerly NPSP, now branded Agentforce Nonprofit) is the most customizable platform in the category. With consultant time and a clear data model, it can do almost anything. The 3.8 to 4.1/5 rating reflects organizations that adopted it under the free 10-license program, did not budget for implementation, and got stuck with a half-configured system. Salesforce retired NPSP development in March 2023 and is pushing customers toward Agentforce Nonprofit, which is a re-implementation rather than an upgrade. Only 25 percent of Salesforce nonprofit consultants have completed Nonprofit Cloud implementations as of early 2026, so the implementation supply is tight.

Blackbaud Raiser's Edge NXT carries different weight. Its depth in major-gift fundraising and wealth screening is unmatched in the category. The 4.0 to 4.2/5 rating reflects the learning curve, which steepens quickly for organizations without fundraising operations staff. For a $20M-budget university foundation, Blackbaud is often the right tool. For a $2M community nonprofit, it is overkill that becomes underused.

The pattern holds: enterprise tools deliver high value to organizations that can support them and low value to everyone else.

## Acquisitions Distort the Trailing Numbers

A static review average can hide a trajectory. Three acquisitions in the past three years are reshaping the data.

Bonterra emerged from the 2022 merger of EveryAction, Social Solutions, CyberGrants, and Network for Good. The Network for Good product still posts strong ratings (4.72/5), but reviews from the past 18 months increasingly cite declining customer service and integration confusion across the merged entity. The product is still well-regarded; the support experience is in transition. A 2026 buyer reading 2023 reviews is reading old information.

Bloomerang acquired Qgiv in 2023. Early signals suggest stable satisfaction, but pricing implications for existing customers are not yet fully clear and the integration is ongoing.

Salesforce's NPSP-to-Agentforce migration is the largest forced re-implementation the category has seen in a decade. Customers are not upgrading — they are rebuilding. Satisfaction data on the new platform is too sparse to draw conclusions.

When evaluating any nonprofit CRM, check the date range on the reviews you are reading. Filter to the last 12 months. A platform's 2023 rating may not reflect its 2026 reality, especially post-acquisition.

## A Practical Evaluation Framework

The review data supports a five-step approach for buyers who want to avoid the most common mistakes.

**Step 1: Define the actual user.** Who logs in every day? If the answer is "the development director and one part-time bookkeeper," the implementation profile is very different from "the development ops team and a Salesforce admin." Pick from the tier that matches the user, not the tier that matches the wishlist.

**Step 2: Filter reviews to the last 12 months.** Three of the major platforms have been in transition since 2023. Older reviews encode an experience that may no longer exist.

**Step 3: Read the ease-of-use sub-score before the overall rating.** This sub-score predicts post-implementation satisfaction better than any other data point in the public review corpus.

**Step 4: Price the implementation, not the subscription.** A $99/month tool that needs a $25,000 consultant to configure is a $25,099 first-year decision. A $499/month tool that works on day one is a $5,988 first-year decision. The subscription line is rarely the dominant cost.

**Step 5: Map grant management onto the same evaluation.** Most pure donor CRMs treat grants as an add-on or skip them entirely. If the organization manages active federal or foundation grants, the right system tracks restricted-fund balances, reporting deadlines, and budget-versus-actual against grant budgets in the same place donor records live. Splitting that across two systems creates a reconciliation problem that grows with the grant portfolio.

## The Single Most Common Mistake

The dominant failure mode in nonprofit CRM selection is buying for the demo, not for the staff.

Demos showcase the platform's ceiling. Daily use happens at the platform's floor. The 20.1 percent of nonprofits that rate their CRM ineffective did not buy a bad product — they bought a product that required staffing, training, or implementation budget the organization did not have. The product never reached the level shown in the sales call.

The countermeasure is to treat the user, not the feature list, as the binding constraint. If the development director cannot configure a new segment, build a saved view, or pull a board-ready report without help, the tool is the wrong fit, regardless of what the demo showed.

## What to Do This Week

For organizations currently evaluating a platform, pull the last 12 months of reviews on Capterra and G2 for every shortlisted product. Sort by the ease-of-use sub-score, not the overall rating. Verify the implementation timeline and cost in writing before committing.

For organizations already on a platform that scores below 4/5, the right move is rarely an immediate switch. The right move is identifying which specific workflows are broken — segment building, gift entry, grant tracking, board reporting — and quantifying the time cost. A platform decision should rest on hours saved per month, not vendor preference.
